"Vessel Engineer" is not a title defined in law for a small workboat. The Workboat Code sets an engineering qualification that someone on board must hold, and what it is depends on the vessel's installed power and how far from a safe haven she works. On a small vessel that person may be the master or a deckhand. The duty to keep the vessel fit to operate without danger is on the owner and the master (Small Workboats and Pilot Boats Regulations 2023, regulation 18). This post is how the Company and the master meet it for machinery.

Authority of the post

The organisation document GSS-COM-ORG-001 Draft 3 has no entry for this post, so the list below is proposed. Authority reserved to the Managing Director is not carried by this post or by anyone standing in for it.

  • Stop their own work, or work they are involved in, where they believe it is unsafe (the universal authority in the organisation document, Part 7).
  • Take machinery or equipment out of service and make it safe (the organisation document says anyone may do so, MST-R05).
  • Refuse to start work on machinery until it is isolated and, where needed, a permit to work is in force.
  • Raise a defect or a technical concern directly with the Operations Manager.
  • Limit: no authority over whether the vessel sails, which is the master's decision, and no authority to defer a statutory examination or change a planned maintenance interval without the Operations Manager.

11. Job advert

Workboat Engineer

Gareloch Support Services (Plant) Ltd, Rosneath, Argyll and Bute

Gareloch Support Services is a marine and diving support company working from the Gare Loch, with its own workboat fleet, a commercial diving team, a marine civils capability and a boatyard and marina.

The role

You will be the assigned engineer for one of our workboats, working up to 150 miles offshore. Answering to the master, you keep the engines, steering, fuel and electrical systems safe, maintained and recorded, and advise the master on the vessel's technical state.

What you will do

  • Carry out and record the daily, weekly and planned maintenance checks
  • Take engine log readings and track running hours against the maintenance schedule
  • Complete machinery checks before every departure
  • Find and fix faults, and say plainly when a defect affects seaworthiness
  • Run isolation, lock out and permits for work on machinery
  • Manage fuel, lubricating oil and oily waste without pollution
  • Send the weekly engine room report and look ahead
  • Take the engine room part in drills and emergencies
  • Help on deck when the job needs it

What you will bring

  • An Approved Engine Course certificate, or a Marine Engine Operator Licence where the vessel and area need one (we will confirm for each vessel)
  • A valid seafarer medical: ENG 1 for work beyond 60 miles, or ML5 for work closer in
  • Sea survival, fire fighting and first aid certificates
  • Hands on experience of marine diesel engines, hydraulics and vessel electrics
  • Training in isolation, permits to work and enclosed space entry

It would help if you also have

  • An engine manufacturer's course
  • A Senior Marine Engine Operator Licence
  • Awareness of cyber security for control systems

We support licence revalidation, refresher training and progression from the Approved Engine Course to the Marine Engine Operator Licence.
