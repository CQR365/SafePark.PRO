RISK ASSESSMENT

Antifoul, coatings and hull washing

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionPressure washing hulls on lift out, scraping and sanding antifouling paint and other coatings, applying antifouling, primers, paints and varnishes by brush, roller and spray, the use of solvents and thinners, and the handling of wash water, scrapings, waste paint and contaminated materials, whether the work is done by yard personnel, by customers or by customers' own contractors
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe wash down area at the boat hoist dock or slipway, the hard standing and lay up areas, sheds and any paint store at the Silvers Marine boatyard and marina at Silverhills, and boats on the berths where coating work is done afloat. The general assessment of hazardous substances is in Hazardous substances (COSHH), waste in Waste handling and storage and spills in Oil and chemical spill response; this assessment covers what is particular to antifouling, coatings and hull washing.
Main equipment usedPressure washers and lances, scrapers, sanders with dust extraction, industrial vacuum cleaners, groundsheets and tarpaulins, portable bunding and any wash water collection or filtration system, brushes, rollers and trays, spray equipment where used, paints, antifouling products, thinners and solvents, a flammable liquids cabinet, waste containers for hazardous waste, and respiratory and other protective equipment. What products and equipment are held, and whether a wash water system exists, are not known to the assessor and are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskYard personnel who wash, scrape, sand and paint, customers and their families working on their own boats, customers' contractors, people on neighbouring boats and passing through the yard, waste contractors, and the water environment of the Gare Loch.
Related permit or procedurePRC 05 COSHH Assessment for every product used by yard personnel, read with PRC 12 Waste and Garbage Management and PRC 24 Oil and Chemical Spill Control. To confirm whether a draft procedure specific to coatings work and hull washing at Silvers is intended. A hot work permit under RA 27 Hot Work applies to burning off paint or using a heat gun on coatings.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Hazardous substancesControl of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002. HSE guidance states that employers must identify the hazardous substances in the workplace, including those created by the work such as dust from sanding, assess who is exposed and how, control the exposure following the hierarchy from substitution through containment and ventilation to protective equipment, and provide monitoring, health surveillance and training where needed. Antifouling paint, its dust, solvents and two pack coatings are within this.
Antifouling productsHSE regulates antifouling products as biocides, and its label guidance under the Control of Pesticides Regulations 1986 distinguishes products for amateur use from those for professional use, each with its own label and conditions. The duty to use a product only in line with its label conditions is stated in the boating body's guidance read for this assessment and is to be confirmed against the Regulations.
IsocyanatesHSE guidance on two pack isocyanate paints states that spraying them is a leading cause of occupational asthma, that spraying must be done in a spray booth or room with extraction, that air fed breathing apparatus must be worn, and that health surveillance and biological monitoring are needed. The guidance is written for vehicle repair and is applied here as the standard for any spraying of two pack coatings on boats.
Fire and explosionDangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002. HSE lists solvents and paints among the dangerous substances covered, and guidance requires the risks to be assessed, controlled and ignition sources avoided where a flammable atmosphere may form.
Environment, ScotlandTo confirm. SEPA is the regulator for discharges to the water environment and for waste. No SEPA page on hull washing or antifouling waste could be opened, so the authorisation needed for wash water and the classification of antifouling waste in Scotland are not stated here as requirements. The industry leaflet read for this assessment treats wash water as contaminated and antifouling waste as hazardous.
General duties and occupierHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, cited as the general framework and not read for this assessment. Occupiers' Liability (Scotland) Act 1960, section 2: the occupier owes persons entering the premises such care as is reasonable in the circumstances, which bears on bystanders exposed to dust and spray from other people's work.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Coatings work and hull washing carried on with no system: products and their hazards not listed, no COSHH assessments, no rule on which methods are allowed in the yard, and no control over what customers and contractors use

Potential consequence. Uncontrolled exposure to biocides, dust, solvents and sensitisers over years. Occupational asthma, dermatitis and other ill health, pollution of the loch and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, customers, contractors, bystanders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. A list is kept of every antifouling product, paint, thinner, stripper and cleaner used by yard personnel, with its safety data sheet and a COSHH assessment under PRC 05 that covers the task as it is done at Silvers and not only the product. Products are chosen for the lowest hazard that will do the job, and where a product is labelled for professional use only it is used only by trained yard personnel in line with the label. The yard sets and publishes its permitted methods: which preparation methods are allowed, where spraying may and may not be done, where washing is done and how waste is handled. Those rules apply equally to yard personnel, customers and contractors. One post is responsible for the paint store, the equipment and the waste arrangements. Yard personnel are trained in the assessments and in the use, fitting and care of their protective equipment.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Compile the product list and safety data sheets, complete the task based COSHH assessments and publish the yard's permitted methods. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Dust and flakes from scraping and sanding antifouling paint and old coatings, including older paints whose content is not known

Potential consequence. Inhalation and ingestion of copper compounds and biocides, and possibly of lead or organotin from old layers. Eye, skin and respiratory irritation, longer term ill health. Dust settling on neighbouring boats, cars and the ground.

Persons at risk. Person doing the work, bystanders, neighbouring customers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Dry sanding and dry scraping of antifouling without extraction is not permitted anywhere in the yard. Preparation is by wet abrading, or by a sander or scraper connected to an industrial vacuum cleaner with a filter suited to hazardous dust, as the industry guidance recommends. Where the age or type of the old coating is unknown it is treated as containing the more harmful substances until shown otherwise, and removal back to bare hull is planned as a separate job with its own assessment. Burning off antifouling is not permitted, and a heat gun is not used on it. Blasting of any kind is done only by a specialist contractor with full enclosure and the yard's written agreement. A groundsheet is laid beneath the work to catch debris, the work is not done in strong wind, and neighbouring boats and people are kept out of the area or the work is screened. Dust is removed with the vacuum or a damp cloth and never by dry sweeping or compressed air. A respirator with a particulate filter of the class the COSHH assessment specifies, eye protection, gloves and a disposable coverall with a hood are worn. Eating, drinking and smoking are kept away from the work, and hands and face are washed before breaks.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Provide vacuum connected sanders and scrapers for yard personnel, decide whether the yard will hire or lend them to customers, and arrange face fit testing for those who wear tight fitting respirators. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Skin and eye contact with antifouling, paints, strippers and solvents during mixing, brush and roller application overhead, and cleaning of equipment

Potential consequence. Dermatitis, skin sensitisation, chemical burns from strippers, eye injury from splashes when painting above head height.

Persons at risk. Person doing the work

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Products are stirred, decanted and thinned over a tray in a well ventilated place and containers are kept closed between uses. Application is by roller on an extension pole where that keeps the hands and face away from the surface. Chemical resistant gloves of the type the safety data sheet specifies, eye protection and a coverall are worn for mixing, applying and cleaning, and a hood or cap for work beneath the hull. Skin is cleaned with a proper cleanser and never with thinners. Contaminated clothing is changed at once. Brushes and rollers are cleaned with the smallest quantity of solvent in a closed container, or are disposed of as contaminated waste. An eyewash station is available near where coatings are used. Yard personnel who use these products regularly are covered by skin checks where the COSHH assessment calls for them.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Confirm the glove types against the safety data sheets, position eyewash where coatings are used and decide whether skin checks are needed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Spray application of coatings, and in particular two pack paints and varnishes containing isocyanates, in the open yard, in a shed or under a tent

Potential consequence. Occupational asthma and sensitisation, which is permanent and can end a working life. Dermatitis. Mist drifting onto bystanders and neighbouring boats.

Persons at risk. Sprayer, helpers, bystanders, neighbouring customers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Antifouling is applied by brush or roller and is not sprayed unless the product label permits spraying, the label conditions can be met and the Silvers Yard Manager has agreed the method. Two pack isocyanate coatings are applied by brush or roller wherever the finish allows, with the precautions on the safety data sheet. Spraying of isocyanate coatings is not carried out in the open yard, in a shed or under an improvised tent. It is done only where the conditions in HSE guidance can be met: an enclosure with extraction that holds the mist, a known clearance time during which nobody enters unprotected, air fed breathing apparatus worn by the sprayer and kept on until the mist has cleared, air for the apparatus of breathing quality, and health surveillance and biological monitoring for those exposed. Where the yard cannot meet those conditions the work is given to a specialist with the facilities or is not accepted. Customers and their contractors are not permitted to spray two pack coatings in the yard. Any other spraying is assessed job by job, screened and timed so that nobody is downwind.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish whether any spraying of coatings is done at Silvers, by whom, with what products and in what facility, and stop the spraying of isocyanate coatings until the conditions in HSE guidance are shown to be met. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Solvent vapour building up in an enclosed or poorly ventilated space: inside a hull, cabin, locker, bilge or tank, under a winter cover or tent, or in a paint store

Potential consequence. Dizziness, loss of consciousness and death from vapour, or from lack of oxygen. Collapse unseen when working alone. Fire or explosion in the space.

Persons at risk. Person doing the work, would be rescuers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Solvent based coatings, strippers and adhesives are not used inside a hull or under a closed cover without a planned method. Water based or low solvent products are chosen for interior work where they will do the job. Hatches, ports and covers are opened and mechanical ventilation that is safe for use in flammable vapour is set up to draw fresh air through the space, and it runs during the work and during drying. The quantity taken into the space is the least needed. Nobody works alone inside a boat with solvent based products; a second person stays outside in contact with the worker. Where the space is a tank, a deep bilge or any place where vapour can collect and escape is difficult, it is treated as a confined space, RA 37 Confined Spaces applies in full and the work does not start without an entry permit. A filter respirator is not relied on in such a space. Anyone who feels light headed leaves at once.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Identify the interior coating jobs that yard personnel carry out and write the method for each, including the ventilation equipment needed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Fire and explosion from flammable paints, thinners and solvents, solvent soaked rags, static and ignition sources during mixing, application and storage

Potential consequence. Flash fire at the work, fire in the paint store or in a stored boat spreading to others. Burns. Death.

Persons at risk. Person doing the work, all persons in the yard

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Stocks of flammable coatings and solvents are kept to a minimum in a ventilated flammable liquids store or cabinet away from ignition sources, exits and the LPG installation, and only the day's need is taken to the boat. Containers are kept closed. Smoking, naked flame, hot work, grinding, heaters and unprotected electrical equipment are kept away from mixing, application and drying, and coating work and hot work are not carried on at the same time on the same or neighbouring boats. Lighting, fans and vacuum cleaners used where flammable vapour may be present are suitable for it. Used rags, wipes and roller sleeves are put in a closed metal container at the end of each job and removed from boats and sheds daily, since some coatings and oils can heat and ignite on rags. Spills are absorbed at once. A suitable fire extinguisher is at hand. The storage and use of flammable coatings is included in the dangerous substances assessment and the premises fire risk assessment. Fuel and oil storage and Fire and emergency evacuation apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm where and in what quantity flammable coatings and solvents are stored, provide a suitable store or cabinet and closed metal bins for used rags, and include the store in the dangerous substances assessment. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Pressure washing hulls: the high pressure jet, debris and fouling thrown back, electricity and water together, hoses and wet weeded ground, noise, and working beside a boat held in the slings

Potential consequence. Injection or laceration injury from the jet, eye injury, electric shock, slips and falls, hearing damage. A person struck or crushed if the boat or hoist moves.

Persons at risk. Operator, hoist team, bystanders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Washing is done in the designated wash down area and nowhere else. The boat is landed on keel blocks with the hoist isolated before anyone washes beneath or beside it, as set out in Boat hoist: lifting, launching and moving boats. The washer is maintained and checked before use, including the lance, trigger lock, hoses and couplings, and a damaged hose is replaced and not taped. An electrically powered washer is supplied through a residual current device from a socket suited to outdoor use, with leads kept out of standing water; Electrical work and isolation applies. The jet is never pointed at a person or used to clean boots or clothing, and the lance is held with both hands from a firm footing on the ground, not from a ladder. Other people are kept clear of the wash down area by barriers. The operator wears a face shield or goggles, waterproof clothing, gloves and safety boots with a good grip, and hearing protection where the noise assessment requires it. The area is cleaned of weed and fouling after each wash. A jet injury is treated as a medical emergency even where the wound looks small.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the type and condition of the pressure washers, the electrical supply to the wash down area and whether noise has been assessed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Wash water, fouling, paint flakes and scrapings running to the slipway, surface drains or the ground and into the loch

Potential consequence. Biocides and paint solids entering the water environment. Harm to marine life, complaint, loss of standing with the regulator and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. The water environment, the Company

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Hull washing is confined to a wash down area where the water can be collected, by a contained and drained pad with settlement and filtration or, where that does not exist, by portable bunding, as the industry guidance recommends. Solids are captured before the water leaves the area, the traps and filters are cleaned out on a set routine, and the sludge is handled as contaminated waste. Wash water is not allowed to run down the slipway or into surface water drains. Boats are not scrubbed on the foreshore, on drying grids or in the water in a way that removes paint. Preparation on the hard standing is done over a groundsheet that is folded and emptied into the waste container at the end of each day, and work stops in rain or wind that would carry debris away. Drain covers and a spill kit are at hand where coatings are used. Whether a SEPA authorisation is needed for the wash water discharge is to be confirmed before this control is treated as complete.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Establish where wash water goes at present, ask SEPA what authorisation the discharge needs, and specify a collection and filtration arrangement for the wash down area. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Handling and storing waste paint, part used tins, thinners, used rollers, groundsheets, filters, dust from vacuum cleaners and contaminated protective equipment

Potential consequence. Skin contact and dust exposure when emptying equipment. Fire in a waste container. Hazardous waste in general skips, refusal by the waste contractor and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, customers, waste contractors

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Separate, labelled, lidded containers for antifouling and paint waste are provided where the work is done and are available to customers, so that tins, scrapings, used rollers and brushes, disposable gloves and coveralls, groundsheets and filters do not go into the general waste. Liquid paint and solvent waste is kept in closed containers in a bunded store and is never poured to drain or onto the ground. Vacuum cleaners are emptied and filters changed in the open air with a respirator, gloves and a coverall, into a sealed bag. Waste is collected by a contractor authorised to take it and the consignment paperwork is kept. How antifouling waste is classified in Scotland and the paperwork that follows are to be confirmed with SEPA or the waste contractor. Waste handling and storage and PRC 12 apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Provide the hazardous waste containers, confirm the classification and consignment arrangements with the waste contractor, and tell customers where the containers are. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Customers and their contractors doing their own preparation and painting: dry sanding, unsuitable or no protective equipment, professional products used by amateurs, spraying, and dust and vapour reaching people on neighbouring boats

Potential consequence. Ill health to the customer and to bystanders including children, contamination of neighbouring boats and the ground, pollution, complaints and claims.

Persons at risk. Customers and their families, customers' contractors, neighbouring customers, yard personnel

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The yard's permitted methods are written into the storage terms and the customer yard rules and are given to every customer before the boat comes ashore: no dry sanding or dry scraping of antifouling without extraction, a groundsheet under all preparation and painting, no burning off, no spraying of two pack coatings and no other spraying without agreement, use of products only as their labels allow, waste into the hazardous waste containers, and children and pets kept away from the work. The industry leaflet on protecting, collecting and disposing is given out with the rules. The yard makes compliance practical by offering vacuum connected tools for hire or loan where it has decided to do so, by providing the waste containers and by pointing customers to the wash down service. Customers' contractors are approved and inducted under PRC 19 and show their COSHH assessments for the products they bring. Yard personnel walking the yard stop work that breaks the rules, and the Silvers Yard Manager follows it up under the storage terms.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Further action required. Publish the permitted methods in the storage terms and yard rules, decide on tool hire or loan, and brief yard personnel on their authority to stop unsafe work. The residual rating stays high because the control depends on the conduct of people the yard does not supervise. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Working beneath and beside the hull between props and cradle legs: overhead work, crouching and kneeling, vibration from sanders, trailing leads and hoses, and disturbing a support

Potential consequence. Neck, shoulder and back injury, hand arm vibration, trips, electric shock from a damaged lead. A prop knocked or moved and the boat left unstable.

Persons at risk. Person doing the work, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Overhead work is broken into short spells with changes of task, and long handled rollers, low stools and kneeling pads are used to reduce reaching and crouching. Sanders are chosen for low vibration, kept in good condition and used within the exposure limits worked out under RA 36 Vibration. Electrical tools are low voltage or battery powered where they can be, and mains tools and leads are checked before use and protected by a residual current device; RA 35 Power Tools applies. Leads and hoses are run clear of walkways and of props. No prop or pad is moved to reach the hull beneath it except by authorised yard personnel using the method in Boats stored ashore and work on them, which adds a prop before one is taken away. Lighting beneath the hull is adequate and is of a type safe for use where solvent vapour may be present.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Assess vibration exposure for yard personnel who sand hulls regularly and record the result. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The product list, safety data sheets and COSHH assessments are reviewed at least annually and whenever a product or method changes.
  • The Silvers Yard Manager walks the lay up areas weekly in the painting season to check that the permitted methods are followed by yard personnel, customers and contractors, and records breaches.
  • Wash down traps, filters and bunding are inspected and cleaned on a set routine and the record is kept.
  • Respirators are examined monthly and face fit test records are kept for each wearer; where air fed apparatus or extraction is used, its test records are kept.
  • Health surveillance results, at group level, are reported to the Risk and Compliance Manager, and any case of asthma or dermatitis linked to the work is investigated under PRC 01.
  • Hazardous waste consignment records are checked each quarter against the waste collected.
  • Every spill, release of wash water to the loch, complaint about dust or overspray and case of ill health is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.

Emergency arrangements

  • Paint, antifouling or solvent in the eyes: irrigate with clean water for at least 10 minutes, holding the eyelids open, and obtain medical attention with the safety data sheet.
  • Product on the skin: remove contaminated clothing and wash with soap and water or skin cleanser. Do not use thinners.
  • Person dizzy, drowsy or collapsed where solvents are in use: raise the alarm and move the person to fresh air if that can be done without entering a space where vapour has collected. Do not enter a tank, bilge or closed cabin to rescue; follow RA 37 Confined Spaces and call 999.
  • Wheezing, chest tightness or breathlessness during or after coating work: stop work, move to fresh air, obtain medical advice and report it the same day, since these may be the first signs of sensitisation.
  • High pressure jet injury: call 999 or go to hospital at once and say that it is a high pressure injection injury, even where the wound looks minor.
  • Fire involving paints or solvents: raise the alarm, call the Fire and Rescue Service, evacuate, and tackle the fire only at the incipient stage with a suitable extinguisher and a clear way out.
  • Spill of paint or solvent, or wash water escaping towards the loch: stop the source, protect drains and the shore, contain and absorb it, and follow the spill response in PRC 24 and Oil and chemical spill response. Tell the Risk and Compliance Manager, who decides whether SEPA is to be told.

PPE requirements

For scraping and sanding: a respirator with a particulate filter of the class set by the COSHH assessment, face fit tested where it is tight fitting, eye protection, chemical resistant gloves and a disposable hooded coverall. For brush and roller application: chemical resistant gloves of the type on the safety data sheet, eye protection, a coverall, and a respirator with the vapour filter the assessment specifies where ventilation alone is not enough. For spraying isocyanate coatings: air fed breathing apparatus, as HSE guidance requires, and only in a facility that meets that guidance. For pressure washing: a face shield or goggles, waterproofs, gloves, safety boots with a good grip and hearing protection where required. Protective equipment is the last control in each case and does not replace extraction, wet methods and ventilation.
