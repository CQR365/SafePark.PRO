Risk assessments / Attending an incident scene and leading the response

RISK ASSESSMENT

Attending an incident scene and leading the response

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionGoing to the scene of an accident, spill, fire or other emergency and taking charge of it or helping at it: the first approach, making the scene safe, using a fire extinguisher, sweeping an area and helping people out, giving first aid including at a spill or release, taking charge until relieved, leading the response from ashore for a vessel emergency, preserving the scene and evidence, returning to investigate, and the effect on those involved afterwards
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins. Here the supplement is the dynamic assessment made by the person in charge on arrival and repeated as the scene changes.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Managing Director and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeAny place where GSS works: the offices, stores and workshops at Silverhills, the Silvers yard, sheds, pontoons and berths, the quayside, civils sites, dive sites and the vessels alongside or at sea, and the office or room from which a vessel emergency is managed from ashore. The emergency plans themselves are in RA 30 Emergency Preparedness, the new draft assessment Fire and emergency evacuation and the new draft assessment Oil and chemical spill response. This assessment covers the risk to the people who respond, which those cover only in part.
Main equipment usedFire extinguishers and fire blankets, the fire alarm, first aid kits and any defibrillator, eyewash, spill kits and drain covers, barrier tape, cones and signs, radios and telephones, torches, high visibility clothing, lifejackets and rescue throw lines, the emergency contact list, the incident log, and a camera or telephone for recording the scene.
Persons at riskSite supervisors, first aiders, fire wardens, department managers, the Risk and Compliance Manager, the Operations Manager, the Designated Person Ashore, the Managing Director, Masters and crew, dive supervisors, and colleagues, customers and members of the public who go to help without being asked.
Related permit or procedurePRC 06 Emergency Preparedness and PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up, read with PRC 24 Oil and Chemical Spill Control and PRC 15 Hazardous Materials Emergency Plan. All are drafts. To confirm which of them sets out who takes charge at a scene and when that person is relieved.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Responding to an emergency is work, and the people asked to do it are owed the same assessment, training and safe system as for any other task. Section 3 of the Act extends the duty to customers, contractors and the public who may be affected by how the response is run.
Emergency proceduresManagement of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, regulation 8. The employer must establish procedures to be followed in the event of serious and imminent danger, nominate enough competent persons to carry out evacuation, and restrict access to danger areas to those who have been adequately instructed. The procedures must allow people to stop work and go at once to a place of safety, and must prevent a return while the danger persists save in exceptional cases set out in the procedures.
Fire, ScotlandFire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006. Regulation 12 requires the duty holder, where necessary, to take measures for fighting fire, to nominate competent persons to implement them, to ensure that their number, their training and the equipment available to them are adequate, and to arrange the necessary contacts with the emergency services. Fire wardens and anybody expected to use an extinguisher are nominated persons for this purpose.
Reporting ashoreReporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013. HSE guidance states that the responsible person must notify the enforcing authority without delay of a death, a specified injury to a worker, a member of the public taken from the scene to hospital for treatment, or a dangerous occurrence, with a report within 10 days, and within 15 days where a worker is incapacitated for more than seven days. The Regulations themselves were not read. No duty under them to preserve the scene was read, and whether the police or HSE require a scene to be left undisturbed after a death or serious injury in Scotland is to be confirmed.
Reporting on vesselsMerchant Shipping (Accident Reporting and Investigation) Regulations 2026, in force from 15 May 2026, which revoke the 2012 Regulations. Regulation 6 requires the master and the ship's owner to notify the Chief Inspector of Marine Accidents as soon as possible after an accident and by the quickest means available. MCA notice MGN 564 (M+F) Amendment 2 explains what counts as a marine casualty and a marine incident and gives the reporting telephone line of the Marine Accident Investigation Branch.
Evidence on vesselsRegulation 10 of the 2026 Regulations requires that charts, log books, recorded information, documents and relevant equipment are kept and that no alteration is made to recordings or entries, for 30 days after notification unless the Chief Inspector releases them sooner or gives notice of an investigation. This bears directly on what a manager attending a vessel after an accident may and may not do.
First aidHSE guidance states that an employer must provide a suitably stocked first aid kit, an appointed person to take charge of first aid arrangements, and information for employees, on the basis of an assessment of need that takes account of workplace hazards requiring special arrangements. The first aid requirements for vessels under merchant shipping law were not read and are to be confirmed.
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply to the response aboard a vessel, where the Master is in command of it, enforced by the MCA. HSE lists cases where reporting under other legislation removes the duty to report under the 2013 Regulations, including merchant shipping legislation; which regime covers an injury on a vessel alongside, or to shore staff aboard, is to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Rushing to the scene of an accident without stopping to look: the hazard that injured the first person is still present (live electricity, a suspended or unstable load, a boat shifting on its supports, moving plant, gas or vapour, traffic, deep or cold water)

Potential consequence. The responder becomes a second casualty. Electrocution, crushing, being overcome, drowning. Death. The first casualty is then not helped either.

Persons at risk. First aiders, site supervisors, managers, the Risk and Compliance Manager, colleagues and bystanders who run to help

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Everybody is taught one rule at induction and it is repeated in every first aid, warden and supervisor briefing: stop, look and ask what hurt the casualty before going in. The first person there raises the alarm before doing anything else, so that help is coming whatever happens next. The source is dealt with from a place of safety where that can be done: power isolated at a point away from the casualty, plant stopped and its key removed, a lift halted and the area under the load kept clear, traffic stopped by a person placed well back. Nobody goes under a suspended load, under a boat that has moved on its cradle or props, into an excavation that has slipped, or into the water, to reach a casualty. A person who cannot be reached safely is spoken to, reassured and watched until the emergency services arrive, and that is treated as the right decision, not a failure. Bystanders and customers are moved back and one person is given the job of keeping them back. The approach is made from upwind and uphill where a substance is involved. Responders carry a torch and a means of communication, and wear high visibility clothing and the footwear and head protection the area requires.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Add the stop and look rule and the isolation points for each area to induction and to first aider, warden and supervisor briefings, and mark the main isolation points on a site plan. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with each department manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Nobody clearly in charge at the scene: several people giving orders or none, the emergency services not called or not met, and roles assumed without being agreed

Potential consequence. Delay in calling for help, conflicting instructions, people sent into danger, the casualty moved when they should not be. A worse outcome for the casualty and injury to responders. Death.

Persons at risk. All responders, the casualty, the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The emergency arrangements state who takes charge in each area and at each time of day: the site supervisor or the senior person present in the yard and on civils sites, the Master aboard a vessel, the dive supervisor at a dive site, and the senior person in the building in the offices. That person says aloud that they are in charge, stands back from the hands on work so that they can see the whole scene, and gives named people single tasks: call 999, meet the ambulance at the gate, fetch the first aid kit and defibrillator, stop the plant, keep people back, keep the log. A manager who arrives later does not take over by rank; they ask for a briefing, and either take charge by saying so plainly or support the person who has it. The Risk and Compliance Manager attends to advise and to begin the record, and does not take command of the scene from the supervisor. When the emergency services arrive, the person in charge briefs their officer, hands over control of the scene and stays available. The caller to 999 gives the site address, the entrance to use and what has happened, and a person is sent to the entrance to guide the crew in. Roles are practised in drills in each area, including out of hours.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. State in PRC 06 who takes charge in each area and out of hours, issue a one page action card for the person in charge, and test it in a drill in the yard, the office and aboard. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Operations Manager and the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Using a fire extinguisher: tackling a fire that is too large, with the wrong type, with no way out behind, or staying too long

Potential consequence. Burns, smoke inhalation, being cut off by fire or smoke. Electric shock or a violent flare up from the wrong extinguisher. Death.

Persons at risk. Fire wardens, site supervisors, any person who picks up an extinguisher

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The first actions at any fire are to raise the alarm and see that the Fire and Rescue Service is called. An extinguisher is used only after that, only by a person trained in its use, and only on a small fire in its first stage, with the person's back to a clear way out. Nobody is required to fight a fire, and the instruction is to leave if in any doubt. The attempt stops and the person leaves, closing the door behind them, if the fire is not out with one extinguisher, if smoke begins to fill the space, or if the way out is threatened. Extinguishers are never used on a fire involving a gas cylinder, the bulk LPG tank, a fuel tank or drum store, or a battery bank or large lithium battery: the area is evacuated and the Fire and Rescue Service is told what is involved. Training is practical, includes discharging an extinguisher, and covers which type is used on which fire and why water and foam are kept off live electrical equipment and burning liquids. Extinguishers are of types suited to the risks in each area, are placed on exit routes so that the user is already between the fire and the door, and are maintained. Fire aboard a vessel is fought under the Master's orders and the vessel's own arrangements.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm who has had practical extinguisher training and when, arrange training for all wardens and supervisors, and check that extinguisher types and positions match the risks in the yard, sheds, stores and offices. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with department managers. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Sweeping an area and helping people out in a fire: searching rooms, sheds, boats ashore and pontoons, helping a person who is slow or unwilling to leave, and going back in

Potential consequence. The warden is overcome by smoke or cut off by fire while others are already safe. Death. Injury on stairs, ladders and staging in the hurry.

Persons at risk. Fire wardens, site supervisors, persons they are assisting

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Each warden has a defined area small enough to sweep on the way out in a short time, and the sweep is a check made while leaving, moving away from the fire, not a search towards it. The warden calls out, looks into each room and shed bay, closes doors behind them and does not open a door that is hot or has smoke coming from it. A warden does not climb onto a boat ashore or go below on one to look for people: they shout and sound a horn or whistle, and report at the assembly point that the boat could not be checked. Nobody goes back into a building or onto a vessel once out, for people, property or pets, and anybody thought to be missing is reported to the Fire and Rescue Service with their last known position. Persons who need help to leave are identified in advance, with a plan for each that does not depend on one helper. A person who refuses to leave is told once and left, and reported. The count at the assembly point uses the sign in record for visitors, customers and contractors as well as staff. Wardens are trained, wear a high visibility vest so that they can be found, and practise the sweep in drills, including a drill with customers and contractors on site. The arrangements in the new draft assessment Fire and emergency evacuation apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Define the sweep area for each warden on a plan, including the sheds, boats stored ashore and pontoons, and decide how persons on boats are to be warned. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager and the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Giving first aid at a spill or release: the casualty is lying in or soaked with fuel, oil, solvent, antifouling, resin, battery acid or another chemical, or is in a space where vapour or gas is present

Potential consequence. The first aider is contaminated through the skin or eyes, breathes the vapour or is overcome. Fire if fuel soaked clothing meets an ignition source. Chemical burns, collapse, death in an enclosed space.

Persons at risk. First aiders, site supervisors, crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The first aider finds out what the substance is before touching the casualty, from the label, the container or the person in charge, and the safety data sheet is fetched for the ambulance crew. Where the casualty can move, they are called out to clean ground upwind. Where they cannot, the first aider goes in only if the substance and the space allow it with the protection available: chemical resistant gloves, eye protection and a disposable apron or coverall from the spill kit as a minimum. A first aider does not enter a tank, hold, bilge, locker or other enclosed space, or a visible gas or vapour cloud, to reach a casualty; RA 37 Confined Spaces applies and the Fire and Rescue Service is called. Ignition sources are kept away from fuel soaked clothing, which is removed or cut off and left at the scene. Eyes and skin are flushed with plenty of water for the time given on the data sheet, with the run off directed away from the first aider. Mouth to mouth breathing is not given where the casualty has swallowed or breathed in a chemical; a pocket mask or chest compressions only are used. Afterwards the first aider washes, changes contaminated clothing and is checked. The spill itself is dealt with by others under the new draft assessment Oil and chemical spill response.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Check that gloves, eye protection, coveralls and a pocket mask are held with the first aid and spill kits, that eyewash or running water is within reach of the places where chemicals are handled, and that first aiders know where the safety data sheets are. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with department managers and each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Taking charge of a spill or release until relieved: staying close to the substance to stop the source, protect drains and direct others, for longer than intended

Potential consequence. Skin contact, breathing vapour, slips on the spilled liquid, fire where the substance is flammable or gas is escaping. Burns, death in the worst case.

Persons at risk. Site supervisors, yard personnel, Masters and crew, the Risk and Compliance Manager on arrival

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The supervisor first decides whether the spill is one the people on site can deal with safely, using the substance, the quantity, whether it is still flowing, and whether there is any smell of gas or sign of vapour. A small spill of a known oil or diesel is contained with the spill kit under the new draft assessment Oil and chemical spill response. For petrol, solvent, an unknown substance, a large or continuing release, or any escape of gas, the supervisor clears the area, keeps people upwind, stops plant, vehicles and hot work nearby, and calls the Fire and Rescue Service: nobody stays to contain it. The supervisor directs from the edge of the affected area and gives the hands on tasks to people wearing the protective equipment from the kit. The source is stopped only where the valve or container can be reached without walking through the liquid. The Operations Manager or the department manager is told at once and relieves the supervisor or confirms that they are to continue, so that the supervisor is not left in charge for hours. Those who have been close to the spill are rotated, wash and change afterwards.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the threshold in plain words between a spill handled on site and one that requires evacuation and the Fire and Rescue Service, and brief supervisors and Masters on it. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Operations Manager and the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Attempting a rescue from the water, from a height or from under a load without equipment or training, and moving or lifting a casualty

Potential consequence. The rescuer enters cold water and drowns, falls, or is crushed. Back injury from lifting a casualty. Contact with blood and body fluids. Death.

Persons at risk. First aiders, yard personnel, site supervisors, crew, customers who try to help

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. For a person in the water the order is fixed: shout and point, throw a line or a lifebuoy, reach with a pole or boathook from a secure position while wearing a lifejacket, and use a boat with a trained crew. Nobody enters the water from the shore or a pontoon to make a rescue. Throw lines, lifebuoys and ladders are in place along the pontoons and quay under RA 18 Working on or Near Water, and people know where they are. A casualty at height or under a load is not moved by hand unless they are in immediate danger where they lie; the Fire and Rescue Service has the equipment and is called early. A casualty is moved only as far as needed, by enough people, using a stretcher or a blanket lift. A recovered person who has been in cold water is kept flat and handled gently. First aiders wear disposable gloves for any contact with blood or body fluids, use a face shield or pocket mask for rescue breaths, cover their own cuts, and wash afterwards; a splash to the eyes or mouth or a needle or sharp injury is reported and medical advice is sought the same day. RA 56 First Aid applies to the treatment.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Check the positions and condition of throw lines, lifebuoys and ladders, and arrange a practical man overboard and water rescue drill at the pontoons and quay. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager and Operations Manager, verified by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Leading the response to a vessel emergency or major incident from ashore: decisions under pressure on incomplete information, over many hours, with calls from the vessel, the Coastguard, the client, families and the media, and travel to the scene at short notice

Potential consequence. A wrong or late decision that worsens the outcome for the crew. Conflicting messages to the vessel. Exhaustion of the leader, and a road crash on the way to the scene or home. Death.

Persons at risk. Managing Director, Operations Manager, Designated Person Ashore, Deputy Designated Person Ashore, Risk and Compliance Manager, Office Manager

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The Master commands the vessel and the Coastguard coordinates search and rescue; the role ashore is to support them, and the emergency arrangements say so, so that the shore team does not try to direct what it cannot see. One person leads ashore and is named at the start. A second person keeps a timed log of information received, decisions taken and why, and a third handles outgoing calls, so that the leader is free to think. Decisions are made against a short checklist for each type of emergency and are spoken aloud to the team before they are acted on, which gives a chance for a mistake to be caught. Unconfirmed information is marked as such. Calls from families and from the media go to the persons named for each, in another room, and never to the leader. The leader hands over after an agreed spell, with the log. Nobody sets off for the vessel or the scene until the leader has agreed that it is needed, and travel follows the controls in the new draft assessment Fatigue, on call duty and hours of rest. A manager who boards the vessel afterwards does so with the Master's permission and under the vessel's own access arrangements. RA 30 Emergency Preparedness sets out the plan.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Name the shore response roles and deputies, write the decision checklists and the log format, set up a room and telephone lines for the response, and hold an exercise that includes a handover. Owner: Managing Director with the Operations Manager and the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Preserving the scene and returning to it to investigate: entering an area that is still unsafe to photograph, measure or collect evidence, or evidence being moved, cleaned up, altered or lost

Potential consequence. The investigator is injured by the same unstable load, damaged structure, energy source or contamination. Death. Loss of evidence, a missed statutory report, and enforcement or legal difficulty for the Company.

Persons at risk. Risk and Compliance Manager, department managers, site supervisors, Masters, the Designated Person Ashore

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Once people are safe and the casualty has gone, the person in charge closes the scene with tape or barriers, posts a person to keep others out, and touches nothing beyond what is needed to make it safe. What has to be moved for safety is photographed first where that can be done without risk, and a note is made of what was moved, by whom and why. Plant and equipment involved are isolated, locked or tagged and are not repaired, cleaned or returned to use. The person in charge tells the Risk and Compliance Manager at once, who decides with the Managing Director whether the event is reportable ashore and makes the report, and who asks the police or HSE, where they are attending, before anything is disturbed. Before anyone goes back in to investigate, the supervisor confirms that the area is safe to enter and what protection is needed, and the investigator does not go in alone. On a vessel the Master and the owner notify the Marine Accident Investigation Branch as soon as possible by the quickest means, and charts, log books, electronic records, documents and equipment are kept unaltered for 30 days unless released sooner; no log entry is corrected or added to after the event except as a dated new entry. Names and contact details of witnesses are taken at the scene, including customers and contractors, and camera recordings are saved before they are overwritten. The scene is released only by the Risk and Compliance Manager, or by the authority that has taken control of it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the scene preservation steps and the reporting decision table (HSE, the Marine Accident Investigation Branch, the MCA, SEPA, the police, insurers and the client) into PRC 01, with the telephone numbers, and check that PRC 01 cites the 2026 marine accident reporting Regulations and not the revoked 2012 Regulations. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Distress after the event in those who gave first aid, saw a serious injury or death, led the response or spoke to the family

Potential consequence. Shock at the time. Poor sleep, intrusive memories and anxiety over the following weeks, which in some people become a lasting condition. Reluctance to act as a first aider, warden or supervisor again.

Persons at risk. First aiders, fire wardens, site supervisors, managers, the Risk and Compliance Manager, crew, witnesses

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The person in charge lists everybody who was at the scene, including those who only watched. They are stood down as soon as they are no longer needed, are not left alone or asked to drive at once, and are spoken to by a manager before going home. They are told what has become of the casualty as soon as it is known. Statements are taken once, with care. Contact is made again after a few days and at about a month, and help to see a doctor or the Company's confidential support is offered to anybody still troubled. The people who led the response and who investigate it are included in this, and are not assumed to be unaffected because of their role. The full controls are in the new draft assessment Work related stress and workload and are relied on here.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Add the list of persons present and the support contacts to the incident report form in PRC 01. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • Emergency drills are held in each area at the intervals set in PRC 06, at least one each year out of normal hours and one with customers and contractors on site, and each drill is observed against this assessment and written up.
  • Training records for first aiders, fire wardens, supervisors and Masters are reviewed each quarter by the Risk and Compliance Manager for expiry and for practical extinguisher training.
  • After every real incident a short review is held within two weeks with those who responded, asking what put responders at risk, and the findings are recorded under PRC 01.
  • First aid kits, spill kits, eyewash, throw lines, lifebuoys and extinguishers are checked at the intervals set for each, and the checks are sampled during workplace inspections.
  • The emergency contact list, the list of persons who take charge in each area and the shore response roles are checked each quarter and whenever a post holder changes.
  • Every notification to HSE, the Marine Accident Investigation Branch, the MCA or SEPA is logged with the time of the event and the time of the report, and the log is reviewed at the management review.

Emergency arrangements

  • On discovering any incident: stop, look for what caused it, raise the alarm, call 999 where life is at risk, and only then approach if it is safe.
  • Fire: sound the alarm, call the Fire and Rescue Service, leave by the nearest exit sweeping your area on the way, go to the assembly point and do not go back in. Tell the fire officer of any gas cylinders, the LPG tank, fuel, batteries and any person unaccounted for.
  • Person in the water: shout, throw, reach, and call 999 for the Coastguard. Do not go in.
  • Casualty in an enclosed space, under a load or in a gas or vapour cloud: do not enter. Keep others out and call the Fire and Rescue Service.
  • Vessel emergency: the Master follows the vessel's emergency arrangements and calls the Coastguard, then the Designated Person Ashore, who starts the shore response and the log.
  • A responder who is injured, contaminated or overcome is treated as a casualty in their own right, replaced in their role, and included in the report.
  • When the emergency is over the scene is closed, the Risk and Compliance Manager is told, and nothing is cleaned up, repaired or restarted until the scene has been released.

Related documents

  • PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness, PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up, PRC 24 Oil and Chemical Spill Control (GSS-ENV-PRC-02), PRC 15 Hazardous Materials Emergency Plan, PRC 19 Induction. All are drafts.
  • RA 30 Emergency Preparedness (GSS-HSE-RA-12), RA 56 First Aid (GSS-HSE-RA-13), Fire and emergency evacuation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Oil and chemical spill response (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), RA 37 Confined Spaces (GSS-HSE-RA-09), RA 18 Working on or Near Water (GSS-MAR-RA-15), RA 40 Lone Worker (GSS-HSE-RA-19), RA 17 Bottled Gasses (GSS-HSE-RA-06), and the new draft assessments Work related stress and workload and Fatigue, on call duty and hours of rest.
  • Incident log, list of persons present, witness details, photographs and saved camera recordings, incident report and investigation under PRC 01, notifications to the authorities, drill reports, training records for first aiders and wardens, and equipment check records.

PPE requirements

High visibility clothing, safety footwear and head protection as the area requires, worn before going to the scene. Disposable gloves, a face shield or pocket mask and eye protection for first aid. Chemical resistant gloves, eye protection and a disposable coverall from the spill kit where a substance is involved. A lifejacket on pontoons, at the quay edge and aboard, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. A high visibility vest marked for wardens and for the person in charge. Breathing apparatus is not provided to shore responders, and any situation that would need it is left to the Fire and Rescue Service; on a vessel, fire fighting equipment is used only by crew trained in it under the Master's orders.
