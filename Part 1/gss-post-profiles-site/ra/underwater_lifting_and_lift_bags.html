Risk assessments / Underwater lifting and lift bags

RISK ASSESSMENT

Underwater lifting and lift bags

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionLifting, lowering and moving loads under water with a diver in the water: the use of air lift bags, and lifts by crane or winch from a vessel, barge or quay to or from the seabed, including rigging, inflation, the ascent or travel of the load, landing it, and recovery to the surface
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Diving Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeAny GSS diving project that involves lifting under water: mooring blocks, chains and anchors, pipes and outfall sections, piles and steelwork, debris and wreckage, sunken craft, and components of quays, jetties, slipways and pontoons, inshore and offshore. Crane lifts above the water are assessed in RA 21 Crane Operations On Board and RA 21a Shore Lifting, and winches in RA 13 Winch Operations; the dive itself is assessed in RA 41 Dive Operations. This assessment covers the part those do not: the load and the lifting equipment under water with a diver beside them.
Main equipment usedOpen parachute type and enclosed air lift bags, their strops, shackles, inverter lines, hold back rigging and dump valves, the air supply and hoses used to fill them, slings, shackles and other lifting accessories used under water, vessel and shore cranes, winches and their wires and hooks, tag lines, marker buoys, and diver and crane communications. The types, numbers and capacities GSS holds are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskThe diver at the load, any second diver in the water, the standby diver, the Diving Supervisor, tenders, the crane or winch operator, the Master and deck crew, the crew of any small boat in attendance, and other vessels and water users near a load or lift bag at the surface.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. Each underwater lift is covered by a written lift plan that forms part of the diving project plan.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Underwater lifting needs a suitable and sufficient assessment and a safe system of work, including for self employed divers working under the Company's control.
DivingDiving at Work Regulations 1997, regulation 6. The diving contractor must ensure that the project is planned, managed and conducted so as to protect those taking part, that suitable plant is available and that it is maintained in a safe working condition. The lift is part of the diving project and is planned in the diving project plan.
Approved codeHSE approved code L104 (second edition, 2014). As read: diving projects will often require the use of lifting equipment including cranes and lift bags (paragraph 56), and special requirements for the periodic examination, testing and certification of lift bags have been established, with the manufacturers' maintenance instructions and testing requirements to be followed (paragraph 127).
LiftingLifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998. Regulation 8 requires every lifting operation involving lifting equipment to be properly planned by a competent person, appropriately supervised and carried out in a safe manner. Regulation 9 requires thorough examination of lifting equipment exposed to conditions causing deterioration at least every 6 months for accessories and equipment for lifting persons and at least every 12 months for other lifting equipment, or under an examination scheme. How far these Regulations, and the equivalent merchant shipping lifting regulations, apply to a lift bag and to a lift made from a vessel is to be confirmed; the planning and examination standard is applied here in any case.
Industry guidanceIMCA D 016, Underwater air lift bags (International Marine Contractors Association). Revision 3 of June 2007 was read from a copy hosted by a lift bag supplier; IMCA has since revised the guidance under the title Guidance on open parachute type underwater air lift bags, and the current revision was not read. As read, it covers inverter lines, hold back rigging, inflation, diver and umbilical position, dump valves, a usual minimum factor of safety of 5 to 1, visual examination at 6 months and load testing at 12 months.
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply to the vessel, her lifting appliances and her crew, enforced by the MCA. The Master decides whether the vessel can make the lift within her stability and the limits of her crane or winch.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. An underwater lift carried out with no lift plan: the weight in water and in air not known, suction or burial not allowed for, no one person in charge, and the method made up between the diver and the deck on the day

Potential consequence. Overloaded bag, crane or rigging. A load that moves in a way nobody expected, with a diver beside it. Death or serious injury, capsize risk to a small vessel, loss of the load.

Persons at risk. Divers, deck crew, crane or winch operator, the vessel

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Every underwater lift is planned in writing by a person competent in lifting and in diving work, normally the Diving Manager with the Diving Supervisor, and the plan is part of the diving project plan. It states the load, its weight in air and in water and how these were established, an allowance for mud suction, marine growth, trapped water and anything attached to the load, the centre of gravity and lifting points, the lifting method and why it was chosen, the equipment and its capacity, the sequence, the positions of the diver and the umbilical at each stage, the signals, the limits of weather, tide and visibility, and what is done if the lift goes wrong. Where the weight cannot be established the plan treats the load as heavier than estimated, adds a load cell or a staged trial lift, and sets a point at which the lift is abandoned. The preferred methods keep the diver away from a moving load: rig, withdraw the diver, then lift. One person, the Diving Supervisor, controls the lift while a diver is in the water, and nothing is hoisted, lowered, inflated or vented except on the Supervisor's word. The plan is briefed to the divers, the crane or winch operator, the Master and the deck crew together before the dive, and any change on the day stops the job until the plan has been revised and briefed again.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Issue a lift plan template for underwater lifts and state in the diving procedures who is competent to plan and approve them. Owner: Diving Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Runaway lift bag or load: air expanding in the bag as it rises, a load that breaks free of suction suddenly, more lift applied than the load weighs, or a bag that comes off the load

Potential consequence. The load and bag accelerate to the surface out of control, strike the diver, the vessel or a small boat, then vent and sink again onto whoever is below. Death, lung injury or decompression illness in a diver carried up.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby diver, crew of vessels at the surface

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Lift bags are used to reduce the weight of a load or to move it close to the bed under control, and a free ascent to the surface on bags alone is avoided wherever the job can be done another way, such as bags to break the load out and a crane or winch wire to raise it. Total lift is matched to the load so that the load cannot become strongly buoyant: the bags chosen cannot together lift much more than the weight in water, several small bags are used in preference to one large one, and where needed dead weight is added so that the combined weight exceeds the lift applied. Every open bag has an inverter line from its crown to the load, strong enough for the snatch of a rising bag and long enough to turn the bag over and spill the air if the bag comes off the load. Hold back rigging from the load to an independent fixed point, able to take the snatch load, is fitted where an uncontrolled ascent would otherwise be possible. Bags are filled slowly and in stages, a bag is not left unattended while filling, the number of bags part filled at one time is kept low, and the diver stops to check the load after each stage. The dump valve and its line are checked before the dive and are within the diver's reach from a safe position. Suction is broken by jetting, digging or a steady pull over time, not by adding more and more air. Nobody is below the load, and the surface above it is kept clear.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set Company rules for lift bag work in the diving procedures: when a free ascent is permitted, the inverter line and hold back standard, and the maximum lift relative to the load. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Diver or umbilical caught in the lift: umbilical, lifeline or equipment fouled in the bag rigging, the inverter line, the slings or the crane wire, or the diver holding on to the load as it moves

Potential consequence. The diver is carried to the surface with the load or dragged down with it when it sinks. Lung over pressure injury, decompression illness, severed or pinched gas supply, drowning.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby diver

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The plan fixes where the diver and the umbilical are at each stage. The umbilical is never routed over or directly under a lift bag or a load, as the IMCA guidance advises, and the tender keeps it tended so that there is no slack to drift into the rigging. The diver rigs from the up current side where there is a current, keeps hoses, bailout and tools clear of the slings, and confirms by voice that body and umbilical are clear before any air goes in or any wire is tensioned. The fill hose is long enough for the diver to inflate from a position to one side and above the level of the load, clear of its path up and down, and the diver does not ride, hold or stand on the load. Where two divers are in the water, no bag is inflated or vented until both have been told and each has confirmed where they and their umbilicals are. Loose line is kept to a minimum, and inverter lines and tag lines are made up to length, not left in coils. The diver carries a knife or cutter that can be reached with either hand. The standby diver is ready throughout and the Supervisor has voice contact with the diver for the whole of the lift.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Include umbilical management in lifting work in the briefing and in the competence record for divers and tenders. Owner: Diving Manager with each Diving Supervisor. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Lift bags and their rigging in poor condition or of unknown capacity: perished fabric, damaged seams or strops, a seized dump valve, unapproved repairs or replacement parts, no certificate, and stated capacity that the bag does not achieve

Potential consequence. Bag or strop fails under load and the load drops, or the bag cannot be vented and runs away. Death or serious injury to the diver below or beside it.

Persons at risk. Divers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Every lift bag is individually identified in the diving plant register with its maker, type, safe working load and certificate. Bags are bought with the maker's certification and are maintained, examined and tested as the maker instructs, as L104 advises. As a working standard taken from the IMCA guidance read for this draft, each bag is visually examined by a competent person at intervals not exceeding 6 months and load tested at intervals not exceeding 12 months, and the results are recorded; the intervals are to be checked against the current IMCA revision and the maker's instructions. No bag is used with a repair, strop, shackle or valve that the maker has not approved. Before each dive the diver or the Dive Storeman checks the fabric, seams, strops, lifting ring, dump valve and line, and the inverter line, and a bag that fails is tagged and withdrawn. Bags are washed in fresh water after use, dried and stored out of sunlight and away from oil and solvents. Shackles, slings and other accessories used with the bags are marked with their safe working load, are thoroughly examined as lifting accessories and are recorded in the same register. Bags are used within their safe working load, and the plan does not rely on a bag giving more lift than the maker states.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. List every lift bag held with its certificate, last examination and last load test, and withdraw any that cannot be traced to a maker and a certificate. Owner: Dive Storeman, checked by the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Crane or winch lift from a vessel or quay with a diver in the water: vessel heave and roll putting snatch loads on the wire and the load, the hook or load swinging under water, the vessel moving off station, and a wire or hook lowered onto the diver

Potential consequence. Diver struck or crushed by the hook, the block or the load. Rigging parted by a snatch load. Load dropped. Crane overloaded or the vessel's stability affected.

Persons at risk. Divers, crane or winch operator, deck crew, the vessel

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The crane or winch, its wire and the accessories are in date for thorough examination, and the lift is within the rated capacity at the radius to be used, after allowing for the dynamic effect of vessel motion, for suction and for the change in weight as the load leaves the water. The Master confirms that the vessel can make the lift within her stability and sets the sea state and wind limits for it, under RA 21 Crane Operations On Board and the assessment titled Vessel loading and stability. The vessel is moored or held on station by a method agreed with the Supervisor, and propellers and thrusters are controlled as RA 41 Dive Operations requires. The hook is lowered on a marked wire or with a guide weight or a downline to a point away from the diver, never directly over the diver, and stops at a depth short of the bed until the diver calls it in. The diver connects the load, checks the rigging, moves to the safe position named in the plan, up current and clear of the load's swing and of the bight of the wire, and reports clear before any weight is taken. Weight is taken slowly, the load is raised just clear and held while the diver reports, and the diver is either recovered or kept in a safe position before the load travels through the water column. The load is not slewed or travelled over the diver or the umbilical. Lifting stops if the vessel's motion begins to snatch the wire.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish the rated capacity and examination status of the crane and winch on each vessel used for diving, and whether any has a derated capacity for lifts through the water or in a seaway. Owner: Operations Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Communication failure between the diver, the Diving Supervisor and the crane or winch operator: a relayed instruction misheard, the operator acting on a signal from someone else, loss of diver communications during the lift, and noise on deck

Potential consequence. The load or hook is moved when the diver is not clear, or is not stopped when the diver calls stop. Diver struck, trapped or dragged.

Persons at risk. Divers, deck crew

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. There is one chain of command for the lift and it is stated in the lift plan: the diver speaks to the Diving Supervisor, and the Supervisor alone gives instructions to the crane or winch operator, directly by voice, by a dedicated radio channel or through one named signaller who stands where both can be seen and heard. The operator acts on no other person's instruction, except that anyone may call stop and the operator obeys it at once. A short set of standard words is agreed at the briefing, including the meaning of up and down as the diver sees them, and each instruction is repeated back before it is carried out. Diver communications are hard wired where practicable and are tested before the dive. If voice contact with the diver is lost at any time, all movement of the load, the hook and the air supply stops and nothing restarts until contact is restored or the diver is recovered. Radios are tested, a spare is at hand, and dive control and the operator's position are arranged so that the instruction can be heard over the compressor and the crane. The operator can see the Supervisor or the signaller, and the lift is not carried out in the dark without lighting that allows it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the standard words and the single chain of command into the lift plan template, and confirm what radios or direct link the crane operator on each vessel has to dive control. Owner: Diving Manager with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Dropped or shifting load under water: sling slipping on a smooth or fouled load, a lifting point that tears out of a corroded structure, the load rotating or rolling as it comes free, and a load landed on an uneven bed

Potential consequence. Diver struck by the falling load, or a limb, the umbilical or the diver trapped beneath or behind it. Death, crush injury, drowning.

Persons at risk. Divers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The load is inspected by the diver and described to the Supervisor before it is rigged, and the plan is confirmed or changed on what is found. Slings are arranged so that they cannot slip: choked or basketed round the load with a means of stopping them sliding, or shackled to lifting points that have been judged sound, and corroded or doubtful lugs, bollards and chain are not relied on. The load is rigged so that it hangs level and stable, with allowance for water or silt inside it shifting. The diver never places any part of the body or the umbilical under a suspended load or between the load and a fixed object, and does not reach under a load to pass a sling where a tool or a messenger line will do. As the load comes free and as it is landed the diver is in the safe position named in the plan, to the side, above the level of the load where bags are used, and clear of the direction in which it could roll or slide. The landing place is checked and prepared first, with bearers or packing where needed, and the load is landed, proved stable and the weight taken off before the diver approaches to unrig. A load raised on bags is not left suspended mid water and is not left unattended on bags.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm what slings, shackles and other lifting accessories are kept for underwater use, their marking and their thorough examination status. Owner: Dive Storeman, checked by the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Air for lift bags taken from the diver's own breathing supply, or from a supply that depletes the gas held for the dive

Potential consequence. The diver's primary or reserve gas is drawn down during the lift and is not there when needed. Out of gas at depth, rapid ascent, drowning.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby diver

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Lift bags are filled from a supply that is separate from the breathing gas of the diver and the standby diver: a dedicated hose from the surface or a dedicated cylinder taken down for the purpose. The diver's bailout and the reserve at the panel are never used to fill a bag. Where the fill supply comes from the same compressor or bank as the breathing gas, the quantity needed for the bags is calculated in the diving project plan and added to the breathing gas requirement, the fill line has its own valve at the panel under the Supervisor's control, and the supply to the divers has priority and is protected against a fall in pressure. The fill hose has a valve at the diver's end that closes when released, and is kept clear of the umbilical. Bank pressure is watched during filling and the lift is paused if it approaches the minimum set for the dive. The proposed assessment Diving plant, compressors and breathing air covers the supply itself.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how lift bags are filled at present and provide a dedicated fill supply if one is not in use. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Load and lift bags at the surface: a bag surfacing under or beside the vessel or a small boat, a load hanging beneath bags in the waves, towing a suspended load, and other craft passing over or near it

Potential consequence. Small boat capsized or crew struck. Bag spilled by wave action and the load dropped back to the bed onto a diver. Tow line snatching. Collision with a passing vessel.

Persons at risk. Deck crew, small boat crew, divers, other water users

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The point at which the load will surface is planned and kept clear: the vessel and any small boat stand off up wind or up current of it, and nobody is in the water beneath. The harbour authority is told of the operation where it is in a harbour or a channel, the diving signal is shown, and a lookout watches for approaching craft. Once at the surface the load is secured without delay, by transferring it to the crane or winch wire, by lashing it alongside, or by securing the bags so that waves cannot spill them, and a load is not left hanging on open bags in a seaway. Bags at the surface are marked so that they can be seen. A load is towed on bags only where the lift plan covers the tow, with enclosed bags or bags secured against spilling, a tow line and speed suited to the load, and a means of marking and recovering the load if it sinks; RA 54 Towing and RA 14 Operations from Small Boat apply. Divers are out of the water or well clear before the load is towed or lifted out. Recovery onto the deck follows RA 21 Crane Operations On Board and RA 16 Working on Deck, with tag lines and nobody under the load.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. State in the diving procedures the conditions under which a load may be towed on lift bags, and the notice to be given to harbour authorities. Owner: Diving Manager with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Deck work for underwater lifts: handling heavy wet bags, slings, shackles and chain, wires and hoses under tension across the deck, a load swinging as it leaves the water, and leaning over the side to connect or release

Potential consequence. Hand and foot crush injuries, back injury, a person struck by the load or a parted line, or a fall overboard.

Persons at risk. Tenders, deck crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The deck is laid out before the lift, with the umbilical, the fill hose and the crane wire given separate runs, and nobody stands in a bight or in line with a wire or tow line under load. Rigging is made up on deck as far as possible so that the diver and the crew have less to do at the load. Heavy bags, chain and shackles are lowered to the diver on a downline or by the crane, not thrown or carried down, and are recovered the same way. A load breaking the surface is steadied with tag lines from a safe distance, and nobody reaches for it by hand until it is landed. Work over the side is done from inside the rails where possible, and a person who must lean out wears a lifejacket and is attached. Only the people needed are on the working deck during the lift. RA 16 Working on Deck, RA 20 Manual Handling and RA 18 Working on or Near Water apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Include the deck layout for lifting in the briefing for each project and confirm that deck crew assisting are briefed by the Diving Supervisor and the Master together. Owner: Diving Supervisor with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The Diving Manager approves every underwater lift plan before the project and checks that the weight, the method, the diver's position and the chain of command are stated.
  • Lift bag examination and load test dates, and the thorough examination dates of slings, shackles, cranes and winches, are reviewed monthly from the plant register and the vessel records.
  • The Diving Supervisor records each underwater lift in the diving operation record, including the checks on the bags and rigging before the dive and any departure from the plan.
  • At least one underwater lift a year is observed on site by the Diving Manager or the Risk and Compliance Manager and compared with the plan.
  • Every runaway or spilled bag, dropped or shifted load, snatch load, fouled umbilical and loss of communications during a lift is reported under PRC 01, whether or not anyone was hurt, and reviewed against this assessment.

Emergency arrangements

  • Any person calls stop: all movement of the load, the hook and the air supply stops at once and the Diving Supervisor establishes the diver's position by voice.
  • Runaway bag or load: the diver stays clear and does not try to hold it, the Supervisor warns the deck and any small boat to stand clear of the surfacing point, and nobody approaches until it has been secured or has sunk and settled.
  • Diver fouled or trapped: stop the lift, hold the load where it is unless holding it increases the danger, keep the diver's gas and communications going, and deploy the standby diver only when the load is secure.
  • Diver carried to the surface: recover the diver, lay flat, give oxygen, and treat as a suspected lung injury or decompression illness under the proposed assessment Diving emergency and recompression, whatever the diver says about how they feel.
  • Loss of communications with the diver during a lift: stop everything, use line signals, and recover the diver before the lift continues.
  • Parted wire, failed crane or loss of vessel position: the Master takes charge of the vessel, the Supervisor recovers the diver, and the load is buoyed and left for a planned recovery.

Related documents

  • The diving project plan and the lift plan for each project. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up and PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness. The draft procedure that governs underwater lifting is to be confirmed.
  • RA 41 Dive Operations (GSS-DIV-RA-01), RA 21 Crane Operations On Board (GSS-MAR-RA-04), RA 21a Shore Lifting (GSS-MAR-RA-12), RA 13 Winch Operations (GSS-HSE-RA-34), RA 16 Working on Deck (GSS-MAR-RA-14), RA 54 Towing (GSS-MAR-RA-13), RA 14 Operations from Small Boat (GSS-MAR-RA-08), RA 20 Manual Handling (GSS-HSE-RA-20), RA 18 Working on or Near Water (GSS-MAR-RA-15), Vessel loading and stability (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026). The proposed new assessments Diving plant, compressors and breathing air and Diving emergency and recompression also bear on this work.
  • Lift plans, lift bag certificates, examination and load test records, thorough examination reports for cranes, winches and lifting accessories, the diving plant register, diving operation records and briefing records.

PPE requirements

The diver wears the diving dress and equipment set by RA 41 Dive Operations, with a harness, a knife or cutter reachable with either hand, and gloves suited to handling wire, chain and shackles. Deck crew and tenders wear a safety helmet during crane and winch lifts, gloves, safety footwear and a lifejacket on the working deck and at the quay edge, with high visibility clothing where a crane operator needs to see them. No personal protective equipment protects a diver under or beside a moving load: the control is position.
