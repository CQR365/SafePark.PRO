RISK ASSESSMENT

Violence, aggression and handling payments

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionDealing face to face, by telephone and in writing with customers, berth holders, customers' own contractors and members of the public at the marina office, in the yard and on the pontoons, including disputes over bills and yard rules; taking, holding and banking cash and taking card payments; securing the site and challenging unauthorised access; and doing any of these alone or out of hours
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins. The supplement records the layout of each public counter, the cash arrangements and the out of hours arrangements for the site.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe Silvers Marine office and any other public counter at Silverhills, the main office reception, the yard, the pontoons and berths, the site entrances and car park, and the route to the bank. It also applies to site supervisors on civils work and to Masters and crew where they deal with the public, harbour users or a client's personnel. Securing the site against intruders is assessed in the new draft assessment Site security and unauthorised access, and working alone in RA 40 Lone Worker; this assessment covers the confrontation itself and the handling of money, which those two do not.
Main equipment usedThe office counter and its layout, door locks and any controlled entry, telephones and radios, any alarm, panic button or camera system, the till or cash box and safe, the card payment terminal, invoices and account records, site lighting and signs, and the incident report form.
Persons at riskThe Silvers Office Manager and office staff, the Silvers Yard Manager, the Silvers Assistant Yard Supervisor and yard personnel, site supervisors, the accounts team and any person who carries cash, reception staff at the main office, department and line managers called to a dispute, Masters and crew at public quays, and customers and visitors who are nearby when an incident takes place.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. No draft procedure on violence at work or on handling payments has been seen. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up applies to the reporting of every incident.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974. HSE guidance on violence states that the Act places a legal duty on employers to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health, safety and welfare of workers, and that this includes protecting them from work related violence. Section 3 extends the duty to persons not in the Company's employment who may be affected, which here includes customers and self employed people on site.
Risk assessment and arrangementsManagement of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. HSE guidance states that employers must assess the risks to workers, including the risk of work related violence, appoint competent people, set up emergency procedures and give workers information and training. Regulation 8 requires procedures for serious and imminent danger, under which a worker must be able to stop work and go to a place of safety.
What counts as violenceHSE defines work related violence as any incident in which a person is abused, threatened or assaulted in circumstances relating to their work. It includes verbal abuse and threats made face to face, online or by telephone, as well as physical attack. This is guidance and the definition is adopted for this assessment and for reporting.
ReportingReporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013. HSE guidance states that an act of violence at work is reportable where it results in death, a specified injury, or a worker being unable to do their normal work for more than seven days, and that incapacity of more than three days must be recorded. The Regulations themselves were not read; the detail is taken from HSE guidance.
Lone workingHSE guidance on lone working states that working alone does not of itself make violence more likely but does make the worker more vulnerable, because no support is at hand, and names late working, holding a position of authority, dealing with people who have been drinking and carrying valuables as factors. All four are present in this work. This is guidance.
ConsultationHSE guidance states that employers have a duty to consult workers or their representatives on health and safety matters. The people who work the counter and the yard are consulted on the measures in this assessment before it is adopted.
Payments and dataThe legal and card scheme requirements that apply to taking card payments, keeping payment records and using camera systems were not read for this draft and are to be confirmed. Nothing in this assessment should be read as stating them.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. No system for managing violence and aggression: no assessment, abuse accepted as part of the job, incidents not reported, and no stated position on what the Company will not tolerate

Potential consequence. Abuse is repeated and grows worse. Staff become anxious, absent or leave. A person known to be aggressive is dealt with again by somebody alone and unprepared, and is assaulted.

Persons at risk. Office and yard personnel, site supervisors, managers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The Company states in writing, in its terms for customers and on a plainly worded notice at the counter, that abuse, threats and violence towards staff are not accepted and may lead to the customer being asked to leave and to the ending of their berthing or storage agreement. Staff are told that abuse is not part of the job, that the HSE definition applies, and that every incident is to be reported, including verbal abuse and threats by telephone or in writing where nobody was hurt. Reporting is made quick: a short form or a note to the manager the same day, entered under PRC 01. The manager acknowledges each report, tells the person what will be done, and does it. Incidents are reviewed together to find the people, places, times and causes that recur. A list is kept of customers and others who have been abusive, and staff are told before that person is next expected, so that nobody meets them alone. The Managing Director supports a manager who bans a customer for violence or threats.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the statement on violence and aggression, add it to customer terms and display it, set up the incident report and review, and brief all staff who deal with the public. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Verbal abuse, threats or assault across the counter at the marina office or main office reception, where the member of staff cannot easily withdraw or call for help

Potential consequence. Fear and distress, lasting anxiety about coming to work. Physical injury from a blow, a thrown object or being grabbed across the counter.

Persons at risk. Silvers Office Manager, office and reception staff, other customers present

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The public area is laid out so that staff can see who is coming in, the counter is deep and high enough that a person cannot easily reach or climb over it, and staff have a way out behind them that does not pass the customer and a door they can lock. The public side is well lit, kept tidy and free of loose objects that could be thrown or used as a weapon. A means of calling help that does not depend on shouting is provided at the counter, such as a discreet alarm, a radio or an agreed telephone signal, and it is known who answers it and that they come at once. Where staffing allows, two people are in the building when the office is open to the public. Staff are trained to recognise rising anger, to stay calm, to keep their distance, to listen and offer a next step, and to end the conversation and withdraw when it is not working. They are told plainly that they may ask a person to leave, may close the counter and may call the police, and that no sale, payment or argument is worth being hurt for. Private office areas, keys and the safe are out of sight and out of reach of the public.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Survey each public counter against this control (sight lines, counter depth, second exit, lockable door, means of calling help) and make the changes found necessary. Arrange conflict management training for counter and yard staff. Owner: Silvers Office Manager and Office Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Disputes over bills and unpaid accounts: a customer challenging a yard, lift, storage or repair charge, or being told that work, a launch or the release of a boat depends on payment

Potential consequence. A heated argument in the office or beside the boat that turns to threats or assault. Damage to property. A customer trying to launch or remove the boat by themselves in anger, putting themselves and yard staff in danger.

Persons at risk. Silvers Office Manager, Silvers Yard Manager, Silvers Assistant Yard Supervisor, accounts staff, yard personnel

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The causes of dispute are reduced first. Charges are set out in a published tariff, work is quoted or estimated in writing and agreed before it starts, additional work is agreed with the customer before it is done, and invoices are itemised and issued promptly. The terms on payment, and on what happens to a boat and a berth when an account is unpaid, are given to the customer in writing at the start and applied in the same way to everybody. Overdue accounts are followed up early, in writing or by telephone, by the accounts function and not left to be raised for the first time at the counter or the slipway. A member of staff faced with a dispute does not argue the bill: they listen, record the complaint, and pass it to the manager who has authority to settle it, and the customer is told when they will hear. A difficult conversation that can be foreseen is held by appointment, in the office, in working hours, with two members of staff present, and never beside the hoist or at the water's edge. Yard staff are told not to carry out or prevent a launch on their own judgement where there is a dispute, and to refer it to the manager. Where a customer has been told that their boat will not be moved until an account is settled, the yard team is told the same day.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that the tariff, the customer terms and the steps for an unpaid account are written, current and legally sound, and decide who has authority to settle a disputed bill. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Financial Controller. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Cash taken and held at the office: robbery or snatch theft from the counter, the till, the cash box or the safe, including at opening and closing

Potential consequence. Threats or violence against the person holding the cash or the keys, possibly with a weapon. Serious injury. Lasting psychological harm.

Persons at risk. Silvers Office Manager, office staff, any person opening or closing the office

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The amount of cash handled is cut down as far as the business allows: customers are steered to card payment and bank transfer, and the Company considers whether cash need be accepted at all. Where cash is taken, a limit is set for the amount held in the till or cash box, anything above it is moved to a locked safe out of public view during the day, and the total held on the premises overnight is kept low and within the limit the insurer allows. Cash is never counted or the safe opened in view of the public or of a window, and not by a person alone with the door unlocked. Keys and the safe combination are held by named persons only. The office is locked and checked before cash is counted at the end of the day. The instruction to staff is plain and is repeated in training: if cash is demanded, hand it over, do not resist, do not follow, note what you can and call the police when the person has gone. Notices state that little cash is held. Any alarm and camera coverage of the counter and the safe is kept in working order.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish how much cash is taken and held, decide whether cash will continue to be accepted, set the till and overnight limits with the insurer, and name the key holders. Owner: Financial Controller with the Silvers Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Carrying cash to the bank or between the Silvers office and the main office, alone, on foot or by car, at predictable times

Potential consequence. Robbery in the car park, on the road or at the bank. Assault and serious injury to the person carrying the cash.

Persons at risk. Silvers Office Manager, accounts staff, any person asked to bank cash

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Less cash taken means fewer journeys, so the control above comes first. Where cash must be banked, the day, time and route are varied, the journey is made in daylight and in working hours, and the cash is carried out of sight in an ordinary bag, not a bank bag or a cash box. The person goes straight there by car, parks as close as possible, keeps the doors locked and does not combine the trip with other errands. A second person goes with them where the amount is above a figure set by the Company, and a cash collection service is used above a higher figure or where the insurer requires it. Somebody at the office knows when the person left and when they are expected back, and acts if they are overdue. The same instruction applies as at the counter: if challenged, give up the cash. Nobody is asked to take cash home overnight or to bank it on their way home.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the amounts above which a second person or a collection service is used, in line with the insurance policy, and record the banking arrangements. Owner: Financial Controller. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Card payments, refunds and account queries taken at the counter or by telephone, and abuse or threats by telephone, email, letter or social media

Potential consequence. Argument over a declined card, a refund or a disputed transaction. Repeated abusive calls or messages aimed at a named member of staff, causing distress and fear outside work as well as in it.

Persons at risk. Silvers Office Manager, office, reception and accounts staff

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The customer uses the terminal themselves and staff do not handle the card or see the number being entered. A declined payment is dealt with in neutral words: the terminal has not accepted the payment, and another means can be used. Refunds are made only by the persons authorised to make them, to the original card or account, under a written rule that staff can point to, so that the decision is the Company's and not the individual's. Card details are not written down or kept. For the telephone, staff are told that they may warn an abusive caller once and then end the call, and that they will be supported for doing so. Abusive calls, emails, letters and online posts are reported like any other incident, kept as evidence and passed to the manager, who replies on behalf of the Company or refers the matter to the police. Staff give a first name only, personal mobile numbers and home addresses are not given out, and replies to complaints go out in the name of the manager.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Write the refund rule and the instruction on abusive calls and messages, and confirm what the card provider requires for payments taken by telephone. Owner: Financial Controller with the Silvers Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Challenging a person who should not be on site: an intruder or suspected thief in the yard or on the pontoons, a trespasser, a group of youths, or a person who has been drinking

Potential consequence. The person challenged turns on the member of staff. Assault, possibly with a tool or a weapon, a fall on the pontoon or into the water during a struggle. Serious injury or death.

Persons at risk. Site supervisors, Silvers Yard Manager, Silvers Assistant Yard Supervisor, yard personnel, any key holder

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The measures in the new draft assessment Site security and unauthorised access reduce the number of times a challenge is needed and are relied on here. The rule for the challenge itself is that staff observe and report, and do not detain, chase, block or lay hands on anybody. An ordinary visitor who has strayed is approached openly, in daylight, from a distance, with a greeting and an offer of help, and is walked to the office or the gate. Where a person appears to be stealing, causing damage, drunk, aggressive or in a group, or it is dark, the member of staff keeps away, goes to a safe place, calls the police and the manager, and notes descriptions, vehicles and direction. Nobody approaches an intruder alone or near the water's edge, and nobody boards a boat to confront a person aboard. Staff carry a telephone or radio when checking the site. Property is not defended at the cost of injury, and staff are told that they will not be criticised for letting a thief leave.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Brief every person who checks or secures the site on the observe and report rule, and agree with the police how calls from the site will be handled. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Working alone and out of hours with the public or against intruders: opening and closing the office and gates, late or weekend customers, attending an alarm, and meeting a customer alone at a boat or berth

Potential consequence. An assault or robbery with nobody to see it, help or raise the alarm. Serious injury that goes undiscovered for hours. Death.

Persons at risk. Silvers Office Manager, Silvers Yard Manager, site supervisors, key holders, any member of staff working late

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The work is arranged so that dealing with the public is not done alone where that can be avoided: counter hours are set to match the hours when two people are present, and meetings about money or complaints are held in those hours. Where a person must work alone, RA 40 Lone Worker applies in full, with a check in at set times to a named person who knows what to do if it is missed. The public door is kept locked when one person is in the office outside counter hours, and callers are spoken to through the door or a window. Opening and closing follow a set routine that keeps the person in lit areas, with the car parked close to the door, and the routine is changed if somebody is seen waiting. A key holder called to an alarm at night does not enter or walk the site alone: they wait at the gate in a locked car for the police or a second person. A member of staff does not meet a customer alone at a boat, in a shed or on a pontoon to discuss a dispute. A person who feels uneasy about a caller or a meeting says so and is given company without having to justify it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish who works alone at the Silvers office and in the yard, at what times, who the key holders are and how alarm calls are answered, and set up the check in arrangement. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Aggression when yard rules are enforced: a customer or a customer's contractor told to stop unsafe work, keep out of a lifting area, wear a lifejacket, move a vehicle or leave the site

Potential consequence. Abuse or assault of the member of staff giving the instruction. Staff stop enforcing the rules, and the yard becomes less safe for everybody.

Persons at risk. Silvers Yard Manager, Silvers Assistant Yard Supervisor, yard personnel, site supervisors on civils sites, Masters at public quays

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The yard rules are given to every customer and contractor in writing when they sign their agreement or sign in, and are displayed at the entrance, so that the member of staff is pointing to a rule already accepted and is not seen as making it up. Rules are few, clear and applied to everybody alike. Staff are trained to give the instruction once, politely, with the reason, and if it is refused to stop the Company's own work where it is affected, such as a lift, to move away and to report to the manager, who takes it up with the customer afterwards. Staff do not argue, raise their voice, touch the person or their equipment, or try to remove them. Repeated refusal or any abuse is dealt with by the manager under the customer or contractor agreement, up to and including withdrawal of permission to work on site. On civils sites and at public quays the same approach applies to members of the public who object to closures, noise or the presence of the works: the supervisor or Master explains once, gives the contact for complaints and does not get drawn into a dispute.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that the yard rules are issued to and signed for by every customer and contractor, and that they state the consequence of refusing an instruction or abusing staff. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. No support for the person after an incident, and managers expected to handle violence and its aftermath without training or guidance

Potential consequence. The person affected is left to carry on at the counter the same day and meets the same customer again. Anxiety, loss of sleep and absence, which may appear weeks later. The manager avoids the matter and nothing changes.

Persons at risk. Any person abused, threatened or assaulted, witnesses, department and line managers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. After any incident the manager first makes sure the person is safe and has the first aid or medical attention they need, takes them off the counter or the task, and stays with them or has somebody else do so. The manager listens, takes a short account while it is fresh, and tells the person what will happen next. Whether to call the police is decided with the person affected, and the Company supports them through any statement or court attendance. The person is asked what would help on their return, for instance a change of duties or hours for a time or not dealing with that customer again, and is contacted again after a few days and after a few weeks, since people react in different ways and not always at once. Confidential support is offered. Managers are trained in these steps and know that they can go to the Risk and Compliance Manager or the Managing Director for advice. Each incident is investigated under PRC 01 to find what triggered it and whether the measures in this assessment worked, and the findings are shared with the team. Support for managers themselves is covered in the new draft assessment Work related stress and workload.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the steps to follow after an incident, decide what confidential support will be offered, and train managers and supervisors. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with department managers. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • Every incident of abuse, threat or assault is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed by the manager within a week. The Risk and Compliance Manager reviews all incidents each quarter for patterns by place, time, cause and person.
  • The Silvers Office Manager checks any alarm, panic button, radio and camera covering the counter each month and records the check.
  • The Financial Controller checks each quarter that cash held and banked has stayed within the limits set, and that banking times and routes are being varied.
  • Overdue accounts are reviewed monthly so that they are followed up before they become a dispute at the counter or in the yard.
  • Staff who deal with the public are asked at least once a year, in a group and without managers leading, whether they feel safe and what would improve matters, and the answers are acted on.
  • Training records for conflict management and for the cash and lone working rules are reviewed each year, and new starters are trained before they work the counter alone.
  • This assessment is reviewed each year and after any assault, robbery or serious threat.

Emergency arrangements

  • A person being threatened withdraws to a safe place, locks the door between them and the aggressor if they can, and calls for help by the agreed means. Call 999 where there is a threat of violence, a weapon, or a person who will not leave.
  • Robbery or a demand for cash: hand it over, do not resist or follow, and call 999 when the person has gone. Close the office, keep witnesses together if they are willing, do not touch what the offender touched, and tell the manager and the Managing Director.
  • Assault: give first aid under RA 56 First Aid, call 999 for an ambulance and the police where needed, and do not leave the injured person alone. Preserve camera recordings at once.
  • Intruder on site: keep away, go to a lit and safe place, call the police and the manager, and do not enter the yard, a shed or the pontoons alone to look.
  • A lone worker who misses a check in is called at once. If there is no answer, the named contact follows the steps in RA 40 Lone Worker and does not go to the site alone at night.
  • After any incident the manager decides the same day whether it is reportable to HSE and tells the Risk and Compliance Manager, who makes the report.

  • Statement on violence and aggression, customer terms and yard rules, tariff, incident reports and the quarterly review, list of persons who have been abusive, cash limits and banking record, key holder list, alarm and camera check records, lone worker check in record, and training records.

PPE requirements

No personal protective equipment is issued against violence, and staff are not given anything intended for use as a defence. A personal alarm or a lone worker device, where the Company decides to issue one, is a means of calling help and is covered in RA 40 Lone Worker. A lifejacket is worn on the pontoons and at the water's edge in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water, including when checking the site.
