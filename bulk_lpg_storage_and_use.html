Risk assessments / Bulk LPG storage and use

RISK ASSESSMENT

Bulk LPG storage and use

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionStorage of liquefied petroleum gas in the bulk tank at the Silvers Marine yard and in cylinders, the pipework and appliances the tank supplies, tanker deliveries, the day to day checks on the installation, and the control of LPG carried on customers' boats ashore and afloat
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe bulk LPG tank at the Silvers Marine boatyard and marina at Silverhills, its compound, valves, service pipework and the buildings and appliances it feeds, any store of full or empty LPG cylinders, the tanker delivery point, and customers' boats in the yard and on the berths so far as their gas affects the site. Oxygen, acetylene and other industrial cylinders and the general handling of cylinders are assessed in RA 17 Bottled Gasses, and liquid fuels in Fuel and oil storage; this assessment covers what is particular to LPG.
Main equipment usedThe bulk LPG tank with its valves, pressure relief valve, contents gauge and regulator, service pipework above and below ground, emergency control valves, the appliances supplied, LPG cylinders and any cylinder cage, impact protection, signs, fire extinguishers and gas detection where fitted. The capacity of the tank, who owns it and what it supplies are not known to the assessor and are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskYard personnel, the person who attends deliveries, tanker drivers, gas engineers and other contractors, office staff in buildings supplied with gas, boat owners and their guests, customers' contractors, neighbours and the public, and firefighters.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. PRC 15 Hazardous Materials Emergency Plan and PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness apply to a leak or fire. A hot work permit is required for any work involving heat, sparks or flame near the tank, pipework or cylinder store, under RA 27 Hot Work.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Dangerous substancesDangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002. HSE guidance states that employers must find out what dangerous substances are in the workplace and what the risks are, put controls in place, prepare plans for incidents and emergencies, inform and train employees, and identify and classify areas where explosive atmospheres may occur and avoid ignition sources there. HSE names LPG as a flammable gas within the Regulations.
Pressure systemsPressure Systems Safety Regulations 2000, regulation 8. The user of an installed system must not operate it or allow it to be operated without a written scheme for periodic examination by a competent person, covering protective devices, pressure vessels and pipework where a defect may give rise to danger. HSE guidance describes the Regulations as covering liquefied gases above 0.5 bar. That the bulk tank is within the Regulations, and who holds the duty where the tank belongs to the gas supplier, is to be confirmed with the supplier and the competent person.
HSE guidance on bulk LPGHSE guidance on the safe use of LPG at small commercial and industrial bulk installations and on bulk LPG storage tanks, for tanks holding up to four tonnes. It sets out the user's part in keeping separation distances, ventilation, housekeeping, tanker access, security, impact protection and emergency arrangements. It is guidance and is used here as the standard for the controls.
Industry codes of practiceTo confirm. The trade association for LPG publishes codes of practice on bulk storage at fixed installations and on the storage of cylinders. The association's site could not be opened, so the codes are not cited by number and the separation distances that apply to this tank are to the code, to confirm.
Gas appliances and pipeworkTo confirm. The Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 are understood to govern work on gas fittings and appliances and the registration of those who do it. The text was not read for this assessment, and how the Regulations apply to the premises and to boats is to be confirmed.
Fire, ScotlandFire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006. Cited as the regime for fire safety at the premises, which include the LPG installation. The text was not read for this assessment.
General duties and occupierHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, cited as the general framework and not read for this assessment. Occupiers' Liability (Scotland) Act 1960, section 2: the occupier owes persons entering the premises such care as is reasonable in the circumstances in respect of dangers on them.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. The LPG installation held with no system: ownership of the tank, its examination status, the route of the pipework and what it supplies not known, no dangerous substances assessment and no named person responsible

Potential consequence. Defects, lapsed examinations and loss of separation go unnoticed. Leak, fire or explosion. Death, multiple casualties and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. All persons on site, neighbours

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A file is kept for the installation, as HSE guidance recommends, holding the supply agreement and who owns the tank, the capacity, the installation drawings and pipework routes above and below ground, the location of every emergency control valve, the written scheme of examination and the latest examination report, the appliances supplied and their service records, the dangerous substances assessment and the supplier's emergency telephone number. One post is named as responsible for the installation day to day. A weekly visual check of the tank, compound, valves, visible pipework, signs, impact protection and housekeeping is made against a checklist and recorded, and defects are reported to the supplier or a gas engineer the same day. The division of duties between the Company and the gas supplier is set out in writing so that nothing falls between the two.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Assemble the installation file, obtain from the supplier the ownership, capacity, installation and examination details, name the responsible post and issue the weekly checklist. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Release of LPG from the bulk tank, its valves, regulator or connections, with vapour that is heavier than air spreading at ground level and collecting in drains, pits, ducts, bilges and low ground

Potential consequence. Vapour cloud reaching an ignition source. Flash fire or explosion, fire engulfing the tank. Suffocation in a low space. Death, multiple casualties.

Persons at risk. All persons in the vicinity, neighbours, firefighters

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The tank stands in the open air with free ventilation on all sides, and nothing is built, stacked or parked so as to enclose it. Drains and gullies near the tank are fitted with water traps or sealed, in line with HSE guidance, and no pit, duct or inspection chamber is opened near the tank without the yard manager's agreement. Valve covers are kept locked and only the supplier and gas engineers operate tank fittings other than the emergency shut off. The weekly check looks for the smell of gas, frosting, hissing, damaged fittings and corrosion. The tank is kept in the colour in which it was supplied. Anybody who smells gas reports it at once, and the leak procedure in this assessment is followed. Yard personnel are told at induction that LPG is flammable, has an added smell, is heavier than air and sinks into low places.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Survey the area round the tank for drains, gullies, ducts and low points and have them trapped or sealed where the code requires. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the gas supplier. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Failure of the tank, relief valve or pipework because it has not been examined under a written scheme, or because defects found have not been put right

Potential consequence. Rupture or uncontrolled release under pressure. Fire and explosion. Death.

Persons at risk. All persons in the vicinity

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The installation is not operated without a written scheme of examination drawn up or certified by a competent person, covering the tank, its protective devices and the pipework where a defect could give rise to danger. Examinations are carried out at the intervals the scheme sets and the reports are held in the installation file. The date of the next examination is entered in the compliance diary and tracked. Any repair, limit or prohibition stated in a report is acted on within the time given, and where the report says the system must not be used it is isolated by the supplier. No repair or alteration is made to the tank, its fittings or the pipework except by the supplier or a competent gas engineer. Where the tank is owned by the supplier, written confirmation is obtained each year that the scheme and the examination are current, and a copy of the latest report is requested.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain the written scheme of examination and the latest examination report for the tank and pipework, and confirm in writing who holds the user duty for each part of the system. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Loss of the separation distance around the tank and cylinder store: boats laid up close by, sheds, containers, skips, pallets, waste, long grass and weeds, and new electrical equipment

Potential consequence. A fire in stored boats or rubbish heats the tank, or a leak finds fuel and an ignition source within reach. Fire and explosion. Death.

Persons at risk. All persons in the vicinity, firefighters

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The separation distance between the tank and any building, boundary or fixed source of ignition is to the code, to confirm, and once confirmed it is marked on the yard plan and on the ground as a keep clear zone. No boat, cradle, trailer, vehicle, container, skip, pallet stack, waste or other combustible material is placed inside that zone, and the yard plan for laying up boats is drawn round it. No structure is put up and no electrical equipment or lighting is installed within the zone without checking against the code with the supplier first. Grass and weeds are kept short, and chlorate based weedkiller is not used near the tank. The zone is checked in the weekly inspection and again at the start of each lay up season, when pressure on space is greatest. The tanker's parking position is kept clear.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the separation distances that apply to this tank and to any cylinder store from the supplier and the trade association code, measure the installation against them and mark the keep clear zone. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Sources of ignition near the tank, pipework, cylinder store or a leak: smoking, hot work, grinding, heat guns, barbecues, vehicles and plant, unsuitable electrical equipment and battery chargers

Potential consequence. Ignition of a release. Flash fire, explosion, burns. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, customers, contractors, the public

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Smoking and naked flame are prohibited at and around the tank and the cylinder store and the prohibition is signed. Hot work, grinding, the use of heat guns for shrink wrapping and any other spark producing work is not carried out near the tank, the pipework or the cylinder store except under a hot work permit and RA 27 Hot Work, after the supplier's advice has been taken where the work is within the separation distance. Vehicles, plant and the boat hoist are not parked or left running beside the tank. Customers are told in the yard rules that barbecues, generators and battery charging are kept away from the tank and the cylinder store. Any area classified as hazardous in the dangerous substances assessment has electrical equipment suitable for it, and Electrical work and isolation applies to any new installation nearby. Unauthorised people are kept away from the tank, and where the capacity is four tonnes or more a security fence that lets air pass is required by HSE guidance.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Carry out and record the dangerous substances assessment for the tank, pipework and cylinder store, including whether any area is to be classified as hazardous, and fit the signs. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. A vehicle, trailer, lift truck, the boat hoist or a boat being moved striking the tank, its valves or exposed pipework

Potential consequence. Fitting sheared or pipe fractured, large release of liquid or vapour, fire. Death.

Persons at risk. Drivers and plant operators, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Where vehicles or plant can reach the tank or its pipework, physical protection is provided by crash barriers or bollards strong enough for the plant that works nearby, since HSE guidance states that a fence or road markings alone are not enough. Hoist routes, lift truck routes and customer parking are set out on the yard plan to keep clear of the tank, and boats are not carried over or past it in the slings where another route exists. Exposed pipework is routed, sleeved or guarded so that it cannot be struck or used as a step, and is marked. Any impact with the tank, a barrier or pipework is reported at once, the area is cleared, and the supplier or a gas engineer checks the installation before it is treated as safe. Workplace transport and deliveries applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Assess the tank and pipework for exposure to vehicles and plant, including the boat hoist, and fit barriers or bollards where they are missing. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Tanker deliveries: access through a working yard, the hose connected during transfer, overfill, a release at the coupling, and people and vehicles passing during the transfer

Potential consequence. Release of liquid LPG at the fill point, cold burns to the driver, ignition by a passing vehicle or a person smoking. Collision between the tanker and pedestrians or stored boats. Death.

Persons at risk. Tanker driver, the person attending, customers and the public nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Deliveries are arranged with the supplier for times when the yard is quiet, and the yard office knows when a delivery is due. A dedicated flat parking position for the tanker is kept clear on delivery days, with a route to it that avoids reversing where possible and is free of stored boats and parked cars. A person from the yard meets the tanker, guides it in, keeps people and vehicles away for the duration of the transfer and stops hoist movements and hot work nearby. Everyone other than the driver stays well back during the transfer. The transfer itself is the supplier's operation under the supplier's procedure, including the measures against driving away with the hose connected and against static, and yard personnel do not handle the hose or the tank fittings. The driver is shown the site emergency arrangements on the first visit. Workplace transport and deliveries applies to the tanker's movement on site.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Agree the delivery arrangements, the tanker route and the parking position with the supplier in writing and mark the position on the ground. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Leak from service pipework between the tank and the buildings or appliances it feeds, including corroded buried metal pipe, and faulty or poorly ventilated gas appliances

Potential consequence. Gas tracking underground into a building and igniting. Explosion and building collapse. Carbon monoxide poisoning from an appliance. Death.

Persons at risk. Occupants of buildings supplied, yard personnel, customers using the facilities

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The route, material and age of all service pipework are established and recorded in the installation file. Buried metallic pipework is inspected and maintained or replaced in line with HSE guidance on such pipework, on the advice of a competent gas engineer. Pipe entries to buildings are above ground and sealed, and an emergency control valve is fitted outside each building, labelled and kept accessible. Appliances are installed, serviced and repaired only by an engineer competent and registered for LPG and for the type of appliance, at the interval the maker sets, and the records are kept. Rooms with gas appliances have the ventilation and flues the appliance needs, kept unblocked, and a carbon monoxide alarm is fitted where a fuel burning appliance is in an occupied room. No excavation, post driving or ground work is done on or near a pipe route without the drawing being checked and the route located. The structural effect of a gas escape on the buildings is considered in the dangerous substances assessment, as HSE guidance advises.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish the route, material and condition of the service pipework and have a competent gas engineer report on any buried metallic pipe. List the appliances supplied and confirm that each has a current service record. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Storage and handling of LPG cylinders on site, full and empty, including any held for the yard's own heaters, torches and lift trucks or for sale and exchange to customers

Potential consequence. Leaking or damaged cylinder releasing gas at low level, a cylinder in a fire rupturing, manual handling injury, cold burns. Death in the worst case.

Persons at risk. Yard and office personnel, customers, delivery drivers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The number of cylinders held is kept to what is needed. Cylinders, full and empty alike, are stored outdoors in a secure, well ventilated cage or compound, upright, with valves closed and caps or plugs fitted, away from drains, pits, building openings, ignition sources and combustible material, and separated from the bulk tank, from oxygen cylinders and from fuel and oil storage by the distances in the code, to confirm. The store is signed, is protected from vehicles, and is kept locked where customers have access. Cylinders are not kept in sheds, containers, offices, below ground or in vehicles. They are moved with a trolley and never rolled on their side or lifted by the valve. Hoses and regulators on the yard's own equipment are of the correct type, in date, checked before use and tested for leaks with leak detection fluid, never with a flame. A cylinder that is damaged, corroded or leaking is moved to the open air away from people and ignition sources and the supplier is called. RA 17 Bottled Gasses applies to the handling and use of cylinders in general.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish how many LPG cylinders are held, for what purpose and where, and whether cylinders are sold or exchanged to customers, and bring the store up to the code once it has been confirmed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. LPG on customers' boats ashore and on the berths: cylinders left connected and turned on, leaking systems, gas collecting in bilges, amateur repairs, heaters and cookers left burning, and spare cylinders stowed below

Potential consequence. Explosion or fire in a boat, spreading to neighbouring boats, the pontoons or the yard. Carbon monoxide poisoning of people aboard. Death, multiple casualties.

Persons at risk. Owners and guests, neighbouring customers, yard personnel, firefighters

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The storage and berthing terms and the yard rules ask owners to turn gas off at the cylinder whenever the boat is left, to remove portable cylinders from boats laid up ashore or to leave them shut off in a locker that drains overboard, and not to stow spare cylinders or petrol below decks. Owners are told that work on a boat's gas system is to be done by a competent gas engineer and that the yard may ask for evidence. Yard personnel do not work on customers' gas systems unless the yard holds that competence. Before hot work, or any work by yard personnel inside a boat, the gas is confirmed off at the cylinder and the space is ventilated, and where there is any smell of gas the work does not start. Yard personnel who smell gas on or near a boat raise the alarm, keep people away and contact the owner; they do not go below, switch anything on or off, or start an engine. Heaters and cookers are not left burning in unattended boats. The yard does not control the condition of customers' gas systems, and that limit is stated plainly in the terms.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Further action required. Write the gas rules into the berthing and storage terms and the customer yard rules, and decide whether the yard will ask for evidence of gas system inspection for boats kept on annual contracts. The residual rating stays high because the condition of customers' boats is outside the yard's control. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Responding to a smell of gas, a leak or a fire at the tank, pipework, cylinder store or a boat, without knowing the valves, the supplier's number or when to withdraw

Potential consequence. Responder caught in a flash fire or explosion. Delay in evacuation and in calling the emergency services. Death.

Persons at risk. First responders among yard and office personnel, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A written emergency procedure for LPG is displayed at the yard office and at the tank, giving the supplier's emergency telephone number as shown on the tank, the location of the tank shut off and each emergency control valve, the evacuation distance and assembly point, and the rule that nobody fights a fire involving the tank or cylinders. Yard and office personnel are shown the valves and the procedure at induction and at least once a year. In a leak, valves are closed only where that can be done without entering the gas, ignition sources are removed, electrical switches are left alone, people are moved upwind and uphill, and drains and low ground are avoided. The procedure covers the pontoons and stored boats as well as the buildings, and it is tested by a drill. Fire and emergency evacuation applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write and display the LPG emergency procedure, label the valves, brief yard and office personnel and hold a drill. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The weekly check records for the tank, compound, pipework and cylinder store are reviewed each month by the Silvers Yard Manager for completeness and open defects.
  • The due dates for examination under the written scheme and for appliance servicing are held in the compliance diary and tracked by the Risk and Compliance Manager.
  • The keep clear zone round the tank is checked against the yard plan at the start and end of each lay up season.
  • Every smell of gas, leak, impact with the tank or pipework, delivery problem and gas incident on a customer's boat is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.
  • The installation file is reviewed at least annually and whenever the supplier, the tank, the pipework or the appliances change.
  • The LPG emergency procedure is exercised at least once a year and the lessons recorded.

Emergency arrangements

  • Smell of gas or suspected leak in the open: keep people away and upwind, remove ignition sources, do not operate electrical switches or start vehicles nearby, close the tank shut off or the emergency control valve if that can be done safely, and call the supplier's emergency number shown on the tank.
  • Smell of gas in a building: turn off the emergency control valve outside, open doors and windows, do not use switches, evacuate and call the supplier's emergency number.
  • Fire at or near the tank or cylinder store: raise the alarm, call 999 for the Fire and Rescue Service and say that bulk LPG is involved, evacuate the yard, the pontoons and neighbouring buildings to the assembly point and beyond as the fire service directs, and do not attempt to fight the fire.
  • Gas or fire on a customer's boat: raise the alarm, clear neighbouring boats and the pontoon or row, call 999, and do not board. Tell the fire crew whether cylinders and fuel are aboard.
  • Cold burn from liquid LPG: flood the area with lukewarm water, do not pull away clothing that is frozen to the skin, and obtain medical attention.
  • Person feeling unwell in a room with a gas appliance: move the person to fresh air, turn the appliance off, call for medical help and do not use the appliance again until an engineer has checked it.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager and the Managing Director are told the same day of any leak, fire or impact, and the installation is not put back into use until the supplier or a competent gas engineer has confirmed it is safe.

PPE requirements

No personal protective equipment is a routine control for the bulk tank, because yard personnel do not operate its fittings. Safety footwear and gloves for handling cylinders. Eye protection and gloves suited to cold contact when connecting or disconnecting a cylinder on the yard's own equipment. High visibility clothing for the person meeting the tanker. Clothing and equipment are not a protection against a gas release: the control is to withdraw.
