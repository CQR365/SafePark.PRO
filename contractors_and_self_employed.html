1. The group

Who is in the groupThree kinds of people. First, self employed divers, skippers and other labour only workers who do the Company's core operational work under its direction; most GSS divers and skippers are in this part of the group. Second, contractor companies and their people working on Company sites, vessels or projects. Third, agency workers. The drafts also place visitors and delivery drivers here.
PurposeAccountable for working safely, competently and to the Company's rules while working for or on behalf of the Company, and for meeting their own duties in law. (Proposed)
Answers toThe manager who engages them. On a site, the site contact. Aboard a vessel, the master. On a dive, the diving supervisor.
StatusA duty group, not a post. The organisation document has no entry for the group. Whether self employed divers and skippers who work under the Company's control are employees for health and safety purposes is to confirm.

This group does core operational work, not only visiting trades, so its legal position matters. Three points were read at source. First, the Company owes a duty under section 3(1) of the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 to everyone affected by its work who is not its employee. Second, since 1 October 2015 a self employed person has their own duty under section 3(2) only if their work is a listed high risk activity (construction is one) or may pose a risk to another person; HSE says the law does not apply to a self employed person who employs nobody and whose work poses no risk to anyone else. Diving and vessel work can plainly put other people at risk, so that duty should be expected to apply, but this is a reading and not a ruling. Third, HSE says a person may be self employed for tax and yet not be self employed for health and safety. HSE's guide on employers' liability insurance says what matters is the real nature of the relationship, and gives as signs of employment that the Company controls where, when and how the person works, supplies the materials and equipment, and the person cannot send a substitute. Where those signs are present, a labour only worker may be an employee for health and safety, owed the full duties of section 2 and counted for employers' liability insurance, whatever the contract says. The legal test itself was not read, and no decision on the point was seen in the data.

Authority of the group

The organisation document GSS-COM-ORG-001 Draft 3 has no entry for this group. The authority below is the universal authority in Part 7 of that document, which applies regardless of employment status, with two limits proposed. Authority reserved to the Managing Director is not carried by this group.

  • Every person working for or on behalf of GSS, without exception and regardless of employment status, has the authority to stop their own work, or work they are involved in, where they believe it is unsafe.
  • No permission is required and nobody may be disadvantaged for exercising it.
  • A stop work decision is never overturned by the person whose work was stopped. It is reviewed by the accountable post for the area and the outcome recorded.
  • A contractor may decline work outside the agreed scope until the site contact has agreed it (draft Contractors Handbook).
  • No authority to instruct Company employees, to change a permit, a method statement or the scope of work without the site contact, or to commit the Company. The master's authority aboard a vessel is not affected.
