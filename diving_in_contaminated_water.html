Risk assessments / Diving in contaminated water

RISK ASSESSMENT

Diving in contaminated water

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionDiving, tending and supporting divers in water that is or may be contaminated with sewage, chemicals, fuel and oil or biological agents, including the choice of equipment, the decontamination of the diver and the equipment, and the health follow up
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Diving Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeAny GSS diving project in harbours, marinas, docks, commercial berths, rivers, canals, reservoirs and inland water, at or near sewage and surface water outfalls, at fish farm and industrial sites, in water affected by a spill, and in the Silvers marina basin, together with the dive control, the tender's working area and the place where the diver and equipment are washed down. The dive itself is assessed in RA 41 Dive Operations. Sewage handled aboard vessels is assessed in RA 52 Sewage Waste Handling, and substances used at work in the assessment titled Hazardous substances (COSHH). This assessment covers contamination that is already in the water the diver enters.
Main equipment usedDiving helmets or full face masks, dry suits with attached boots and gloves, umbilicals and surface supplied equipment, wash down hoses and clean water supply, brushes, disinfectant or detergent, containment for wash water, waste bags, hand washing and shower facilities, first aid and eye wash, and personal protective equipment for the tender. What GSS holds for contaminated water work is listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskDivers and standby divers, tenders and others who handle the diver, the umbilical and wet equipment, the Diving Supervisor, vessel crew on the working deck, the Dive Storeman who later cleans and services the equipment, and anyone at home or in a vehicle who comes into contact with contaminated kit.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. The diving project plan for each project identifies the contamination and the methods to protect the diver. Read with PRC 05 COSHH Assessment and PRC 12 Waste and Garbage Management.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Diving in contaminated water needs a suitable and sufficient assessment and a safe system of work, including for self employed divers working under the Company's control.
DivingDiving at Work Regulations 1997, regulation 6. The diving contractor must ensure that the project is planned, managed and conducted so as to protect those taking part, and that suitable plant is available. HSE approved code L104, paragraph 49 as read, states that diving can expose the diver to waters that may be contaminated and that the diving project plan should identify the methods put in place to protect the diver. The code gives no further detail on suits or decontamination, so the detailed controls below rest on industry practice.
Hazardous substancesControl of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002, regulation 7. Exposure to substances hazardous to health must be prevented or, where that is not reasonably practicable, adequately controlled, by measures applied in order of priority: the design of the work and engineering controls first, control at source next, and personal protective equipment only in addition where the others cannot achieve adequate control. The measures must include appropriate hygiene measures and adequate washing facilities. Regulation 7 contains further provisions for biological agents. The definition in regulation 2, regulation 11 on health surveillance and Schedule 3 were not read for this draft and their application to diving in sewage contaminated water is to be confirmed.
Biological hazardsHSE information on leptospirosis. Weil's disease is spread through contact with rat urine, often through contaminated water, and HSE lists divers and sewage workers among those at risk. HSE's stated controls are covering wounds, protective gloves and clothing, good hygiene, and showering promptly after exposure to water.
Differential pressureHSE Diving Information Sheet No 13 (rev 1), Differential pressure hazards in diving. Relevant at outfalls, intakes, sluices and pumped discharges, where contamination and a flow hazard occur together. The sheet advises avoiding the hazard, equalising pressure before diving, isolation and lock off under a permit to dive, and surface supplied equipment only.
Industry guidanceIMCA D 021, Diving in contaminated waters (International Marine Contractors Association, revision 2.1, November 2023). Named as the recognised industry guidance on hazards, risk assessment and control measures. Only the publisher's summary was read for this draft, not the document itself.
Marine and environmentMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply to the vessel and crew, enforced by the MCA. Wash water and waste from decontamination must not be put back into the water environment in a way that causes pollution; SEPA is the regulator, and the applicable rules are to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Diving without knowing what is in the water: no information from the client, the harbour authority or the outfall operator, no check on recent discharges or spills, and contamination assumed to be absent because the water looks clear

Potential consequence. The diver is sent in with equipment that gives no protection against what is there. Acute poisoning, chemical burns, serious infection, and a team with no decontamination set up.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, tenders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Before the project is accepted the Diving Manager asks the client in writing what is known about the water and the bed at the site: outfalls and their discharge times, storm overflows, industrial discharges, fuel berths, past spills, contaminated sediment, and any sampling results. The harbour authority, the outfall or sewerage operator and, where relevant, SEPA are asked for what they hold. The answers, or the fact that none was given, are recorded in the diving project plan. Where the information is not enough to rule contamination out, the water is treated as contaminated and the plan is written on that basis, with sampling arranged where the hazard could be chemical. Each site is placed in one of a small number of contamination categories set by the Diving Manager, each with a fixed minimum standard of equipment and decontamination, so that the Diving Supervisor has a clear rule and does not have to judge it on the day. The best control is to avoid the dive: the plan records whether the work could be done by remotely operated vehicle, camera, from the surface, at a cleaner state of tide, or after the discharge has been stopped. On the day the Supervisor checks for recent heavy rain, visible sheen, smell, discolouration and dead fish, and stops the job if conditions differ from the plan.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the contamination categories and the questions to put to the client into the diving procedures, and add them to the tender and project start checks. Owner: Diving Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Sewage in the water at or near outfalls, storm overflows, marina berths and vessel discharges: swallowing water, contamination of the mouth, eyes, ears and broken skin

Potential consequence. Gastroenteritis, hepatitis A, ear, eye, skin and wound infections. Time off work, and in some cases serious or lasting illness.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, tenders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Diving at an outfall is timed for when the discharge is stopped or at its lowest, by agreement with the operator, and not during or soon after heavy rain when storm overflows run. The dive is planned from up current of the outfall where the work allows, and the time in the water is kept to what the task needs. The diver's head is fully enclosed and the suit is dry and sealed, to the standard for the contamination category, so that no water reaches the mouth, nose, eyes, ears or skin. Cuts and grazes are covered with waterproof dressings before dressing in, and a diver with an open wound that cannot be covered, or who is unwell, does not dive in contaminated water. Nobody eats, drinks, smokes or vapes at dive control or in the wash down area, and hands and face are washed with clean water and soap before doing so elsewhere. Drinking water and food are kept in a clean area away from the kit. The diver showers as soon as practicable after decontamination. RA 52 Sewage Waste Handling applies to sewage handled aboard.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Identify the outfalls, overflows and marina berths at which GSS regularly dives and record the operator's contact and the discharge pattern for each. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Flow at an outfall, intake, sluice or pumped discharge starting or changing while the diver is near it

Potential consequence. Diver drawn onto or into the opening and held by differential pressure, with little chance of rescue. Death. Sudden release of a concentrated discharge onto the diver.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Any outfall, intake or pumped system at the site is identified in the diving project plan with its owner. The diver does not work at or near it until the flow has been stopped and the pump, valve or penstock has been isolated, locked off and tagged under a permit to dive agreed with the operator, with the key or tag held by the Diving Supervisor until the diver is out of the water. Where a valve is the only isolation, a second barrier is sought, as HSE Diving Information Sheet No 13 advises. Pressure is equalised before the diver approaches. Only surface supplied equipment with voice communications is used, and the diver approaches from the low pressure side, with the umbilical tended to keep the diver out of the hazard area. Automatic and remote starts are disabled and the control room is told that a diver is in the water. If isolation cannot be achieved and proved, the dive does not take place.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Issue a permit to dive as a controlled form for work at outfalls, intakes and pumped systems, and agree with each operator who isolates and who holds the lock. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Chemical, fuel and oil contamination in harbours and at industrial sites: floating fuel and oil, dissolved chemicals, antifoul residues and contaminated sediment disturbed by the work

Potential consequence. Skin and eye burns, dermatitis, breathing of vapour at the surface, poisoning through the skin or by swallowing, and long term effects from repeated exposure. Fire risk from fuel on the surface.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, tenders

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Where a chemical hazard is known or suspected the substance is identified, its safety data sheet or equivalent information is obtained from the client or site operator, and a COSHH assessment is made under PRC 05 before the dive is planned. The assessment establishes whether diving equipment can protect against that substance at all; where it cannot, or where the substance is not identified, the dive does not take place. Diving is not carried out through a floating layer of fuel or oil: the entry point is moved, the layer is boomed off and cleared, or the job waits. Where a sheen cannot be avoided the diver enters and leaves through an area kept clear by a water spray or boom, and the surface is treated as a fire hazard with no ignition sources at dive control. Disturbance of sediment is kept to the minimum the task needs, and the plan says where sediment is known or likely to be contaminated. The equipment standard is the highest category: fully enclosed helmet with positive pressure, sealed dry suit with attached gloves and boots, and materials the maker confirms are compatible. Exposure time is limited and recorded. The assessment titled Oil and chemical spill response applies where a spill is in progress, and diving in a live spill is not undertaken as routine work.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Decide which categories of chemical contamination GSS will and will not dive in, and record that as a Company limit in the diving procedures. Owner: Diving Manager with the Managing Director. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Leptospirosis (Weil's disease) and other biological agents in harbour, river, canal and inland water, including water contaminated by rats, and blue green algae in still fresh water

Potential consequence. Flu like illness that can progress to jaundice, meningitis and kidney failure, and can be fatal if not treated. Skin, eye and stomach illness from algal toxins.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, tenders

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. All inland, river, canal, dock and harbour water where rats may be present is treated as carrying a risk of leptospirosis, whatever it looks like. The controls HSE gives are applied in full: cuts and grazes covered with waterproof dressings before work, skin kept out of the water by a dry suit, gloves and an enclosed head, wet kit handled with gloves, hands washed before eating, drinking or smoking, and a shower as soon as practicable after the dive. Every diver and tender is told the symptoms and is given a card to show a doctor, stating that they work in water and may have been exposed to leptospirosis, since early treatment depends on the doctor knowing. Anyone who develops a fever, severe headache, muscle pain or vomiting in the weeks after such work reports it to the Diving Manager and sees a doctor without delay. Stores of food and waste at the dive site and the dive store are kept so as not to attract rats. Where a water body carries a blue green algae warning, or a visible scum or bloom, diving is postponed unless the work is urgent and the highest equipment category is used. Whether any immunisation is appropriate for this work is to be confirmed with an occupational health provider; none is assumed here.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Issue the medical contact card to every diver and tender, including the self employed, and take occupational health advice on immunisation and on which infections it should cover. Owner: Diving Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Equipment that does not keep the water out: scuba with a half mask and mouthpiece, a wetsuit, a neck dam or cuff that leaks, a demand valve or exhaust that lets water back into the helmet, and a leaking dry suit

Potential consequence. Contaminated water in the mouth, nose, eyes and ears and against the skin for the whole dive. The illnesses and injuries described above.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The equipment for each contamination category is fixed in the diving procedures and stated in the diving project plan. In water known or suspected to be contaminated, scuba with a half mask and mouthpiece and wetsuits are not used. The minimum is a full face mask or helmet and a dry suit in good repair with attached boots and sealed gloves. For sewage and chemical contamination the standard is surface supplied equipment with a helmet that mates to the suit to enclose the head completely, fitted with the maker's arrangement to stop water returning through the exhaust, and run so that the helmet stays at a slight positive pressure. The suit is of a smooth, non absorbent material that can be decontaminated, and neoprene and fabric faced suits are avoided where chemicals or sewage are present. The suit, seals, gloves and helmet are inspected and the suit is leak tested before the dive. The standby diver is equipped to the same standard. A diver who feels water enter the suit or helmet tells the Supervisor at once and the dive is ended. The equipment is used within the limits the maker gives for contaminated water.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what helmets, masks and suits GSS and its self employed divers actually use, and whether any are suited to sewage or chemical contamination. Where divers supply their own equipment, set the standard they must meet. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Fuel, oil, solvents or other chemicals in the water attacking the diving equipment: suit material and seams, seals, helmet parts, umbilical and hoses

Potential consequence. A suit or seal fails during the dive and lets contaminated water in. A weakened umbilical or hose fails later, on another job. Loss of breathing gas, drowning.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, later users of the equipment

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Where hydrocarbons or other chemicals are present, the maker's information on the resistance of the suit, the seals, the helmet and the umbilical to that substance is obtained before the dive, and equipment not shown to be compatible is not used. Exposure time is limited to what the maker allows. After the dive the equipment is decontaminated as the maker instructs, then inspected for swelling, softening, stickiness, cracking and staining, and anything doubtful is quarantined and taken out of use. Equipment that has been exposed to chemicals is identified in the plant register with the date and the substance, so that its history is known at the next service. Soft parts are replaced at a shorter interval where the maker advises it. The proposed assessment Diving plant, compressors and breathing air covers the register and the examination of umbilicals and hoses.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain the makers' chemical compatibility information for the suits, helmets and umbilicals in use and keep it with the plant register. Owner: Dive Storeman, checked by the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Decontamination of the diver done badly or not at all: undressing before washing down, contamination carried on the helmet, suit and gloves to the face and hands, no clean water at the site

Potential consequence. The diver is contaminated at the moment of undressing, after a dive in which the equipment worked. Infection, chemical burns, eye injury.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, tenders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. A decontamination area is set up before the first dive, between the water exit and the clean area, with a dirty side and a clean side marked, and nobody crosses it except through the wash. Enough clean fresh water for the day's dives is on site, with a hose or spray, brushes and the cleaning agent the equipment maker and the COSHH assessment allow. The diver is washed down fully dressed, from the head down, with the helmet on and the gas still flowing, paying attention to the neck seal, the gloves, the harness and the boots, and for sewage or chemical contamination is scrubbed with the cleaning agent and rinsed. Only then is the helmet removed, by the tender, who wears protective equipment, followed by the gloves and suit, turned away from the diver. The diver washes hands and face straight away and showers as soon as practicable. Any water that reached the skin is reported and the area washed. The same sequence is used for the standby diver if deployed. The sequence is practised by the team before the first dive and is supervised, not left to a tired diver.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the decontamination sequence as a short procedure for each contamination category and confirm what wash down equipment and clean water supply each vessel and shore set up can provide. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Exposure of the tender and the surface team: handling the wet umbilical, lifeline, tools and recovered items, splashes and spray during wash down, and contaminated deck and quay surfaces

Potential consequence. Infection through the hands, eyes and mouth, including leptospirosis. Chemical burns and dermatitis. Slips on a fouled deck.

Persons at risk. Tenders, Diving Supervisor, vessel crew

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The tender is treated as exposed, not as a bystander. The tender wears waterproof gloves, waterproof clothing and boots that can be washed, and eye and face protection during wash down, to the standard set for the contamination category. The umbilical is led so that it can be rinsed as it comes in, and is not coiled against the body or across the clean area. Recovered tools, debris and samples go into a marked container on the dirty side. Wash down is done at low pressure to limit spray and aerosol, with the tender standing up wind, and nobody without protection stands close. The tender's cuts are covered, and the same rules on eating, drinking, smoking and hand washing apply. The deck or quay in the dirty area is rinsed down at intervals, and kept clear to prevent slips. The tender is decontaminated last, removes gloves and outer clothing on the dirty side, and washes. Vessel crew are briefed to keep out of the dirty area unless equipped.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Specify the tender's protective equipment for each contamination category and confirm it is held in the sizes needed. Owner: Diving Manager with the Dive Storeman. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Cuts and puncture wounds in contaminated water and sediment: scrap metal, glass, fishing hooks and line, discarded needles, and marine growth on structures

Potential consequence. A breach of the suit or glove followed by a contaminated wound. Wound infection, tetanus, and blood borne infection from a needle.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, tenders handling recovered debris

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The diving project plan notes where debris and sharps are likely, such as under berths, at outfalls and in urban water. Where visibility is poor the diver works by touch with care, does not sweep the bed with open hands, and uses a tool or probe to move debris. Outer work gloves that resist cuts are worn over the sealed suit gloves where the task allows. Debris is lifted in a bag, bucket or basket and is not carried against the suit. Any cut, puncture or suit breach ends the dive. The wound is washed thoroughly with clean water and soap after decontamination, covered, and recorded, and medical advice is sought the same day for any puncture wound, any wound from a needle and any wound that later reddens or swells, with the doctor told that the water was contaminated. Divers are advised to keep their tetanus protection up to date through their own doctor. Recovered sharps are placed in a rigid container and disposed of as waste under PRC 12.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that cut resistant outer gloves and a rigid sharps container are part of the standard kit for harbour and urban diving. Owner: Dive Storeman, checked by the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Contaminated equipment, wash water and waste leaving the site: wet kit put into vehicles, the vessel's accommodation, the dive store and divers' homes, and wash water run back into the harbour or onto the quay

Potential consequence. People who were never at the dive are exposed, including the Dive Storeman and families. The store and vehicles become contaminated. Pollution and a complaint or enforcement by the harbour authority or SEPA.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, drivers, crew, households, the public

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Equipment is decontaminated at the site before it is packed. It travels in closed, washable boxes or bags marked as used in contaminated water, kept apart from clean kit, food and personal bags, and is not taken into accommodation or a vehicle cab. At the store it is cleaned again in a designated wash area by a person wearing gloves, eye protection and waterproof clothing, disinfected as the maker allows, dried and inspected before return to stock. Helmets, masks and demand valves are stripped and cleaned as the maker instructs. Personal undersuits and towels are bagged and laundered on a hot wash, and divers are told not to mix them with household washing. Where the contamination is chemical, how the wash water is contained and removed is agreed with the client and the site before the dive and written in the plan, and it is not discharged to the water or a surface drain. Disposable items, used absorbents and dressings are bagged and removed under PRC 12 and the assessment titled Waste handling and storage. Vehicles and boxes are washed out after the job.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Designate a wash area at the dive store with drainage that is acceptable to SEPA or the sewerage undertaker, and confirm how wash water from decontamination is to be disposed of at client sites. Owner: Diving Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. No health follow up: exposures not recorded, illness after a dive not connected to the work, no health surveillance or immunisation advice, and self employed divers left outside whatever arrangements exist

Potential consequence. A treatable infection is diagnosed late. Repeated low level exposure goes unnoticed. The Company cannot show that exposure was controlled.

Persons at risk. Divers, tenders, the Company

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Every dive in contaminated water is identified as such in the diving operation record, with the contamination category, the substances known or suspected, the time in the water and any suit breach, leak or wound. The record is kept so that a diver's exposures can be traced later. Divers and tenders are briefed before the job on the specific hazards and the symptoms to watch for, and are asked to report any illness in the following weeks to the Diving Manager, who records it and looks for a link to the work. The diver's annual medical examiner is told by the diver about contaminated water work. Occupational health advice is taken on whether health surveillance is appropriate for those who do this work regularly and on which immunisations, if any, should be offered, and the advice is recorded and acted on. The arrangements apply equally to the self employed divers and tenders engaged on the project. A case of illness linked to the work is reported under PRC 01 and considered for reporting to the enforcing authority.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain occupational health advice on health surveillance and immunisation for divers and tenders, to confirm, and decide how the cost and the offer apply to the self employed. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The Diving Manager checks each diving project plan for the client's contamination information, the contamination category and the equipment and decontamination standard before the plan is issued.
  • The Diving Supervisor records the contamination category, suit leak test, decontamination and any breach or wound in the diving operation record for every dive in contaminated water.
  • Records are sampled after each project, and at least one contaminated water job a year is observed on site by the Diving Manager or the Risk and Compliance Manager.
  • Illness reported by divers and tenders after such work is logged and reviewed by the Diving Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager for a link to the work.
  • Equipment exposed to chemicals is flagged in the plant register and checked at its next inspection.
  • Every suit or helmet leak, wound, failed isolation at an outfall and case of illness is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.

Emergency arrangements

  • Water enters the helmet or suit, or the suit is cut: the diver tells the Supervisor, the dive is ended, and the diver is recovered and decontaminated in the normal sequence unless a medical emergency overrides it.
  • Medical emergency in a contaminated diver: life saving care comes first. Recover the diver, give a rapid wash down if it does not delay resuscitation, remove the helmet and treat. First aiders wear gloves and eye protection, and the ambulance crew and hospital are told what the contamination is.
  • Contaminated water in the eyes or mouth: rinse the eyes with clean water or eye wash for at least 10 minutes, rinse the mouth and spit, do not swallow, and obtain medical advice.
  • Chemical on the skin: remove contaminated clothing, flood the skin with clean water, follow the first aid measures on the safety data sheet and obtain medical attention, taking the data sheet.
  • Fever, severe headache, muscle pain, vomiting or jaundice in the weeks after the work: see a doctor the same day, show the medical contact card, and tell the Diving Manager.
  • Diver held at an outfall or intake by flow: stop the flow at source, do not send the standby diver into the same flow, and follow the proposed assessment Diving emergency and recompression.

Related documents

  • The diving project plan for each project. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up, PRC 05 COSHH Assessment, PRC 12 Waste and Garbage Management and PRC 15 Hazardous Materials Emergency Plan. The draft procedure that governs diving in contaminated water is to be confirmed.
  • RA 41 Dive Operations (GSS-DIV-RA-01), RA 52 Sewage Waste Handling (GSS-ENV-RA-01), Hazardous substances (COSHH) (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Oil and chemical spill response (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Waste handling and storage (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), RA 56 First Aid (GSS-HSE-RA-13), RA 18 Working on or Near Water (GSS-MAR-RA-15), RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls (GSS-HSE-RA-29). The proposed new assessments Diving plant, compressors and breathing air and Diving emergency and recompression also bear on this work.
  • Client contamination information and sampling results, COSHH assessments and safety data sheets, permits to dive and isolation records, diving operation records marked for contaminated water, suit leak tests, equipment exposure entries in the plant register, medical contact cards issued, illness reports and occupational health advice.

PPE requirements

For the diver: a fully enclosed helmet or, as the minimum in the lowest category, a full face mask, with a sealed dry suit of a material that can be decontaminated, attached boots and sealed gloves, and cut resistant outer gloves where debris is present. For the tender and anyone washing down: waterproof gloves, waterproof clothing and washable boots, eye and face protection, and a lifejacket at the water's edge in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. For cleaning equipment at the store: waterproof gloves, eye protection and an apron or waterproofs. The exact specification for each contamination category is to be set by the Diving Manager, and personal protective equipment is the last control, after avoiding the dive, timing it and stopping the discharge.
