Risk assessments / Diving plant, compressors and breathing air

RISK ASSESSMENT

Diving plant, compressors and breathing air

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionRunning breathing air compressors, filling and handling high pressure cylinders including oxygen, testing air quality, running the gas panel and the compressed air supply to divers, and the maintenance, examination and testing of diving plant in the dive store and at the dive site
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Diving Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe dive store at Silverhills, any compressor, cylinder bank, filling point, gas panel or dive control set up on a vessel, quay, pontoon or vehicle for a diving project, and the transport of cylinders and plant between them. The dive itself is assessed in RA 41 Dive Operations. General cylinder storage is assessed in RA 17 Bottled Gasses and general use of compressed air in RA 34 Use of Compressed Air; this assessment covers what those two do not: air that people breathe, pressure high enough to fill diving cylinders, oxygen, and the supply to a diver in the water.
Main equipment usedLow pressure and high pressure breathing air compressors and their engines or motors, air receivers and filter housings, filling panels and whips, cylinder banks, bailout and scuba cylinders, oxygen cylinders and regulators, gas panels, umbilicals, hoses and fittings, pressure gauges, relief valves, analysers and air quality test equipment. The makes, numbers and capacities held are not established and are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskThe Dive Storeman and anyone else running a compressor or filling cylinders, the Diving Supervisor at the gas panel, divers and standby divers who breathe the gas, tenders, vessel crew and yard personnel near the plant, drivers carrying cylinders, and contractors who examine or repair the plant.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. The diving project plan for each project sets the gas quantities, the reserve supply and the checks before the dive.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Running, filling and maintaining diving plant is a work activity needing a suitable and sufficient assessment and a safe system of work, including for self employed divers and storemen working under the Company's control.
DivingDiving at Work Regulations 1997, regulation 6(3). The diving contractor must ensure that suitable and sufficient plant is available for the project, including for emergencies, and that the plant is maintained in a safe working condition. The Company is the diving contractor for its own projects, so the duty rests on the Company and is delivered through the Diving Manager.
Approved codeHSE approved code L104, Commercial diving projects inland/inshore (second edition, 2014). As read: gas quantities for primary, secondary and therapeutic use should be calculated (paragraph 61); procedures for checking and maintaining gas purity should be provided (paragraph 62); each diver should carry an independent reserve supply (paragraph 101); oxygen must be stored and handled correctly and oxygen plant kept clean of hydrocarbons (paragraphs 103 and 104); a system of planned maintenance should be established, examination and testing based on the manufacturers' recommendations, and plant checked before each dive (paragraphs 121 to 124). L103 applies in place of L104 to offshore projects and was not read for this draft.
Breathing gas qualityHSE Diving Information Sheet No 9 (rev 2), Diver's breathing gas standard and the frequency of examination and tests. It refers to BS EN 12021:2014 for the composition of breathing air, advises tests at least every three months where the source is a compressor system and more often where quality cannot be assured, and advises that the compressor inlet is checked so that contaminated gas is not drawn in. The sheet states that following it is not compulsory; it is the benchmark an inspector would use.
Pressure systemsPressure Systems Safety Regulations 2000. Regulation 8 prohibits the operation of a pressure system within its scope unless there is a written scheme for periodic examination by a competent person, covering protective devices, pressure vessels and the pipework where a defect may give rise to danger. Regulations 9, 11, 12 and 14 deal with examination under the scheme, operation, maintenance and records. Which items of GSS diving plant fall within the Regulations, and which cylinders are instead transportable pressure equipment under separate rules, is to be confirmed with the competent person.
Work equipment and noiseProvision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 and the Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005 are understood to apply to compressors, tools and noise exposure ashore. Neither was read for this draft, so their detailed requirements are to be confirmed before adoption.
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply to the vessel and her crew where diving plant is carried or run aboard, enforced by the MCA. The Master's agreement to where plant and cylinders are sited aboard is needed in every case.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Diving plant used with no system behind it: no plant register, no planned maintenance, no record of examination and test, and no means of knowing that an item is out of date before it goes to a job

Potential consequence. A compressor, cylinder, regulator, hose or gauge fails in use. Loss of breathing gas to a diver, a pressure release in the store, death or serious injury, and no evidence that the contractor's duty to maintain plant was met.

Persons at risk. Divers, the dive team, store personnel, the Company

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Every item of diving plant is entered in a plant register with a unique identity, the maker's maintenance instructions, the examination and test it needs, the interval, the date last done, the date next due and where the certificate is held. Intervals are taken from the manufacturer's recommendations as L104 advises, and from the written scheme of examination where the item is a pressure system. A planned maintenance schedule is run from the register by the Dive Storeman and checked by the Diving Manager each month. Plant that is out of date, defective or without a certificate is tagged, quarantined in a marked area of the store and cannot be loaded for a job. Before a project the plant for that job is checked against the register and the list is signed. At the site the dive team carries out the visual inspection and function check before each dive and records it, and the Diving Supervisor does not start the dive if it has not been done. Repairs use the maker's parts and are done by a person trained for that item, or by the maker or an approved service agent.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Compile the diving plant register and the planned maintenance schedule, since what plant is held and its certification status have not been established for this draft. Decide who signs plant out for a project. Owner: Diving Manager, verified by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Compressor drawing contaminated air at the intake: engine exhaust from the compressor itself, a vessel, a generator or a vehicle, fumes from painting, antifoul, fuel or solvents, or smoke

Potential consequence. Carbon monoxide or other contaminant delivered to the diver at pressure. Headache, confusion, loss of consciousness under water, drowning. Several divers affected if a bank of cylinders was filled from the same source.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, any person breathing from cylinders filled on the plant

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The compressor is sited so that the intake draws clean air: upwind of and above every exhaust, clear of fuel stores, paint and solvent work, the battery charging area and smoking areas, and with the intake extended by a hose of the correct bore where the plant cannot be moved. The position of the intake is checked every time the compressor is set up and again whenever the wind, the vessel's heading or the work nearby changes, as HSE Diving Information Sheet No 9 advises. An engine driven compressor has its own exhaust led well away from and downwind of the intake. No vehicle or plant is left running near the intake, and the area is marked. On a vessel the position is agreed with the Master, taking account of the main engine and generator exhausts at the heading held during the dive. Compressors are not run inside the store or another enclosed space unless the intake is led to clean outside air and the space is ventilated. Where a compressor supplies a diver directly, carbon monoxide monitoring on the delivery line with an alarm at the panel is the preferred safeguard. Filling stops at once if anyone smells or tastes anything in the air, and the gas already filled is quarantined until tested.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Fix and mark the compressor and intake positions at the dive store, and set a rule for siting on each vessel used for diving. Decide whether carbon monoxide monitoring is to be fitted to each compressor. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Filtration spent, wrongly assembled or bypassed, a compressor overheating or passing oil, and air quality not tested or tested late

Potential consequence. Oil, water, carbon dioxide or carbon monoxide in the breathing air. Diver incapacitated under water. Water freezing or corroding regulators and cylinders. Lung injury from oil mist over time.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Each compressor is run, serviced and lubricated as its maker instructs, with the oil the maker specifies for breathing air service. Filter elements are changed on running hours or elapsed time, whichever comes first, and sooner in warm or humid conditions, with the hours and the change recorded on a log kept at the machine. Condensate drains are operated at the intervals the maker sets. Filtration and compressors are not modified without the advice of the maker. Air from each compressor system is sampled and tested against BS EN 12021 at least every three months, as HSE Diving Information Sheet No 9 advises, and again after any major overhaul, any filter fault, any report of taste or smell and before the plant goes to a project if the last test is close to expiry. The test is carried out with calibrated equipment or by an outside laboratory, and the certificate is kept with the plant register and a copy carried to the site. A failed test takes the compressor out of use, and every cylinder filled since the last good test is emptied and refilled. Bought in gas is accepted only with the supplier's certificate of analysis.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish the date and result of the last air quality test for each compressor, who carries out the test and with what equipment, and set the three monthly test in the maintenance schedule. Owner: Diving Manager with the Dive Storeman. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Filling high pressure cylinders: a cylinder that is out of test, corroded or damaged, overfilling, a filling whip or fitting failing under pressure, and heat generated by fast filling

Potential consequence. Cylinder rupture or a whipping hose in the store. Death, loss of limbs, eye and hearing injury, and structural damage to the store.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, others in or near the store

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Only persons trained and authorised in writing by the Diving Manager fill cylinders. Before each fill the cylinder is checked for a current test stamp, its working pressure, damage, corrosion and the condition of the valve and thread, and a cylinder that fails any check is not filled and is quarantined. Cylinders are filled slowly, to the working pressure marked on them and no higher, from a panel fitted with a relief valve set and tested for that pressure and with gauges that are in calibration. Filling whips are rated for the pressure, are restrained with whip checks or tethers at each end, are inspected before every use and are replaced at the interval the maker gives. The fill valve is opened slowly with the operator standing to the side, never over the valve or in line with the gauge. Nobody other than the operator stands in the filling area during a fill, and the area is marked. Connections are never tightened or slackened under pressure; the line is vented first. The compressor has an automatic shut down at its set pressure and an emergency stop within reach of the filling point. Periodic inspection and test of cylinders is carried out by a test station competent for that type of cylinder, and the records are kept in the plant register.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm who is authorised to fill, what training they hold, the test status of every cylinder held, and whether the filling point has relief protection, whip restraints and a marked exclusion area. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Wrong or unverified gas in a cylinder: an unlabelled or wrongly labelled cylinder, an oxygen enriched mix not analysed, or a cylinder of unknown content or pressure taken to the dive site

Potential consequence. Diver breathing a gas unsuited to the depth, or a cylinder that is empty when the reserve is needed. Convulsion or loss of consciousness under water, drowning.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers, casualties given oxygen

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Every cylinder is labelled with its contents, and where the contents are anything other than air, with the analysed oxygen percentage, the date and the initials of the person who analysed it. Air and oxygen cylinders are stored apart and have fittings that cannot be cross connected. Any mix other than air is analysed with a calibrated analyser after filling and again by the diver or the Diving Supervisor before use, and both readings are recorded. Full, part used and empty cylinders are kept in separate marked bays and tagged. The pressure of every primary, reserve and bailout cylinder is checked with a gauge at the site before each dive and entered in the dive record, and a cylinder found below its planned pressure is changed, not topped up from an unverified source. The gas quantities for the job, including the reserve and the oxygen for treatment, are calculated in the diving project plan and loaded against that list.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm whether GSS uses any breathing gas other than air, and if so set out the mixing, analysis and labelling rules in the diving procedures. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Oxygen: contact between high pressure oxygen and oil, grease or other contamination, rapid opening of a valve, unsuitable regulators or hoses, and oxygen enrichment of a store, container or cabin

Potential consequence. Violent fire or explosion in a regulator, hose or panel. Clothing and materials burning fiercely in an enriched atmosphere. Severe burns, death.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, Diving Supervisor, dive team, crew nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Oxygen is kept to the quantity the diving project plan calls for. Oxygen cylinders are stored upright and secured, in a ventilated place, away from fuel, oil, grease, paint and other combustible material and away from heat, under the arrangements in RA 17 Bottled Gasses. Only regulators, hoses, valves and gauges made and cleaned for oxygen service are used, they are kept capped and bagged when not connected, and they are never used for another gas. No oil, grease or thread compound is applied to any oxygen fitting, and hands, gloves and tools are clean. Cylinder valves are opened slowly, with the regulator backed off and nobody in line with it. Oxygen is not decanted between cylinders by anyone who is not trained and equipped for it. Leaks are found with a leak detection fluid approved for oxygen. Smoking, naked flame, hot work and grinding are prohibited near oxygen in use or in store, with signs. Oxygen is not vented into an enclosed space, and where it is used in a cabin, container or chamber the space is ventilated and the oxygen level checked. Clothing that may have been enriched is aired well away from any ignition source before the wearer goes near one.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how much oxygen is held, where, with what fittings, and whether the store and each vessel have a designated oxygen stowage. Check that oxygen regulators and hoses are marked for oxygen service and are within their service life. Owner: Diving Manager with the Dive Storeman. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Failure of an umbilical, hose, whip, coupling or fitting under pressure in the store or at the dive site, including a wrong or mixed thread and a fitting damaged in transit

Potential consequence. Whipping hose or ejected fitting striking a person. Eye injury, hearing damage, lacerations, gas injected through the skin. Sudden loss of gas to the panel or the diver.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, tenders, Diving Supervisor, divers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Hoses, umbilicals and fittings are bought to a stated pressure rating for breathing gas service and are identified individually in the plant register. Each is visually examined along its length and at the end fittings before use, and is pressure and leak tested at the interval set by the maker or the written scheme, with the result recorded. A hose that is kinked, abraded, blistered, stiff, contaminated with oil or past its service life is withdrawn and cut up so that it cannot return to use. Thread types and pressure ratings are not mixed, adaptors are kept to the minimum, and no fitting is made off or repaired except by a person trained to do it with the correct parts. High pressure hoses are restrained at their ends. Systems are pressurised slowly, with nobody standing over a connection, and are vented before any connection is broken. Umbilicals are laid out clear of traffic, sharp edges, hot exhausts and moving machinery, are not driven over, and are protected where they pass over a quay edge or a gunwale. Plant is packed and secured for transport so that valves, gauges and fittings are not struck. RA 34 Use of Compressed Air applies to the general use of compressed air in the store.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Identify every umbilical and high pressure hose in the register with its age, last test and retirement date. Owner: Dive Storeman, checked by the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Loss or interruption of the breathing gas supply at the gas panel during a dive: compressor stop, empty bank, wrong valve operated, a regulator freezing or failing, or the panel left unattended

Potential consequence. Diver without primary gas at depth. Rapid ascent, drowning, decompression illness, lung injury.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The panel is arranged so that each diver, and the standby diver, has a primary supply and a secondary supply from an independent source that can be brought on line at the panel at once, and each diver carries an independent reserve that the diver can switch to without help, as L104 advises. The quantities held at the start of the dive are enough for the planned dive, for the standby diver and for an emergency, and are calculated in the diving project plan. Supply pressures are watched throughout, with a minimum bank pressure at which the dive is ended. Where a low pressure compressor is the primary supply, a receiver of adequate volume, a non return valve and an audible or visible low pressure warning are fitted, and the compressor is fuelled and checked before the dive and is attended while it runs. Valves and gauges on the panel are labelled by diver and by source. The panel is operated only by the Diving Supervisor or a competent person under the Supervisor's direct control, and is not left while a diver is in the water. Changeover from primary to secondary is practised by the team. The checks before the dive include a function test of each supply and of each diver's reserve.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the configuration of each gas panel held and that each provides an independent secondary supply and a low pressure warning. Record the changeover drill. Owner: Diving Manager with each Diving Supervisor. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Pressure vessels and protective devices in the diving plant (air receivers, filter housings, any compression chamber, relief valves, gauges) operated without a written scheme of examination or past the date of examination

Potential consequence. Rupture of a vessel or failure of a relief valve to lift. Blast and fragments in the store or on deck. Death or serious injury, and a breach of the pressure systems duties.

Persons at risk. Store personnel, dive team, crew, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Every part of the diving plant that forms a pressure system is identified with the competent person, and a written scheme of examination is drawn up or certified by that competent person before the system is operated. The scheme covers the pressure vessels, the protective devices and the pipework where a defect may give rise to danger, and states the nature and frequency of examination. Examinations are booked from the plant register so that none falls overdue, the reports are kept, and any repair or limit the competent person sets is acted on before the plant is used again. The safe operating limits are marked on each vessel and are known to the operator. Relief valves are not adjusted, gagged or isolated. No vessel is modified or repaired by welding or other means except to a method agreed with the competent person. Receivers are drained as the maker instructs and are protected from impact. Plant hired or borrowed for a project is accepted only with its current report of examination.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish which items of diving plant are pressure systems within the Pressure Systems Safety Regulations 2000, whether a written scheme of examination exists for each, who the competent person is, and when each was last examined. Owner: Diving Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Maintenance and repair of compressors and other diving plant: stored pressure in a system thought to be vented, moving belts and fans, hot cylinders and pipework, electricity, fuel, and solvents and oils used in servicing

Potential consequence. Injection or blast injury from residual pressure, entanglement, burns, electric shock, fire. Plant returned to service in an unsafe state.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, maintenance contractors

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Before work starts the plant is stopped, isolated from its power or fuel, vented to zero at every stage and proved to be at zero on the gauge and by opening a drain, and the isolation is locked or tagged so that nobody can restart it. Electrical work on a compressor is done only by a competent electrician under the assessment titled Electrical work and isolation. Guards over belts, couplings and fans are in place whenever the machine runs and are replaced before a test run. Hot parts are left to cool. Only the parts, lubricants and cleaning materials the maker specifies for breathing air service are used, and cleaning solvents are kept away from the air path and covered by a COSHH assessment. After any work that opens the air path, the system is reassembled, leak tested, run and the air quality tested before the plant is released, and the work and the release are recorded in the plant register. RA 19 Plant Maintenance, RA 25 Hand Tools and RA 35 Power Tools apply to the general hazards of the work and are not repeated here. Work beyond the storeman's training goes to the maker or an approved service agent.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set out in writing what maintenance the Dive Storeman is trained and authorised to carry out on each type of plant and what must go to the maker or a service agent. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Handling, moving, securing and transporting cylinders and heavy plant between the store, vehicles, quays and vessels

Potential consequence. A falling cylinder crushing a foot or hand, or shearing its valve and becoming a projectile. Back and shoulder injury. Cylinders or a compressor shifting on a vehicle or a deck.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, dive team, drivers, vessel crew

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Cylinders are stored and carried secured, with valves closed and protected by a cap, guard or frame, and are never left free standing. They are moved in a trolley, a rack or a lifting frame made for the purpose, not rolled on the base over distance, dragged or lifted by the valve. Large cylinders, banks and compressors are lifted by mechanical means under RA 21a Shore Lifting or RA 21 Crane Operations On Board, using lifting points provided by the maker. On a vehicle, cylinders and plant are restrained against movement in every direction, the load space is ventilated, and the rules for carrying compressed gas by road are followed. Aboard, cylinders and plant are lashed in a position agreed with the Master, clear of escape routes, heat and the working deck, and are included in the loading plan under the assessment titled Vessel loading and stability. Regulators are removed before a cylinder is moved. RA 20 Manual Handling and RA 17 Bottled Gasses apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm what cylinder trolleys, racks and lifting frames are held, and what rules apply to the quantities of compressed gas GSS carries by road. Owner: Diving Manager with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. Noise from compressors, engines and venting gas in the store, on deck and at dive control

Potential consequence. Permanent hearing loss and tinnitus over time. Instructions and alarms at dive control not heard, and communication with the diver degraded.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, Diving Supervisor, tenders, crew nearby

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Compressors are bought and kept with the maker's acoustic enclosure and silencers in place. The compressor is sited as far from dive control and from other workers as the hose run and the clean air requirement allow, and is screened where it cannot be moved. Venting is done through a silencer or slowly, and away from people. Dive control is positioned so that diver communications and panel alarms can be heard clearly, and the dive does not start if they cannot. The area around a running compressor is marked as a hearing protection zone, time spent in it is kept short, and hearing protection is worn inside it. The measures in RA 55 Noise apply, including assessment of exposure and health surveillance where the assessment calls for it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Measure the noise from each compressor at the operator position and at dive control and record the result against RA 55 Noise. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The Diving Manager reviews the plant register each month for examinations, tests and services falling due, and reports any overdue item to the Risk and Compliance Manager.
  • Air quality test certificates are checked for each compressor every three months, and no compressor is used for breathing air without a current certificate.
  • Compressor running hours, filter changes and condensate drain records are sampled at each workplace inspection of the dive store.
  • The Diving Supervisor's record of the checks before each dive, including bank, reserve and bailout pressures, is sampled when dive records are returned after a project.
  • Reports of examination under the written scheme are reviewed when received, and any defect or limit is entered in the plant register and tracked to closure.
  • Every loss of gas supply, failed air test, hose or fitting failure, oxygen incident and report of taste or smell in breathing air is reported as an incident, whether or not anyone was harmed, and reviewed against this assessment.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager audits the dive store against this assessment at least once a year.

Emergency arrangements

  • Loss of primary gas to a diver: the Diving Supervisor brings the secondary supply on line, the diver goes to the reserve if needed, the dive is ended and the diver recovered under the diving project plan.
  • Suspected contaminated air: stop the dive, change the diver to the alternative supply, recover the diver, give oxygen and obtain medical advice. Stop the compressor, quarantine the compressor and every cylinder filled from it, and keep a sample for test.
  • Hose or fitting failure under pressure: close the supply at the source if it can be reached safely, keep clear of the whipping hose, and treat any injury where gas may have entered the skin as a medical emergency even if the wound looks small.
  • Fire involving cylinders or oxygen: raise the alarm, close the cylinder valve only if it is safe to reach, evacuate, call the Fire and Rescue Service and tell them that cylinders, including oxygen, are involved and where. Do not go back to move cylinders that have been heated.
  • Cylinder dropped or damaged, or a valve leaking: clear the area, ventilate, do not use the cylinder, and quarantine it for inspection by the test station.
  • Aboard a vessel the Master is told at once of any gas release or fire, and the vessel's emergency procedures take charge.

Related documents

  • The diving project plan for each project. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up, PRC 05 COSHH Assessment and PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness. The draft procedure that governs the dive store and diving plant is to be confirmed.
  • RA 17 Bottled Gasses (GSS-HSE-RA-06), RA 34 Use of Compressed Air (GSS-HSE-RA-30), RA 19 Plant Maintenance (GSS-HSE-RA-26), RA 20 Manual Handling (GSS-HSE-RA-20), RA 25 Hand Tools (GSS-HSE-RA-16), RA 35 Power Tools (GSS-HSE-RA-27), RA 41 Dive Operations (GSS-DIV-RA-01), RA 55 Noise (GSS-HSE-RA-23), RA 21 Crane Operations On Board (GSS-MAR-RA-04), RA 21a Shore Lifting (GSS-MAR-RA-12), Electrical work and isolation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Vessel loading and stability (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Fuel and oil storage (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026). The proposed new assessment Diving emergency and recompression covers any compression chamber in use.
  • Diving plant register, planned maintenance schedule, written schemes of examination and reports of examination, cylinder test records, air quality test certificates, compressor running logs, gas analysis records, checks before each dive, filling authorisations and training records.

PPE requirements

Eye protection and hearing protection when filling cylinders, venting or working beside a running compressor. Safety footwear with toe protection in the store and when moving cylinders. Clean gloves, free of oil and grease, for handling oxygen equipment, and no oily clothing near oxygen. Gloves suited to the cleaning materials used in maintenance, as the COSHH assessment sets out. A lifejacket where plant is set up on a quay, a pontoon or an open deck, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. Personal protective equipment does not make it safe to stand over a valve or a connection under pressure: position and slow operation are the control.
