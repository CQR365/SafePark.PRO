1. The post

PurposeAccountable for statutory returns and company secretarial matters: the statutory registers, the filings and the company record.
Reports toManaging Director
DepartmentFinance
Stand inTo confirm. No stand in is named in the organisation document or the drafts.
DirectsNo one in this capacity.
Status of the postIn place and filled. Held with the Financial Controller post by the same person, who is not a director.

A private company is not required to have a secretary (Companies Act 2006 section 270). The Company has chosen to have one. The qualification rule in section 273 applies to public companies only, so the law sets no qualification for this post. A secretary who is appointed is an officer of the Company (section 1121), the Company must give notice of the appointment to the registrar (section 279G), and under Scots law the secretary is one of the people who may sign a document for the Company (Requirements of Writing (Scotland) Act 1995, Schedule 2 paragraph 3). The filing and record duties in the Act sit on the Company and its director. GOV.UK guidance is plain that the Managing Director stays legally responsible when someone else does the work. This post is how the Company carries those duties out.

Authority of the post

The organisation document GSS-COM-ORG-001 Draft 3 gives authority to the combined post of Financial Controller and Company Secretary, and all three items are financial, so they are shown in the Financial Controller profile. It gives nothing for company secretarial work, so the list below is proposed. Authority reserved to the Managing Director is not carried by this post or by anyone standing in for it.

  • File statutory returns and notices at Companies House for the Company, once the Managing Director has approved the content.
  • Keep the register of members and the minute book, and give inspection and copies as the Companies Act 2006 requires.
  • Sign a document as secretary where Scots law allows it and the Managing Director has authorised the document.
  • No authority to take a decision that belongs to the Managing Director or to the shareholders, to approve the accounts, or to change the Company's registered particulars without the Managing Director's approval.

Each standard is described in one sentence, followed by what it needs from this post.
