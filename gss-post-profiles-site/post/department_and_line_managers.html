1. The group

Who is in the groupEvery post that directs the work of other people: the department managers, their deputies and assistants when standing in, and line managers. Many draft clauses address managers together with masters, skippers and supervisors; those posts have their own profiles.
PurposeAccountable for delivering health and safety, quality and environmental requirements in the work they control, and for the people they manage.
Answers toDepartment managers answer to the Managing Director. Line managers answer to their department manager.
StatusA duty group, not a post. The group has no entry. Each manager's own post has its own profile; this profile holds the duties the drafts give to managers in general.

The word manager has no definition in the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 or the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. The law puts its duties on the employer, which is the Company. The Company can only meet them through the people who control the work, so the core functions below are the employer's duties written as the work of a manager. Two duties do fall on the manager personally: the duty of every employee under section 7 of the Act, and section 37, under which a manager can be guilty of an offence committed by the Company with the manager's consent or connivance, or through the manager's neglect.

Authority of the group

Each manager's own authority is set out in the profile for the post. Authority reserved to the Managing Director is not carried by any manager or by anyone standing in.

  • Stop their own work, or work they are involved in, where they believe it is unsafe. No permission is required and nobody may be disadvantaged for exercising it.
  • Stop any work in the area they manage.
  • Refuse to start or continue work where the risk assessment, the equipment or the competence of the people is not in place.
  • Review a stop work decision made in their area and record the outcome. A stop work decision is never overturned by the person whose work was stopped.
  • Exercise the authority of a post they stand in for only to the extent recorded in advance.
  • No authority reserved to the Managing Director, no authority beyond the limits delegated in writing to the post, and no authority to overrule a master at sea.

7. The ISO management system: what it needs from this group

The Company is certificated to three ISO standards and the work of the GSS departments is inside the certificated scope; managers at Silvers work inside the management system but outside the certificated scope. Each standard is described in one sentence, followed by what it needs from this group.

StandardWhat it isWhat it needs from this group
ISO 9001, quality managementThe standard for delivering what the client was promised, the same way every time, and improving how that is done.Each manager runs the processes they own to the documented method (8.1), keeps the people competent with records (7.2), and acts on nonconformities in their area (10.2).
ISO 45001, occupational health and safetyThe standard for preventing injury and ill health at work, with workers involved in how that is done.Hazards in the area identified and assessed (6.1.2), people competent and aware (7.2, 7.3), work planned and controlled (8.1), change managed (8.1.3), emergencies prepared for (8.2), workers consulted (5.4) and incidents reported and acted on (10.2).
ISO 14001, environmental managementThe standard for knowing how the Company's work affects the environment, controlling it and meeting environmental law.The environmental aspects of the department's work kept current in the register (6.1.2), with spill and waste controls applied (8.1) and emergencies prepared for (8.2).
