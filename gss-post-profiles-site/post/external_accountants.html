No law requires a private company to have an external accountant. Where one is used, the legal duties stay with the Company: it must keep adequate accounting records (Companies Act 2006 section 386), and its accounts must be audited unless it qualifies for an exemption (section 475). The firm has duties of its own. A firm that provides accountancy services to other persons is an "external accountant" under the money laundering regulations and must be supervised and must carry out due diligence on its client. If an audit is required, only a member of a recognised supervisory body may carry it out (section 1212).
