1. The group

Who is in the groupEmployees trained and named as first aiders, people appointed to take charge of first aid arrangements, and employees nominated as fire wardens. The drafts also use fire marshal and senior warden. First aid and fire duties of crew aboard vessels are in the vessel profiles.
PurposeAccountable for giving first aid within their training, and for carrying out the evacuation and fire procedures for their area, when an emergency happens.
Answers toTheir own line manager for their normal work. In an emergency, the person in charge of the emergency. The Risk and Compliance Manager assures the arrangements.
StatusA duty group, not a post. The group has no entry. Each member holds the role in addition to their own post.

The duty to provide first aid is on the employer, under the Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981. A first aider is a person the employer provides who has had appropriate training and holds a valid certificate. Fire safety in Scotland is governed by the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006, not by the English Fire Safety Order. That law does not use the titles fire warden or fire marshal. It requires the employer, or the person in control of the premises, to nominate competent persons to carry out fire fighting measures and evacuation. The duty stays with the employer: regulation 26 is headed to the effect that a nominated person's act or omission gives the employer no defence.

Authority of the group

The list draws on what the draft procedures already give first aiders and wardens. Authority reserved to the Managing Director is not carried by this group.

  • A first aider may decide whether a casualty needs an ambulance or further treatment (draft PRC 02 and PRC 10).
  • A fire warden may order people to leave their area and may refuse to let anyone go back in until the fire service or the person in charge allows it.
  • The senior warden present takes charge of an evacuation (draft PRC 08).
  • Stop their own work, or work they are involved in, where they believe it is unsafe. No permission is required and nobody may be disadvantaged for exercising it.
  • No authority to give treatment beyond their training, to fight a fire beyond their training or at risk to themselves, or to declare the premises safe to go back into. Who approves the return to work after an emergency is to confirm.
