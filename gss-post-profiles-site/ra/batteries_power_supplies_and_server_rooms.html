Risk assessments / Batteries, power supplies and server rooms

RISK ASSESSMENT

Batteries, power supplies and server rooms

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionKeeping, using and looking after battery systems and the rooms that hold them: the uninterruptible power supply and any battery bank that supports the servers and communications equipment, routine entry to the server room, the charging of batteries for tools and equipment in the office, the workshop and the dive store, the storage and disposal of batteries, and attendance by third party technicians to service or replace them
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Office Manager and Diving Manager
Location and scopeThe server and communications room or cupboard at the Silverhills office, any other place ashore where an uninterruptible power supply or battery bank is installed, and the places where batteries are charged or stored in the office, the workshop, the stores and the dive store. Battery charging in the Silvers boatyard is included where the same chargers and batteries are used. Batteries installed aboard the vessels, and engine starting batteries in vehicles and plant, are not assessed here except where they are brought ashore to be charged. Work on the fixed electrical installation is assessed in Electrical work and isolation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).
Main equipment usedTo confirm by inventory. Expected to include an uninterruptible power supply with internal or separate batteries, server and network equipment in racks, room cooling, chargers and battery packs for cordless tools, torches, radios, cameras, survey and diving equipment, lead acid batteries and their chargers, and laptops, telephones and tablets. Makes, ratings, battery types, voltages and quantities have not been established.
Persons at riskThe post or contractor responsible for IT, office staff who enter the server room, the Dive Storeman, divers, workshop and stores personnel, third party technicians, cleaners, people working alone or out of hours, and anyone in the building if a fire starts, including visitors and firefighters.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. Work on the uninterruptible power supply and its batteries is electrical work and falls under the controls in Electrical work and isolation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Battery systems and charging are work equipment and work activities that must be covered by a suitable and sufficient assessment. The duty reaches the self employed and contractors who work on the equipment or are affected by it. Neither instrument was read in this session and the point is stated in general terms only.
Electrical safetyElectricity at Work Regulations 1989, read as made. The definition of electrical equipment includes anything used to store electrical energy, and the definition of injury includes fire or explosion initiated by electrical energy, so a battery and its charger are within the Regulations. Regulation 4 requires systems to be constructed and maintained so as to prevent danger so far as is reasonably practicable. Regulation 14 restricts work on or near live conductors. Regulation 15 requires adequate working space, access and lighting. Regulation 16 requires technical knowledge, experience or supervision where these are needed to prevent danger.
Electrical safety, guidanceUsing electric storage batteries safely, INDG139(rev1), Health and Safety Executive. Guidance, not law. It sets out the precautions against explosion of charging gas, short circuit, electric shock from large batteries and contact with electrolyte that this assessment adopts. The HSE guide to the Regulations (HSR25, third edition, 2015) was identified but only its publication page was read.
Fire, ScotlandFire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006 apply to the premises at Silverhills. Battery charging and the server room are ignition risks that the fire safety risk assessment for the premises should take into account. Neither instrument was read for this assessment and the detailed duty is to be confirmed against Fire and emergency evacuation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).
Dangerous substancesDangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002. To be confirmed whether the hydrogen given off when vented lead acid batteries are charged, in the quantities held, brings any charging area within these Regulations. The Regulations were not read. The HSE guidance read gives hydrogen limits and ventilation advice, which are adopted as controls.
Product safety, lithium ionStatutory guidelines on lithium ion battery safety for electric bicycles, Office for Product Safety and Standards, 12 December 2024. These are written for those who make and supply electric bicycle batteries and do not place duties on the Company as a user. They are used here only as an authoritative description of thermal runaway and of why a battery must be charged with a compatible charger. HSE guidance specific to lithium ion batteries at work was searched for and none was found or read.
WasteWaste batteries are to be kept and sent for disposal in line with the duty of care for waste, with SEPA as the regulator in Scotland. The instruments were not read. To be confirmed against Waste handling and storage (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Battery systems and charging with nobody in charge: no inventory of the uninterruptible power supply, battery banks, chargers and battery packs, no owner, no maintenance record, no rule on where charging is allowed

Potential consequence. Old, damaged or unsuitable batteries and chargers stay in use until one fails. Fire, electrical injury, loss of the servers and communications, and no record to show what was done.

Persons at risk. All personnel in the buildings, contractors, the Company

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. An inventory lists every uninterruptible power supply, battery bank and charging point, giving its location, battery type, voltage, age, the maker's service interval and the date the batteries are due for replacement. Each has an owner by post: the post responsible for IT for the server room equipment, the Dive Storeman for the dive store, and the workshop or stores supervisor for tool batteries. Charging is allowed only at designated charging points, which are marked, and nowhere else. The maker's instructions for each system are held where the work is done. Batteries are replaced at the age or condition the maker gives and not run to failure. Battery systems and charging points are included in the workplace inspection and in the fire safety risk assessment for the premises. A defect is reported the same day, the item is taken out of use and labelled, and the defect is tracked to closure.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Compile the inventory of battery systems and charging points for the office, the workshop, the stores and the dive store, since none was seen, and name the owner of each by post. Owner: Office Manager and Diving Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Stored electrical energy in the uninterruptible power supply and battery bank: output terminals and battery strings that stay live when the mains supply is switched off, and a battery voltage that may be high enough to give a shock

Potential consequence. Electric shock, electric burn or arc flash to a person who believes the equipment is dead. Death in the worst case. The HSE guidance read notes that some large batteries produce more than 120 volts direct current.

Persons at risk. The post or contractor responsible for IT, third party technicians, electricians, anyone who opens the equipment

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Nobody at the Company opens an uninterruptible power supply, removes its covers or disconnects its batteries unless that person has the technical knowledge and experience for the system, or is supervised by someone who has. Until competence is confirmed this work is done only by the maker's or supplier's technician. The equipment carries a warning label stating that it stays live from its batteries when the mains is off, and the distribution board and any circuit it feeds are labelled as having a second source of supply. The written isolation procedure for the system covers the mains input, the bypass, the output and the battery, and is followed before any work on the equipment or on the circuits it feeds. Live conductors and battery terminals are insulated or shrouded. Work is done with the equipment dead where that can be done, and any live work is justified, planned and controlled as set out in Electrical work and isolation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026). Access to the room is limited to authorised posts. Routine users are limited to reading the display and responding to alarms.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish the type, rating and battery voltage of each uninterruptible power supply and battery bank, obtain the maker's isolation procedure, and label the equipment and the circuits it feeds. Owner: IT, with the Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Short circuit across battery terminals or connectors by a tool, a watch, a ring, a loose cable or a dropped metal object, during replacement, cleaning or handling of batteries

Potential consequence. Very high current, rapid heating, molten metal thrown out, severe burns to hands and face, eye injury, and fire or the battery bursting.

Persons at risk. Technicians, the post responsible for IT, Dive Storeman, workshop and stores personnel

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Battery terminals are kept covered, and temporary insulating covers are fitted to exposed terminals while work is done. Tools used on batteries are insulated and single ended. Watches, rings, chains and bracelets are removed and pockets are emptied of metal objects before working over batteries. Nothing is stored on top of or against a battery, and metal shelving above open batteries is avoided. Connectors and terminals are kept clean and tight. Spare and loose batteries, including small cells and tool packs, are stored with their terminals protected, in their cases or original packing, and are not carried loose in a bag, pocket or toolbox with metal items. The charger is switched off before leads are connected or removed.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Provide insulated tools and terminal covers where batteries with exposed terminals are handled, and give those who handle them a recorded briefing. Owner: Diving Manager and Operations Manager, each for their own stores. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Charging of lithium ion batteries for cordless tools, torches, radios, cameras, survey and diving equipment, laptops and telephones: a damaged, wet, faulty or counterfeit battery, the wrong charger, or charging left unattended

Potential consequence. Thermal runaway: the cell heats without control, vents flammable and toxic gas, and burns fiercely with little warning. A fire that spreads quickly, is hard to put out and can start again. Death or serious injury, and loss of the building.

Persons at risk. All personnel in the building, in particular those working nearby or alone; firefighters

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Batteries and chargers are bought from the equipment maker or a reputable supplier, and only the charger made for the battery is used. Charging is done at a designated charging point on a hard surface that does not burn, clear of paper, packaging, rags, fuel, paints and gas cylinders, and never on an escape route, under a stair or in a sleeping or rest area. Smoke detection covers each charging point. The number of chargers on one socket outlet is limited, extension leads are not joined together, and chargers are part of the routine inspection and testing of portable electrical equipment. Batteries are taken off charge when full, and so far as it can be arranged charging is done while the building is occupied and not overnight or over a weekend. Before each charge the user checks the battery, and does not charge one that has been dropped, crushed, pierced, flooded, is swollen, hot, smelling, hissing or leaking, or that no longer holds its charge. Batteries that have been in salt water, which is foreseeable for diving and survey equipment, are treated as damaged until the maker's guidance says otherwise. Batteries are kept out of direct sunlight and away from heaters. A suspect battery is moved outdoors to a marked place clear of buildings only if that can be done without risk, and is then quarantined for disposal.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Designate and mark the charging points in the office, the workshop, the stores and the dive store, confirm that each has smoke detection, and decide whether a fire resistant charging cabinet is justified for any of them. Owner: Diving Manager, Operations Manager and Office Manager, each for their own area, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Charging of vented lead acid batteries in the workshop, the stores or the dive store: hydrogen and oxygen given off during charging in a space with poor ventilation or a source of ignition

Potential consequence. Ignition of the gas at the battery, the battery bursting, acid and fragments thrown out. Burns, eye injury, and fire. The HSE guidance read states that hydrogen in air ignites readily across a very wide range of concentrations.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, workshop and stores personnel, others nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Lead acid batteries are charged only in a dedicated charging area with ventilation at high and low level, so that hydrogen, which rises, cannot collect under a ceiling, shelf or cover. The charging area is not a cupboard, a container with the doors shut or a room with no air change. Flames, sparks, smoking, grinding, hot work, heaters and telephones are kept away from batteries on charge. Battery vent caps and compartment lids are set as the maker instructs for charging. The charger is one that controls the charge and stops when the battery is full, and the battery is not charged faster than the maker allows. The positive lead is connected to the positive terminal, leads are not moved while the charger is on, and a vented battery is left to stand for at least 20 minutes after charging before it is disconnected or moved, as the HSE guidance read advises. Charging of lead acid batteries is kept apart from the charging of lithium ion batteries and from the storage of breathing gas and oxygen cylinders in the dive store.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm whether any vented lead acid batteries are charged ashore, where, and whether the ventilation of each place is adequate, taking advice where a battery bank is installed in a room. Owner: Diving Manager and Operations Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Contact with battery electrolyte from a leaking, cracked, overfilled or burst battery, or when topping up or moving batteries

Potential consequence. Chemical burns to the skin and permanent damage to the eyes from sulphuric acid or an alkaline electrolyte. Harm from lead and other battery materials carried on the hands.

Persons at risk. Dive Storeman, workshop and stores personnel, technicians, cleaners

Initial rating. L 3 x S 3 = 9 MEDIUM

Control measures. Sealed, maintenance free batteries are chosen where the equipment allows, so that topping up is avoided. Batteries are kept upright, on a tray or shelf that resists acid and will hold a leak. Gloves and goggles or a visor are worn for any handling of batteries that contain liquid electrolyte, with an apron and suitable boots where acid is handled. Clean water for eye washing is within reach of any place where such batteries are handled, and its position is marked. A spill kit suitable for battery acid is held, and a leak is neutralised and cleaned up at once. Hands are washed after handling batteries and before eating, drinking or smoking. Leaking batteries are placed in a container that resists acid and sent for disposal. Electrolyte and the products used are covered by Hazardous substances (COSHH) (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Confirm that eye wash, a suitable spill kit and the safety data sheets are in place wherever batteries with liquid electrolyte are handled. Owner: Diving Manager and Operations Manager, each for their own stores. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Heat in the server room: failed or inadequate cooling, blocked vents, equipment packed too closely, the room used as a store, and batteries ageing faster in a warm room

Potential consequence. Equipment and batteries overheat. Battery failure, swelling or fire, loss of the servers and communications, and heat stress to a person working in a small unventilated room.

Persons at risk. The post or contractor responsible for IT, office staff, technicians

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The room is cooled or ventilated to keep the temperature within the range the equipment makers give, and the cooling is serviced. The temperature is monitored, with an alarm that reaches a person who can act, including out of hours. Equipment is installed with the clearances the maker gives, and vents and fans are kept clear. The server room is not used as a store: paper, cardboard, packaging, cleaning materials, spare furniture and personal items are kept out. Cables are managed so that air can move and so that nothing has to be forced into place. Dust is controlled by planned cleaning carried out by a briefed person with the right equipment. If the cooling fails the owner is told at once, and the load is reduced or the equipment is shut down in the planned order before it overheats. Time spent working in the room when it is hot is kept short.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what cooling and temperature monitoring the server room has, who receives the alarm, and what the plan is if the cooling fails. Owner: IT, with the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Fire in the server room or at a charging point, and the means of fighting it: electrical fire behind closed doors, a lithium ion battery fire that does not respond to an extinguisher, and any fixed gas suppression system, to confirm whether one is fitted

Potential consequence. Smoke and toxic gas through the building, a fire that grows unseen out of hours, burns and death. If a gas suppression system discharges into an occupied room, harm from the gas or from lack of oxygen. To confirm.

Persons at risk. All persons in the building, firefighters, anyone in the room if a suppression system discharges

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The server room and every designated charging point are covered by automatic fire detection linked to the building fire alarm, and by the fire safety risk assessment for the premises. The room is kept shut, its door and any openings for cables keep the standard of fire separation the fire risk assessment sets, and combustible material is kept out. Extinguishers suitable for electrical equipment are sited outside the room door, and staff are told that a first aid extinguisher is for a small fire in its first moments only. Nobody tries to fight a battery that is venting, smoking heavily or burning: the alarm is raised, the building is evacuated, the door is closed and the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service is called and told that batteries are involved. Smoke from a battery fire is treated as toxic and nobody goes back in. If a fixed gas suppression system is fitted, its type, warning, hold off and lock off arrangements are understood by everyone allowed in the room, the system is locked off when the maker requires it for work in the room, it is serviced, and the room is not entered again after a discharge until it has been ventilated and declared safe. The general arrangements are in Fire and emergency evacuation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm what fire detection and suppression the server room and the charging points have, and have the fire safety risk assessment for the premises amended to cover battery charging and the server room. Owner: Office Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Restricted space in the server room or cupboard: little room to stand or turn, low or high racks, cables and raised floor tiles, poor lighting, a door that may lock or be blocked, and heavy items handled at awkward heights

Potential consequence. Trips, falls, head and hand injuries, strain from lifting batteries and servers in a cramped posture, contact with live or hot parts for lack of room, and a person who is hurt and cannot get out or be reached.

Persons at risk. The post or contractor responsible for IT, technicians, office staff

Initial rating. L 3 x S 3 = 9 MEDIUM

Control measures. The room has enough clear space in front of the equipment to work on it safely, with good lighting, as regulation 15 of the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 requires where work may give rise to danger. The floor is kept clear, floor tiles are replaced as soon as work under them stops, and cables do not cross the floor. The door opens from the inside without a key and is never blocked. Racks are fixed so they cannot topple, and heavy items are mounted low. Batteries and servers are heavy: their weight is checked before a lift, a second person or a lifting aid is used, and the work follows RA 20 Manual Handling (GSS-HSE-RA-20). Steps of a suitable type are used for high equipment, not a chair or the rack itself. Where the room is too small to work in safely with the equipment live, the work is planned with the equipment shut down.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Inspect the server room for working space, lighting, the door and the fixing of racks, and record what needs to change. Owner: IT, with the Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Working alone in the server room, the dive store or the workshop, including out of hours call outs to a power or equipment failure

Potential consequence. A person who suffers an electric shock, a burn, a fall or is overcome by smoke or gas is not found in time. Death in the worst case.

Persons at risk. The post or contractor responsible for IT, Dive Storeman, workshop and stores personnel

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Work inside an uninterruptible power supply or on a battery bank is never done alone: a second person who knows how to cut off the supply and call for help is present. Routine lone entry to read a display, reset equipment or collect a charged battery is allowed, with somebody told before entry and on leaving. Out of hours attendance follows RA 40 Lone Worker (GSS-HSE-RA-19), with an agreed check in time and a named contact who will act if the check in is missed. A person called out to a fault first finds out what the alarm is, and does not enter a room where there is smoke, a smell of burning, a suppression alarm or a hissing battery. A telephone or radio that works inside the room is carried.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Decide which battery and server room tasks may be done alone and which may not, and write that into the lone working arrangements for the office and the dive store. Owner: Office Manager and Diving Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Third party technicians servicing or replacing the uninterruptible power supply, batteries, cooling, suppression or network equipment without control by the Company

Potential consequence. Work started on live equipment without warning, isolation removed by someone else, fire alarm or suppression set off or left disabled, power to the servers lost, injury to the technician or to staff nearby.

Persons at risk. Technicians, office staff, the Company

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The contractor is selected for competence in the make and type of equipment, and gives a risk assessment and method statement for the visit before it takes place. The technician signs in, is briefed on the fire arrangements, the suppression system if one is fitted, the limits of the work and the Company contact, and is escorted to the room. The Company contact agrees with the technician what will be isolated, what stays live, and who else must be told that the servers or communications may go down. Any disabling of fire detection or suppression is agreed with the Office Manager, limited to the time of the work, covered by a watch while it lasts, and restored and checked before the technician leaves. The technician works with a second person present for any work inside the equipment. Old batteries are removed by the contractor or placed in the agreed quarantine. A service report is obtained and filed, and any defect it records is given an owner and a date.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm who services each system, under what contract and at what interval, and set the Company contact and sign in arrangement for their visits. Owner: IT, with the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. Storage, transport and disposal of spare, used and damaged batteries: stock piled together, terminals exposed, damaged lithium ion batteries put in general waste or left in a store

Potential consequence. Fire in a store, a waste container or a waste vehicle, acid leaks, pollution, and a breach of the duty of care for waste.

Persons at risk. Stores, workshop and dive store personnel, cleaners, waste contractors, the public

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Stock is kept to what is needed. Batteries are stored cool and dry, off the floor, apart by type, with terminals protected, away from fuel, gas cylinders and combustible stock, and where a detector covers them. Used batteries go into a marked collection container suitable for the type and are never put in general waste or a skip. A damaged or suspect lithium ion battery is kept on its own, outdoors or in a container that will not burn, clear of buildings, until a waste contractor who accepts damaged lithium batteries collects it. Batteries sent by courier or carried in vehicles and on vessels are packed as the maker and the carrier require. Disposal is through a licensed waste contractor with the paperwork kept, as set out in Waste handling and storage (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026).

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set up marked collection points and a quarantine place for damaged batteries, and confirm that the waste contractor accepts each battery type, including damaged lithium ion batteries. Owner: Operations Manager and Diving Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The owner of each charging point checks it each week for damaged batteries and chargers, combustible material nearby, overloaded sockets and batteries left on charge, and records the check.
  • The post responsible for IT checks the uninterruptible power supply display and alarms, the room temperature and the housekeeping of the server room at an interval to be set, and records each check.
  • The uninterruptible power supply, its batteries, the room cooling and any fire suppression system are serviced by a competent contractor at the maker's interval, and the service reports are kept with the inventory.
  • Chargers and other portable electrical equipment are inspected and tested within the Company arrangements for portable equipment, and failed items are destroyed or repaired.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager samples the inventory, the checks and the service reports during workplace inspections and internal audits, and reports the findings to the management review.
  • Any battery that swells, overheats, leaks, smokes or catches fire, and any electric shock or short circuit, is reported as an incident or near miss and investigated.
  • This assessment is reviewed each year, and sooner after an incident, a change of equipment or battery type, a move of the server room or new guidance from the HSE or the fire service.

Emergency arrangements

  • A battery that is hot, swelling, hissing, smoking or smelling: stop charging by switching off at the socket if this can be reached safely, leave the battery where it is unless it can be moved outdoors without risk, keep people away and tell the owner of the area at once.
  • Fire or heavy smoke from a battery or from the server room: raise the alarm, leave by the nearest exit, close doors behind you, call 999 and tell the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service that batteries or an uninterruptible power supply are involved. Do not go back in.
  • Electric shock: do not touch the person until the supply is off, remembering that an uninterruptible power supply stays live from its batteries. Switch off or isolate, call 999, and give first aid as trained, following RA 56 First Aid (GSS-HSE-RA-13).
  • Electrolyte in the eye or on the skin: wash with plenty of clean water at once and keep washing, remove contaminated clothing, and get medical help. Take the safety data sheet or the battery label with the casualty.
  • Discharge or alarm of a fixed gas suppression system, if one is fitted: leave the room, close the door, keep everyone out and do not enter again until the room has been ventilated and declared safe by a competent person.
  • Loss of cooling or of mains power to the server room: tell the post responsible for IT, who follows the planned shutdown order. Nobody opens the uninterruptible power supply to try to restore power.
  • After any of these events the area is made safe, the item is quarantined, the event is reported the same day to the department manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager, and the equipment is not put back into use until it has been checked by a competent person.

PPE requirements

No personal protective equipment is needed to put a sealed battery pack on its charger or to enter the server room for routine checks. For handling batteries that contain liquid electrolyte: chemical resistant gloves and goggles or a visor, with a plastic apron and suitable boots where acid is handled or topped up, as the HSE guidance read advises. For lifting batteries and equipment: safety footwear and gloves that give grip. Metal jewellery and watches are removed before working over exposed terminals. Any protective equipment for work inside an uninterruptible power supply is specified by the competent technician doing that work and is not issued to routine users. Protective equipment is the last control and does not replace isolation, covered terminals and ventilation.
