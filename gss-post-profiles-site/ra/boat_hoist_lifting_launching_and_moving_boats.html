RISK ASSESSMENT

Boat hoist: lifting, launching and moving boats

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionLifting boats out of the water and launching them with the boat hoist, travelling through the yard with a boat in the slings, setting boats down onto cradles, props, trailers and transport, and lifting them again for relaunch or for moves within the yard
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe hoist dock or slipway, the hoist runways and travel routes, the wash down area and every part of the Silvers Marine yard at Silverhills where a boat is lifted, carried or set down by the boat hoist. Lifts by mobile or fixed crane, including mast stepping, remain under RA 21a Shore Lifting and are covered here only where the same boat is passed between crane and hoist. Storage of the boat once it is set down is assessed in Boats stored ashore and work on them.
Main equipment usedThe boat hoist, its slings, spreaders, shackles and other lifting accessories, sling tie lines and keepers, cradles, props, keel blocks and chocks, boat trailers and transport, mooring lines and fenders, radios or agreed hand signals, barriers and signs for the lift area. The make, model and safe working load of the hoist are not known to the assessor and are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskThe hoist operator, the banksman and slingers, yard personnel placing cradles and props, boat owners and their crew, customers' own contractors, delivery and boat transport drivers, other customers and members of the public in the yard and on the pontoons, and children.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. No draft lifting procedure for the Silvers yard has been seen. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up and PRC 19 Induction apply. RA 21a Shore Lifting applies to crane lifts.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Lifting operationsLifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998, regulation 8. Every lifting operation involving lifting equipment must be properly planned by a competent person, appropriately supervised and carried out in a safe manner. Each lift, launch and move of a boat with the hoist is a lifting operation.
Thorough examinationLifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998, regulation 9. Lifting equipment must be thoroughly examined at least every 12 months, and lifting accessories and equipment for lifting people at least every 6 months, or in line with an examination scheme, and after exceptional circumstances liable to jeopardise its safety. This applies to the hoist and to its slings and shackles.
Work equipmentProvision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998. HSE guidance states that work equipment must be suitable for its intended use, maintained in efficient working order and good repair, inspected where conditions cause deterioration, and used only by people who have had adequate training. The hoist is mobile work equipment and is exposed to salt water.
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Cited as the general framework for assessment, safe systems of work and the protection of people who are not employees. The text was not read for this assessment.
Occupier, ScotlandOccupiers' Liability (Scotland) Act 1960, section 2. The occupier owes a person entering the premises such care as is reasonable in all the circumstances to see that the person does not suffer injury from dangers due to the state of the premises or to anything done or omitted on them. Customers and visitors in a yard where boats are lifted are within this duty.
Industry standardTo confirm. No published HSE guidance specific to boat hoists was found, and the trade body code of practice is available to members only and was not read. The hoist manufacturer's operating manual is the primary technical standard and has not been seen.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Boats lifted, launched and moved with no planned system: the weight and lifting points of the boat not known, nobody appointed to plan or supervise, and no record of the lift

Potential consequence. Overload of the hoist or slings, a boat dropped or tipped, a person crushed. Death, multiple casualties, loss of a customer's boat and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. Hoist team, boat owners, customers, the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Every lift is planned before the boat is in the slings. A generic lift plan covers routine lifts of boats within set limits of weight, length, beam and hull form, and names the hoist, the sling arrangement, the travel routes, the team and the weather limits. A boat outside those limits, or one that is damaged, waterlogged, of unknown weight, of unusual hull form or to be lifted in tandem with a crane, is treated as a non routine lift and has its own written plan approved by the appointed person before the day. The weight of each boat is established from the builder's data or the owner's declaration and is checked against the hoist load indicator as the boat comes clear of the water; where the indicated weight exceeds the plan the lift stops and the boat is lowered. A booking record is kept for each boat showing the declared weight, the draught, the keel type, the sling positions used and any defects noted, so that the next lift starts from known facts. One named person supervises each lift and has authority to stop it. The plan is briefed to the whole team before the lift, including the owner where the owner is present.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Appoint in writing the competent person who plans lifting operations at Silvers, write the generic lift plan and the limits for a routine lift, and issue the boat lift record as a controlled form. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Failure of the hoist, its slings or other lifting accessories through defect, corrosion, overdue examination or overload

Potential consequence. Sling parting or structural failure with a boat in the air. Boat falls onto people or into the dock. Death.

Persons at risk. Hoist team, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The hoist is thoroughly examined by a competent person at the interval the Regulations set or under a written examination scheme, and the slings, shackles and spreaders are examined as lifting accessories at their own interval. The current reports are held at the yard and the next due dates are entered in the yard diary. The hoist is not used when an examination is overdue or when a report records a defect that affects safety. The safe working load is marked on the hoist and on each accessory, and no lift is made above it. The hoist is maintained to the manufacturer's schedule and the maintenance is recorded. The operator carries out and records a check before first use each day, covering slings and stitching, sling keepers, shackles and pins, wire ropes or chains, hydraulic hoses and leaks, tyres, steering, brakes, the load indicator, the horn and beacon, and the emergency stop. Slings are rinsed, dried and stored out of sunlight, and a sling that is cut, chafed, contaminated or of unknown history is withdrawn and destroyed. After any overload, impact, immersion of parts not designed for it or other exceptional event the hoist is taken out of use until it has been examined.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain the current thorough examination reports for the hoist and every accessory, confirm the examination arrangement with the third party that carries it out, and set up the daily check record. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager, verified by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Slings placed in the wrong position for the boat: on shafts, propellers, saildrives, stabilisers, log impellers or unsupported hull, or so that the boat can slide forward or aft out of the slings

Potential consequence. Boat slips or tips in the slings and falls. Hull or stern gear crushed. Person struck or crushed. Death.

Persons at risk. Hoist team, owner and crew, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Sling positions are taken from the builder's lifting marks or lifting diagram where the boat has them, and otherwise from the owner's information and the record of the last lift. Where neither exists the underwater profile is established before the lift, by drawings, by photographs from the last time ashore or by inspection, and the positions chosen are recorded. Slings are kept clear of shafts, propellers, drives, rudders, skegs, transducers and trim tabs. Fore and aft slings are tied together, or sling keepers are fitted, wherever the hull shape would let a sling slide. Sling spread and length are adjusted so that the boat lifts level and the slings do not bear on guardrails, stanchions or rubbing strakes. Backstays, forestays and aerials are checked for clearance from the hoist beams before the boat is driven into the dock, and rigging is slackened or removed only by a competent rigger. The boat is lifted a short distance and held while the supervisor checks trim, sling seating and the indicated weight before the lift continues. Nobody adjusts a sling by hand while it is under load.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Start a sling position record for each boat lifted and require the lifting diagram or declared sling points on the booking form. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. People under or close to a suspended boat, or aboard it while it is lifted, including work on the hull while it hangs in the slings

Potential consequence. Crushed by a falling or swinging boat, or thrown from the deck. Death.

Persons at risk. Hoist team, yard personnel, owners and crew, customers' contractors

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Nobody is under a boat in the slings and nobody stands between a suspended boat and a fixed object. A boat is not lifted, travelled or lowered with anyone aboard; the owner and crew step ashore before the lift and board again only when the boat is afloat and the supervisor says so. Pressure washing, scraping, inspection and survey of a boat in the slings are done only with the boat lowered onto keel blocks so that its weight is carried by the ground and the slings act as a steady, with the hoist engine controls isolated and the operator in attendance. A boat is not left hanging in the slings over a break or overnight unless it has been landed on blocks in that way. A lift area is set out around the dock and the wash down area and is closed with barriers and signs for the duration of each lift, and the supervisor confirms it is clear before the operator moves.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Mark out the lift area and the wash down area, provide barriers and signs, and write the rule for work on a boat held in the slings into the yard rules given to customers and contractors. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Travelling through the yard with a boat in the slings: restricted view from the operating position, load swing, slopes, soft or uneven ground, stored boats, vehicles, overhead cables and structures

Potential consequence. Pedestrian run over or crushed against a stored boat. Hoist overturns or boat swings into a prop and brings down a stored boat. Contact with an overhead line. Death.

Persons at risk. Operator, banksman, customers, contractors, the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Travel routes are fixed, kept clear and wide enough for the hoist and the beam of the boat, and are on ground proved to carry the wheel loads. Boats are laid up to a yard plan that keeps those routes open. The boat is carried as low as the route allows, at walking pace, with the slings tied and the boat steadied before the hoist moves. A banksman walks ahead and in sight of the operator on every move, with agreed signals or a radio, and the operator stops at once if sight or contact is lost. Where a second person is needed to watch the blind side, one is provided. The horn and beacon are working. Vehicles and pedestrians are held clear of the route for the duration of the move, and customers' cars and trailers are not parked on it. Moves are not made across slopes or over ground softened by rain beyond what the manufacturer allows. Mast height is checked against every overhead cable, tree and structure on the route, and no boat with its mast up is taken under or close to an overhead power line. Workplace transport and deliveries applies to the interface with other traffic.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Draw the yard plan showing hoist routes, set down areas, overhead cables and no parking zones, and confirm the ground bearing capacity of the routes and runways. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Working at the hoist dock, runways or slipway edge: the hoist driven off a runway or over the edge, a person falling into the dock, and a person caught between the boat and the dock wall

Potential consequence. Hoist and boat into the water with the operator. Drowning, cold water shock, crush injury. Death.

Persons at risk. Operator, banksman, line handlers, owner and crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Runways and dock edges have wheel stops or kerbs strong enough for the hoist, and the end of travel is marked so that the operator can see it. The operator lines up on the runways under the direction of the banksman and does not rely on judgement alone. The dock edge is kept clear of ropes, hoses and debris and is treated for weed and ice. Everyone working at the dock edge, on the runways or handling lines wears a lifejacket, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. Lines are handled from the dockside with boat hooks and heaving lines, and nobody reaches or steps between the boat and the wall. A lifebuoy with a line and a means of getting out of the water are at the dock. Where a slipway is used, the limit of travel down the slope is marked and the state of the tide is planned so that the boat floats without the hoist going beyond it. A second person is always present when the hoist is at the dock.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Inspect the dock, runways and slipway for wheel stops, edge marking, ladders and lifesaving equipment, and have the structural condition of the runways confirmed by a competent person. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Lifting or travelling in strong or gusting wind, with masts up, high topsides or covers adding windage, or in poor visibility, ice or heavy rain

Potential consequence. Boat swings or is blown in the slings, strikes the hoist frame or a person, or cannot be controlled in the dock. Hoist becomes unstable. Death.

Persons at risk. Hoist team, owner and crew, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The lift plan states a maximum wind speed for lifting and a lower limit for boats with masts up or with high windage, taken from the hoist manufacturer's limits. The forecast is checked when the day's lifts are set and the wind at the dock is measured before each lift. The supervisor postpones a lift when the limit is reached or when gusts make control of the boat in the dock doubtful, and a customer's timetable or the tide is not a reason to go ahead. Sails, covers and canopies are stowed or removed before the lift. Lifts are made in daylight or under lighting good enough for the operator and banksman to see each other, the slings and the route. In ice or standing water the route and dock edge are treated before the lift. Lightning and severe weather applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain the manufacturer's wind limits for the hoist, set the yard limits in the lift plan and provide a means of measuring wind speed at the dock. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. The hoist operated, or a lift directed, by a person who is not trained, assessed and authorised, including use by a stand in when the usual operator is away

Potential consequence. Errors in slinging, travel and set down. Any of the outcomes above. Death.

Persons at risk. All persons in the yard

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Only persons named on a written authorisation operate the hoist, and the same applies to those who act as banksman and slinger. Authorisation follows training on the hoist in use, from the manufacturer, the supplier or an accredited provider, together with a practical assessment at Silvers, and it is reviewed at a set interval and after any incident. A person in training operates only under the direct supervision of an authorised operator. The key or isolator is removed and held securely when the hoist is not in use, so that customers, contractors and unauthorised personnel cannot start it. There are always enough authorised people on site to make up the team in the lift plan, and a lift is not made short handed. Operators are fit for the work and are not under the influence of alcohol or drugs; Drug and alcohol testing applies. The competence record for each person is held with the yard training records.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what training and assessment each person who operates or directs the hoist has had, identify a recognised course for boat hoist operation, and issue written authorisations. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Handover between the hoist and the cradle, props, trailer or transport: placing and adjusting supports beneath a boat that is still in the slings, and releasing the slings before the boat is stable

Potential consequence. Boat settles, rolls or drops onto the person setting the supports. Hands and arms trapped. Boat falls after the hoist has left. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel setting supports, hoist team, transport drivers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The cradle or keel blocks are set out and checked before the boat arrives, on ground prepared for it, so that time under the boat is kept to the minimum. The boat is lowered until the keel takes the weight on the blocks and is held there by the slings while supports are brought in from the side. Nobody goes under the hull; props and pads are positioned with the body outside the line of the hull and are adjusted from the side. The slings stay on and loaded enough to steady the boat until the supervisor has checked every support, the chains or ties between props and the level of the boat, and has told the operator to release. Slings are then slackened in stages while the supports are watched. The supervisor records that the boat has been handed over to storage and who set the supports. Loading onto a trailer or a transporter is done with the driver present and the vehicle braked and chocked, and the driver confirms the boat is secure before the slings come off. The reverse sequence applies before relaunch: the slings are on and holding before any prop is moved.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the set down and pick up sequence into the lift plan and agree the hold point at which the supervisor releases the hoist. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Launching: a boat taking in water once afloat, engines started in the dock, lines under load, and the owner boarding between the dock and the boat

Potential consequence. Boat sinks in the slings or the dock. Person falls into the water between boat and wall, or is struck by a parting line. Drowning or crush injury. Death.

Persons at risk. Owner and crew, line handlers, hoist team

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Before the launch the owner or the yard confirms that seacocks, skin fittings, drain plugs, log impellers and stern glands have been checked, and this is recorded on the lift record. The boat is lowered until it is afloat and is held in the slings while a person goes aboard by a safe access to check the bilges. The slings are not released until that check is reported clear. If the boat takes water it is lifted again at once. Engines are started only when the boat is afloat, with the slings clear of the propellers and nobody in the water or on the dock ladders. Boarding is by a fixed or secured access and not by stepping across a gap. Mooring lines are sound and are tended from the dockside. The boat is moved out of the dock promptly to a berth, under the owner's or a competent person's control.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Add the pre launch checks and the afloat hold point to the boat lift record. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Customers, their families, their contractors and members of the public entering the lift area or the travel route while a lift or move is in progress

Potential consequence. Person walks or drives under or beside a suspended boat, distracts the operator or is struck by the hoist. Death.

Persons at risk. Customers, children, contractors, the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Lifts are booked, and the day's programme is known to the yard office so that customers can be told when and where the hoist will be working. The lift area and the route are closed with barriers for each lift and a member of the team is posted where people are likely to approach. Owners who wish to watch do so from a marked place outside the lift area. Owners are told at booking, and again on the day, that they take instructions from the lift supervisor, that they do not help with slings or supports unless asked, and that children and dogs are kept out of the area. Customers' contractors working on nearby boats stop work and move away when asked. The operator stops whenever a person enters the area. Site rules, signs at the yard entrance and the induction of contractors under PRC 19 set out these rules. Site security and unauthorised access applies to people who should not be in the yard at all.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Further action required. Write the customer and contractor yard rules for lifting days, sign the lift area and viewing point, and decide how public and customer access to the yard is controlled during lifts. The residual rating stays high until segregation on the ground has been confirmed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. Handling slings, blocks, props and lines, noise and exhaust from the hoist, and slips on wet and weeded surfaces around the dock and wash down area

Potential consequence. Back and shoulder injury, trapped fingers, hearing damage over time, sprains and fractures from slips.

Persons at risk. Hoist team, yard personnel

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Wet slings, blocks and props are moved by two people or by mechanical aid where their weight needs it, and blocks and cradles are brought to the set down point by lift truck under Telehandler and lift truck operation. Gloves are worn for handling slings, shackles and lines. Hands are kept out of the bight of a sling and away from sling keepers as the load comes on. The dock edge, the wash down area and the runways are cleaned of weed and are gritted in frost. Noise at the operating position and for the banksman is assessed under RA 55 Noise and hearing protection is used where that assessment requires it, of a type that still allows signals and radio messages to be heard. RA 20 Manual Handling and RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Confirm whether a noise assessment has been made for the hoist. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The daily check record for the hoist and the boat lift records are reviewed each month by the Silvers Yard Manager for completeness and for repeated defects.
  • Thorough examination due dates for the hoist and its accessories are tracked, and a report recording a defect is passed to the Risk and Compliance Manager on the day it is received.
  • The Silvers Yard Manager observes a sample of lifts each quarter against the lift plan, including the set down sequence and the control of the lift area, and records what was seen.
  • Operator and banksman authorisations are reviewed at least annually and after any incident.
  • Every dropped or slipped load, contact with a stored boat, overload indication, near miss with a pedestrian and entry of a person into the lift area is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.
  • The yard plan and travel routes are checked at the start and end of each lay up season, when the number of boats ashore changes most.

Emergency arrangements

  • Boat slipping or unstable in the slings: stop, sound the horn, clear everybody from the area and lower the boat to the water or the ground by the shortest safe movement. Nobody approaches to adjust a sling.
  • Person trapped or struck: stop the hoist, do not move the load unless that is the only way to free the person and the supervisor directs it, call 999, give first aid under RA 56 First Aid and send a person to the yard entrance to meet the ambulance.
  • Person in the water at the dock: stop the hoist and any engine, throw the lifebuoy, keep the person in sight, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard, and recover the person by the ladder or from the dockside. Nobody enters the water.
  • Hoist failure with a boat suspended: isolate the hoist, widen the barriers, keep the area clear, support the boat from the ground only on the instruction of the supervisor, and call the maintenance contractor.
  • Boat taking water on launch: lift it again at once if the slings are still on; if they are not, move it to the dock and bring the slings back under it.
  • Hydraulic oil or fuel leak from the hoist: stop, contain the leak and follow the spill response in PRC 24, particularly at the dock where oil can reach the water.
  • After any of the above the hoist is not used again until the Silvers Yard Manager has released it, and the Risk and Compliance Manager is told the same day.

PPE requirements

Safety footwear with slip resistant soles, high visibility clothing and gloves for the whole hoist team. A safety helmet for the banksman, slingers and anyone setting supports. A lifejacket for everyone at the dock edge, on the runways or handling lines, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. Hearing protection where the noise assessment requires it. Waterproof and warm clothing suited to the weather, since cold hands and hoods that block hearing and sight both raise the risk at the dock.
