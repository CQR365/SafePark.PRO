RISK ASSESSMENT

Boats stored ashore and work on them

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionStoring boats ashore on cradles, props, shores and keel blocks, inspecting and adjusting those supports, moving a supported boat within the yard, and work on stored boats by yard personnel, by customers and by customers' own contractors, including access to decks by ladders and staging
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe hard standing, lay up areas, sheds and any other part of the Silvers Marine yard at Silverhills where a boat stands out of the water, from the moment the boat hoist or crane hands the boat over to its supports until the slings are back on for relaunch. The lift itself is assessed in Boat hoist: lifting, launching and moving boats. General work at height, ladders and towers are assessed in RA 32 Working at Height, RA 28 Ladders and RA 58 Scaffold Towers, and this assessment covers what is particular to a boat standing on supports.
Main equipment usedBoat cradles, adjustable props and shores, keel blocks, chocks, wedges, pads, prop chains and ties, boat trailers, ladders and ladder ties, staging, trestles and tower scaffolds, winter covers and frames, yard power supplies and leads. The types, ratings and numbers held are not known to the assessor and are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskYard personnel, boat owners and their families and guests, customers' own contractors, surveyors and riggers, delivery and transport drivers, other customers and members of the public walking through the yard, and children.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. No draft procedure for boat storage ashore has been seen. A hot work permit applies to welding, cutting and grinding on or beside a stored boat under RA 27 Hot Work. PRC 19 Induction applies to contractors.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Work at heightWork at Height Regulations 2005, regulation 6. Work is not to be carried out at height where it is reasonably practicable to do it safely otherwise; where it is, suitable and sufficient measures must be taken to prevent, so far as is reasonably practicable, any person falling a distance liable to cause personal injury, and then to minimise the distance and consequences of a fall. The deck of a boat ashore is a place of work at height.
Work equipmentProvision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998. HSE guidance states that work equipment must be suitable for its intended use, maintained in good repair and inspected where conditions cause deterioration. Cradles, props and staging provided by the yard are work equipment for the yard's own personnel.
Occupier, ScotlandOccupiers' Liability (Scotland) Act 1960, section 2. The occupier owes a person entering the premises such care as is reasonable in all the circumstances to see that the person does not suffer injury from dangers due to the state of the premises or to anything done or omitted on them. Section 2(3) leaves room for risks a person has willingly accepted, which is relevant to owners working on their own boats but does not remove the yard's duty for the supports and the site.
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Cited as the general framework for assessment, safe systems of work and the protection of people who are not employees. The text was not read for this assessment.
Fire, ScotlandFire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006. Cited as the regime for fire safety at the premises, which include boats stored close together. The text was not read for this assessment.
Standard for supporting boats ashoreTo confirm. No published HSE standard specific to the storage of boats ashore was found. The trade body code of practice is available to members only and was not read. Until a standard is confirmed, the manufacturer's rating and instructions for each cradle and prop are the technical basis.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Boats stored ashore with no system: no yard plan, no record of who set the supports or what they are rated for, no routine inspection and no rule on who may alter them

Potential consequence. A defect or an unauthorised change to a support goes unnoticed until a boat falls. Death, multiple casualties, loss of customers' boats and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. All persons in the yard

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. A yard plan shows where each boat stands, the spacing between boats, the hoist and vehicle routes and the fire access. A storage record is kept for each boat ashore showing the type of support used, who set it, the date and any limits such as mast up or mast down. Only authorised yard personnel set, adjust or remove a support, and this rule is written into the terms given to every customer and contractor. All supports are inspected by a nominated person at a set interval, and in addition after high wind, heavy rain, frost and thaw, any impact and any report of movement, and the inspection is recorded. A defect is acted on the same day: the area round the boat is closed until the support has been made good. Customers are asked to report any movement, noise or looseness at once, and the yard office records and passes on each report.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Draw the yard plan, start the storage record and the support inspection record, and nominate the inspectors in writing. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. A boat falling from its cradle or props because the supports are unsuitable for its weight and hull form, too few, wrongly placed, damaged, corroded or improvised

Potential consequence. Boat rolls or drops onto a person working beside or beneath it. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, owners, contractors, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The weight of the boat is carried on the keel, on blocks of sound hardwood or purpose made stands set level on prepared ground, and the cradle or props steady the boat and do not carry it, unless the cradle is designed to take the full weight. A cradle or set of props is chosen for the weight, length, beam and hull form of the boat and is used within the rating marked on it or stated by its maker. The number and position of props follow the maker's guidance and the hull structure, with pads bearing on bulkheads or other strong points and set square to the hull. Opposing props are chained or tied together so that they cannot spread, and adjustable props are pinned or locked. Fin keel, bilge keel, long keel, motor boat and multihull forms each have a set support arrangement in the yard's method, and a boat that does not fit one of them is assessed individually before the lift. Oil drums, pallets, loose timber offcuts, bricks and tyres are not used as supports. Every cradle and prop is identified, inspected before each use and withdrawn if bent, cracked, heavily corroded or with a seized or stripped thread.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Survey every cradle and prop in the yard, record its rating or mark it as unrated, withdraw what cannot be shown to be suitable, and write the yard's method for each hull form. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Ground beneath the supports that is soft, sloping, uneven, undermined by water, or changed by frost and thaw

Potential consequence. A block or prop sinks or slides, the boat leans and the remaining supports are overloaded. Boat falls. Death.

Persons at risk. All persons near the boat

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Boats are stored only on areas of the yard identified on the yard plan as suitable: firm, drained and close to level. Heavier boats are placed on the best ground. On anything other than concrete or sound hard standing, spreader plates or timber bearers of adequate size are placed under keel blocks and prop feet. Supports are not set over drain covers, service ducts, the edges of slabs or made up ground. Where a slope cannot be avoided the boat is set with its keel across the fall as little as possible, blocks are built level and chocked, and extra props are added on the low side. Surface water is kept from running under supports, and ground that has been washed out or rutted is made good before it is used again. The inspection after heavy rain, frost and thaw looks for sinking, leaning and gaps at pads.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Assess the lay up areas for bearing capacity, drainage and slope and mark the result on the yard plan. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. High wind acting on boats stored with masts up, on high topsides, flybridges, winter covers and shrink wrap, and one falling boat knocking over the next

Potential consequence. A boat blown off its supports and a row of boats brought down in turn. Multiple deaths among people working in the yard, and major loss.

Persons at risk. All persons in the yard, neighbours

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The yard decides, for each class of boat and each type of support, whether storage with the mast up is permitted, and the decision is recorded. Where it is permitted, the support is a cradle or prop set rated for it, extra props are fitted, sails, furled headsails, boom covers, sprayhoods and dodgers are removed, and halyards are frapped. Covers and frames are tied to the boat or to ground anchors and never to props or cradle legs, so that a flogging cover cannot pull a support out. Boats are spaced so that one boat falling would not strike the supports of the next, so far as the yard allows, and where that cannot be achieved the row is treated as one risk and given added bracing. Exposed positions are used for low windage boats. When a gale is forecast the nominated person inspects all supports beforehand, tightens what has worked loose, and the yard is closed to customers and to work on boats while the wind is above the limit set by the yard. Supports are inspected again before the yard reopens. Lightning and severe weather applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the yard's rules for mast up storage, the spacing between boats and the wind speed at which the yard closes to work on boats, and tell customers in writing. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. A customer or contractor moving, slackening or removing a prop or pad to paint or repair the hull beneath it, tying a cover or ladder to a prop, or adding improvised supports

Potential consequence. Boat left unsupported on one side. Boat falls onto the person or onto others later. Death.

Persons at risk. Owners, customers' contractors, yard personnel, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The rule that nobody but authorised yard personnel touches a support is given to every customer and contractor in writing before the boat comes ashore, is repeated on signs in the lay up areas and is a condition of working in the yard. The yard offers to move pads for antifouling and hull repair on request, and does so by fitting an additional prop next to the one to be moved before it is released, never by taking one away. Requests are logged so that every temporary change is put back and checked. Yard personnel walking the yard look for slackened props, missing chains, ropes tied to supports and wedges knocked out, and put them right at once. A customer or contractor found to have altered a support is stopped from working until the Silvers Yard Manager has spoken to them, and repeated breaches are dealt with under the berthing or storage terms.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Further action required. Write the rule into the storage terms and the contractor agreement, sign the lay up areas, and set up the log of pad move requests. The residual rating stays high because the control depends on the conduct of people the yard does not supervise, and it is to be reviewed once the terms and the yard patrols are in place. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Moving a boat that is already on supports: shifting a cradle with a boat in it, moving a boat on a yard trailer, or adjusting blocks to level or raise a boat

Potential consequence. Supports displaced under load. Boat topples during the move. Person crushed. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A boat on loose props is never dragged, pushed, jacked or towed. It is moved only by being lifted in the hoist or by crane, or on a cradle or trailer designed to be moved with a boat in it and within that design. Before any such move the boat is secured to the cradle or trailer, the route is checked for slope, surface and width, and the move is made at walking pace with a banksman and with everybody else clear. Raising, lowering or levelling a boat on its blocks is done with the hoist taking the weight, or with jacks and a method approved by the Silvers Yard Manager for that boat, one end at a time, with props followed up as the boat moves and nobody beneath the hull. Work on neighbouring boats stops while a boat is moved past them. After any move the supports are checked and the storage record is updated.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Identify which cradles and trailers at the yard are designed to be moved under load and mark the rest as static only. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Getting on and off a boat ashore by ladder: ladder unsecured, too short, on poor ground, resting on guardrails, or used while carrying tools and materials

Potential consequence. Fall from deck height onto hard standing, props or a cradle. Head and spinal injury. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, owners and their families, contractors, surveyors

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Where a boat will be worked on over a period, or by yard personnel as routine, access is by a stair tower or a platform with handrails in preference to a ladder. Where a ladder is used it is in good condition, long enough to extend well above the stepping off point, set on firm level ground at the correct angle, and tied to a strong point on the boat such as a cleat or stanchion base so that it cannot slip sideways or kick out. It is not tied to or rested on a prop. The stepping off point is at a gate or opening in the guardrails with something firm to hold. Tools and materials are passed up by a rope or a hoist line and not carried on the ladder. The yard's own ladders are identified and inspected under RA 28 Ladders. Customers are told in the yard rules that they are responsible for the condition of their own ladder, that it must be tied, and that a ladder left against a boat is taken down or locked when the boat is unattended so that children cannot climb it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Decide where stair towers or platforms are to be provided in place of ladders for yard personnel, and write the ladder rules for customers. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Working on deck, on coachroofs, over the side and on topsides of a boat ashore, with guardrails low, removed or incomplete, wet or frosty decks, and open hatches

Potential consequence. Fall from the deck to the ground, or through a hatch. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, owners, contractors, riggers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Work is planned so that as much as possible is done from the ground or from a platform beside the hull and not from the deck. Where yard personnel work on deck for more than a short task, or where guardrails are low or have been taken off, edge protection is provided by a staging or tower with handrails alongside, and where that cannot be done a work restraint lanyard is used on an anchor point that has been assessed as suitable. A boat's own guardrails are not relied on as edge protection. Hatches are closed or guarded when not in use. Decks are cleared of ice, frost and loose gear before work starts, and work on deck stops in high wind. Nobody works aloft on a mast of a boat that is ashore unless the Silvers Yard Manager has approved the method for that boat and its supports. Nobody works beneath a person working on deck. RA 32 Working at Height applies in full.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Assess the routine deck tasks done by yard personnel and decide for each the means of preventing a fall. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Staging, trestles and tower scaffolds put up around hulls on uneven ground, between props, and altered or borrowed by others

Potential consequence. Platform collapses or tips, or a person falls from it. Fracture, head injury. A tower striking a prop and disturbing the boat's support.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, owners, contractors

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Towers are erected, altered and taken down only by a person trained to do so, following the maker's instructions, and are inspected before use and after any alteration under RA 58 Scaffold Towers. Platforms are fully boarded with guardrails and toe boards where a person could fall a distance liable to cause injury. Feet are on firm ground with sole boards, and castors are locked. Staging and towers stand clear of props and cradle legs, are never tied to or braced against them, and are not moved with anyone on the platform. Trestles and planks are used only for low level work on a level base. The yard's own access equipment is marked, and customers and contractors do not use it unless it has been issued to them. A customer's or contractor's own staging is subject to the same rules and is checked by the yard before use.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. List the towers and staging the yard holds, confirm who is trained to erect them and set up the inspection record. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Customers and their own contractors working on boats ashore outside the yard's supervision: power tools, hot work, work at height, work alone and out of hours, and children on site

Potential consequence. Fall, fire, electric shock or injury to the customer or to others nearby, undiscovered for some time if the person is alone. Death.

Persons at risk. Owners, their families and guests, customers' contractors, neighbouring customers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Written yard rules are given to every customer with the storage agreement and set out what an owner may and may not do: no alteration of supports, ladders tied, no hot work or open flame without the yard's permit, no spraying without agreement, electrical leads and tools in good condition and protected by a residual current device, waste removed, children supervised at all times and kept off ladders and out of the working yard. Customers' contractors are approved before they start, provide evidence of insurance and competence, receive the site induction under PRC 19 and work to their own risk assessment, which the yard has seen. The yard sets the hours when work on boats is allowed, and asks customers who work alone to tell the office or sign in and out so that somebody knows they are on site. Yard personnel challenge unsafe work when they see it and have the authority to stop it. Sleeping aboard a boat ashore is permitted only where the yard has agreed it and the fire and access risks have been considered. Visitors, customers and the public on site more generally are assessed separately.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Further action required. Issue the customer yard rules and the contractor approval and induction arrangement, and decide the permitted hours and the lone working arrangement for customers. The residual rating stays high until these are in force and are seen to be followed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Fire starting in or beside a stored boat and spreading along a row: hot work, heaters and dehumidifiers left running, battery charging, gas, fuel in tanks, solvents, shrink wrap and covers

Potential consequence. Rapid fire spread between closely stored boats, gas cylinder and fuel tank involvement, people trapped on deck with the ladder as the only way down. Death and major loss.

Persons at risk. All persons in the yard, firefighters

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The yard plan keeps fire breaks between blocks of boats and a clear route for fire appliances. Hot work on or beside a boat is done only under a permit and RA 27 Hot Work, with combustible covers removed, a fire watch and extinguishers at hand. Shrink wrapping with a heat gun is treated as hot work. Heaters, dehumidifiers and chargers left running unattended are allowed only where the yard has agreed the equipment and the supply. Customers are asked to remove portable gas cylinders, petrol and outboard fuel tanks from boats laid up ashore, or to shut cylinders off at the valve, in line with Bulk LPG storage and use. Solvents and paints are kept to the day's need at the boat. Fire extinguishers and call points are provided through the lay up areas, and the areas are covered by the premises fire risk assessment. Fire and emergency evacuation applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that the premises fire risk assessment covers the lay up areas, the spacing of stored boats and customers' activities, and mark fire breaks and appliance access on the yard plan. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. A vehicle, trailer, lift truck or the boat hoist striking a prop, cradle, ladder or staging

Potential consequence. Support knocked out and the boat falls, or a person on a ladder or platform is thrown off. Death.

Persons at risk. Persons working on or near the boat, drivers and plant operators

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Routes for the hoist, lift trucks and vehicles are marked on the yard plan and kept clear of stored boats by a margin that allows for the swing of the hoist and its load. Customers' cars and vans are kept to parking areas and to set routes at walking pace, and are not driven between rows of boats except where the yard has allowed it for loading. Props and cradle legs that stand beside a route are made conspicuous and, where the exposure is high, protected by barriers or kerbs. Plant is operated only by authorised operators with a banksman where the view is restricted. Any contact with a support, however slight, is reported at once, the area is closed and the support is inspected before work resumes. Workplace transport and deliveries and Telehandler and lift truck operation apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Mark vehicle routes and parking on the ground and on the yard plan, and fit protection to supports that stand beside a route. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The support inspection records are reviewed each month by the Silvers Yard Manager, and after every gale the post storm inspection is confirmed as done before the yard reopens to customers.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager samples the storage records and inspection records each quarter and walks the lay up areas against the yard plan.
  • The log of pad move requests is checked weekly so that no temporary change to a support is left open.
  • Every report of a boat moving on its supports, a prop found slack or altered, contact by a vehicle, a fall from a ladder or deck and any fire is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.
  • Cradles, props, ladders and towers held by the yard are inspected and the result recorded at the start of each lay up season.
  • Breaches of the yard rules by customers and contractors are recorded and reviewed for patterns at the monthly management meeting.

Emergency arrangements

  • Boat moving or leaning on its supports: clear everybody away to a distance greater than the height of the boat and its mast, keep them away, tell the Silvers Yard Manager, and do not go beneath or beside the boat to add a prop. The boat is made safe with the hoist or a crane taking the weight.
  • Boat fallen with a person trapped: call 999 and ask for the Fire and Rescue Service and an ambulance, keep others clear of the boat and of the boats next to it, do not attempt to lift or move the boat unless the hoist team can do so safely under the direction of the lift supervisor, and give first aid only from a safe position.
  • Fall from a ladder, deck or platform: do not move the casualty unless there is a further danger, call 999, give first aid under RA 56 First Aid and send a person to the yard entrance to meet the ambulance.
  • Fire in or near a stored boat: raise the alarm, call the Fire and Rescue Service, evacuate the lay up area including people aboard other boats, tell the fire crew about gas cylinders and fuel, and do not attempt to fight a fire beyond the incipient stage.
  • Gale warning: carry out the pre storm inspection, close the yard to work on boats at the set wind limit and inspect again before reopening.
  • After any of the above the area is kept closed until the Silvers Yard Manager has released it, and the Risk and Compliance Manager is told the same day.

PPE requirements

Safety footwear and high visibility clothing for yard personnel throughout the lay up areas. A safety helmet when setting or adjusting supports, when working beside a hull where others are working above, and whenever a boat is being moved. Gloves for handling props, chains and timber. A work restraint harness and lanyard only where the assessment of the deck task calls for it and a suitable anchor point has been identified. Customers and contractors are told in the yard rules what the yard expects them to wear, and protective equipment for coatings work is set out in Antifoul, coatings and hull washing.
