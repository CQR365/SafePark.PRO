Risk assessments / Diving emergency and recompression

RISK ASSESSMENT

Diving emergency and recompression

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionResponding to a diving emergency: recovering an injured or unconscious diver from the water, deploying the standby diver, giving first aid and oxygen, recognising and acting on suspected decompression illness, moving the casualty to a compression chamber or operating a chamber on site, calling in the emergency services, and looking after the team afterwards
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Diving Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeEvery GSS diving project, from a vessel, a quay, a pontoon, a slipway or the shore, inshore and offshore, including remote lochs and coast where road access and telephone coverage are poor, and the route from each site to the compression chamber named in the diving project plan. The planning and conduct of the dive is assessed in RA 41 Dive Operations, the general emergency arrangements in RA 30 Emergency Preparedness and general first aid in RA 56 First Aid. This assessment covers what those do not: the diver casualty, decompression illness, transfer to a chamber and a chamber run on site.
Main equipment usedDiver recovery equipment (ladder, stage, davit, winch or other lifting aid, recovery strop or harness), the standby diver's equipment, diver and surface communications, oxygen administration equipment and cylinders, first aid kit, automated external defibrillator where carried, stretcher, thermal protection, VHF radio and telephones, and any compression chamber with its gas supplies, built in breathing system, communications and fire fighting provision. What GSS actually holds is listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskThe diver in the water, the standby diver sent to assist, the Diving Supervisor, tenders and others recovering and treating the casualty, any chamber operator and attendant, vessel crew, and emergency service personnel attending.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. The diving project plan for each project carries the contingency procedures, the chamber arrangements and the emergency contacts.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Procedures for serious and imminent danger, and contact with the emergency services, are part of the Company's general duties and extend to the self employed divers working on its projects.
DivingDiving at Work Regulations 1997, regulation 6. The diving contractor must ensure that the project is planned, managed and conducted so as to protect those taking part, that there are enough competent people to carry out the project and to deal with any reasonably foreseeable emergency, and that suitable plant is available for the emergency procedures. A diving project plan must be prepared before the project starts.
Approved code, emergenciesHSE approved code L104 (second edition, 2014). As read: the diving project plan must provide contingency procedures for any foreseeable emergency, including retrieving injured or unconscious divers from the water (paragraph 38); the case of a team member who is injured or ill where no doctor is at the site should be considered (paragraph 63); a standby diver should be in immediate readiness and remain on the surface unless needed (paragraphs 83 and 84); at least two people in the team should be qualified in first aid (paragraph 93); oxygen should be immediately available at every location (paragraph 120).
Approved code, recompressionL104 paragraphs 114 to 116, as read. The diving contractor is responsible for ensuring that a diver can be recompressed in an emergency. The code sets minimum standards by depth and planned decompression: in some cases the nearest suitable operational two person, two compartment chamber must be identified and must under no circumstances be more than 6 hours travelling distance from the dive site, and in others such a chamber must be provided for immediate use at the site. Where the chamber is not on site the plan should demonstrate that a diver can be transported and recompressed, and reliance on the emergency services should be kept under review, for example as the weather changes.
First aidHSE guidance, First aid requirements for diving projects. Commercial divers obtain separate first aid and oxygen administration qualifications in training, valid for three years. For inland and inshore work at least two of the team should be qualified in first aid. For offshore work under L103 the guidance states that all members of the dive team should hold a current first aid qualification and that at least one member other than the supervisor should be a diver medic, a qualification renewed every two years. The Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981 were not read for this draft.
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply to the vessel and her crew, enforced by the MCA. The Master remains in command of the vessel in an emergency and the Diving Supervisor in control of the diving operation; how the two work together is set down before the dive.
ReportingThe Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 are understood to require certain diving incidents and cases of decompression illness to be reported. They were not read for this draft, so what is reportable, and by whom where the diver is self employed, is to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. A diving emergency at a site where the plan has not been made specific: no named chamber, no checked route, no agreed way to raise help, and a team that has not practised

Potential consequence. Minutes lost while the team works out what to do. A recoverable casualty dies or is left with permanent injury. Further casualties among those improvising a rescue.

Persons at risk. Divers, the dive team, vessel crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. No diving starts without a diving project plan that holds the contingency procedures for this site: loss of gas, loss of communications, a trapped or fouled diver, an injured or unconscious diver, suspected decompression illness, and the loss of the vessel's position or power. The plan names the compression chamber to be used, its telephone number, the means and time of travel to it from this site, the source of diving medical advice, the nearest hospital, the location reference to give to the emergency services and the point where an ambulance or helicopter can reach the team. The Diving Supervisor briefs the whole team, including the Master and crew, on these before the first dive and confirms that each person knows their task in an emergency. Emergency contact numbers are displayed at dive control. Recovery of an unresponsive diver is practised by the team at this site, or on this vessel, before work at depth begins, and the drill is recorded. The Supervisor checks at the start of each day that the chamber is operational and that the weather and tide still allow the planned evacuation, and does not dive if they do not.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write a standard emergency section for the diving project plan with the site specific entries listed above, and set how often the recovery drill is run and recorded. Owner: Diving Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Recovering an injured or unconscious diver from the water to the deck, quay or shore: weight of the diver and equipment, freeboard, tidal range at a quay wall, vessel movement, propellers and thrusters

Potential consequence. Delay in starting resuscitation. The casualty dropped, struck against the hull or wall, or drowned at the surface. Back and crush injuries to those lifting.

Persons at risk. The diver, standby diver, tenders, crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The means of recovering a diver who cannot help is decided for each site before the dive and is rigged and tested, not left in a locker. Where the lift from the water is more than can be managed by hand, a mechanical aid is provided: a stage, a davit and winch, a crane rated and examined for lifting persons, or a recovery strop to a lifting point. The diver wears a harness with a lifting point by which the diver can be raised without the equipment being removed in the water. The recovery point is chosen clear of propellers, thrusters, intakes and the gap between a vessel and a wall, and machinery that could harm a diver at the surface is stopped and isolated on the Supervisor's word. At a tidal quay the plan states how the casualty is landed at low water. The umbilical or lifeline is used to bring the diver to the recovery point and is tended throughout. Enough people are on site to carry out the recovery while the Supervisor keeps control and one person gives first aid, which the team size in the plan must allow for. Where the lift uses the vessel's crane or winch, RA 21 Crane Operations On Board and RA 13 Winch Operations apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what diver recovery equipment each vessel and each shore set up has, whether any crane or winch used is certified for lifting persons, and whether the team can lift an unresponsive diver at the lowest state of tide. Owner: Diving Manager with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Standby diver sent to an emergency: entering the hazard that has disabled the first diver, entanglement of two umbilicals, hurried dressing and checks, and no cover for the standby diver

Potential consequence. A second casualty. Standby diver trapped, out of gas or injured. Both divers lost.

Persons at risk. Standby diver, the diver

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The standby diver is dressed and checked before the working diver leaves the surface, with the same type of equipment where surface supply is used, an umbilical or lifeline long enough to reach the diver's furthest point, and a tender of their own, as L104 advises. The standby diver's gas, communications and reserve are function tested with the working diver's. The standby diver stays on the surface, in immediate readiness and not given other work that would delay entry. Before sending the standby diver the Diving Supervisor establishes, so far as can be done in seconds, what has happened, and does not commit the standby diver into a hazard that cannot be controlled, such as a differential pressure, a collapsed structure or a load still moving, until it has been made safe. The standby diver follows the working diver's umbilical, stays in voice contact and reports continuously. The supervisor keeps the surface team in their places and brings in the Master and crew for the recovery. The time the standby diver spends at depth is recorded, since the standby diver may also need decompression or observation afterwards.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm in the diving procedures the readiness standard for the standby diver and that team size on every project allows a tender for the standby diver where surface supply is used. Owner: Diving Manager with each Diving Supervisor. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Casualty care at the surface: too few people trained and in date to give first aid, resuscitation delayed by removing equipment, no defibrillator, the first aider also needed for another task, and hypothermia

Potential consequence. Cardiac arrest, drowning or a serious injury not treated effectively in the first minutes. Death.

Persons at risk. The casualty, the dive team

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The diving project plan names the people on the team who hold a current first aid qualification and a current oxygen administration qualification. For inshore work under L104 at least two of the team are qualified in first aid. For offshore work under L103 every member of the team holds a current first aid qualification and at least one member other than the Supervisor is a diver medic, as the HSE guidance read for this draft states. The Supervisor arranges duties so that a first aider is free to treat the casualty without leaving the standby diver or the panel uncovered. The team is practised in getting a casualty out of a helmet or mask and harness quickly. A first aid kit suited to diving, a means of giving rescue breaths, a stretcher or other means of moving the casualty and thermal protection are at dive control. An automated external defibrillator is carried where the assessment for the site calls for one, kept dry and used only once the casualty is on a dry surface. The casualty is kept lying flat, warm and under observation, and is not left alone. RA 56 First Aid applies to the general provision.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Compile the first aid, oxygen administration and diver medic qualifications and expiry dates of every diver and supervisor engaged, including the self employed, and decide whether a defibrillator is carried on every diving project. Owner: Diving Manager with the Crewing Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Emergency oxygen: not at the site, not enough to last the journey to the chamber, equipment that does not deliver a high concentration, or oxygen given near an ignition source or in an unventilated cabin

Potential consequence. The main first aid treatment for a diving casualty runs out or is ineffective, and the outcome of decompression illness or near drowning is worse. Fire in the cabin or vehicle.

Persons at risk. The casualty, first aiders, crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Oxygen for first aid is at dive control on every project, as L104 advises, checked for contents before the first dive each day and separate from any gas used for the work. The quantity is calculated in the diving project plan to treat one casualty at a high flow for the whole of the planned travel time to the chamber, with a margin for delay, and a second casualty where two divers could be affected. The equipment delivers as near to 100 per cent oxygen as can be achieved, by a demand valve or a tight fitting mask with a reservoir, and those who will use it have trained with that equipment. Oxygen is started at once for any diver with symptoms after a dive and is continued without breaks until a diving doctor or the receiving chamber says otherwise. Smoking and naked flame are prohibited near oxygen in use, the cabin, vehicle or boat is ventilated, and oil and grease are kept from the equipment. The cylinder is secured in the vehicle or boat during transfer. The handling of oxygen cylinders is covered in the proposed assessment Diving plant, compressors and breathing air.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what oxygen administration equipment and how many cylinders are held, and calculate the quantity needed for the longest transfer GSS plans for. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Decompression illness not recognised or not reported: symptoms that appear after the diver has left site, are put down to tiredness or strain, or are hidden so as not to lose work, and a diver who drives, flies or travels over high ground after diving

Potential consequence. Treatment delayed by hours or days. Permanent neurological injury, loss of the diver's career, death in severe cases.

Persons at risk. Divers, standby divers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Every diver is briefed that any unusual symptom after a dive is to be reported to the Diving Supervisor at once, and that reporting carries no penalty and no loss of standing, which matters most where the diver is self employed and paid by the day. After surfacing the diver stays at the site, under observation and within reach of the oxygen, for a period set in the diving project plan, and the Supervisor asks the diver directly how they feel before the diver is released. On any report the Supervisor stops diving, lays the casualty flat, starts oxygen, records the dive profile and the symptoms with times, and telephones the diving doctor or the chamber named in the plan for advice without waiting to see whether the symptoms settle. A neurological check is carried out by a team member trained to do it, and repeated, so that the receiving doctor has a baseline. The diver is given a card with the dive details and the emergency number on leaving site, is told not to fly or cross high ground for the period the plan sets, and is not left to travel or spend the night alone after a dive that carried a significant decompression obligation or any incident. A treated diver returns to diving only when a doctor competent in diving medicine has cleared them.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the source of diving medical advice GSS uses and its telephone number, issue the diver's card, and set the observation period and the rules on flying and travel in the diving procedures. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Transfer of a casualty to a chamber that is not on site: travel time beyond what the plan assumed, a chamber that is closed or occupied, single track roads, ferries, weather that grounds a helicopter, altitude on the route, and a casualty who deteriorates on the way

Potential consequence. Recompression starts too late to prevent permanent injury or death. A road traffic collision during an urgent drive.

Persons at risk. The casualty, escorts and drivers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. For each site the diving project plan identifies the chamber against the minimum standard in L104 for the depth and decompression planned, and where the code calls for a chamber for immediate use at the site, one is provided on site and this hazard does not arise. Where the code allows a chamber off site, it is a suitable, operational two person, two compartment chamber, and the travelling time from the dive site, by the means actually available on the day, is worked out and written in the plan. It is never more than the 6 hours the code sets as the outer limit, and the Diving Manager sets a shorter Company limit wherever the dive profile, the remoteness of the site or the difficulty of the route gives reason to. The chamber is telephoned before the project to confirm that it will accept a commercial diving casualty, and on each diving day to confirm that it is operational. The plan gives a second chamber in case the first is unavailable. The route avoids high ground where a lower route exists, and the plan states how the casualty gets from the vessel or shore to the road, who drives, who escorts and what vehicle is kept at the site. Where the plan relies on a lifeboat, an ambulance or a helicopter, it also states what happens if that service cannot come, and the dive does not go ahead in weather that would prevent the evacuation. The casualty travels flat, on oxygen, with an escort who can give first aid and who carries the dive record.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Further action required. This hazard stays HIGH until the chamber, the second chamber, the travel time and the means of evacuation have been verified for each area in which GSS dives. Compile that list, test the travel times, and set the Company limit on travelling time. Owner: Diving Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Operating a compression chamber on site: an operator who is not trained or in practice, loss of gas supply or control of pressure, a door or medical lock opened under pressure, too few people to run the chamber and care for the occupant, and the build up of carbon dioxide

Potential consequence. Barotrauma, uncontrolled decompression or loss of pressure during treatment, injury from a lock or door released under pressure. Death of the casualty or the attendant.

Persons at risk. The casualty, the attendant inside, the chamber operator

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Where a chamber is provided on site it is a two person, two compartment chamber suitable for the purpose, as L104 advises, in date for examination under its written scheme, with its certificates on site. It is set up, connected and function tested before the first dive, including the main and reserve gas supplies, the built in breathing system, the communications, the lighting, the analysers and the locks, and the test is recorded. Gas for a full therapeutic treatment, with a reserve, is calculated in the diving project plan and held at the chamber. The chamber is operated only by a person trained and assessed as competent to operate it, named in the plan, who is on site whenever diving is in progress and has no other duty during a treatment. An attendant trained in first aid goes in with the casualty, and the attendant's own exposure is recorded and managed. The team is large enough to run the chamber, tend the occupant and keep control of the site at the same time. Treatment is carried out to the procedures in the plan and on the advice of the diving doctor, who is contacted at the start. Interlocks and the operating procedure prevent a door or medical lock being opened under pressure. Oxygen and carbon dioxide levels in the chamber are monitored and the chamber is flushed to keep them within the limits set.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish whether GSS owns or hires a compression chamber, who is trained and current to operate it, and which projects in the last year needed one on site. No existing draft assessment covers chamber operation, so the operating procedure must be written or adopted before the next project that needs one. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Fire in a compression chamber: oxygen enrichment of the chamber atmosphere from the breathing masks, with clothing, bedding, electrical equipment or a prohibited item as fuel and ignition

Potential consequence. A fire that spreads in seconds in a sealed space the occupants cannot leave. Death of the occupants.

Persons at risk. The casualty and the attendant in the chamber

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Oxygen is delivered to the occupants only through the built in breathing system, with exhaled gas led outside the chamber where the system allows, and masks are worn properly and closed off when not in use. The oxygen level in the chamber is monitored continuously and the chamber is ventilated to keep it below the limit set in the operating procedure. Nothing goes into the chamber that could ignite or burn readily: no lighters, matches, smoking materials, vapes, telephones, batteries, hand warmers, oils, greases, alcohol based products, aerosols or unapproved electrical items. The operator checks the occupants and their pockets before pressurising and checks everything passed through the medical lock. Bedding and clothing inside are of low flammability and are kept to what is needed, and wet or oily diving dress is removed first. Electrical equipment in the chamber is of a type made for it. The chamber is kept clean and free of oil and dust. A means of fighting a fire inside the chamber, suitable for use under pressure, is fitted or carried and the attendant knows how to use it, and a fire extinguisher is at the control position outside. The chamber is sited away from fuel, hot work and other fire risks on the deck or quay, and oxygen supply lines can be shut off at the control position.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm what fire fighting provision any chamber GSS uses has inside and outside, and write the prohibited items check into the chamber operating procedure. Owner: Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Communications failing when help is needed: no mobile signal or VHF coverage at the site, the emergency services unable to find or reach it, confusion over who calls whom, and loss of voice contact with the diver during the emergency

Potential consequence. Help arrives late or goes to the wrong place. Diving medical advice not obtained. The Supervisor loses control of the response.

Persons at risk. The casualty, the dive team

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Before the project the Diving Supervisor proves, at the site, the means by which help will be called: a telephone that connects from dive control, VHF contact with the Coastguard, and a satellite telephone or other backup where neither is reliable. The diving project plan states who makes each call, in what order, and what is said: for an emergency afloat or on the coast the Coastguard is called on VHF channel 16 or by 999, and ashore inland the ambulance service by 999, and the caller states that the casualty is a diver and that recompression may be needed. The location is given in a form the services can use, with the grid reference, the access point and any gate code written at dive control, and a person is sent to meet the ambulance. The Master handles the vessel's communications on the Supervisor's request. Voice contact with the diver is hard wired where practicable, with line signals as the fallback, and a loss of communications with the diver is itself treated as a reason to end the dive. The Diving Manager and the Designated Person Ashore are told as soon as the casualty is in care, and one of them takes over contact with the client, the family and the authorities so that the Supervisor is not drawn away.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the call out sequence and the script for the first call in the diving procedures, and decide what backup communications are carried to sites without telephone coverage. Owner: Diving Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Moving the casualty between the vessel, the shore and a vehicle or helicopter: transfer over water, steps and ladders at a quay, a small boat in a seaway, and manual handling of a stretcher

Potential consequence. The casualty or a carrier falls, or falls into the water. Further injury to the casualty, back injury to the carriers, delay.

Persons at risk. The casualty, the dive team, crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The landing point for a casualty is chosen in the diving project plan: where possible the vessel goes alongside a berth with level or ramped access that an ambulance can reach, in preference to a transfer by small boat or up a vertical ladder. The Master decides how and where the vessel lands the casualty. A stretcher that can be lifted and secured, and the means to lift it where a quay ladder is the only access, are carried. Enough people carry the stretcher, with one person giving the commands, and those at the edge wear lifejackets. Transfer between vessels follows RA 7 Personnel Transfer, and landing at a pier follows RA 1 Boarding at Pier and RA 59 Access and Egress. Where a helicopter is sent, the team follows the instructions of the Coastguard and the aircraft crew, clears and secures loose gear on deck and does not touch the winch wire until it has earthed.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that each vessel used for diving carries a stretcher suitable for lifting, and identify the casualty landing points for the areas in which GSS regularly dives. Owner: Operations Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. The effect on the team after a serious incident or a death: acute stress, team members carrying on diving or driving home while shaken, blame, later psychological injury, and evidence lost before the investigation

Potential consequence. A second incident caused by distraction or fatigue. Lasting psychological harm. Experienced divers leaving the work. An investigation that cannot establish what happened.

Persons at risk. The dive team, vessel crew, shore staff who take the calls

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Diving stops after any serious incident and does not restart until the Diving Manager has authorised it, having considered the state of the team as well as the cause. Nobody who was closely involved dives again that day, and the Supervisor is relieved as soon as another competent supervisor can take over. The team is brought together the same day by the Diving Manager or another senior person, told what is known, and asked what each of them needs. No one is questioned in a way that assigns blame at that stage. Travel home is arranged for anyone not fit to drive. Each person, employed or self employed, is contacted again in the following days and weeks, and is told how to reach confidential support and their own doctor. The diver's equipment, the gas supply, the dive record and any recordings are secured untouched for the investigation, and reporting to the enforcing authority is handled by the Risk and Compliance Manager. The proposed assessment Attending an incident scene and leading the response covers those who attend from the office.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what confidential support GSS can offer after an incident and whether it extends to self employed divers and skippers. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The Diving Manager checks each diving project plan before issue for a named chamber, a second chamber, a stated travel time, a source of medical advice and a tested means of calling help.
  • The Diving Supervisor records the recovery drill, the daily chamber check and the daily oxygen check in the dive record, and the records are sampled when returned.
  • First aid, oxygen administration and diver medic qualifications are tracked for every diver and supervisor, and nobody is counted towards the first aid cover on a project with an expired qualification.
  • Where a chamber is used on site, its examination certificates, function test and operator's competence record are checked by the Diving Manager before mobilisation.
  • Every diving emergency, every use of emergency oxygen, every report of symptoms after a dive and every deployment of the standby diver is reported under PRC 01, investigated and reviewed against this assessment.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager observes at least one emergency drill on a diving project each year and reports the findings to the Managing Director.

Emergency arrangements

  • Diver unresponsive or in difficulty: the Diving Supervisor takes control, deploys the standby diver when it is safe to do so, has the diver brought to the recovery point and lifted clear, and has machinery that could harm a diver at the surface stopped.
  • At the surface: open the airway, start resuscitation if the diver is not breathing normally, give oxygen at the highest concentration available, keep the casualty flat and warm, and do not leave them.
  • Call for help at once and in parallel: the Coastguard on VHF channel 16 or by 999 when afloat or on the coast, the ambulance service by 999 ashore. Say that the casualty is a diver and that recompression may be needed, and give the location as written at dive control.
  • Suspected decompression illness, however mild: stop diving, oxygen without breaks, telephone the diving doctor or chamber named in the diving project plan, record the profile and symptoms with times, and transfer as advised. Do not put the diver back in the water to treat symptoms.
  • Chamber on site: start treatment only under the procedure in the plan and with medical advice, with a trained operator at the controls and an attendant inside. Fire in the chamber: shut off oxygen to the chamber, fight the fire with the means fitted, and bring the occupants out as fast as the procedure allows.
  • After the casualty is in care: secure the equipment and records untouched, tell the Diving Manager, the Designated Person Ashore and the Risk and Compliance Manager, and stand the team down.

Related documents

  • The diving project plan for each project, with its contingency procedures. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up and PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness. The draft procedure that governs diving emergencies and chamber operation is to be confirmed.
  • RA 41 Dive Operations (GSS-DIV-RA-01), RA 30 Emergency Preparedness (GSS-HSE-RA-12), RA 56 First Aid (GSS-HSE-RA-13), RA 7 Personnel Transfer (GSS-HSE-RA-24), RA 21 Crane Operations On Board (GSS-MAR-RA-04), RA 13 Winch Operations (GSS-HSE-RA-34), RA 1 Boarding at Pier (GSS-HSE-RA-05), RA 59 Access and Egress (GSS-HSE-RA-02), RA 17 Bottled Gasses (GSS-HSE-RA-06), Working in Cold Conditions (GSS-MAR-RA-01). The proposed new assessments Diving plant, compressors and breathing air and Attending an incident scene and leading the response also bear on this work.
  • Diving operation records, emergency drill records, daily chamber and oxygen checks, first aid and oxygen administration certificates, chamber examination certificates and operator competence records, the diver's card, incident reports and investigation records.

PPE requirements

The standby diver wears full diving dress and equipment, checked and ready, throughout the dive. Those recovering a diver at the water's edge or over the side wear lifejackets, gloves and safety footwear, and a harness and line where they must lean out. First aiders use disposable gloves and a face shield or mask with a one way valve for rescue breaths. Thermal protection is held for the casualty. No personal protective equipment is worn inside a compression chamber other than what the operating procedure allows, and clothing worn inside is of low flammability.
