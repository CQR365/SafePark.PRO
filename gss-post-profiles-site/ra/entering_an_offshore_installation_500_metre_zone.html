Risk assessments / Entering an offshore installation 500 metre zone

RISK ASSESSMENT

Entering an offshore installation 500 metre zone

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionTaking a Company workboat into, working inside and leaving the 500 metre safety zone around an offshore oil and gas installation, and the equivalent approach to an offshore wind farm structure. It covers knowing that the zone is there, permission and communications with the installation, checks before entry, the approach, holding position, weather and sea state limits, watchkeeping and fatigue, loss of propulsion or position, and work alongside such as the transfer of people and the handling of cargo.
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Operations Manager and Designated Person Ashore
Location and scopeAny safety zone around a fixed or floating oil and gas installation, a drilling unit, or a subsea installation with a designated zone, in waters around the United Kingdom, and any safety zone or close approach at an offshore wind turbine, substation or other renewable energy structure, including during its construction. General navigation and watchkeeping are covered in RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations; this assessment covers the added risk of working close to a structure offshore. Work under a foreign jurisdiction needs its own check of the local rules.
Main equipment usedTo be confirmed for each vessel: main propulsion and steering, including any second engine, thruster or other redundancy; position keeping arrangements; radar, electronic charts, satellite positioning and the automatic identification system; fixed and hand held radios; fendering; deck crane or winch where fitted; the client's or the installation's data card, checklist and field charts.
Persons at riskThe Master and watchkeepers, deck crew, the Vessel Engineer, self employed skippers and crew, passengers, technicians, divers and client personnel being carried or transferred, the people on the installation, the crews of other vessels in the zone, and divers or remotely operated vehicles working from the installation or another vessel.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. The installation's or the field operator's own marine procedures, data card and entry checklist apply inside the zone and are obtained before the job. Where the vessel's checklist and the installation's differ, the difference is settled before entry.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Offshore safety zonesPetroleum Act 1987, sections 21 to 24. A safety zone exists automatically around an oil and gas installation that is stationed, being assembled or being dismantled, and extends 500 metres from any part of it; the Secretary of State may also establish a zone by order, which is how subsea installations are covered. Under section 23 a vessel must not enter or remain in a zone except as regulations provide or with consent, and where it does the owner and the Master each commit an offence, with a defence only where the zone was not known, and would not on reasonable enquiry have become known, to the Master.
Offshore safety zones, permitted entryOffshore Installations (Safety Zones) Regulations 1987, regulation 2. A vessel may enter or remain in a zone, among other cases, to provide services for an installation in the zone or to transport persons or goods to or from it, for work on a submarine cable or pipeline in the zone, to save or attempt to save life or property, owing to stress of weather, or when in distress. The legal permission does not replace the installation's own permission, which industry practice requires before every entry.
Regulator guidanceHSE leaflet INDG189, Safety zones around oil and gas installations in waters around the UK. It confirms the 500 metre zone, the offence under the 1987 Act, that some subsea installations have zones set by statutory instrument, and that a listening watch is kept on VHF channel 16 near installations. The leaflet dates from 2011 and its statement of penalties is to be confirmed as current.
Offshore renewablesEnergy Act 2004, section 95, and MGN 372 Amendment 1 (M+F) Safety of Navigation: Guidance to Mariners Operating in the Vicinity of UK Offshore Renewable Energy Installations. Safety zones at renewable installations are not automatic: they are declared by notice, by the Scottish Ministers for installations in Scottish waters. MGN 372 says that zones of up to 500 metres may be set during construction, major maintenance and decommissioning, that zones in the operating phase are decided case by case, and that they are published by notices to mariners.
Marine, general dutiesMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997, enforced by the MCA: the general duty in regulation 5, risk assessment in regulation 7 and coordination with other employers in regulation 13, as made. The Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers, 2026 edition, has chapter 31 Ships serving offshore oil and gas installations and chapter 32 Ships serving offshore renewables installations; neither was read and both are to be confirmed.
Industry guidanceMarine Safety Forum Safety Alert 16-02 and IMCA Safety Flash 06/15 were read and are used for the approach, checklist and communication controls below. The Guidelines for Offshore Marine Operations and the Step Change in Safety guidance on marine operations in the 500 metre zone are the recognised industry standards for this work but could not be read; their requirements, and whether a client makes them a contract condition, are to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Entering or remaining in a safety zone without permission, or without knowing that the zone is there, including a subsea zone or a zone around a unit that has recently arrived

Potential consequence. The vessel is close to a structure, to other vessels, to divers or to subsea equipment with nobody expecting it. Collision or snagging. Prosecution of the owner and the Master.

Persons at risk. All on board, people on the installation, divers and other vessels in the zone

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The passage plan for every offshore job marks each installation, each charted safety zone and each renewable energy site on or near the route, taken from up to date charts and from current notices to mariners and navigation warnings, since drilling units move and new zones are declared. Zones the vessel has no business in are given a wide berth and are set as guard zones or marked areas on the electronic chart where the equipment allows. For a zone the vessel is going to work in, the Master holds the client's instruction, the installation's data card and the field's marine procedures before sailing. No entry is made until the installation has given permission by radio for that entry, at that time, for that purpose; a standing arrangement or a permission given earlier in the day is not relied on. If the vessel is forced into a zone by weather, distress or to save life, the Master tells the installation and the Coastguard at once. Any entry made in error is reported to the installation, to the Designated Person Ashore and under PRC 01.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how notices to mariners and chart corrections reach each vessel, and add safety zones and renewable energy sites to the passage planning check. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Checks before entry not done, done too far away or too long before the entry, or done against a checklist that conflicts with the installation's

Potential consequence. A fault in propulsion, steering, controls or communications is found only when the vessel is close to the structure. Confusion on the bridge during the approach. Collision.

Persons at risk. All on board, people on the installation

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The vessel stops outside the zone, in a position where it would drift clear, and completes its entry checklist there, immediately before asking for permission to enter. The checklist is not completed at a distance or on passage. It covers, as a minimum: propulsion, steering and each control station tested in the mode to be used inside the zone; any second engine, thruster or other redundancy running and proved; machinery spaces checked and the Vessel Engineer standing by; communications with the installation proved on the working channel with a second means agreed; the weather, sea, tide and visibility now and forecast against the limits; the bridge manned as this assessment requires; the deck secure and the crew briefed on the job; and the escape heading agreed. The entry checklist of the installation or the field operator is obtained in advance and compared with the vessel's, and any conflict, for example on the use of radar or of automatic steering inside the zone, is settled with the installation before entry and not during the approach. A check that fails means no entry. The completed checklist is kept.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write a Company entry checklist for the fleet, suited to vessels of this size, and check it against the Guidelines for Offshore Marine Operations once those have been read. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Collision with the installation during the approach: too much speed, a heading aimed straight at the structure, the approach made from the side from which wind and tide set the vessel on, or attention fixed on one instrument

Potential consequence. Heavy contact with a leg, riser, conductor or boat landing. Damage to the structure with a risk of a release of oil or gas, fire and explosion. Hull damage, flooding and loss of the vessel. Multiple deaths.

Persons at risk. All on board, people on the installation

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The approach is planned before entry and briefed to everyone on the bridge. The vessel never steers directly at the installation: it approaches on a course that passes clear to one side, so that a failure of engines or steering takes it past and not into the structure. Wherever the job allows, the approach and the working position are on the side from which wind, sea and tide carry the vessel away. Speed is reduced before the zone boundary and kept low enough inside it to stop the vessel well short, in line with the safe speed rule of the collision regulations and with any speed limit the field sets. Control is changed from passage mode to manoeuvring mode outside the zone, and automatic steering is not used on the final approach. The vessel is brought to a stop at a set up position clear of the structure, and the Master watches how it lies to wind and tide for long enough to be sure before closing further. One person has the controls and a second watches distance, speed and the instruments and calls them out. RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations and RA 3 Coming Alongside apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Brief every Master and watchkeeper who may work in a field on the approach rules in this assessment, using Marine Safety Forum Safety Alert 16-02 as the case study, and record the briefing. Owner: Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Loss of propulsion, steering, power or position keeping while inside the zone, including a fouled propeller, fuel contamination or a control failure

Potential consequence. The vessel drifts onto the structure, onto another vessel or over subsea equipment. Collision, damage to risers or cables, flooding. Multiple deaths.

Persons at risk. All on board, people on the installation, other vessels

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Vessels are selected for work inside a zone on their ability to keep clear after a single failure: the Operations Manager and the Designated Person Ashore confirm, vessel by vessel, what redundancy in propulsion, steering and power each has, and a vessel with none works inside a zone only in a position from which it would drift clear and only where the client and the installation accept that in writing. Machinery is in its most reliable state before entry: fuel tanks and filters checked, no maintenance or changeover of systems carried out inside the zone, and the Vessel Engineer in or beside the machinery space. The working position is chosen so that wind and tide set the vessel away, and the time spent on the side from which they set it on is refused or kept to the least the job needs with the installation's express agreement. Lines, hoses and loose gear are kept out of the water around the propellers. On any failure or alarm the Master moves clear at once if the vessel can, tells the installation without delay, and does not wait to diagnose the fault inside the zone. Anchoring is not relied on inside a field because of pipelines and cables.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Record for each vessel its propulsion, steering and power redundancy and its position keeping ability, and decide which vessels may work inside a safety zone and under what limits. Owner: Operations Manager with the Designated Person Ashore, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Poor or failed communications with the installation, and work going on at the same time that the vessel has not been told about: crane lifts, overboard discharges, flaring or venting, helicopter movements, other vessels, and divers or remotely operated vehicles in the water

Potential consequence. The vessel moves into a lift, a discharge, a diver's working area or another vessel's path. A diver is struck or an umbilical is cut by the propeller. Collision. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board, divers, people on the installation, other vessels

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Before entry the Master agrees with the installation by radio the purpose of the visit, the side and position to be worked, the expected duration, and what else is happening in the zone. The Master asks directly whether divers or remotely operated vehicles are in the water, whether any other vessel is in or due in the zone, and whether crane work, discharges, venting or helicopter operations are planned, and does not close until the answers are clear. A working channel is agreed and kept, with a second means of contact, and a continuous listening watch is kept on it and on VHF channel 16. Messages are short, in plain standard phrases, and are read back. Where the people on the bridge and on the installation do not understand each other, the vessel stops or stays outside. Any change of plan, by either side, is treated as a new entry and agreed again. The installation is told when the vessel is leaving and when it is clear of the zone. A vessel of this fleet supporting Company divers inside a zone works under the diving project plan and RA 41 Dive Operations as well.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain the field operator's marine procedures and data card for each installation the fleet is contracted to visit, and hold them on the vessel and ashore. Owner: Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Weather, sea state, tide and visibility at or beyond the limits for the vessel and the job, or worsening while the vessel is inside the zone

Potential consequence. The vessel cannot hold position or cannot get clear. Heavy movement alongside. Collision, people hurt on deck or lost overboard. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board, people being transferred

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Limits for wind, sea state, tidal stream and visibility are set for each vessel and each kind of work inside a zone before the job starts, agreed with the client, written in the task specific supplement and known to the Master and the installation. Where the installation's or the field's limits are tighter, the tighter limit applies. The forecast is obtained before sailing and updated on location, and the Master compares what is observed at the set up position with the forecast before closing. Work on the side from which wind and sea set the vessel on is subject to lower limits than work on the other side. The Master may refuse or stop any operation on grounds of weather at any time, and that decision is not open to commercial pressure from the client, the installation or the office; the Operations Manager supports it. When conditions approach a limit the vessel finishes or suspends the work and leaves the zone in good time, without waiting for the limit to be passed. In restricted visibility the vessel does not enter unless the installation agrees and the Master is satisfied with the radar picture. The draft assessment Lightning and severe weather applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set and record the operating limits for each vessel for work inside a zone and for transfer and cargo work alongside, and state in the charter terms that the Master's decision on weather is final. Owner: Operations Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Watchkeeping lapses inside or near the zone: one person alone on the bridge, distraction by phones, paperwork or deck work, and fatigue from long passages, long spells standing by and a small crew

Potential consequence. A drift toward the structure, a change in the weather or an approaching vessel goes unnoticed. Slow or wrong reaction to a failure. Collision. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board, people on the installation

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Inside a zone the bridge is manned by the Master, or a watchkeeper the Master has judged competent for close work at that installation, at the controls, with a second person as lookout and to handle the radio; the second person has no other duty at that time. The Master is on the bridge for every entry, approach, period alongside and departure. Mobile phones, paperwork, meals and conversations not connected with the job are kept off the bridge while inside the zone. The time spent inside is kept to what the work needs: the vessel does not wait, stand by or shelter inside the zone, and withdraws outside it between tasks. The job is planned with the Crewing Manager so that the hours of rest of the Master and watchkeepers are kept across the passage out, the work and the passage home, with enough competent people on board to relieve the bridge. A Master who judges that the bridge team is too tired to enter says so, and the entry waits. Watch handovers are done outside the zone. RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the manning and the hours of rest plan for offshore field work against each vessel's safe manning, and how hours of rest are recorded for self employed skippers and crew. Owner: Crewing Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Transfer of people between the vessel and the installation or another vessel inside the zone

Potential consequence. A fall into the water between the vessel and the structure, crushing against a landing or ladder, a fall from height. Death.

Persons at risk. People being transferred, deck crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The method of transfer is agreed with the client and the installation before the job and is one for which the vessel is equipped and its crew are trained, and those being transferred are trained and equipped for it as the installation requires. RA 7 Personnel Transfer applies in full. Inside the zone the Master decides whether conditions allow a transfer at that moment, having watched the vessel's movement at the transfer point, and either party may stop it. The vessel is held steady at the transfer point by the Master at the controls, with a deck crew member in direct contact with the bridge controlling when each person steps. One person crosses at a time, on the crew member's word, with both hands free, wearing a lifejacket and the other equipment the method requires. Nobody stands in the gap or the crush zone at the bow or the side. A person in the water is the immediate priority: the propellers are taken out of gear on that side, the installation is told, and recovery follows the vessel's person overboard drill.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm which methods of transfer each vessel is equipped and its crew trained for, and what training and equipment the clients require of those transferred. Owner: Operations Manager with the Crewing Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Cargo, hose and lifting work alongside: loads lifted by the installation's crane over the vessel's deck, hoses passed down, and the vessel held close to the structure for the length of the work

Potential consequence. A person struck or crushed by a swinging or dropped load, or caught between cargo. The crane hook or a hose fouls the vessel. Loss of position while tied to a load. Loss of stability from a load badly placed. Death.

Persons at risk. Deck crew, all on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Cargo work inside a zone is carried out only where the vessel's deck, stability and position keeping suit it and the job has been planned with the installation: the loads, their weights, the order of lifts and where each is to land are known before entry. The installation's crane operator, the vessel's deck crew and the bridge share one radio channel, and the bridge can stop the lift. The deck crew stay in a safe position clear of the landing area while a load is overhead, approach only when it is landed or at a height to be steadied by a tag line, and never stand between a load and a fixed object or under a load. The Master holds the vessel in position throughout and breaks off if position cannot be held; no load is left connected to both the crane and the vessel while the vessel manoeuvres. Loads are stowed and secured, and the effect on stability is checked, as the draft assessment Vessel loading and stability requires. Hose work follows the installation's procedure, with the hose supported clear of the propellers. RA 21 Crane Operations On Board and RA 16 Working on Deck apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish whether any vessel in the fleet carries out cargo or hose work with offshore installations and, if so, write the task specific supplement for it. Owner: Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Working among offshore wind farm structures: turbines, substations, cables and construction vessels, with pushing on to a boat landing, little sea room, and effects on radar

Potential consequence. Contact with a tower or a landing. A vessel or structure hidden by a turbine or lost in radar clutter. Anchor or gear fouling a cable. Collision with a crew transfer or construction vessel. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board, technicians being transferred, other vessels

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Before working at a renewable energy site the Master obtains the site's marine coordination procedures, its current notices to mariners and any safety zone notice in force, since zones at these sites are declared case by case and are common during construction and major maintenance. The vessel reports to the site's marine coordination centre on entering and leaving and follows its traffic instructions. The same rules apply to closing a turbine as to an installation: checks done before the approach, a low speed, an approach that does not point at the structure until the final stage, and the Master at the controls. Pushing on to a boat landing is done only by a vessel fitted and fendered for it, within the limits set for that vessel, and is covered by the Workboat Code section on bow push up operations, which was not read. The lookout allows for turbines hiding other vessels and for false and multiple radar echoes close to the structures, and radar settings are not adjusted so far that small targets are lost. The vessel does not anchor within the site except in an emergency. Air draught is checked against blade clearance where it matters, and floating structures are given room for their movement on their moorings.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish which wind farm and other renewable energy sites the fleet works at, obtain each site's marine coordination procedures, and confirm which vessels are fitted for push on transfer. Owner: Operations Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. An emergency on the installation or on the vessel while the vessel is inside the zone: a gas release, fire or alarm on the installation, a person overboard, or fire or flooding on the vessel

Potential consequence. The vessel is caught in a gas cloud and becomes a source of ignition, or its own emergency endangers the installation. Fire, explosion, multiple deaths.

Persons at risk. All on board, people on the installation

Initial rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Control measures. The entry briefing covers what the installation's alarms mean for the vessel and which way the vessel will leave. On an installation alarm, or on being told to leave, the vessel stops work, releases any load or hose under the installation's direction, and leaves the zone at once by the agreed escape heading, upwind of any gas where it can. The crew do not wait for instructions to make ready to leave. The vessel then stands by outside the zone to give help if asked, since saving life is a permitted reason to be in the zone. On an emergency aboard the vessel, the Master moves clear of the structure first if the vessel can still move, tells the installation at once, and then deals with the emergency under the assessment Fire on board and abandoning the vessel and RA 30 Emergency Preparedness. Naked flames, smoking on deck and hot work are forbidden inside the zone unless the installation has agreed. Every such event is reported to the Designated Person Ashore.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Include an installation alarm and a loss of propulsion inside the zone in the scenarios used for vessel drills. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The completed entry checklist for each entry is kept on the vessel and a sample is reviewed by the Designated Person Ashore after each offshore field job.
  • The vessel's log records the time of each request to enter, each permission, each entry and each departure from a zone, and is checked at the internal audit of the vessel.
  • Any contact, near miss, loss of position, failed check, refusal of entry or complaint from an installation is reported under PRC 01 the same day and reviewed against this assessment.
  • Hours of rest records for the Master and watchkeepers on offshore field work are checked by the Crewing Manager after each job.
  • Safety alerts from the Marine Safety Forum, IMCA and the Workboat Association that concern safety zones are passed to Masters by the Designated Person Ashore, with a record of who has read them.
  • This assessment is reviewed before the fleet takes on a new kind of work inside a zone, after any incident in a zone, and at least once a year.

Emergency arrangements

  • Loss of propulsion, steering or position inside the zone: tell the installation at once, use whatever power and steering remain to get clear, make a distress or urgency call if contact with the structure is possible, and get the crew to a safe position with lifejackets on.
  • Installation alarm or an instruction to leave: stop work, disconnect under the installation's direction, leave by the agreed escape heading, upwind of any gas, and stand by outside the zone.
  • Person overboard inside the zone: take the propellers out of gear on that side, keep the person in sight, tell the installation and the Coastguard, and recover the person under the vessel's drill.
  • Contact with the structure: tell the installation, move clear if the vessel is able, check for injuries and for water coming in, and report to the Coastguard and the Designated Person Ashore.
  • Fire or flooding on the vessel: move clear of the structure first where that can be done, tell the installation, and follow the assessment Fire on board and abandoning the vessel.
  • Loss of communications with the installation: stop the operation, move to a position clear of the structure, try the second means of contact, and leave the zone if contact is not restored.

Related documents

  • To confirm which draft procedure governs this work. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up and PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness are relevant, together with each vessel's safety management system and the field operator's marine procedures and data cards.
  • RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations (GSS-HSE-RA-33), RA 7 Personnel Transfer (GSS-HSE-RA-24), RA 3 Coming Alongside (GSS-HSE-RA-08), RA 21 Crane Operations On Board (GSS-MAR-RA-04), RA 16 Working on Deck (GSS-MAR-RA-14), RA 41 Dive Operations (GSS-DIV-RA-01), RA 30 Emergency Preparedness (GSS-HSE-RA-12), RA 54 Towing (GSS-MAR-RA-13), Vessel loading and stability (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Lightning and severe weather (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026). RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations covers navigation and watchkeeping in general; this assessment adds the controls for working close to an offshore structure.
  • Passage plan, entry checklist, log entries for permission and entry, installation data cards and field marine procedures, operating limits for each vessel, record of each vessel's redundancy and position keeping ability, hours of rest records, crew briefing records, charter terms and client instructions.

PPE requirements

A lifejacket worn by every person on deck inside the zone and by every person being transferred, with any personal locator beacon and immersion or transfer suit the client, the installation or the transfer method requires. Safety helmet, safety footwear, gloves and high visibility clothing for deck crew during cargo, hose and transfer work. Eye protection where the task requires it. Warm and waterproof clothing suited to the season, in line with Working in Cold Conditions. Hand held radios for deck crew in contact with the bridge. Personal protective equipment does not reduce the risk of collision, which is controlled by the planning, the checks and the handling of the vessel.
