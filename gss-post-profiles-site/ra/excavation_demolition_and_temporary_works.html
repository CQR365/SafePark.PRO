Risk assessments / Excavation, demolition and temporary works

RISK ASSESSMENT

Excavation, demolition and temporary works

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionMarine civils work that involves digging, breaking out, dismantling or temporarily supporting ground and structures: excavations and trenches on quays, foreshore and sea defences, demolition and dismantling of parts of quays, jetties, slipways and walls, and the design, installation, use and removal of temporary works including propping, shoring, cofferdams and working platforms
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Civils Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeGSS civils sites on and beside the water: quays, piers, jetties, slipways, pontoon abutments, sea walls, rock armour and other sea defences, the foreshore between the tide marks, and the Silverhills site where GSS carries out construction work for itself. Piling is assessed in RA 12 Piling Operations, lifting in RA 21a Shore Lifting, work at the water's edge in RA 18 Working on or Near Water, and diving in support of civils work in RA 41 Dive Operations. No existing draft assessment covers collapse of ground or structures, buried services or temporary works, which is the subject here.
Main equipment usedExcavators and attachments including breakers, dumpers, telehandlers, cranes, barges and pontoons used as working platforms, trench sheets, boxes, props and shoring, sheet piles and cofferdam components, pumps, cable and pipe locating equipment, hand digging tools, edge protection and barriers, cutting and breaking tools, and rescue equipment for the water. What plant and temporary works equipment GSS owns or hires is listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskCivils operatives, plant operators and banksmen, the Site Supervisor, divers and vessel crew working beside the works, subcontractors, the client's staff, harbour and marina users, the public on adjoining quays, paths and beaches, and occupiers of nearby buildings served by the buried services.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. Each job needs a construction phase plan under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015. A permit to dig, a temporary works register and a permit to load or strike are proposed here as controlled forms; whether they exist is to be confirmed.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. The work needs a suitable and sufficient assessment and a safe system of work, and the Company must protect the public and others not in its employment, which matters on quays and shores that stay open during the work.
Construction, managementConstruction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015. Excavation, demolition, dismantling and marine construction are construction work. The duties of client, designer, principal designer, principal contractor and contractor apply according to the role GSS holds on each job, and that role must be settled before the work is priced. The Regulations on those roles and on the construction phase plan were not read for this draft; Part 4 was.
Stability and demolitionCDM 2015 regulations 19 and 20, as read. All practicable steps must be taken to ensure that a new or existing structure does not collapse if the construction work may make it unstable or it is in a temporary state of weakness. Temporary supports must be designed, installed and maintained to withstand foreseeable loads, and a structure must not be loaded so as to make it unsafe. Demolition or dismantling must be planned and carried out so as to prevent danger or reduce it as far as reasonably practicable, and the arrangements must be recorded in writing before the work begins.
Excavations and cofferdamsCDM 2015 regulations 22 and 23, as read. All practicable steps must be taken to prevent danger from the collapse of an excavation, from material falling or being dislodged, and from a person being buried or trapped; people, plant and material must be prevented from falling in, and the ground beside must not be overloaded. Work must not be carried out in a supported excavation unless a competent person has inspected it at the start of the shift and after any event likely to have affected its stability. A cofferdam or caisson must be suitably designed and constructed, equipped so that workers can gain shelter or escape if water or material enters, and properly maintained, with similar inspection.
Services and drowningCDM 2015 regulations 25 and 26, as read. Construction work that is liable to create a risk from an underground service must not be carried out unless suitable and sufficient steps have been taken to prevent the risk, and there are requirements for overhead electric cables. Where a person is liable to fall into water with a risk of drowning, steps must be taken to prevent the fall, to minimise the risk of drowning and to provide rescue equipment.
HSE guidanceHSE guidance on excavations, demolition and temporary works, and HSG47 Avoiding danger from underground services (third edition, 2014), which sets out three elements: planning the work, locating and identifying buried services, and safe excavation. HSE states that there are no specific legal requirements on how temporary works are managed and recommends the appointment of a temporary works coordinator; BS 5975 is the recognised standard. Only the HSE summary page for HSG47 was read, not the full document, and BS 5975 was not read.
Marine and environmentWork from a vessel or barge is also subject to the Merchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers, enforced by the MCA. Works below high water and on the foreshore may need a marine licence, the harbour authority's consent and SEPA's agreement; these were not researched for this draft and are to be confirmed for each job.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Work started with the management arrangements missing: the Company's role under the construction regulations not settled, no construction phase plan, no information from the client on the ground, the structure and the services, and no one appointed to control temporary works

Potential consequence. Each of the hazards below is met on site for the first time, with no design, no drawings and no authority to stop. Collapse, service strike or drowning, with death or serious injury and enforcement action.

Persons at risk. All persons on and near the site, the Company

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Before a job is priced the Civils Manager establishes in writing who the client is and whether GSS is the only contractor, a contractor to a principal contractor, or the principal contractor, and whether GSS is also designing any part of the works, temporary works included. The client is asked for the information it holds: drawings and records of the structure, ground investigation, service drawings, tidal and wave data, the history of the site and any asbestos information. A construction phase plan is written for every job, in proportion to its size, and holds the sequence, the temporary works, the excavation and demolition methods, the tidal working limits, the emergency arrangements and the names of those appointed. A temporary works coordinator is appointed in writing for every job that has temporary works, with the authority to stop work, as HSE recommends. A competent Site Supervisor is on site whenever the work is in progress. Work that falls outside the plan stops until the plan has been revised. Subcontractors are selected for their competence in the specific work and work to the same plan.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what role GSS normally holds under the construction regulations on its civils jobs and write a standard start up checklist for civils work. Decide who acts as temporary works coordinator and what training that person needs. Owner: Civils Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Collapse of the sides of an excavation or trench, made more likely on marine sites by made ground behind quay walls, saturated sand and silt, tidal water in the ground and wave action

Potential consequence. A person in the excavation is buried or crushed. A cubic metre of soil weighs more than a tonne. Death by crushing or asphyxiation, and injury to those who go in to dig the person out.

Persons at risk. Operatives in or beside the excavation, rescuers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Entry is avoided where the work can be done from outside, with the machine, long handled tools or prefabricated sections placed by plant. Where entry is needed, the support or batter is decided before digging starts by a competent person, on the ground information available, and is not left to judgement on the day. No depth is treated as safe to enter unsupported without that decision, and fill, sand, silt and any ground below the water table are treated as unable to stand. Sides are battered to a safe angle, flatter in wet ground, or are supported with trench sheets, a trench box or a proprietary system installed from outside the excavation in the sequence the supplier gives. The support equipment is on site before digging begins. Spoil, materials and plant are kept back from the edge by a distance set in the plan. Water is controlled by pumping from a sump, with the effect of pumping on the sides and on nearby structures considered. A competent person inspects the excavation at the start of every shift, after every tide that has reached it, after heavy rain, after any fall of material and after any change to the support, and nobody enters until that person has confirmed it is safe and recorded the inspection. A ladder or other safe way out is within a short distance of everyone inside, and nobody works alone in an excavation.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Identify who is competent to decide excavation support and to carry out the shift inspections, issue an excavation inspection record as a controlled form, and establish what trench support equipment GSS owns or would hire. Owner: Civils Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Striking buried services on quays, slipways and in the yard: electricity cables to shore power, lighting, cranes and fuel pumps, gas and LPG pipework, fuel lines, water mains, sewers and telecoms, many of them unrecorded on older marine sites

Potential consequence. Electrical explosion and burns, gas fire or explosion, fuel release to the water, flooding of the excavation, loss of supply to the site and to others. Death or serious injury.

Persons at risk. Plant operators, operatives hand digging, persons nearby, the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The three steps in HSG47 are followed and recorded on a permit to dig. Planning: service drawings are obtained from the utilities, the client, the harbour authority and the site owner before the job, the route of the work is chosen to avoid services where it can be, and services are isolated and proved dead by their owner where the work allows. Locating: the area is surveyed on the day by a trained person with a cable and pipe locator used in each of its modes, covers and marker posts are lifted and looked at, and the lines found are marked on the ground. Drawings are treated as a guide and never as proof that the ground is clear. Safe digging: trial holes are dug by hand with insulated tools to expose and confirm each service before a machine works near it, mechanical excavation and breakers are kept away from a located service by the distance set on the permit, and services found are supported and protected and never used as a step or a handhold. Any unidentified pipe or cable is treated as live until its owner says otherwise. The permit names the services, the isolation, the person who surveyed and the limits for machine digging, and is signed by the Site Supervisor and briefed to the operator and banksman. At Silverhills, the buried pipework from the bulk LPG tank and the shore power and fuel services to the pontoons are identified before any excavation.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Issue a permit to dig as a controlled form, establish what locating equipment GSS holds and who is trained to use it, and obtain or commission a service drawing for the Silverhills site. Owner: Civils Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Underwater and foreshore services and obstructions: submarine power and telecom cables, outfall and intake pipes, moorings and ground chains, and old piles and debris in the bed where excavation, dredging or breaking out is to take place

Potential consequence. Electric shock to a diver or an operator through a damaged cable. A ruptured outfall or rising main discharging onto the works. Loss of power or communications to an island or a harbour. Plant fouled and pulled off balance.

Persons at risk. Plant operators, divers, vessel crew, the public served by the service

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The search for services extends below the high water mark. Admiralty charts, the harbour authority, the seabed owner, the power and telecom operators, the water and sewerage undertaker and fish farm or marina operators are asked for the routes of cables, pipes and moorings, and the answers are recorded in the plan. Where records are poor the bed is surveyed before work, by a diver, a camera or a sonar or magnetometer survey as the job justifies, and the routes are buoyed or marked on the working drawing with a stand off distance. Cables are isolated by their owner where the work comes close, and a diver does not work on or beside a cable that has not been proved dead. Outfalls and intakes are isolated as the proposed assessment Diving in contaminated water sets out. Excavation by machine from a barge or the shore is positioned by measurement or satellite positioning against the marked routes, and digging stops at once if the bucket meets unexpected resistance. Anchors and spud legs are placed clear of the marked routes.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Add the underwater service search to the civils start up checklist and state who commissions a bed survey where the records are not adequate. Owner: Civils Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Tidal working: an excavation, a cofferdam or a working area on the foreshore flooding on the rising tide or in a surge, operatives and plant cut off, ground softened and scoured between tides, and wave overtopping

Potential consequence. Drowning. Plant bogged and submerged, with fuel and oil released. Excavation sides and temporary supports undermined so that they fail on the next shift.

Persons at risk. Operatives and plant operators on the foreshore, rescuers

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Tidal work is planned around predicted tide times and heights for the site, set out for each shift in a tidal working window with a stated time to stop work and a stated time by which people and plant are clear. The Site Supervisor checks the forecast for wind, swell and surge each day, shortens the window when the sea is running higher than predicted, and does not open the excavation on a tide that leaves too little time. One person watches the water and has the authority to clear the site. The escape route for people and for plant is fixed before work starts, kept open, and is on the landward side. Plant is parked above the high water mark between shifts, and refuelling is done above it. Excavations left open over a tide are made as safe as possible, by backfilling, plating or flooding under control, and are inspected by the competent person before anyone enters after the tide has been in them. Scour of the foreshore, of foundations and of temporary supports is checked for after each tide. Lifejackets are worn on the foreshore and at the edge, rescue equipment is at the workface, and a safety boat is in attendance where a person could be carried into deep water. RA 18 Working on or Near Water and the assessment titled Lightning and severe weather apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the tidal working window into the construction phase plan template and state the minimum margin between stopping work and the water reaching the workface. Owner: Civils Manager with each Site Supervisor. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Existing structure made unstable by the work: excavating in front of or behind a quay wall, sea wall or slipway toe, removing fill, ties, fenders, bracing or part of a deck, cutting into a wall, or surcharging old masonry and corroded steel with plant and materials

Potential consequence. Sudden collapse or movement of the wall, deck or jetty. People and plant fall with it or are buried, into the water or onto the foreshore. Death and multiple casualties, and loss of the structure.

Persons at risk. Operatives, plant operators, divers working at the toe, the public above or below

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Before the work is planned, the form and condition of the structure are established from records and by inspection above and below water, including what holds it up: gravity, ties and anchors, piles, or the fill behind it. Where that cannot be established, or the structure is old masonry, corroded sheet piling or timber of unknown strength, an engineer competent in maritime structures assesses it and states what may be removed, in what sequence, what temporary support is needed first and what loads the deck or the ground behind the wall can carry. Excavation at the toe or behind a wall is done in short lengths to the engineer's limits and is not left open longer than needed. No bracing, tie, waling, fender pile or part of a deck is cut or removed unless the sequence allows it and any temporary support is in place and signed off. Load limits for plant and stored materials on quays, decks and behind walls are stated in the plan and marked on site, and plant heavier than the limit is kept off. The structure is watched for movement, cracking and loss of fill, with simple monitoring marks or instruments where the engineer asks for them, and work stops and the area is cleared at any sign of movement. Divers are not at the toe of a wall while plant works above it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Identify the engineer or consultancy GSS uses for assessment of maritime structures and for temporary works design, and whether GSS carries out any such design in house. Owner: Civils Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Demolition and dismantling of parts of quays, jetties, slipways, walls and steel or timber structures: premature or uncontrolled collapse, falling material, sections dropping into the water, and stored energy in tensioned ties, fixings and stressed members

Potential consequence. People struck or crushed by falling material or carried down with a collapsing section. Debris striking divers, vessels or the public. Death or serious injury.

Persons at risk. Operatives, plant operators, divers, vessel crew, the public

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The arrangements for the demolition or dismantling are written down before the work begins, by a competent person, and for anything beyond simple work the method is reviewed by a competent engineer. The method is based on a survey of the structure and sets out the sequence, the state of stability at each stage, the temporary supports, the plant and its position, the exclusion zones on land and on the water, and how material is lowered or lifted away. The sequence is chosen so that the structure cannot collapse out of control, working from the top down and from the free end back, and sections are cut to a size the crane or plant can hold before the last cut is made. Remote methods by machine are preferred to people working on or under the structure. Exclusion zones are fenced or patrolled on land, and are kept clear on the water by a guard boat or by agreement with the harbour authority; no diver is in the water inside the zone during breaking or lifting. Services are isolated and proved dead first. Tensioned ties and anchors are released under control to the engineer's method. Material is not allowed to accumulate on a deck or behind a wall beyond its load limit, and debris that reaches the water is recovered. Lifting of sections follows RA 21a Shore Lifting, and cutting follows RA 24 Grinding and Cutting and RA 27 Hot Work.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish what demolition and dismantling GSS undertakes itself and what it gives to specialist subcontractors, and write the requirement for a recorded demolition method into the civils start up checklist. Confirm what notice or warrant Scottish building standards require for demolition, which was not researched. Owner: Civils Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Temporary works that have not been designed, checked or controlled: props, shoring, falsework, working platforms for plant, barge and pontoon platforms, access scaffolds over water and lifting frames, installed from experience, altered on site, loaded early or removed too soon

Potential consequence. Failure of the temporary works and collapse of whatever they supported, or of the plant standing on them. Death or serious injury, often to several people.

Persons at risk. Operatives, plant operators, others near the works

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Every item of temporary works on the job is listed in a temporary works register kept by the temporary works coordinator, with its design, its designer, the check carried out, and its status. Each item is designed by a competent person to withstand the foreseeable loads, including wave, current, tide, vessel impact and the plant that will stand on or beside it, and the design is checked independently to a level that matches the risk; standard solutions are used only within the limits the supplier publishes. The design is issued to site as a drawing or sketch with the sequence and the limits. The works are installed to the drawing and inspected by the coordinator or a competent supervisor, who signs a permit to load before any load is applied. They are inspected at intervals and after any tide, storm or impact that could have affected them. Nothing is altered, removed or loaded beyond the design without the designer's agreement, and removal is authorised by a permit to strike once the permanent works can carry the load. Working platforms for cranes and excavators on ground, on a quay deck or on a barge are treated as temporary works and are designed for the plant. Scaffolds follow RA 58 Scaffold Towers and RA 32 Working at Height.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set up the temporary works register and the permits to load and strike as controlled forms, and adopt a written temporary works procedure in line with BS 5975, which was not read for this draft. Owner: Civils Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Cofferdam or caisson: inundation from overtopping, a failed sheet, seal or prop, a blow in the base, or pump failure, and a contaminated or oxygen deficient atmosphere at the bottom

Potential consequence. Rapid flooding with people inside and no quick way out. Drowning, crushing by displaced members, asphyxiation.

Persons at risk. Operatives inside the cofferdam, divers working on its outer face, rescuers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A cofferdam is designed by a competent engineer for the highest water level, wave and current expected during its life, with the ground conditions established, and is built to that design and recorded in the temporary works register. It is equipped so that those inside can reach shelter or escape if water or material enters: at least two ladders or stairs placed apart and kept clear, and an alarm that can be heard throughout. A competent person inspects it at the start of every shift and after any event that could have affected its strength or stability, and nobody enters until it has been confirmed safe and the inspection recorded. Water levels inside and outside, seepage, movement of the walls and the state of props and walings are watched through the shift by a named person at the top who can sound the alarm. Pumps have a standby and a power supply that does not depend on a single source, and the plan states the water level at which the cofferdam is evacuated. Work inside stops ahead of forecast surge or heavy weather. The atmosphere at the bottom of a deep cofferdam is tested before entry, and RA 37 Confined Spaces applies where it could become dangerous. Vessels are kept off the outside face, with fendering or marking where traffic passes. Divers working on the outer face are protected from differential pressure through any gap or opening by the controls in RA 41 Dive Operations.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm whether GSS builds or works in cofferdams or caissons and, if so, who designs them and who is competent to inspect them. Owner: Civils Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Plant working near edges and water: an excavator, dumper, telehandler or crane overturning or running over a quay edge, a slipway side, an excavation edge or soft foreshore, and plant working from a barge or pontoon

Potential consequence. Operator trapped in a submerged or overturned cab. Drowning or crushing. People below struck. Fuel and oil released to the water.

Persons at risk. Plant operators, banksmen, operatives and divers nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Plant is chosen for the ground and the reach so that it can work back from the edge. The bearing capacity of the quay deck, the ground behind the wall and the foreshore is established as part of the temporary works, and tracked plant on soft ground works from mats or a prepared platform. Stop blocks, kerbs or barriers strong enough for the plant are fixed at quay and excavation edges where plant must approach, and dumpers tip against a stop block and never at an unprotected edge. Plant keeps the distance from an excavation edge set in the plan. Operators are trained and authorised for the machine, wear the seat belt, and work with a banksman where they cannot see the edge or the people near it. On a slipway the gradient and the surface, including weed and slime, are assessed and plant is restrained or kept off where grip is doubtful. Plant on a barge or pontoon is secured against movement, the barge's stability and deck loading are confirmed for that plant by a competent person, and the operator has a clear way out and wears a lifejacket of a type suited to an enclosed cab, on advice to be confirmed. Nobody works or dives beneath or beside plant working at an edge. Rescue equipment and a means of raising the alarm are at the workface. The assessments titled Telehandler and lift truck operation and Workplace transport and deliveries, and RA 53 Barge Movements, apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the plant GSS owns and hires for civils work, the operators' training records, and the advice on lifejackets and escape for operators in enclosed cabs beside water. Owner: Civils Manager with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. People falling into excavations, and operatives and the public struck by plant, on sites that stay open to harbour users, marina customers and walkers

Potential consequence. Fall injury, or a fall into a flooded excavation. A person crushed by a slewing or reversing machine. Death or serious injury, including to a child.

Persons at risk. Operatives, the public, marina customers, the client's staff

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The working area is separated from the public and from other users of the quay by fencing or barriers that cannot easily be moved, with a signed alternative route, agreed with the harbour authority or site owner before work starts. Every excavation is guarded with rigid barriers or covered with secured plates that will take the traffic that may cross them, and is not left open and unguarded at any time, including at breaks; on the foreshore, where barriers will not survive the tide, excavations are backfilled or made safe before the site is left. Guard rails and toe boards are fitted where a person could fall a distance likely to cause injury. Pedestrians and plant are kept apart by barriers and a planned layout, plant has working visibility aids and alarms, and a banksman controls reversing and slewing near people. Lighting covers the workface and the access in winter hours. The site is checked and secured at the end of each shift. The proposed assessment Visitors, customers and the public on working sites applies, with the assessment titled Site security and unauthorised access.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Agree with each client and harbour authority how the public are kept out of civils work areas, and hold a stock of barriers and plates suited to quay work. Owner: Civils Manager with each Site Supervisor. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. Hazardous materials released by digging and breaking out: asbestos in old pipes, sheeting and buildings, contaminated fill behind quay walls, tar and creosote treated timber, lead and antifoul paint on steelwork, silica dust from concrete and stone, and noise and vibration from breakers

Potential consequence. Lung disease and cancer from asbestos and silica, lead poisoning, skin burns and sensitisation from tar and creosote, hearing loss and hand arm vibration injury. Pollution of the water.

Persons at risk. Operatives, plant operators, persons nearby

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The client's information on asbestos and on ground contamination is obtained before the job, and where a structure is to be demolished or broken into, an asbestos survey of the right type is required first; the assessment titled Work near asbestos containing materials applies and work stops if suspect material is found. Fill behind old quay walls and at boatyards is assumed to be contaminated until shown otherwise, is kept damp, is stockpiled on sheeting away from the water and drains, and is removed as waste under PRC 12. Breaking and cutting of concrete and stone is done with water suppression or extraction on the tool, with the machine cab closed and filtered, and with others kept back. Paint on old steelwork is tested for lead before hot cutting or grinding, and cold cutting is preferred. Treated timber is handled with gloves and covered skin, and is not burned. Breakers mounted on machines are used in preference to hand held breakers, and exposure to noise and vibration is managed under RA 55 Noise and RA 36 Vibration. A COSHH assessment under PRC 05 covers the dusts and substances expected, and the assessment titled Working with concrete and cement applies to new concrete.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Add questions on asbestos, contaminated ground and lead paint to the information requested from the client, and establish what dust suppression and face fit tested respiratory protection the civils team has. Owner: Civils Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The Civils Manager checks before each job starts that the construction phase plan, the service information, the temporary works register and the appointments are in place, and records the check.
  • Excavation and cofferdam inspection records are completed at the start of every shift and after every tide or event affecting stability, and are sampled weekly by the Site Supervisor's manager.
  • Permits to dig, permits to load and permits to strike are returned with the job file and sampled by the Risk and Compliance Manager.
  • The temporary works coordinator reviews the register at least weekly while temporary works are in place and after any storm or impact.
  • Structures affected by the work are checked for movement at the intervals the engineer sets, and the readings are kept.
  • Every service strike, collapse or fall of material, movement of a structure, inundation and near miss with plant at an edge is reported under PRC 01, whether or not anyone was harmed, and reviewed against this assessment.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager inspects at least one live civils site each quarter against this assessment.

Emergency arrangements

  • Collapse with a person buried or trapped: raise the alarm, call 999 and ask for the Fire and Rescue Service, do not enter an unsupported excavation or go under an unstable structure, stop plant, and uncover the head and chest by hand only if it can be done from a safe position.
  • Electricity cable struck: the operator stays in the cab if the machine may be live, or jumps clear without touching the machine and the ground together; everyone keeps well back, nobody touches the machine or the cable, and the network operator is called to make it dead.
  • Gas or LPG pipe struck: stop engines if safe, no ignition sources, clear the area up wind, call the gas emergency service, or the supplier and the Fire and Rescue Service for LPG, and do not try to stop the leak or backfill.
  • Rising water or inundation: sound the alarm, everyone leaves by the escape route at once, plant is abandoned if moving it would delay people, and a head count is taken at the assembly point above high water.
  • Person in the water: throw a line or a lifebuoy, raise the alarm, call the Coastguard by 999 or on VHF channel 16, launch the safety boat where one is in attendance, and follow RA 18 Working on or Near Water.
  • Movement or cracking of a structure or temporary works: stop work, clear the area including the water beneath, tell the temporary works coordinator and the engineer, and do not return until they have assessed it.
  • Fuel or oil reaching the water, or a sewer or outfall damaged: follow the assessment titled Oil and chemical spill response and tell SEPA and the harbour authority.

PPE requirements

Safety helmet, high visibility clothing, safety footwear with toe and midsole protection, and gloves on every civils site. A lifejacket on the foreshore, at any unprotected edge beside water and on barges and pontoons, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. Eye protection, hearing protection and respiratory protection of the type and fit the COSHH assessment sets when breaking, cutting or handling dusty or contaminated material. Insulated hand tools for digging near electricity cables. Waterproof and thermal clothing for tidal work. Personal protective equipment gives no protection against burial, collapse or a live cable: support, isolation and exclusion are the controls.
