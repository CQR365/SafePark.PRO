RISK ASSESSMENT

Fatigue, on call duty and hours of rest

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionThe planning and control of working hours and rest for vessel crews, dive teams, yard and civils personnel and duty holders ashore: manning levels and rosters, watchkeeping and command at sea, the recording of hours of rest, travel to join and leave a vessel, on call duty out of hours for the vessels and for accidents and incidents, and long hours worked in response to an emergency
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins. For a vessel the supplement is the manning and watch plan for the charter or passage.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Operations Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeAll GSS vessels wherever they are working, including passages and work up to 150 miles offshore and periods alongside or at a mooring; dive sites; the Silvers yard and civils locations where extended or tidal hours are worked; and the homes and vehicles of the duty holders who are on call out of hours. Rest aboard while moored is assessed in RA 11 Rest Periods at Mooring and watchkeeping in RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations, and both are relied on here.
Main equipment usedThe crew roster and the crew management system, the table of shipboard working arrangements and the hours of rest records for each vessel, the on call roster and contact list, on call telephones, the vessel's watch alarm where one is fitted, crew accommodation aboard and ashore, and Company and private vehicles used to travel to and from vessels and sites.
Persons at riskMasters and skippers, mates, engineers and deck crew, most of whom are self employed; dive supervisors and dive teams; yard and civils personnel on extended or tidal hours; the Designated Person Ashore, the Deputy Designated Person Ashore, the Operations Manager and the Managing Director when on call; the Crewing Manager and Assistant Crewing Manager who set the rosters; and other vessels, clients' personnel and the public who would be harmed by an error.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. No draft procedure on hours of work, hours of rest or on call duty has been seen. PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness applies to the out of hours response.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General duties ashoreHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. HSE guidance states that the legal duty is on employers to manage the risks from fatigue, irrespective of any individual's willingness to work extra hours or preference for a shift pattern, and that changes to working hours need to be risk assessed.
Working time ashoreWorking Time Regulations 1998. Regulation 4 limits working time, including overtime, to an average of 48 hours in each seven days over the reference period unless the worker has agreed in writing to work more. Regulation 10 entitles a worker to a rest period of not less than eleven consecutive hours in each 24 hour period, and regulation 11 to an uninterrupted rest period of not less than 24 hours in each seven day period, or the alternatives it allows over 14 days. HSE states that compliance with these Regulations alone is insufficient to manage the risks of fatigue. How on call time at home counts under the Regulations was not read and is to be confirmed.
Seafarers: which rulesRegulation 18 of the Working Time Regulations 1998 excludes workers to whom the Merchant Shipping (Maritime Labour Convention) (Hours of Work) Regulations 2018 apply. Those Regulations apply to sea going United Kingdom ships wherever they are, and do not apply to pleasure vessels, fishing vessels, warships or vessels not ordinarily engaged in commercial activities. MCA notice MSN 1877 (M) Amendment 2 states that they cover vessels certificated under the MCA codes of practice for small commercial vessels. Whether every GSS vessel is sea going within the definition is to be confirmed.
Seafarers: minimum restRegulation 5 of the 2018 Regulations places a duty on the shipowner, the master and the employer to ensure that a seafarer is provided with at least the minimum hours of rest. MSN 1877 (M) Amendment 2 gives the minimum as not less than ten hours in any 24 hour period and 77 hours in any seven day period, with the daily rest divided into no more than two periods, one of at least six hours, and no more than 14 hours between rest periods. The text of regulation 6 itself could not be opened for this draft and the figures are taken from the MCA notice.
Seafarers: table, records and exceptionsMSN 1877 (M) Amendment 2 states that the shipowner must produce a table of scheduled duties and hours of rest, which the master posts where the crew can see it; that records of hours of rest are kept for all seafarers, endorsed by the master or authorised person and the seafarer, with a copy to the seafarer; that the master may require work during rest hours in an emergency and must then give an adequate rest period; and that a seafarer whose rest is disturbed by a call out should have compensatory rest.
Self employed seafarersThe 2018 Regulations define a seafarer as any person, including a master, who is employed or engaged or works in any capacity on board a ship and whose normal place of work is on a ship. MSN 1877 (M) Amendment 2 states that a person who is self employed for tax purposes may still count as an employed seafarer. The minimum rest duty is therefore treated here as applying to self employed masters and crew, and the position on leave is to be confirmed.
Marine guidance and codesMGN 505 (M) Amendment 1, Human element guidance, Part 1: Fatigue and fitness, is the MCA guidance on the causes of fatigue and on fatigue management plans. Only its summary page was read. The manning and hours provisions of the Workboat Code Edition 3, and of the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers, were not read and are to be confirmed. The Merchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 apply aboard, enforced by the MCA.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. No system for managing fatigue: hours and rest not planned, not recorded or not looked at, and nobody with the authority to stop a job because the people doing it are too tired

Potential consequence. Fatigue goes unseen until it causes an error: a grounding or collision, a person crushed or lost overboard, a diving accident, a road crash. Death. Enforcement action against the Company and the Master.

Persons at risk. All vessel crew, dive teams, yard and civils personnel, duty holders ashore, other vessels and the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Fatigue is treated as a hazard to be managed like any other, and a single written policy states the limits the Company works to at sea and ashore, who plans hours, who records them and who reviews them. The Operations Manager is responsible for seeing that each vessel is manned and rostered so that the minimum rest can be taken, and the Master for seeing that it is taken on board. Each department manager is responsible for hours in their own area. Every person is told at induction that they must say when they are too tired to work safely, that a Master, dive supervisor or site supervisor who stops or delays a job for that reason will be backed by the Company, and that a client's programme does not override rest. Hours of rest records and overtime records are returned ashore and reviewed, and a breach is reported and investigated as a nonconformity, not passed over. The commercial team is told the manning and rest limits so that work is not sold on terms the crew cannot meet.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the fatigue policy and the limits the Company will work to, having first read the manning and hours provisions of the Workboat Code Edition 3 and MGN 505 in full. Owner: Operations Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. A Master or skipper in command while fatigued: long days combining navigation, the job itself, paperwork and client liaison, with broken sleep and nobody qualified to relieve

Potential consequence. A lapse of attention or falling asleep on watch. Grounding, collision, contact with a structure, a tow or lift going wrong. Loss of the vessel and deaths among crew or others.

Persons at risk. Master or skipper, crew, client personnel aboard, other vessels

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Each job is planned so that the Master can take the minimum rest: not less than ten hours in any 24 hour period, in no more than two periods of which one is at least six hours, with no more than 14 hours between rest periods, and 77 hours in any seven days. Where the job or the passage is longer than one person can cover within those limits, a second person qualified to take the watch is carried, and the vessel does not sail without one. The Master's hours include navigation, working the job, maintenance and paperwork, and all of it is counted. Passage plans show who has the watch and when, and a long passage is not started at the end of a working day. A lookout is kept in addition to the watchkeeper in darkness and restricted visibility, and the watch alarm, where fitted, is switched on. The Master has the authority to delay sailing, anchor, go alongside or stop the job to rest, tells the Designated Person Ashore when doing so, and is not asked to justify it to the client. Paperwork that can be done ashore is taken off the Master. RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations applies to the watch itself.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Review each vessel and each regular type of job to establish whether one watchkeeper can meet the rest limits, and record where a second is needed. Confirm which vessels have a watch alarm. Owner: Operations Manager with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Manning levels and rosters that do not allow the rest to be taken: too few crew for the job, back to back trips, crew changes that are late or cancelled, and gaps filled at short notice

Potential consequence. The whole crew accumulates fatigue over days. Errors in deck work, towing, lifting and engine room work. Crushing, falls overboard, machinery injury, death. Crew who stop accepting work.

Persons at risk. All vessel crew, the Crewing Manager and Assistant Crewing Manager who are pressed to fill the gaps

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Manning for each vessel and job is set by the Operations Manager and is not reduced by the Crewing Manager to solve a shortage. The number and qualifications needed are worked out from the hours the job runs, the watches to be kept, the deck work and the length of the trip, and are never below the minimum on the vessel's certificate. The roster is built so that each person can take the minimum rest every day, shows a planned crew change date, and limits the number of weeks aboard without leave. Before a person is offered a trip the Crewing Manager checks when they last signed off, from GSS or from any other operator they have said they work for, and does not send somebody straight from one trip to the next without rest. A pool of relief crew is kept so that sickness or a late crew change does not fall on those already aboard. Where a relief cannot be found, the Operations Manager decides whether the job is cut back, delayed or stopped, and the decision is recorded. The table of scheduled duties and rest for each vessel is drawn up ashore with the Master and posted aboard.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the manning for each vessel by type of job in writing, set the maximum length of a trip and the minimum time off between trips, and confirm what the crew management system can check and warn of. Owner: Operations Manager with the Crewing Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Hours of rest not recorded, recorded late from memory, or written to show compliance, and no table of working arrangements aboard

Potential consequence. The Company and the Master cannot see that rest is being missed, so nothing changes. Fatigue related accident. Detention or enforcement at an MCA inspection, and no defence afterwards.

Persons at risk. All vessel crew, the Master, the Company

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. A table of scheduled duties at sea and in port, with the minimum daily rest, is posted aboard each vessel where the crew can see it. A record of hours of rest is kept for every person aboard, including the Master and self employed crew, filled in each day, endorsed by the Master and by the person, and a copy given to that person. Records are sent ashore each month and kept for at least one year. The record shows what was actually worked. A Master who records a breach truthfully, with the reason, is supported; a record that has been made to fit is treated as a serious matter. The Designated Person Ashore reviews the records each month, takes up any breach with the Operations Manager, and checks a sample against the vessel log, the daily reports and the job times. Work required in an emergency during rest hours is recorded as such, and the rest given afterwards is recorded with it.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm whether a table and hours of rest records exist aboard each vessel, in what form, and whether they have been reviewed ashore. Issue a controlled record form if none exists. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Rest aboard that is broken or of poor quality: call outs for mooring, weather, cargo or the client, six hours on and six hours off watches, noise, motion, heat or cold, and shared accommodation

Potential consequence. A person who has been in a bunk for the required hours but has not slept. Fatigue building over the trip, slow reactions, poor judgement, injury during deck or engine room work, death.

Persons at risk. All vessel crew, dive teams living aboard

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Work is planned so that each person has one unbroken rest period of at least six hours in every 24, and planned call outs such as tide times, crew transfers and bunkering are put in the plan and not sprung on a resting crew. Drills, safety meetings and routine maintenance are held at times that disturb rest least. A person called out during a rest period is given the lost rest back as soon as the job allows, and the Master records it. Where watches of six hours on and six off are worked, the length of the trip on that pattern is limited and the pattern is reviewed with the crew. Sleeping spaces are kept dark, quiet, ventilated and at a comfortable temperature so far as the vessel allows, noisy work near them is avoided while people sleep, and RA 8 Accommodation applies. Where the vessel cannot give proper rest for the length of the job, accommodation ashore is arranged. At a mooring or anchor the watch arrangements in RA 11 Rest Periods at Mooring are followed so that those off watch can sleep. Meals and hot drinks are available to those working at night.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Ask the Masters and crews which jobs and vessels give the worst rest and why, and review watch patterns and accommodation in the light of the answers. Owner: Operations Manager with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Driving to join a vessel or site before a long working day, or driving home after a trip, a night call out or an extended shift

Potential consequence. Falling asleep at the wheel. Road traffic collision, death of the driver, passengers or other road users.

Persons at risk. Vessel crew, dive teams, civils personnel, duty holders called out at night, other road users

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Travel time to join a vessel or a distant site is counted when the job is planned, and a person who has travelled for several hours does not go straight onto a full watch or a dive. Joining travel is arranged for the day before where the distance needs it, with accommodation. Nobody is expected to drive a long distance home straight after a night watch, a long final day or an emergency response: the choices offered are a rest aboard or ashore before leaving, public transport, a shared car with a rested driver, or a paid overnight stay. A person who says they are too tired to drive is believed, and the cost of the alternative is met without argument. A duty holder called out at night considers first whether the matter can be dealt with by telephone, and if they must travel, tells somebody the route and the expected time of arrival. Travel arrangements for crew are made through the Crewing Manager so that they are seen. RA 29 Vehicle Use applies to the driving itself.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set limits for the combined length of travel and work in one day, and state in writing that accommodation or transport will be provided after long duties. Owner: Crewing Manager with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. On call duty ashore held by the same person night after night with no roster: the Designated Person Ashore, the deputy, the Operations Manager or the Managing Director as the out of hours contact for the vessels and for accidents

Potential consequence. Interrupted sleep over weeks, never fully off duty, and a full working day after a disturbed night. A poor decision made alone at night on a vessel emergency. Long term ill health for the duty holder.

Persons at risk. Designated Person Ashore, Deputy Designated Person Ashore, Operations Manager, Managing Director

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. On call duty is shared on a written roster among enough trained people that each has regular nights and weekends completely free, and the roster is sent to the vessels, the yard and the office so that calls go to the person on duty and not to whoever usually answers. The on call person is free of alcohol, within reach of a telephone signal and able to get to the office or the vessel in the time the emergency arrangements assume. Masters and supervisors are told what warrants a call at night and what can wait until morning. A second person is named for each night whom the on call person can ring to talk a decision through, so that a serious decision is not taken alone and half awake. Calls taken and their length are logged. After a night with a long or repeated call out, the person starts late or stands down the next day and hands the on call telephone to the next person on the roster. A period on call is followed by a period off, and leave is covered by the roster and not by keeping the telephone switched on. Lone decision making out of hours is also covered in RA 40 Lone Worker.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish who is on call now, for what and how often, draw up the roster and the list of what warrants a night call, and train enough people to share the duty. Owner: Managing Director with the Operations Manager and the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Responding to a vessel emergency or major incident from ashore over many hours or days, with no relief and no handover

Potential consequence. Judgement fails as the hours mount: wrong information passed, a critical call missed, a poor decision on the vessel, the crew or the next of kin. Collapse or a road crash on the way home.

Persons at risk. Managing Director, Operations Manager, Designated Person Ashore, Deputy Designated Person Ashore, Risk and Compliance Manager, Office Manager

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The emergency arrangements name a deputy for every role in the response and state a maximum length for one spell in charge, after which the role is handed over whether or not the incident has ended. From the first hour the person leading the response decides who is sent home to sleep so that they can take over later, and does not keep everybody in the room. A written log is kept so that a handover can be given properly, and the handover is made face to face or by telephone with the log open. Food, drink and somewhere quiet to rest are arranged. Nobody who has been in the response overnight drives home; transport or a room is arranged. Travel to the vessel or the scene at short notice follows the control for driving above. The response role is exercised with the handover included, so that the first handover is not made during a real event. RA 30 Emergency Preparedness sets out the response itself.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Name deputies for each response role, set the maximum spell, and include a handover in the next emergency exercise. Owner: Managing Director with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Self employed skippers, crew and divers arriving for work already fatigued from other work, other operators' vessels or long travel, which the Company cannot see

Potential consequence. A person starts a trip or a dive without rest, and the Company's own roster looks compliant. Error in command, on deck or in the water. Death.

Persons at risk. Self employed masters, crew, divers and dive supervisors, and those working with them

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The terms on which self employed crew and divers are engaged state that they must arrive rested and must tell the Crewing Manager or the Diving Manager of other work done in the days before. The Crewing Manager asks the question when offering the work and records the answer. On joining, the Master or dive supervisor asks each person when they last worked and how far they have travelled, and adjusts the first watch or the first dive, or delays the start, where the answer calls for it. A person who declares that they are not rested loses nothing by saying so. Hours of rest records aboard cover self employed crew in the same way as everybody else. The dive supervisor considers the fatigue of the whole team, including standby diver, tender and supervisor, when planning the length of the diving day, and RA 41 Dive Operations applies to the diving.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Add the rest and declaration terms to the engagement of self employed crew and divers, and add the question to the joining and dive briefing checks. Owner: Crewing Manager and Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Extended, early, late or tidal hours ashore in the yard and on civils work, including overtime at busy periods and operating lifting plant or vehicles at the end of a long day

Potential consequence. A lapse while operating the boat hoist, a crane, the telehandler or a vehicle, or while working at height or beside water. Crushing, a dropped load, a fall, drowning, death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, plant operators, civils teams, site supervisors, customers and others nearby

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Working hours in the yard and on civils sites are planned ahead around the tides and the launch and lift programme, so that early and late tides are covered by a changed start time or a split team and not by one team working both ends of the day. Daily rest of eleven hours and a weekly rest day are kept, and overtime is agreed in advance by the manager, recorded and limited. Lifts, hoist movements and other safety critical tasks are scheduled for the first part of the working day where the tide allows, and the supervisor may postpone one where the operator has been at work too long. Breaks are taken away from the work, with shelter and hot drinks in winter. Operators are told they must say when they are too tired to operate, and another trained operator is available at peak periods so that the choice is real. Hours worked are recorded and reviewed monthly by the manager, and any written agreement to work more than an average of 48 hours is voluntary and does not remove the duty to manage fatigue.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Review hours worked in the yard and on civils sites over the last busy period against these limits, and plan cover for the next one. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager and Civils Manager, with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The Designated Person Ashore reviews the hours of rest records from each vessel every month, reports breaches and their causes to the Operations Manager, and checks a sample against vessel logs and daily reports.
  • The Crewing Manager reports each month the number of trips extended, crew changes missed and gaps filled at less than 48 hours notice.
  • The on call roster and call log are reviewed each quarter by the Managing Director for the number of nights each person has held the duty and the number of calls taken.
  • Overtime and hours records for the yard, civils and office are reviewed monthly by each department manager and quarterly by the Risk and Compliance Manager.
  • Every accident, incident and near miss investigation under PRC 01 records the hours worked and rest taken by those involved over the previous 72 hours and considers fatigue as a cause.
  • Vessel inspections and internal audits check that the table of working arrangements is posted and that the records match what the crew say they work.
  • This assessment is reviewed each year, after any fatigue related incident, and whenever manning, vessels or the pattern of work change.

Emergency arrangements

  • A person who is too fatigued to continue safely stops, makes the work safe and tells the Master or supervisor, who relieves them. On a vessel under way the Master slows, anchors, goes alongside or calls the relief, and tells the Designated Person Ashore.
  • Where a Master is the only watchkeeper and cannot safely continue, the vessel is brought to a safe position, the job is suspended and the Designated Person Ashore is told at once. The client's programme does not alter this.
  • Work required of crew during rest hours in an emergency is ordered by the Master, logged, and followed by an adequate rest period as soon as the emergency has passed.
  • A person too tired to drive does not drive. The supervisor or the Crewing Manager arranges a room or transport.
  • A person found asleep or unresponsive on watch or at a control is relieved at once and checked for illness, and the event is reported under PRC 01 as an incident and not as a matter of discipline in the first instance.
  • During a long emergency response ashore the person leading it hands over at the end of the agreed spell, using the log.

Related documents

  • To confirm which draft procedures govern hours of work, hours of rest, manning and on call duty, as none has been seen. PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness, PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up and PRC 19 Induction bear on this assessment.
  • RA 11 Rest Periods at Mooring (GSS-MAR-RA-10), RA 6 Wheelhouse Operations (GSS-HSE-RA-33), RA 8 Accommodation (GSS-HSE-RA-03), RA 29 Vehicle Use (GSS-ADM-RA-01), RA 40 Lone Worker (GSS-HSE-RA-19), RA 30 Emergency Preparedness (GSS-HSE-RA-12), RA 41 Dive Operations (GSS-DIV-RA-01), RA 50 Working Abroad (GSS-HSE-RA-35), and the new draft assessments Work related stress and workload and Attending an incident scene and leading the response.
  • Crew roster, manning statement for each vessel and type of job, table of shipboard working arrangements, hours of rest records, on call roster and call log, overtime and hours records ashore, travel and accommodation bookings, and any written agreements to exceed the 48 hour average.

PPE requirements

No personal protective equipment controls fatigue. The equipment required for the task in hand is set by the assessment for that task. Fatigue makes people less likely to wear it properly, so supervisors pay particular attention to lifejackets, harnesses and hearing protection late in a long shift or watch.
