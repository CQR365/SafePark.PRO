Risk assessments / Fire on board and abandoning the vessel

RISK ASSESSMENT

Fire on board and abandoning the vessel

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionPreventing, detecting and fighting a fire aboard a Company workboat at sea, and abandoning the vessel where the fire, flooding or another emergency cannot be controlled. It covers fire in the engine room, the accommodation and the galley, the use of fixed and portable firefighting equipment, the use of breathing apparatus if it is carried (to confirm), the muster and the drills, abandoning to a liferaft, survival in cold water, raising a distress alert, and the added risk of working far offshore with a small crew.
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Operations Manager and Designated Person Ashore
Location and scopeEvery vessel in the Company workboat fleet while manned, under way, at anchor, on a mooring or working on location, out to the limit of its certificated area of operation, which for the fleet is up to 150 miles offshore. Fire at the Silverhills premises and on the pontoons, and the shore side response to a fire on a vessel alongside, are covered in the draft assessment Fire and emergency evacuation. Preparedness in general is covered in RA 30 Emergency Preparedness. This assessment covers what those two do not: fighting a fire on the vessel itself and leaving it.
Main equipment usedTo be confirmed for each vessel from its certificate and equipment record: fire detection and alarm, the fixed fire extinguishing system for the machinery space and its remote release, remote fuel shut offs and ventilation closures, fire pump and hoses where fitted, portable extinguishers and fire blanket, breathing apparatus if carried, liferaft and its release, lifejackets, immersion suits or thermal protective aids, lifebuoys, the radio installation, the emergency position indicating radio beacon and any other locating devices, distress flares, the grab bag and the first aid kit.
Persons at riskThe Master and crew, including self employed skippers and crew, the Vessel Engineer, divers and the dive team when embarked, client personnel, industrial personnel and passengers carried, shore staff aboard on passage, and those who come to the rescue.
Related permit or procedurePRC 06 Emergency Preparedness. To confirm which draft procedure and which part of each vessel's safety management system set out the fire and abandonment drills, the muster list and the checks on fire and lifesaving equipment. A hot work permit is required for hot work on board under RA 27 Hot Work.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Marine, general dutiesMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997, enforced by the MCA. As made, regulation 5 places the general duty on the employer to ensure the health and safety of workers so far as is reasonably practicable, regulation 7 requires a suitable and sufficient risk assessment, and regulation 13 requires coordination where workers of another employer are on board. How the current, amended text applies to self employed crew is to be confirmed.
Marine, vessel standardThe Workboat Code Edition 3 (MCA), given effect by the Merchant Shipping (Small Workboats and Pilot Boats) Regulations 2023. Its contents list shows section 14 Life-Saving Appliances, section 15 Fire Safety, section 16 Fire Appliances, section 17 Radiocommunications Equipment, section 28 Manning, section 31 Safety Management and Appendix 8 Safety Management System. The reader stopped at paragraph 3.9.3.5, so none of the requirements in those sections were read, and what each vessel must carry and do is to be confirmed from the Code and from the vessel's certificate.
Marine, code of practiceCode of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers, 2026 edition (MCA). Chapter 2 says that a person joining a vessel is given a clear explanation of the alarm signals and instruction on the emergency assembly stations and the fire drill and team requirements. Chapter 4 Emergency drills and procedures and chapter 5 Fire precautions were not read and are to be confirmed.
Marine, drillsMGN 71 (M+F) Amendment 1, Musters, drills, on board training and instructions, and decision support systems (MCA, 24 April 2025). Only the publication page was read. Whether its drill intervals apply to vessels certificated under the Workboat Code, or whether the Code sets its own, is to be confirmed.
Marine, lithium ion batteriesMGN 550 (M+F) Electrical Installations: Guidance for Safe Design, Installation and Operation of Lithium-ion Batteries (MCA). It applies to vessels fitted with lithium ion battery installations and describes thermal runaway and the toxic and flammable gases given off. It does not address small portable batteries, which are assessed in Batteries, power supplies and server rooms.
Fire, ScotlandFire (Scotland) Act 2005, section 53, requires each employer to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the safety of employees in respect of harm caused by fire in the workplace. Whether and how far the Act applies to a vessel alongside or ashore at Silverhills, as distinct from the premises, is to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Fire in the engine room or other machinery space: fuel or oil spraying onto a hot surface, an electrical fault, oily rags and bilges, or hot work

Potential consequence. Rapid, intense fire with thick smoke. Loss of propulsion, power, steering and pumps. Spread to the accommodation and wheelhouse. Burns, smoke inhalation, death and loss of the vessel.

Persons at risk. Vessel Engineer, Master, crew, all on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The fire is prevented first: fuel and oil lines, flexible hoses and their fittings are inspected under the planned maintenance system and leaks are repaired at once, hot surfaces are lagged or shielded where the vessel's standard requires it, bilges are kept clean and dry, oily rags go into a closed metal bin, and nothing is stowed against exhausts or on top of machinery. Hot work in a machinery space is done only under a permit as RA 27 Hot Work requires. The space has fire detection that sounds in the wheelhouse, tested at the interval the vessel's safety management system sets. On an alarm or a report of fire the Master is told at once, the crew muster, and the space is not entered to investigate while there is smoke. The engine is stopped where the situation allows, fuel is shut off from outside the space, ventilation is stopped and every opening is closed before the fixed extinguishing system is used. A distress or urgency call is made early. RA 9 Engine Room applies to the routine work in the space.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. For each vessel, record the fire detection, the remote fuel shut offs, the ventilation closures and the fixed extinguishing system actually fitted, where each is operated from and when it was last tested, and put a simple engine room fire card in the wheelhouse. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master and Vessel Engineer. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Release of a fixed fire extinguishing system into a machinery space: released with a person still inside, released with openings not closed, or the space opened again too soon

Potential consequence. Asphyxiation of a person in the space. The system fails to put the fire out and there is no second charge. The fire flares up again when air is let in. Death and loss of the vessel.

Persons at risk. Vessel Engineer, crew, all on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Only the Master, or the person the Master names, orders the release. Before release every person is accounted for at the muster by name and the space is confirmed empty. The engine is stopped, fuel is shut off, fans are stopped, and doors, hatches, vents and dampers are closed, because the system works only in a closed space. The release point is outside the space, is marked, is kept clear and carries the operating instructions for that system. Once released the space stays shut. The boundaries are watched and cooled from outside where hoses or buckets allow, and the space is not opened to look, since the vessel carries one charge only. The decision to open is the Master's, taken after the boundaries have cooled and with the advice of the Coastguard or the shore where it can be had, and not while the vessel can instead be towed or reach port closed down. Nobody enters afterwards until the space has been ventilated and the atmosphere tested, as RA 37 Confined Spaces requires. Accidental release is prevented by guarding the release and by telling visitors and contractors where it is.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the type of fixed system on each vessel, its servicing dates and the maker's instructions for release and for opening the space afterwards, and include a walk through of the release in the drills. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Fire in the accommodation, wheelhouse or stores, including while crew are asleep: electrical faults, heaters, chargers and lithium ion batteries for phones, tools and equipment, smoking and drying clothes

Potential consequence. Smoke filling a small space within a few minutes. Sleeping crew overcome before they wake. Escape routes cut off. Loss of the wheelhouse, the radios and the controls. Death.

Persons at risk. Master, crew, passengers and industrial personnel

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Smoke detection covers the sleeping, mess and wheelhouse spaces where the vessel's standard requires it, is tested, and is never covered or disabled. Each cabin and space has its escape route, and its second means of escape where one is fitted, kept clear and able to be opened from inside; hatches are not locked, lashed over or blocked by stores. Portable heaters are used only where the vessel provides them and are never left on in an empty cabin or used to dry clothing. Phones, tablets, tool batteries, torches and other rechargeable equipment are charged only at the points the Master allows, on a hard surface, with the maker's charger, and not in bunks or overnight while everyone is asleep unless the Master has agreed the arrangement. Damaged or swollen batteries are taken out of use and stowed outside the accommodation until landed. Smoking is allowed only where the Master permits. The wiring and the fixed electrical installation are maintained by a competent person and personal mains equipment brought aboard is checked. A watch is kept on a manned vessel so that an alarm is heard and acted on. RA 8 Accommodation and RA 11 Rest Periods at Mooring apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Check each vessel's sleeping spaces for working smoke detection and a usable second means of escape, and set the rule for charging rechargeable equipment on board. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Fire in the galley: hot oil or fat, an unattended cooker, a gas leak where gas is used for cooking, and cloths or packaging left near heat

Potential consequence. Burns and scalds. Fire spreading through the accommodation. Gas explosion where gas is fitted.

Persons at risk. Crew preparing food, all on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Cooking is not left unattended and the cooker is turned off at the end of each use and before the vessel gets under way in heavy weather. Deep frying in open pans is not carried out at sea. Pan guards or fiddles are fitted and used. Extraction filters and the area behind and under the cooker are kept free of grease. A fire blanket and an extinguisher suited to the cooking are fixed within reach but not over the cooker, and the crew are shown how to smother a pan fire and why water is never used on burning fat. Where gas is used, the installation meets the vessel's standard, the cylinder is shut off at the bottle when the cooker is not in use, the gas detector is tested, and any smell of gas is treated as an emergency with no switches operated. Electrical appliances are those supplied to the vessel. RA 15 Galley applies to the routine work.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm which vessels cook with gas and that each such installation has been examined against the Workboat Code appendix on gas installations, which was not read. Owner: Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Tackling a fire with portable extinguishers, a fire blanket or a hose: the wrong extinguisher, a fire too large, the way out lost, or smoke breathed in

Potential consequence. Smoke inhalation, burns, electric shock from water or foam on live equipment, a person trapped. Death.

Persons at risk. Master, crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Portable extinguishers of the types and numbers the vessel's certificate requires are fixed at the exits from the spaces they protect, are checked each month by the crew and serviced at the interval the maker and the vessel's standard set. Each crew member is shown at the vessel induction where they are and which type is used on which fire. The alarm is raised and the Master told before any attempt is made. A first attack is made only on a small fire at an early stage, by a person with the way out behind them and a second person present, and it stops as soon as the fire does not go out with one extinguisher, the smoke thickens or the heat grows; the person then leaves, closing the door. Nobody goes into a smoke filled space without breathing apparatus. Power to the affected equipment is isolated where that can be done from outside. After any fire, however small, the area is watched for it starting again, and a fire involving a lithium ion battery is watched for longer because it can start again after it appears to be out.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that every skipper and crew member, including the self employed, holds current basic fire training of the kind the vessel's manning standard requires, and record it in the crew management system. Owner: Crewing Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Use of breathing apparatus to enter smoke for search, rescue or firefighting, if breathing apparatus is carried (to confirm)

Potential consequence. A wearer who is untrained or out of practice becomes lost, runs out of air or collapses from heat. A second casualty and no team left to recover them. Death.

Persons at risk. Crew nominated to wear breathing apparatus

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. It is first established whether any vessel carries breathing apparatus and whether its standard requires it. Where none is carried, the rule on every vessel is that no person enters a smoke filled or burning space, and the response is to shut the space down from outside. Where sets are carried, they are worn only by crew who have been trained to use them, are medically fit to do so and have practised with the vessel's own sets in drills. A wearer never goes in alone: two go in together with a line or hose, a third person stays at the entrance with a means of communication, and the time in is controlled against the air in the cylinder with a margin to get out. On a vessel whose crew is too small to provide that team, the sets are used for escape and for the rescue of a person known to be just inside a space, not for searching or for fighting a fire inside. Sets and spare cylinders are checked each month, kept charged, and serviced at the maker's interval. Emergency escape breathing devices, where carried, are for getting out only and are never used to go in.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish which vessels carry breathing apparatus or emergency escape breathing devices, who on each crew is trained and fit to wear them, and whether the crew numbers allow a safe team. Decide and write down for each vessel what the sets are to be used for. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with the Crewing Manager, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. The muster and the drills: a crew that has not practised together, changing self employed crew, and passengers, divers or client personnel who do not know what to do

Potential consequence. Confusion and delay in the first minutes of a fire. A person missed at the head count. Equipment that nobody can work. An emergency that could have been controlled becomes an abandonment. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Each vessel has a muster list or emergency card that gives every person on board a duty for fire and for abandonment, written for the number of crew the vessel actually sails with. Every person joining, including a relief skipper or crew member for a single trip, is given the vessel induction before sailing: the alarms, the muster point, their duty, the escape routes, the lifejacket and immersion suit, the liferaft and how the distress alert is sent. Passengers, industrial personnel, divers and client staff are given a safety briefing before departure and are counted on and off. Fire and abandonment drills are held at the interval the vessel's safety management system sets and whenever a significant part of the crew has changed, are made realistic by varying the scene and the time, and include the fixed system walk through, shutting the space down, preparing the liferaft, donning immersion suits and sending a simulated distress alert. Each drill is recorded with what went wrong, and the faults are put right. A head count by name is the first act at every muster.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the drill intervals that apply to the fleet, check that each vessel has a current muster list and drill record, and review a sample of drill records for crews with frequent changes. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. A distress alert that is not sent, is sent too late or is not received: the Master waits to see whether the fire can be controlled, power or the wheelhouse is lost, or the vessel is beyond the range of the equipment it has

Potential consequence. Nobody knows the vessel is in trouble or where it is. Rescue starts hours late or not at all. Death of all on board.

Persons at risk. All on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The alert is sent early. On any fire that is not out at once, any flooding not under control or any doubt, the Master makes a distress or urgency call by the vessel's digital selective calling radio and by voice, giving the position, the number on board and the nature of the emergency, and it can be cancelled or downgraded later. Every crew member is shown how to send the alert, because the Master may be the casualty. The radio installation suits the sea area in which the vessel is working, which for work far offshore means a means of alerting that does not depend on being within range of a coast station. The emergency position indicating radio beacon is registered, in date, stowed so that it floats free or can be taken into the raft, and is activated on abandonment. A hand held radio and any other locating devices the vessel carries are kept charged and go into the liferaft. The radios have the emergency power supply the vessel's standard requires and it is tested. A passage plan and reporting schedule are left with the shore so that an overdue vessel is noticed.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. For each vessel, check the radio installation, the beacon registration and the battery and release dates against the area in which it is actually working, and confirm the shore side arrangement for an overdue vessel. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Abandoning to a liferaft: the raft does not launch or inflate, is launched too early or too late, drifts away, or people are hurt or lost in getting into it

Potential consequence. People in the water with no raft. Injury from jumping. Separation of the crew. Death by drowning or cold.

Persons at risk. All on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The decision to abandon is the Master's and is taken as a last resort, since the vessel is the best shelter while it floats and is not burning under the crew; but the raft is made ready early, as soon as a fire is not controlled, so that the decision is not taken too late. The raft is of a capacity for everyone on board, in date for service, with its release unit in date, stowed clear so that it can be launched by hand and will float free, and its painter is made fast to the vessel at the weak link. To launch, the painter is checked, the raft is put over the lee side clear of the fire, and the painter is pulled out fully to inflate it. People board dry where they can, from the deck or a ladder, without jumping onto the canopy. Before leaving, everyone puts on warm clothing, an immersion suit or thermal protective aid where carried, and a lifejacket; the grab bag, the beacon, the hand held radio, flares, water and the first aid kit go into the raft. Once aboard, the painter is cut, the raft is cleared from the vessel, the drogue is streamed, the canopy is closed, the floor is inflated and a head count is taken. Rafts are kept together.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Check the liferaft capacity, service date, release unit date and stowage on each vessel against the largest number it carries, including divers and client personnel, and include a liferaft launch talk through in the drills. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Entering cold water during a fire or abandonment, around the Scottish coast and offshore, where the sea is cold in every season

Potential consequence. Cold water shock and drowning in the first minutes. Loss of the use of the hands and of the ability to swim. Hypothermia. Collapse during or just after rescue. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The aim is that nobody enters the water: abandonment is to the raft, dry, wherever that can be done. A lifejacket with a light, a whistle and, where fitted, a spray hood and a crotch strap is available for every person, is sized and adjusted at the induction, and is worn on deck as RA 18 Working on or Near Water requires, so that a person forced over the side by fire is already wearing one. Immersion suits or thermal protective aids are carried as the vessel's standard requires for its area of operation, each person is shown how to put one on and has practised it, and they are stowed where they can be reached if the accommodation is on fire. A person who must enter the water does so as low and as slowly as the situation allows, keeps the lifejacket fastened, floats and gets their breathing under control before trying to swim, keeps clothing on, and stays with others and with the raft. A person recovered from the water is handled gently, kept flat, sheltered and warmed slowly, and medical advice is sought by radio. Working in Cold Conditions applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm what immersion suits or thermal protective aids each vessel carries for the area in which it works, whether there is one in the right size for every person carried, and that donning is practised and recorded. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Working far offshore with a small crew: up to 150 miles from land, hours from a lifeboat or helicopter, with no second team, and with the crew already tired

Potential consequence. A fire that a larger crew could contain is lost. One injured person removes a large part of the vessel's capability. Casualties with burns and smoke inhalation wait a long time for treatment. Survivors spend many hours in a raft. Death.

Persons at risk. All on board

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The response is planned for the crew the vessel actually has, not for a larger one. Each vessel's emergency card puts the actions in order of what matters most with few hands: raise the alarm and account for everyone, send the alert, shut the space down and use the fixed system, prepare to abandon. The crew do not attempt to fight a developed fire inside a space. Manning for a job far offshore is decided with the distance and the likely time to rescue in mind and is not reduced below the vessel's safe manning without the agreement of the Designated Person Ashore. Before a job beyond the vessel's usual area, the Master and the Operations Manager check the area of operation on the certificate, the lifesaving and radio equipment against that area, the first aid and medical stores and the training of those who will use them, the nearest rescue resources, and whether another vessel or the client's installation can give help. Hours of rest are kept so that the crew can respond. The shore holds an emergency contact list and a person who can be reached at any hour.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set a check for jobs beyond a stated distance offshore, covering certificate area, manning, lifesaving, radio and medical equipment and rescue cover, and confirm the shore side emergency contact arrangement out of hours. Owner: Operations Manager with the Designated Person Ashore and the Crewing Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. Fire and lifesaving equipment that is out of date, defective, missing, blocked by stores or not suited to the work the vessel is now doing

Potential consequence. The equipment fails when it is needed. A controllable emergency becomes fatal. The vessel is detained or its certificate is put in question.

Persons at risk. All on board, the Company

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Each vessel has a list of its fire and lifesaving equipment taken from its certificate, with the service, test and expiry date of each item, held on board and ashore. The crew carry out a monthly check against the list: extinguishers in place and charged, detectors and alarms tested, the fixed system release sealed and its cylinder in date, fire flaps, dampers and fuel shut offs worked, escape hatches opened, the liferaft and its release in date, lifejackets, lights and immersion suits inspected, flares and the beacon in date. Servicing of liferafts, release units, inflatable lifejackets, extinguishers and fixed systems is done by an approved service station at the maker's interval and is planned ahead so that a vessel is never at sea with an item out of date. A defect found is reported to the Designated Person Ashore the same day, and the Master does not sail with a critical item missing or out of date. Deck cargo, dive equipment and stores are never stowed so as to block a raft, a release, an extinguisher or an escape. The third party certifying body examines the equipment at the vessel's examinations.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Compile the fire and lifesaving equipment list and date record for each vessel, check it against the Workboat Code Edition 3 requirements before the transition deadline of 13 December 2026, and set up the monthly check as a controlled form. Owner: Designated Person Ashore, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The monthly check of fire and lifesaving equipment on each vessel is recorded and returned to the Designated Person Ashore, who follows up any missing return.
  • Drill records are returned after each drill and reviewed each quarter for the interval kept, the people who took part and the faults found and closed.
  • Service and expiry dates for liferafts, release units, lifejackets, extinguishers, fixed systems, flares and beacons are held on one register and reviewed monthly, with each item booked before it falls due.
  • Induction and passenger briefing records are sampled at the internal audit of each vessel, with particular attention to relief and self employed crew.
  • Crew fire, sea survival and first aid training is held in the crew management system and checked by the Crewing Manager before a person is assigned to a vessel.
  • Every fire, false alarm, release of a fixed system, failed item of emergency equipment and missed drill is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.
  • This assessment is reviewed after any fire or abandonment in the fleet, after any change to a vessel's area of operation or equipment, and at least once a year.

Emergency arrangements

  • Any person finding a fire raises the alarm and tells the Master at once, before attempting to put it out.
  • The Master musters the crew, takes a head count by name, and sends a distress or urgency call with the position, the number on board and the nature of the emergency.
  • Machinery space fire: do not enter. Stop the engine where the situation allows, shut off fuel, stop fans, close every opening, confirm the space is empty, release the fixed system and keep the space shut.
  • Accommodation or galley fire: get everyone out, close doors behind you, isolate power or gas from outside, and attack only a small fire with the way out behind you.
  • Prepare to abandon early: lifejackets and immersion suits on, the raft made ready on the lee side, the grab bag, beacon, hand held radio and flares to hand. Abandon on the Master's order only.
  • In the raft: cut the painter, get clear, stream the drogue, close the canopy, take a head count, activate the beacon, treat the injured and keep watch.
  • The Master tells the Designated Person Ashore as soon as the situation allows, and the shore tells the Managing Director and supports the Coastguard with the crew list and the vessel details.

PPE requirements

A lifejacket for every person on board, with light and whistle, worn on deck and at the muster. Immersion suits or thermal protective aids as the vessel's standard requires for its area of operation, in sizes for those carried. Warm clothing, hat and gloves put on before abandonment. For a first attack on a small fire, the working clothing already worn, covering the arms and legs, with gloves and eye protection where they are at hand; ordinary working clothing gives no protection inside a burning or smoke filled space and nobody enters one in it. Fire protective clothing and breathing apparatus only where the vessel carries them and only for trained wearers, to confirm for each vessel. Emergency escape breathing devices, where carried, for escape only.
