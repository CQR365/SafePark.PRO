Risk assessments / Pontoons, berths and shore power

RISK ASSESSMENT

Pontoons, berths and shore power

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionUse, inspection and upkeep of the marina pontoons, finger berths and access bridges, berthing and attending to boats afloat, provision and use of shore power at the pedestals, and attendance at the water's edge by yard and office personnel, first aiders, customers, contractors and visitors
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeThe floating pontoons, finger berths, access bridges and their landings, the quay and shore edge that serve them, the shore power pedestals and the supply to them, and the water within the berths at the Silvers Marine marina at Silverhills. Work near water in general is assessed in RA 18 Working on or Near Water, electrical work in Electrical work and isolation and first aid in RA 56 First Aid; this assessment covers what is particular to a marina open to customers and the public. Boarding the Company's own vessels is assessed in RA 1 Boarding at Pier and RA 59 Access and Egress.
Main equipment usedFloating pontoons, fingers, piles or anchor chains, access bridges and landings, cleats, fendering, safety ladders, lifebuoys and throwing lines, pontoon lighting, shore power pedestals with sockets and protective devices, customers' shore power leads, water points and hoses, berthing trolleys, the yard workboat, lifejackets and radios or telephones. The layout, number of berths, tidal range, and the number and position of ladders, lifebuoys and pedestals are not known to the assessor and are listed under matters to confirm.
Persons at riskYard and office personnel, first aiders and fire wardens, berth holders and their families and guests, visiting crews, customers' contractors, electricians and other maintenance contractors, divers working in the marina, delivery drivers, members of the public, and children.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. No draft marina operating procedure has been seen. Electrical work on the pontoon supply is done only under the isolation arrangements in Electrical work and isolation. PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness and PRC 19 Induction apply.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Electrical safetyElectricity at Work Regulations 1989, regulation 4. All systems must at all times be of such construction as to prevent danger so far as is reasonably practicable, must be maintained so as to prevent danger, and every work activity on or near a system must be carried out so as not to give rise to danger. The pontoon supply and the pedestals are a system within this.
Electrical installation standardTo confirm. BS 7671, Requirements for Electrical Installations, section 709, Marinas and similar locations, is understood to be the installation standard for the pontoon supply and pedestals. It is not free to read and was not read, so nothing in this assessment is stated as a requirement of it.
Occupier, ScotlandOccupiers' Liability (Scotland) Act 1960, section 2. The occupier owes a person entering the premises such care as is reasonable in all the circumstances to see that the person does not suffer injury from dangers due to the state of the premises or to anything done or omitted on them. Berth holders, guests, children and the public on the pontoons are within this duty.
Benchmark for water edgesSafety in docks, Approved Code of Practice and guidance, L148. It sets out provision at water edges in docks: lifesaving equipment that is conspicuous and maintained, ladders reaching to 1 metre below the water line at any foreseeable state of the tide, handholds, lighting, and lifejackets for work within 1 metre of an unprotected edge. Whether a leisure marina is dock premises within the code has not been established, so it is used here as a benchmark and not as a requirement.
Work at height and work equipmentWork at Height Regulations 2005, regulation 6, requires suitable and sufficient measures to prevent a fall liable to cause personal injury, which the docks code applies to falls into water. Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998, as described in HSE guidance, requires equipment provided for work to be suitable, maintained and inspected.
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, cited as the general framework for assessment and for the protection of people who are not employees. The text was not read for this assessment.

Cited as the regimes for first aid provision and for fire safety at the premises. The text was not read for this assessment, and how far the fire regime reaches onto the pontoons and berthed boats is to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. Falling into the water from a pontoon, finger berth, quay edge or when stepping between a boat and the pontoon

Potential consequence. Cold water shock, inability to swim in clothing, drowning. Head injury on the way in. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard and office personnel, berth holders and guests, contractors, the public

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Pontoons and fingers are wide and stable enough for their use, are kept level and afloat on sound buoyancy, and have a surface that grips when wet. Edges are kept free of trip points and are marked where the light is poor. Yard and office personnel wear a lifejacket whenever they are on the pontoons, the bridges or within 1 metre of an unprotected water edge, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water, and this applies to short visits as much as to long tasks. Customers are strongly advised in the berthing terms and on signs to wear lifejackets on the pontoons, and children and those who cannot swim are asked always to do so. Boats are boarded from the finger at the boarding point with hands free, not by jumping or stepping across a gap, and gear is passed across separately. Trolleys are provided for loads so that people are not carrying heavy or awkward items along the pontoons. Running is discouraged. Lighting is provided along the walkways and at the bridge.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Survey the pontoons, fingers and edges for width, freeboard, stability, surface and lighting, and write the lifejacket rule for all Company personnel on the pontoons. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. A person in the water unable to get out or be recovered: too few ladders, ladders that do not reach below the surface or cannot be found, no lifebuoy within reach, high freeboard, and nobody aware

Potential consequence. Exhaustion and drowning within minutes in cold water, including of a would be rescuer who jumps in. Death.

Persons at risk. Any person who falls in, would be rescuers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Safety ladders are fitted along the pontoons at a spacing set by assessment, with the docks code used as the benchmark, so that a person in the water is never far from one. Each ladder extends well below the surface so that a cold and tired person can get a foot on it, is kept clear of moored boats and fendering, and is marked so that it can be seen from the water and from the pontoon by day and night. Lifebuoys with buoyant lines, or throwing lines, are provided at marked points at intervals also set by assessment, are conspicuous, and are checked weekly and after any report of loss or damage. Signs at the bridge show where the ladders and lifebuoys are and how to call for help. Yard personnel are trained in the sequence of shout, reach, throw and call for help, and in recovering a casualty from the pontoon without entering the water, and they practise it. The yard workboat is available as a recovery platform where one is kept afloat. Missing or damaged lifesaving equipment is replaced the same day.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Count and plot every ladder and lifebuoy against the pontoon layout, compare the result with the docks code benchmark and any trade body standard, and make good the gaps. Set up the weekly check record. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager, verified by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Slips and trips on the pontoons and fingers: algae, bird fouling, frost and ice, wet decking, mooring lines, hoses, shore power leads, cleats, damaged or lifting deck boards and gear left on the walkway

Potential consequence. Fracture, head injury, and a fall into the water as a result of the slip.

Persons at risk. All persons on the pontoons

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The pontoons are cleaned of algae and fouling on a set routine through the year and are treated in frost, with a treatment that is not harmful to the water. Deck boards, fixings, non slip surfaces and edge strips are covered by the routine pontoon inspection and defects are repaired or closed off the same day. Berth holders are told in the berthing terms to keep lines, hoses and leads tidy, to run leads and hoses along the edge and not across the walkway, and not to leave tenders, bicycles, gear or rubbish on the pontoons or fingers. Yard personnel remove obstructions when they see them. Hose reels and lead hooks are provided at the pedestals where practical. Footwear with a good grip is worn by Company personnel, and customers are advised to do the same. In snow and ice the pontoons are closed to all but essential access until they have been treated. RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the cleaning and winter treatment routine and the pontoon inspection checklist, and issue them as controlled forms. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Access bridges between shore and pontoons: steep at low water, slippery, moving at the hinge and rollers, narrow for trolleys, and with pinch points at the landing

Potential consequence. Fall on the bridge, runaway trolley, fingers or feet trapped at the roller end, fall into the water at the landing. Fracture, crush injury.

Persons at risk. All persons using the bridge, particularly those with loads, the elderly and children

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Each bridge has handrails on both sides with intermediate rails or infill, a surface with cleats or grip suited to its steepest angle, and a cover or guard over the gap and rollers at the pontoon end. The state of the bridge at low water springs is assessed, and where the slope is steep a sign warns users and advises against taking loaded trolleys down at low water. Trolleys have working brakes or are used with two people on a steep bridge. Hinges, rollers, landing plates and handrails are covered by the routine inspection and are maintained. The bridge and its landings are lit. The bridge head on shore is kept clear of vehicles and stored gear. People with limited mobility are offered help or advised of the best state of tide. The bridge is closed if it is damaged or iced until it has been made safe. RA 59 Access and Egress applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Measure the bridge slope at the lowest tides, check the handrails, surface and roller guards, and fit warning signs where needed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Company personnel working alone on the pontoons, or after dark, in poor weather or out of hours, including checks on boats, lines and pedestals and attendance at alarms

Potential consequence. A fall into the water or a collapse that nobody sees. Drowning. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard and office personnel, security or on call personnel

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Routine work on the pontoons is planned for daylight and for times when a second person is on site. Work from the pontoon edge, over the water or from the workboat is not done alone. Where a lone visit to the pontoons cannot be avoided, the person wears a lifejacket fitted with a light, carries a waterproof means of communication, tells a named person before going onto the pontoons and again on return, and a time is agreed after which that person raises the alarm. Out of hours attendance, for example to a boat reported adrift or sinking in a gale, is made by two people, and where two are not available the attendance is limited to what can be done from the shore. The pontoon lighting is maintained and a torch is carried after dark. The limits on what one person may do are written down and known to those on call. RA 40 Lone Worker applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish who attends the marina out of hours and on what arrangement, and write the lone working limits and the check in routine. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Shore power pedestals and customers' leads: damaged pedestals and sockets, water in enclosures, failed or absent protective devices, leads that are damaged, undersized, joined, coiled or trailing in the water, and overloading

Potential consequence. Electric shock to a person on the pontoon or aboard, fire at a pedestal or in a boat. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, berth holders and guests, contractors, electricians

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The pontoon electrical installation is designed, installed and altered only by a competent electrical contractor to the installation standard for marinas, to confirm, and is inspected and tested at the interval the contractor recommends, with the report held and its recommendations acted on. Each socket is protected by its own residual current device, and those devices are tested at the interval the installer sets and the tests recorded. Pedestals are included in the routine pontoon inspection, which looks for impact damage, broken covers and sockets, signs of water or overheating and tampering; a damaged pedestal is isolated and locked off the same day. Yard personnel do not open pedestals or reset anything beyond the customer facing controls unless they are authorised under Electrical work and isolation. The berthing terms require customers to use one continuous lead of the correct rating and connector type for each boat, fully uncoiled, in good condition, kept out of the water and off the walkway, connected at the boat first and the pedestal last, and unplugged at the pedestal before it is disconnected from the boat. Leads found damaged or lying in the water are unplugged by yard personnel and the owner is told.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Obtain the latest inspection and test report for the pontoon installation, confirm the standard it was installed to and the test interval, set up the pedestal check and residual current device test records, and write the lead rules into the berthing terms. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Electric current leaking into the water from a faulty boat, lead or pedestal, affecting a person who has fallen in, a swimmer, a diver or a rescuer

Potential consequence. Paralysis of the muscles and drowning, with no warning to those on the pontoon. Death, and a second casualty if a rescuer enters the water.

Persons at risk. Any person in the water in the marina, divers, rescuers

Initial rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Control measures. Swimming in the marina is prohibited and signed. Nobody enters the water to recover a casualty, to free a rope or to work on a hull. Recovery of a person from the water is from the pontoon or the workboat, and where an electrical fault is suspected the supply to that pontoon is switched off at the isolator before the casualty is touched, where that can be done at once. The location of the pontoon isolators is known to all yard personnel and shown on a plan at the yard office. Any diving in the marina is carried out only by the Company's diving team or an approved diving contractor under RA 41 Dive Operations, with the shore power to the pontoons concerned isolated and locked off before the diver enters the water, boats nearby prevented from starting engines, and the isolation recorded on the dive plan. The protective devices at the pedestals are maintained and tested as set out above. A report of tingling felt by anyone in contact with the water, a boat or a ladder is treated as an electrical fault: the supply is isolated and an electrician called.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Mark the pontoon isolators on a plan, brief yard personnel and the diving team, and add the shore power isolation step to the plan for any dive in the marina. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Diving Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Inspection and maintenance of the pontoons, piles, chains, bridges and services: work from the edge and from the workboat, lifting deck panels, handling chains and floats, and structural failure after storm damage

Potential consequence. Fall into the water, crush between pontoon sections or between workboat and pontoon, a section breaking away with people or boats on it. Death.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel, maintenance contractors, berth holders

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The pontoons, connections, pile guides or chains, anchors, bridges and services are inspected to a written schedule by a nominated person, with a fuller periodic survey by a competent person at an interval to be set, and after any gale or impact. Defects are recorded, given an owner and a date, and a section that is unsafe is closed with barriers until repaired. Maintenance is planned, done by at least two people wearing lifejackets, in daylight and in settled weather, with lifting of heavy panels, floats and chains by mechanical means under RA 21a Shore Lifting or from the workboat under RA 14 Operations from Small Boat. Openings left by lifted panels are guarded. Hands and feet are kept out of the joints between sections, which move with the swell. Underwater inspection and work on chains and anchors is diving work under RA 41 Dive Operations. Contractors are approved and inducted under PRC 19. Before a forecast gale, moorings and connections are checked and customers are asked to double their lines.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the pontoon inspection schedule, establish when the structure, moorings and bridges were last surveyed by a competent person and arrange a survey if no record exists. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Giving first aid on a pontoon, finger or quay edge: the first aider or casualty falling in, a casualty recovered from cold water, and moving a casualty along narrow pontoons and up a steep bridge

Potential consequence. A second casualty in the water. Delay in treatment. Collapse of a casualty after rescue from cold water. Death.

Persons at risk. First aiders, casualties, helpers, ambulance crews

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. First aiders put on a lifejacket before going onto the pontoons, as every member of Company personnel does, and lifejackets are kept at the bridge head for that purpose. The first aider's first act is to make the scene safe: move the casualty away from the edge where the injury allows, ask helpers to stand on the water side, and stop boat movements alongside. A casualty is treated where found if moving them would risk a fall into the water, and help is called early. First aid kits, a means of keeping a casualty warm and a defibrillator, where one is held, are located so that they can be brought to the pontoons quickly, and their location is signed. First aiders are briefed on cold water immersion: a person recovered from the water is kept lying flat, handled gently, protected from wind and assessed by the ambulance service even if they appear to have recovered. The route for carrying a casualty ashore is planned for each pontoon, including the bridge at low water, and the ambulance service is told at the time of the call that the casualty is on a pontoon so that suitable help is sent. RA 56 First Aid applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Include the pontoons in the first aid needs assessment, decide where kits, blankets and any defibrillator are kept, and run an exercise for moving a casualty ashore at low water. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Children, visitors and members of the public on the pontoons without supervision or knowledge of the hazards, including out of hours and after drinking

Potential consequence. A child or adult falls in unseen. Drowning. Death.

Persons at risk. Children, guests, the public, intruders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Access to the pontoons is limited to berth holders, their guests, visiting crews and people with business there, by a gate at the bridge head where one is fitted, and by signs. The berthing terms and signs at the bridge state that children are supervised by an adult at all times on the pontoons, that children and those who cannot swim wear lifejackets, that there is no swimming, fishing from the walkways, running or cycling, and where the lifesaving equipment is. Visitors who come to the office are escorted or directed, and contractors are inducted. Lifejackets for visitors escorted by Company personnel are kept at the office. Yard personnel speak to anyone seen behaving unsafely, including adults who appear to be affected by drink, and unaccompanied children are taken back to shore. Lighting, lifebuoys and ladders are provided for the hours of darkness as well as the working day, since many falls happen when returning to a boat at night. Site security and unauthorised access applies to people who have no business on the pontoons.

Residual rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Further action required. Decide how access to the pontoons is to be controlled, review the signs at each bridge head and write the rules for children and visitors into the berthing terms. The residual rating stays high because the control depends on the conduct of customers and the public, and it is to be reviewed once access control and lifesaving provision have been confirmed. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Boats manoeuvring in the berths and mooring lines under load: a person caught between a boat and the pontoon, struck by a parting line, or pulled in while taking a line

Potential consequence. Crushed hand, arm or leg, amputation of fingers at a cleat, fall into the water between boat and pontoon. Death in the worst case.

Persons at risk. Yard personnel helping to berth boats, berth holders and crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Yard personnel who help a boat to berth take lines from the pontoon and make them fast to a cleat; they do not fend off a moving boat with hands, feet or body and do not step between the boat and the pontoon. Lines are surged round a cleat and never held with a turn round the hand. Nobody stands in the bight of a line or in line with a rope under strain. Fenders are used in place of people. Boats moved by yard personnel are handled only by those the Silvers Yard Manager has authorised as competent, with enough hands and in conditions within the yard's limits, and moves are put off in strong wind. A speed limit applies in the marina and is signed. Cleats, fairleads and fendering are covered by the pontoon inspection. A person in the water near a manoeuvring boat is the first priority: engines are put out of gear at once.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Establish which yard personnel move customers' boats and how their competence is recorded, and set the weather limits for doing so. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

12. Fire on a berthed boat or at a pedestal spreading along the pontoon, with gas cylinders and fuel aboard and the bridge as the only way off

Potential consequence. People cut off on the pontoon by fire between them and the shore, forced into the water. Burns, smoke inhalation, drowning. Death, multiple casualties.

Persons at risk. Berth holders and guests, people living or sleeping aboard, yard personnel, firefighters

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The premises fire risk assessment covers the pontoons, the pedestals and the boats berthed there, including the means of raising the alarm, the routes ashore from each pontoon and what people at the far end are to do if the route is blocked. Fire extinguishers are provided at marked points along the pontoons and are inspected. The yard knows which boats have people sleeping aboard. The berthing terms deal with gas shut off when the boat is left, the refuelling rules, barbecues, unattended heaters and the charging of batteries, and Bulk LPG storage and use applies to gas on customers' boats. Refuelling of petrol from cans on the berths is controlled. Yard personnel raise the alarm, call the Fire and Rescue Service, help people ashore and, where it can be done without risk, isolate the shore power and move neighbouring boats; they do not board a burning boat. Access for the fire service to the bridge head is kept clear. Fire and emergency evacuation applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm that the premises fire risk assessment covers the pontoons and berthed boats and that the fire service has been consulted on access and on a fire at the far end of a pontoon. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The weekly check of lifebuoys, throwing lines, ladders, lighting and pedestals is recorded, and the records are reviewed each month by the Silvers Yard Manager.
  • The pontoon inspection schedule and defect log are reviewed monthly, and any defect open beyond its date is raised at the monthly management meeting.
  • The inspection and test report for the pontoon electrical installation and the residual current device test records are tracked by the Risk and Compliance Manager against their due dates.
  • Every fall into the water, near miss at the edge, electric shock or tingle, tripped supply that recurs, damaged pedestal and fire is reported under PRC 01, whether it involved Company personnel or a customer, and is reviewed against this assessment.
  • The Risk and Compliance Manager walks the pontoons at least twice a year, once at low water springs and once after dark, and records what is seen.
  • Person in the water and casualty recovery drills are held at least annually and the lessons recorded.
  • The wearing of lifejackets by Company personnel on the pontoons is checked by observation and breaches are dealt with by the department manager.

Emergency arrangements

  • Person in the water: shout for help, keep the person in sight, throw a lifebuoy or line, direct them to the nearest ladder, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard. Do not enter the water. Stop boat engines nearby.
  • Person in the water where an electrical fault is suspected, or anyone reporting a tingle from the water: switch off the supply to that pontoon at the isolator, keep everyone out of the water, recover the casualty with a pole or line from the pontoon, and call 999.
  • Casualty recovered from the water: keep them lying flat, handle gently, remove wet clothing if dry cover is to hand, insulate from wind and cold, begin resuscitation if they are not breathing, and hand over to the ambulance service even if they appear to have recovered.
  • Electric shock on the pontoon: do not touch the casualty until the supply is off; isolate at the pedestal or the pontoon isolator, then give first aid and call 999.
  • Fire on a boat or at a pedestal: raise the alarm, call the Fire and Rescue Service, get people off the pontoon by the route away from the fire, isolate the shore power if it can be done safely, and do not board the boat. People cut off at the end of a pontoon are taken off by boat where one can reach them safely.
  • Injury on a pontoon: the first aider puts on a lifejacket, makes the scene safe from the edge, treats the casualty where found if moving them is unsafe, and tells the ambulance service that the casualty is on a pontoon and the state of the bridge.
  • Pontoon section, pile or bridge damaged or adrift: close the section, move people ashore, tell the Silvers Yard Manager, and warn boats in the marina. The Risk and Compliance Manager and the Managing Director are told the same day of any of the above.

PPE requirements

A lifejacket of a type suited to the wearer and the work, serviced and checked before use, for all Company personnel on the pontoons, bridges and within 1 metre of an unprotected water edge, with a light for work after dark or alone. Footwear with slip resistant soles. High visibility and waterproof clothing suited to the weather. Gloves for handling lines, chains and deck panels. A torch and a waterproof means of communication for lone or night visits. Electrical protective equipment is for authorised electrical personnel only, under Electrical work and isolation.
