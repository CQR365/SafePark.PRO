Risk assessments / Shore staff boarding and inspecting vessels

RISK ASSESSMENT

Shore staff boarding and inspecting vessels

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionManagers, the Designated Person Ashore and deputy, surveyors, auditors and other shore staff going aboard Company vessels that are alongside, laid up, on the slip or ashore under maintenance, in order to inspect, audit, survey or attend repair work. It covers getting aboard and ashore, moving about the decks, entering machinery spaces, being near the work of others, and the inspection of spaces that may be enclosed spaces.
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Operations Manager and Designated Person Ashore
Location and scopeCompany vessels berthed at Silverhills, at the Silvers marina and yard, at other ports and client berths, and in a third party yard or dry dock, whether in service, laid up or under repair. It covers the visit of a person who is not part of the crew. It does not cover the crew's own work, which stays under the vessel assessments, and it does not cover boarding a vessel at sea, which is assessed in RA 7 Personnel Transfer.
Main equipment usedGangways, boarding ladders and steps, yard access towers and ladders to vessels ashore, torches and portable lighting, a personal gas monitor where one is issued, a means of communication, inspection tools such as a hammer, scraper, camera and measuring equipment, and the personal protective equipment listed below.
Persons at riskThe Operations Manager and assistant, the Designated Person Ashore and deputy, the Risk and Compliance Manager when auditing, other managers and office staff who go aboard, surveyors and examiners from the third party certifying body, client and insurer representatives escorted by the Company, and crew or contractors affected by what a visitor does on board.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. The vessel's own permits to work, where the vessel operates them, apply to the visitor in the same way as to the crew, including any permit for hot work and for entry into an enclosed space.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997, enforced by the MCA. As made, regulation 5 places the general duty on the employer to ensure the health and safety of workers so far as is reasonably practicable, regulation 7 requires a suitable and sufficient risk assessment, and regulation 13 requires consultation and coordination where workers of another employer are on board. How the current text applies to a shore manager who is aboard only for a visit is to be confirmed.
Marine, enclosed spacesMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Entry into Enclosed Spaces) Regulations 2022. An enclosed space is one not designed for continuous worker occupancy that has limited openings for entry and exit, inadequate ventilation, or both. Regulation 7 requires a system of work and risk assessment for entry, makes the Master responsible for seeing that it is observed, and prohibits entry by any person not authorised under it. That prohibition applies to a visiting inspector as it does to the crew. Which of regulations 6, 8 and 9 apply to the Company's vessels is to be confirmed, since small vessel classes are excluded from some of them.
Marine, code of practiceCode of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers, 2026 edition (MCA). Chapter 1 states that the Company must protect the health and safety of seafarers and others so far as is reasonably practicable and that the Master represents the Company on board. Chapter 2 sets out the safety induction that a person new to a vessel is given, including the alarm signals and assembly stations. Chapters 11, 14, 15, 20, 22 and 24 on movement on board, permits to work, enclosed spaces, machinery, boarding arrangements and hot work were not read and are to be confirmed.
Marine, vessel standardThe Workboat Code Edition 3 (MCA), given effect by the Merchant Shipping (Small Workboats and Pilot Boats) Regulations 2023. Paragraphs 1.25 to 1.27 state that the owner and the Master are responsible for the health and safety of those working on the vessel and that the 1997 Regulations and the Code of Safe Working Practices apply where persons are employed aboard.
Marine, accessMGN 591 (M+F) Provision of safe means of access to fishing vessels and small vessels in ports (MCA). Amendment 1 was read; it has been withdrawn and replaced by Amendment 2, which was not read. It places primary responsibility for safe access on whoever provides the means of access, the vessel or the berth operator, and ranks a gangway above stepping across, a fixed ladder and a portable ladder.
General duties ashoreHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 apply to the Company as the employer of the shore staff and as the occupier of the Silverhills berths and yard. Where the line falls between these and the marine regulations for a vessel alongside or ashore in the yard, and so between HSE and the MCA, is to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. A shore visitor going aboard unannounced or without a briefing: nobody on board knows the person is there, the visitor does not know what work is in progress, and it is not clear whose authority applies

Potential consequence. The visitor walks into live work, an isolated system or an open space. Nobody looks for the visitor in an emergency. A manager's seniority ashore is taken as permission to override the vessel's controls. Death or serious injury.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, crew, contractors on board

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. A visit is arranged in advance with the Master, or with the person in charge of the vessel where there is no Master aboard, so that the purpose, the spaces to be seen and the time are known and the visit can be fitted around the work. On arrival the visitor reports to that person before going anywhere else, signs the vessel's visitor record and signs out on leaving. The visitor is given the vessel's safety induction at the level the visit needs: the alarm signals, the assembly station, the ways off the vessel, what work is in progress and which spaces are out of bounds. Every person on board, whatever their post ashore, is under the authority of the Master in matters of safety, complies with the vessel's rules and permits, and may be refused access to a space or stopped by the Master or the person in charge. An auditor or inspector who needs to see a space or a system asks for it to be shown and does not help themselves. A visitor who is not familiar with the vessel is accompanied by a member of the crew or the Vessel Engineer.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the short rule for shore staff boarding Company vessels, covering notice, reporting, the visitor record, induction and the Master's authority, and brief every post that goes aboard. Confirm who is the person in charge of a vessel that has no Master aboard. Owner: Operations Manager with the Designated Person Ashore, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. Getting aboard and ashore: a gangway that is unsecured, too steep or unlit, stepping across a gap, a quay ladder, crossing another vessel, and a berth with a large tidal range

Potential consequence. Fall between the vessel and the quay or pontoon, crushing between hull and wall, drowning, cold water immersion, head injury from a fall onto the deck.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, crew assisting

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The visitor uses only the means of access the vessel or the berth has provided and does not improvise one. The order of preference is a properly rigged and secured gangway with rails on both sides, then a level step across where the vessel is moored hard in and the gap is small, then a fixed quay ladder, with a portable ladder as the last choice. Nobody jumps. Where the means of access is not safe, because of the tide, the vessel's movement, ice, missing rails or poor lighting, the visitor does not board and asks the Master or the person in charge to put it right. Both hands are kept free: bags, tools and equipment are passed across or carried in a rucksack. A lifejacket is worn when boarding, when on a pontoon and on any open deck, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water, and a lifebuoy with a line is to hand at the crossing point. Boarding in darkness is avoided where the visit can be made in daylight. Access across another vessel is used only with the agreement of that vessel. RA 1 Boarding at Pier and RA 59 Access and Egress apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Check the means of access and the lighting at each regular Company berth at Silverhills against RA 1 Boarding at Pier and the MCA guidance on access to small vessels, and record who provides the access at each berth. Owner: Operations Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Boarding and moving around a vessel that is out of the water: on the slip, in the boat hoist or chocked ashore in the yard

Potential consequence. Fall from deck height to hard standing, a ladder slipping, the visitor struck by yard plant or by a load, the vessel shifting on its supports. Death or serious injury.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, yard personnel, contractors

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The visitor reports to the person in charge of the yard as well as to the person in charge of the vessel, and follows the yard rules for visitors. Nobody boards a vessel while it is being lifted, moved or set down, or stands beneath it. Access to the deck is by the stair tower, platform or secured ladder the yard has provided for that vessel, and the visitor does not climb the hull, the supports or a ladder that is not tied or footed. Deck edge protection is checked on stepping aboard, because rails, bulwark doors and stanchions are often removed during repair; where the edge is open the visitor stays back from it or the inspection is put off until protection is in place. The underside of the hull is inspected only with the agreement of the person in charge of the yard, clear of any work overhead, and never by reaching under or between the supports while the vessel is being adjusted. RA 32 Working at Height and RA 28 Ladders apply. In a third party yard the visitor follows that yard's induction and rules.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Agree with the Silvers yard the standard means of access to a Company vessel ashore and the rule for visitors under a suspended or newly landed vessel. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Slips, trips and falls on deck and below: wet or oily plates, mooring ropes, hoses and leads, raised sills, low deckheads, steep ladders, and floor plates, gratings or hatches lifted for maintenance

Potential consequence. Fracture, head injury, fall through an open hatch or into a bilge, fall overboard.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The visitor asks at the briefing which plates, gratings and hatches are open and which decks have been washed or painted. Openings left for maintenance are fenced or guarded by the vessel, and a visitor who finds one unguarded stops and reports it. The inspection is planned as a route so that the visitor is not walking backwards, writing or using a camera or phone while moving. One hand is kept for the vessel on ladders and companionways, which are descended facing the ladder. A torch is carried in every space below deck and in poor light on deck. Slip resistant safety footwear is worn; office shoes are not worn aboard. A safety helmet or bump cap is worn in machinery spaces and wherever there is work overhead. The visitor keeps off mooring lines and out of bights, and stays clear of the deck during mooring, lifting and winch work. RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls and RA 16 Working on Deck apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Issue shore staff who go aboard with footwear, head protection and a torch suited to the work, and record the issue. Owner: Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Machinery in engine rooms and on deck that is running, or that is started, turned or tested while the visitor is beside it, together with hot surfaces and pressurised systems

Potential consequence. Entanglement in shafts, belts and couplings, crushing, burns from exhausts and pipework, injection or scalding from a released system. Amputation or death.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, Vessel Engineer

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The visitor enters a machinery space only with the knowledge of the Vessel Engineer or the Master and is told what is running, what may start automatically and what has been isolated. Where a close inspection of machinery is needed it is done with the machinery stopped and isolated by the vessel, and the visitor sees the isolation before putting a hand, a tool or a torch near moving parts. Guards are not removed for a visitor's convenience while machinery is running. Nothing is started, turned or tested until the Vessel Engineer has confirmed that everyone is clear. Loose clothing, lanyards, scarves and hanging identity cards are removed or tucked in, long hair is tied back and rings are taken off. The visitor does not lean on or step over shafts, pipework or exhausts and treats every surface as hot. A running trial that the visitor has come to witness is watched from a position agreed with the Vessel Engineer. RA 9 Engine Room and RA 19 Plant Maintenance apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how machinery is isolated and proved for an inspection on each vessel, and that the method is written down where a shore visitor can be shown it. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Vessel Engineer. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Noise and heat in machinery spaces with engines, generators or compressors running

Potential consequence. Hearing damage from repeated exposure, warnings and alarms not heard, heat stress during a long inspection in coveralls.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Inspection of a machinery space is done with the machinery stopped wherever the purpose of the visit allows. Where it has to be running, hearing protection is worn before the door is opened and kept on until the visitor is out, and the visitor carries their own rather than relying on the vessel's stock. Time in the space is kept short and the briefing, the questions and the note taking are done outside. Hand signals for stop and leave are agreed with the escort before going in, because speech and alarms may not be heard. In hot conditions the visit is split into shorter spells with water taken between them. RA 55 Noise and Working in Hot Conditions apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Confirm whether noise levels in the machinery spaces of each vessel have been measured and whether shore staff who go aboard often should be included in hearing checks. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. The work of others going on around the visitor during maintenance and survey: hot work, grinding, lifting overhead, painting and solvent use, pressure testing and work on opened systems

Potential consequence. Burns, eye injury, being struck by a load or a dropped object, exposure to fume and vapour, fire started beside the visitor. Death or serious injury.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, crew, contractors

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The visit is timed, where it can be, for a point when the hazardous work is stopped. The visitor is told at the briefing where hot work, lifting, painting and testing are taking place and keeps out of those areas unless the person in charge of that work has agreed and the work has paused. The visitor does not walk under a suspended load, through a hot work area or past a barrier or tag. Where the visit is itself to inspect hot work or a repair in progress, the visitor stands where the person doing the work says, wears eye protection and does not look at an arc. A manager who sees work being done unsafely stops it through the person in charge of the vessel, unless the danger is immediate, in which case anybody may stop it. RA 27 Hot Work, RA 24 Grinding and Cutting, RA 26 Welding and RA 21 Crane Operations On Board apply to the work itself.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how work by different contractors on a vessel under repair is coordinated day to day and who tells a visitor what is in progress. Owner: Operations Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Entering or leaning into a space that may be an enclosed space in order to inspect it: tanks, void spaces, chain lockers, steering compartments, lazarettes and deep bilges

Potential consequence. Collapse from lack of oxygen or from toxic or flammable gas. Death of the visitor and of a person who goes in to help without protection.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, crew, would be rescuers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A shore visitor does not enter, or put their head and shoulders into, any tank, void, locker or other space that is not designed for people to work in continuously, unless the Master has authorised the entry under the vessel's enclosed space system. An inspection is done from outside wherever that will serve, using a torch, a mirror or a camera on a pole. Where entry is needed, RA 37 Confined Spaces applies in full: the space is ventilated, the atmosphere is tested before and during the entry by a competent person with a calibrated instrument, a permit is issued, an attendant stays at the entrance, and the rescue arrangement is in place before anyone goes in. A space is treated as unsafe until it has been tested, even if it has been open for days, and the test is repeated after any break. A personal gas monitor does not replace the test before entry. If a person collapses in a space, nobody goes in after them without breathing apparatus and a second person; the alarm is raised first.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. List the enclosed spaces on each vessel, mark their entrances, and confirm that each vessel has atmosphere testing equipment or a stated source for it. Confirm which shore posts are trained to enter and that the training is recorded. Owner: Designated Person Ashore with each Master, assured by the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Working alone aboard a vessel that is laid up or unmanned: no crew on board, little or no lighting or power, and nobody aware of the visit

Potential consequence. A fall, a collapse or a fall into the water that nobody sees. Long delay before help arrives. Death by drowning or from an untreated injury.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A visit to an unmanned or laid up vessel is made by two people wherever it involves going below deck, into a machinery space, onto a vessel on a mooring, or onto an open deck in darkness or poor weather. Where a short visit to an open deck or wheelhouse is made alone, a named person ashore is told the vessel, the purpose and the time the visitor expects to be off, the visitor carries a charged phone or radio, and the named person acts if the visitor has not reported off by that time. A lone visitor does not enter a machinery space with machinery running, does not enter any enclosed space, does not start machinery and does not work at height or over the side. A lifejacket is worn from the quay or pontoon until back ashore. The visitor takes their own lighting and does not rely on the vessel's power. RA 40 Lone Worker applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the rule for which visits to a laid up vessel may be made alone, name who receives the report off call in and out of office hours, and decide whether a lone worker device is needed. Owner: Operations Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. The visitor operating or disturbing the vessel's systems during an inspection or audit: valves, switches, breakers, hatches and doors, shore power connections, and the controls and releases of fire and lifesaving equipment

Potential consequence. Electric shock or burns from opened equipment, a fixed fire extinguishing system released into an occupied space, flooding, a spill, machinery started with a person in the way, a vessel left in an unsafe state.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, crew, contractors

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A visitor looks and asks; the crew operate. No visitor opens or closes a valve, breaker, switch or sea connection, removes a lock or tag, or opens an electrical panel, and where a system has to be operated for an inspection or a survey the Master, the Vessel Engineer or a crew member does it. Controls and release points for fixed fire extinguishing systems, fuel shut offs and emergency stops are pointed out to the visitor at the briefing so that they are not touched by mistake, and a functional test of any of them is carried out only by the vessel under its own procedure with the space confirmed empty. Electrical equipment is inspected closed, or isolated and proved dead by a competent person under the draft assessment Electrical work and isolation. Anything the visitor has asked to be opened, lifted or switched off is put back by the crew and confirmed to the person in charge before the visitor leaves. Faults found are reported to the Master and recorded, not put right by the visitor.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how survey and audit tests of fire, bilge, alarm and lifesaving systems are planned with the third party certifying body so that each test has a named crew member to carry it out. Owner: Designated Person Ashore. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. An emergency while the visitor is on board: fire, flooding, a person overboard or a casualty, on a vessel the visitor does not know and that may have only a part crew or none

Potential consequence. The visitor does not recognise the alarm, cannot find the way out through smoke or darkness, or is not accounted for. Death or serious injury.

Persons at risk. Shore visitors, crew

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The briefing on arrival covers the alarm, the assembly point ashore or on board, and two ways out of each space the visitor will enter. On hearing an alarm the visitor stops, leaves by the nearest safe route, goes to the assembly point and reports to the person in charge so that the visitor record can be checked. The visitor does not go back for equipment and does not fight a fire beyond the use of an extinguisher on a small fire with the way out behind them. On an unmanned vessel the visitor raises the alarm ashore and calls the emergency services before doing anything else. While a vessel is under repair and its own fire detection, alarms or extinguishing systems are out of service, the person in charge tells the visitor so and says what is in their place. The draft assessment Fire and emergency evacuation covers the shore side arrangements, and RA 30 Emergency Preparedness and the assessment Fire on board and abandoning the vessel cover the vessel.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm the emergency arrangements for a Company vessel alongside or ashore at Silverhills with a part crew or none, including who calls the emergency services and how visitors are accounted for. Owner: Operations Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager and the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The visitor record of each vessel is sampled at the internal audit of that vessel to check that shore staff are signing on and off and receiving the induction.
  • The Designated Person Ashore checks at each visit that the means of access is as RA 1 Boarding at Pier requires and records any defect to the Master.
  • Report off calls for lone visits are logged by the person who receives them and the log is reviewed each quarter by the Operations Manager.
  • Enclosed space entries by shore staff are recorded on the vessel's permit, and the permits are reviewed by the Risk and Compliance Manager at least once a year.
  • Any injury, near miss or unsafe condition met by a shore visitor on a vessel is reported under PRC 01 and reviewed against this assessment.
  • This assessment is reviewed after any change to the fleet, to the yard arrangements at Silverhills or to the posts that go aboard, and at least once a year.

Emergency arrangements

  • Person in the water at the berth: shout for help, throw the lifebuoy, keep the person in sight, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard, and recover the person by the nearest ladder or the vessel's recovery equipment. Do not enter the water.
  • Person collapsed in a tank, void or other enclosed space: do not enter. Raise the alarm, tell the Master or the person in charge, call 999, and follow the rescue arrangements in RA 37 Confined Spaces.
  • Fire on board: raise the alarm, leave by the nearest safe route, go to the assembly point and report to the person in charge. Call the Fire and Rescue Service on 999 where the vessel is alongside or ashore.
  • Injury on board: tell the Master or the person in charge, give first aid under RA 56 First Aid, and call 999 where the casualty cannot walk ashore. Give the berth and the access route for the ambulance.
  • Lone visitor overdue: the named person ashore calls the visitor, then sends somebody to the vessel, then calls the emergency services if the visitor is not found.
  • Every emergency involving a shore visitor is reported to the Designated Person Ashore and the Risk and Compliance Manager the same day.

Related documents

  • To confirm which draft procedure governs this work. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up, PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness and PRC 19 Induction are relevant, together with the vessel's own safety management system and permits to work.
  • RA 1 Boarding at Pier (GSS-HSE-RA-05), RA 59 Access and Egress (GSS-HSE-RA-02), RA 9 Engine Room (GSS-MAR-RA-05), RA 19 Plant Maintenance (GSS-HSE-RA-26), RA 37 Confined Spaces (GSS-HSE-RA-09), RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls (GSS-HSE-RA-29), RA 16 Working on Deck (GSS-MAR-RA-14), RA 18 Working on or Near Water (GSS-MAR-RA-15), RA 27 Hot Work (GSS-HSE-RA-17), RA 32 Working at Height (GSS-HSE-RA-36), RA 28 Ladders (GSS-HSE-RA-18), RA 40 Lone Worker (GSS-HSE-RA-19), RA 55 Noise (GSS-HSE-RA-23), RA 7 Personnel Transfer (GSS-HSE-RA-24), RA 30 Emergency Preparedness (GSS-HSE-RA-12), Electrical work and isolation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026), Fire and emergency evacuation (new draft 0.1 of 5 October 2026). Those assessments cover the crew's work in each area; this one covers the person who comes aboard to look at it.
  • Vessel visitor record, vessel induction record, permits to work and enclosed space entry permits, lone visit report off log, inspection, audit and survey reports, training records for shore staff who go aboard, issue record for personal protective equipment.

PPE requirements

Slip resistant safety footwear with toe protection for every visit. A lifejacket of a type and size suited to the wearer, worn when boarding, on pontoons and on open decks, and in date for service. A safety helmet in the yard, under a vessel ashore, in machinery spaces and wherever there is work overhead; a bump cap may be enough in low spaces where nothing is being lifted. Hearing protection carried and worn in any machinery space with machinery running. Eye protection near grinding, chipping, hot work and pressure testing. Gloves suited to the surfaces being handled. Coveralls or close fitting clothing with no loose ends. High visibility clothing in the yard and on the quayside. A torch, and a second light for any space below deck. A personal gas monitor where the Company issues one, used as a warning only and never in place of a test before entry.
