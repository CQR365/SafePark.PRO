RISK ASSESSMENT

Work related stress and workload

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionOffice, management and support work in which the demands of the post, the way the work is organised or an event at work can cause stress or distress: posts that are a single point of dependence, posts holding two roles, month end, year end, payroll, filing and tender deadlines, the volume of duties placed on one post, calls to next of kin and crew families during a major incident, and exposure of first aiders, fire wardens and managers to distressing events
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins. For this subject the supplement is a short assessment for each team or post, made with the people who do the work.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Managing Director and Risk and Compliance Manager
Location and scopeAll posts ashore at Silverhills, including the main office, the accounts function, the Silvers office and the yard, and managers and supervisors wherever they work, including at home and when travelling. Stress and distress among vessel crew and dive teams arise from the same causes and the principles apply, but hours of work and rest at sea are assessed in the new draft assessment Fatigue, on call duty and hours of rest, and aggression from customers and the public in the new draft assessment Violence, aggression and handling payments.
Main equipment usedNone in the usual sense. The working tools are the list of posts and deputies, the calendar of fixed deadlines, the on call and holiday rosters, the absence and overtime records, the record of one to one meetings, and the contact scripts and call log used in a major incident.
Persons at riskThe Managing Director, the Financial Controller (who also holds the Company Secretary post), the Risk and Compliance Manager, the Office Manager, the accounts team and the Accounts Assistant, department and line managers, first aiders and fire wardens, any person who holds two posts or covers a vacant one, and any person who attends or deals with the results of a serious incident.
Related permit or procedureTo confirm which draft procedure governs this work. No draft procedure on stress, workload or support after a distressing event has been seen. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up and PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness apply to the events that give rise to distressing calls and scenes.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
General dutiesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. HSE guidance states that employers have a legal duty to protect workers from stress at work by doing a risk assessment and acting on it, and that the assessment must be written down where there are five or more workers.
HSE Management StandardsThe Management Standards are guidance, not law. They set out six areas of work design that cause stress if not managed: demands, control, support, relationships, role and change. HSE states that an employer who follows the Management Standards approach correctly is treated as having made a suitable and sufficient assessment. This assessment is built on those six areas.
Working timeWorking Time Regulations 1998. Regulation 4 limits working time, including overtime, to an average of 48 hours in each seven days over the reference period unless the worker has agreed in writing to work more, and regulations 10 and 11 give entitlements to daily and weekly rest. Long hours at peak periods are therefore a legal matter as well as a cause of stress. How the Regulations apply to each GSS post is to be confirmed.
Emergency arrangements and informationManagement of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, regulation 8, requires procedures for serious and imminent danger and enough competent persons to carry them out. The people named in those procedures, including first aiders, fire wardens and the person who calls next of kin, are exposed to distress because of that role, so their support forms part of the arrangement.
First aidHSE guidance on first aid states that the employer's assessment of first aid needs should consider mental health as well as physical health, and that the employer may decide it is beneficial to have people trained to recognise and support somebody experiencing a mental health issue. This is guidance. The wording of the Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981 was not read for this draft and is to be confirmed.
ConsultationHSE guidance states that employers have a duty to consult workers or their representatives on health and safety matters, under the Safety Representatives and Safety Committees Regulations or the Health and Safety (Consultation with Employees) Regulations, depending on whether a trade union is recognised. A stress assessment made without asking the people concerned would not meet the Management Standards approach.
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply to crew aboard the vessels, enforced by the MCA. What they say about stress was not read for this draft and is to be confirmed before this assessment is applied aboard.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. No system for finding and acting on work related stress: no assessment by team or post, nobody asked, and early signs such as long hours, missed leave, errors and short temper not picked up

Potential consequence. Stress builds unseen until a person is absent for a long period, leaves, or makes a serious error in safety, financial or statutory work. Anxiety, depression and physical ill health. Loss of a post holder the Company depends on.

Persons at risk. All personnel ashore, managers in particular

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Stress is treated as a hazard like any other and assessed for each team or post against the six Management Standards areas (demands, control, support, relationships, role and change), with the people who do the work taking part. Each manager holds a regular one to one meeting with each person who reports to them, at which workload, deadlines, leave and anything that is causing pressure are asked about directly, and a short note is kept. The Managing Director's own workload is reviewed in the same way, with a person agreed for that purpose. Hours worked beyond the normal week, leave not taken, and absence where stress is given as a cause are recorded and looked at together each quarter, so that a pattern is seen before a person fails. Any person can raise a concern about their own workload, or a colleague's, with their manager or directly with the Risk and Compliance Manager, without it counting against them. Where a concern is raised, the manager agrees a specific change to the work, records it and checks that it has worked. A confidential source of support outside the line of management is made known to everybody.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Carry out the first assessment by team using the HSE Management Standards approach, starting with the posts named in this assessment, and record the findings and actions. Decide what confidential support the Company will provide and tell everybody how to reach it. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Managing Director and each department manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. A post that is a single point of dependence: one person holds a duty, a permission or the knowledge of how something is done, with no deputy, so cannot be ill, take leave or switch off

Potential consequence. The person works through illness and leave, is contacted at all hours, and carries the worry of what happens if they are not there. Exhaustion and long term ill health. For the Company, a statutory or safety critical duty lapses the first time that person is absent.

Persons at risk. Managing Director, Financial Controller, Risk and Compliance Manager, Designated Person Ashore, Office Manager, any post with no named deputy

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. A list is kept of every duty that only one person can carry out, including bank and payroll authorisation, statutory filings, regulatory contacts, the regulation 7 appointment, emergency contact duties and system administration. For each one a deputy is named in writing, given the access and authority to act, and briefed well enough to carry the duty for at least two weeks. Where no second person can be trained in house, a cover arrangement with an outside adviser is agreed in advance. The steps for each critical duty are written down and kept where the deputy can find them. Leave is planned at the start of the year for these posts, a handover is given before it, and the person on leave is not contacted except for a matter only they can decide. Out of hours contact is shared on a roster and is not left with one person by default. The cover arrangements for the Managing Director, including who may take decisions if the Managing Director cannot be reached, are set out in writing.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Compile the list of single points of dependence, name and brief a deputy for each, and settle in writing who acts if the Managing Director is unavailable. Owner: Managing Director with the Risk and Compliance Manager and each department manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. One person holding two posts, or covering a vacant post on top of their own, with competing priorities and no agreed split of time

Potential consequence. Both roles are done under pressure and neither fully. Conflict between the two sets of duties, long hours, error in a statutory filing or a safety decision, and sustained stress for the post holder.

Persons at risk. Financial Controller and Company Secretary, Commercial Manager and Civils Manager, Accounts Assistant working on two sets of accounts, any person covering the vacant Silvers Yard Manager post

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Where one person holds two posts, the duties of each are written down separately and the Managing Director agrees with the post holder which take priority when they clash, and roughly how the time divides. The fixed dates of both posts are put on one calendar so that two peaks falling in the same week are seen months ahead and one of them is moved, started early or given help. Duties that do not need that person are passed to somebody else. Cover for a vacant post is agreed for a stated period, with a review date, and the duties the covering person will set aside in the meantime are agreed and recorded, so that cover does not become permanent by default. The post holder has one line manager for both roles, or the two managers agree priorities between themselves and do not leave the post holder to settle it. The arrangement is reviewed at each one to one meeting.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Review each post held with another, and each vacant post being covered, against this control, and record the agreed priorities and review dates. Owner: Managing Director. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Fixed peak deadlines: month end, year end, payroll, statutory filings, tender returns, audits and certification dates, met by a small team or by one person

Potential consequence. Long hours for days at a time, work taken home, missed breaks and leave, mistakes in payroll or filings and the pressure of putting them right. Sustained stress and fatigue at each peak, and conflict within the team.

Persons at risk. Accounts team, Accounts Assistant, Financial Controller, Office Manager, Commercial Manager, Senior Quantity Surveyor, Risk and Compliance Manager

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. A calendar of every fixed deadline for the year is kept and shared, and work that can be done ahead of each peak is brought forward. Each peak has a simple plan: who does what, what information is needed from other departments and by which day, and who checks the result. Departments that supply figures, timesheets and returns are given a firm cut off date, and the Managing Director backs the accounts team when it is missed. Leave, training and other projects are kept clear of the peak for the people involved, and extra help is arranged in advance where the plan shows it is needed. Hours are watched during the peak: breaks are taken, nobody works late alone without telling somebody, and time worked beyond the normal week is recorded and given back afterwards. A second person checks payroll and statutory filings so that one tired person is not the only safeguard. After each year end and each large tender the team spends a short time on what made it harder than it needed to be, and the plan is changed.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Draw up the deadline calendar for the accounts, company secretarial, commercial and compliance functions, and agree the cut off dates with department managers. Owner: Financial Controller with the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. The volume and range of duties placed on one post by the draft management system documents, greater than one person can carry out, with no support post

Potential consequence. Duties are done late, in part or not at all, and the post holder carries the knowledge that statutory work is slipping. Sustained stress and long hours. For the Company, assurance that exists on paper only.

Persons at risk. Risk and Compliance Manager, any department manager given duties by the drafts without the time or support to meet them

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. Before the draft documents are adopted, the tasks they give to each post are counted and an honest estimate is made of the time they take across a year, set against the hours the post has. Where the total cannot be met, the Managing Director decides which tasks are moved to department managers, which are given to a support post or an outside provider, which are done less often, and which are dropped, and the documents are changed to match. Delivery of health and safety stays with department managers in their own areas, so that the regulation 7 post holder assures and advises and is not left to do each department's work. Work that arrives at a fixed time, such as audits, renewals and the Workboat Code transition, is planned on the deadline calendar with help arranged for the peak. Requests from outside bodies and from other departments come through one route so that they can be put in order. The workload of the post is a standing item at the one to one meeting with the Managing Director.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Complete the count of tasks by post from the draft documents, agree what is moved, supported or dropped, and decide whether a support post is to be filled. Owner: Managing Director with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Speaking to next of kin and crew families during a major incident, with no training, no script, no second person and no relief

Potential consequence. Acute distress for the person making or taking the calls, both at the time and in the weeks after. Lasting psychological harm. A wrong, early or badly given message that adds to the family's distress and to the caller's own.

Persons at risk. Office Manager, Crewing Manager, Managing Director, any person who answers the telephone during a major incident

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The persons who will speak to next of kin are named in advance in the emergency arrangements, with deputies, and nobody else is asked to do it. They are trained for the task and take part in the emergency exercises. Notification of a death is a matter for the police; the Company does not give that news, and callers know this and know what they may and may not say. A short script and a list of facts confirmed by the person leading the response are used, and nothing is passed on that has not been confirmed. Calls are made from a quiet room away from the response, with a second person present to keep the log and to support the caller. Each caller is relieved after an agreed time and is not left on the telephone for a whole day. Reception and switchboard staff are told at once to pass family and media calls to the named persons and not to answer questions themselves. Media calls go to a different person. After the calls the caller is released from normal work for the rest of the day if they wish, is spoken to by a manager the same day and again within the week, and is offered confidential support.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Name the persons who will contact next of kin and their deputies, arrange training, write the contact script and call log, and test them in the next emergency exercise. Confirm that next of kin details are current and can be reached out of hours. Owner: Managing Director with the Crewing Manager and the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. Exposure of first aiders, fire wardens and others at the scene to a serious injury, a death, a drowning or a failed resuscitation, with no support afterwards

Potential consequence. Shock at the time. Intrusive memories, poor sleep, anxiety and avoidance in the following weeks, which in some people become a lasting condition. Unwillingness to act as a first aider or warden again.

Persons at risk. First aiders, fire wardens, site supervisors, managers attending the scene, colleagues of the injured person, vessel crew and dive team members

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. First aiders and wardens are volunteers who are told plainly at appointment what the role can involve and may stand down from it without penalty. Training is kept current so that the person can act with confidence, since doubt about whether enough was done is itself a cause of distress. After any serious incident the person leading the response records who was present, including bystanders. Those people are stood down from the scene as soon as they are no longer needed, are not left alone, are not asked to drive straight away, and are spoken to by a manager before they go home. The manager tells them what happened to the casualty as soon as that is known. Contact is made again within a few days and at about a month, because reactions can appear late, and anybody still troubled at that point is helped to see their doctor or the Company's confidential support. Witness statements are taken with care, once, and by a person who knows the witness may be distressed. The person is not pressed to talk before they are ready, and no blame is suggested.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write the support steps into the emergency and incident arrangements, decide who makes the contacts at each stage, and brief first aiders and wardens on what support they can expect. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with department managers and each Master. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Managers asked by the drafts to deal with violence, aggression or distress in others, without training, guidance or anybody to turn to themselves

Potential consequence. The manager avoids the matter or handles it badly, the affected person is not supported, and the manager carries the worry of having got it wrong. Stress for both. Grievance or claim.

Persons at risk. Department and line managers, site supervisors, Masters

Initial rating. L 3 x S 3 = 9 MEDIUM

Control measures. Managers are given clear written guidance on what they are expected to do when a member of their team is distressed, has been threatened or assaulted, or reports stress: listen, make the person safe, record it, agree a change to the work where one is needed, and pass on what is beyond them. They are trained in holding that conversation and in recognising the early signs of stress. Each manager knows who they can go to for advice on a case, and that the Risk and Compliance Manager and the Managing Director will support a reasonable decision. Managers are not expected to act as counsellors, and the route to medical or confidential support is the same for them as for their teams. A manager who has dealt with a serious case is asked afterwards how they are. Cases are kept confidential and shared only with those who need to know.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Write the manager guidance and arrange training for all department and line managers, site supervisors and Masters. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Managing Director. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Change that is poorly explained: the redraft of the management system, new post titles and reporting lines, posts vacant or newly created, and duties moving between posts

Potential consequence. People do not know what is expected of them or who decides. Rumour, duplicated or dropped work, loss of trust and resistance to the new system. Stress from uncertainty about the job itself.

Persons at risk. All personnel, holders of posts whose title, duties or manager are changing

Initial rating. L 4 x S 3 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. Each change to posts, duties or documents is explained to the people affected before it takes effect, by their own manager, with the reason for it and the date. Each post holder is given the draft description of their own post and asked to mark it up, and is told what was done with their comments. Drafts are labelled as drafts, and nobody is held to a duty in a document that has not been adopted and issued to them. Changes are brought in by stages with time to learn each one, and not all at once on top of a peak period. Training in a new duty is given before the duty passes. During the change there is one named person to whom questions go, and the questions and answers are shared. Where a post is vacant, staff are told who is covering which duties and until when.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 3 = 6 LOW

Further action required. Set out the order and dates in which the draft documents and post changes will be brought in, and how each team will be told. Owner: Managing Director with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Poor working relationships: conflict within or between departments, bullying, harassment or unacceptable behaviour by a colleague, manager, client or contractor

Potential consequence. Anxiety, loss of confidence, depression and absence in the person on the receiving end. A team that stops reporting problems. Grievance, resignation or claim.

Persons at risk. All personnel, including self employed crew and divers and those working alone with one other person

Initial rating. L 3 x S 4 = 12 HIGH

Control measures. The Company states in writing what behaviour is not accepted at work, including on vessels and at client sites, and that the same standard applies to managers, clients and contractors. Each person knows at least two routes for raising a concern, one of which is outside their own line of management. A concern is acknowledged promptly, looked into fairly by somebody not involved, and the person who raised it is told the outcome so far as confidentiality allows. Nobody is treated worse for raising a concern in good faith. Managers deal with friction between departments early, by getting the people concerned into one room and agreeing who does what, and do not leave it to harden. Where the behaviour comes from a client or a contractor, the manager takes it up with that organisation and does not leave the individual to deal with it. Aggression from customers and the public is controlled under the new draft assessment Violence, aggression and handling payments.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm whether a current policy on bullying and harassment and a grievance route exist and reach self employed crew and divers, and brief all staff on them. Owner: Managing Director with the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • Each department manager confirms each quarter that one to one meetings have been held and reports, without naming individuals, any workload concern that has not been resolved.
  • Hours worked beyond the normal week, leave not taken and absence where stress is given as a cause are reviewed each quarter by the Managing Director and the Risk and Compliance Manager, looking for patterns by team and by time of year.
  • The list of single points of dependence and named deputies is reviewed at least once a year and whenever a post holder changes or a post falls vacant.
  • The team assessments against the six Management Standards areas are repeated each year and after any reorganisation, and the actions from them are tracked to closure.
  • After every serious incident the Risk and Compliance Manager checks that the support contacts for those involved were made at each stage and records that they were.
  • Stress and workload are a standing item at the health and safety meeting and the management review, reported in a way that does not identify individuals.

Emergency arrangements

  • A person in acute distress at work is taken somewhere quiet, is not left alone, and is asked what they need. Their manager is told, and with the person's agreement a relative or friend is contacted to take them home.
  • Where a person may be at risk of harming themselves, stay with them, call 999 if the risk is immediate, or NHS 24 on 111 for urgent advice, and tell the Managing Director.
  • After a serious incident the person leading the response lists everybody who was present or who took distressing calls, and gives the list to the Risk and Compliance Manager the same day so that the support contacts can be made.
  • During a major incident the persons making calls to next of kin are relieved at agreed intervals, and the person leading the response checks on them as part of running the response.
  • Where a post holder who is a single point of dependence is suddenly absent, the named deputy takes over the listed critical duties and the Managing Director is told the same day.

Related documents

  • To confirm which draft procedures govern stress, workload and support after an incident, as none has been seen. PRC 01 Accident and Incident Reporting and Follow Up, PRC 06 Emergency Preparedness and PRC 19 Induction bear on this assessment.
  • RA 30 Emergency Preparedness (GSS-HSE-RA-12), RA 56 First Aid (GSS-HSE-RA-13), RA 31 Office Duties (GSS-MAR-RA-07), RA 40 Lone Worker (GSS-HSE-RA-19), RA 43 DSE (GSS-HSE-RA-11), and the new draft assessments Fatigue, on call duty and hours of rest, Violence, aggression and handling payments, and Attending an incident scene and leading the response.
  • Team stress assessments, notes of one to one meetings, the list of single points of dependence and deputies, the deadline calendar, overtime, leave and absence records, the next of kin contact script and call log, and the record of support contacts after an incident.

PPE requirements

No personal protective equipment controls this hazard. First aiders attending a casualty use the gloves, face shield and other items held in the first aid kit under RA 56 First Aid, which protect against infection and not against distress.
