Treatment System

Primary Treatment - Pressure Vessel Filtration

System Type: Dual-media pressure filtration
Configuration: Two pressure vessels in parallel operation
Design Flow Rate: 15 m³/hour per vessel
  • Vessel 1 - Turbidex Filtration:
    • Media: Turbidex (iron oxide-coated sand)
    • Depth: 1.2m filter bed
    • Function: Iron/manganese removal, turbidity reduction
    • Backwash: Automatic based on differential pressure
  • Vessel 2 - Silica Sand Filtration:
    • Media: Graded silica sand (0.5-1.2mm)
    • Depth: 1.0m filter bed
    • Function: Suspended solids removal, polishing
    • Backwash: Automatic based on differential pressure

Secondary Treatment - Cartridge Filtration

  • Filter Type: 20" polypropylene pleated cartridge
  • Micron Rating: 5 micron absolute
  • Configuration: Single housing with bypass
  • Flow Rate: 20 m³/hour maximum
  • Replacement Frequency: Monthly or based on differential pressure

Disinfection System - UV Treatment

Critical Treatment Step: Primary disinfection barrier for pathogen inactivation
  • UV System Specifications:
    • Chamber Length: 1950mm
    • Lamp Power: 75W low-pressure mercury vapour
    • Design Dose: 40 mJ/cm² (400 J/m²)
    • Flow Rate: 15 m³/hour maximum
    • Quartz Sleeve: High transmission borosilicate
    • UV Sensor: Photodiode with digital display
  • Performance Monitoring:
    • UV intensity monitoring with alarm
    • Lamp hours counter
    • Flow rate monitoring
    • UV transmittance measurement (quarterly)

Distribution System

Gravity-Fed Operation with Pressure Management

System Type: Gravity-fed distribution with pressure vessel support
Operating Pressure: 2.0 - 2.5 bar maintained through gravity head and pressure vessels
No Pumping: System operates entirely on gravity flow and stored pressure
  • Gravity Head: Natural elevation difference provides primary driving force
  • Pressure Vessels: Accumulator tanks maintain consistent pressure
  • Operating Range: 2.0 - 2.5 bar throughout distribution system
  • Flow Capacity: Limited by available gravity head and pipe sizing
  • Pressure Control: Pressure reducing valves and vessels for regulation
  • No Backup Pumps: System relies entirely on gravity operation

Distribution Network

  • Main Distribution: 110mm MDPE main from treatment plant
  • Branch Connections: 63mm and 32mm MDPE to park areas
  • Service Connections: 25mm MDPE to individual accommodation units
  • Total Network Length: Approximately 2.5km
  • Maximum Head Loss: Critical factor in gravity-fed system design
  • Isolation Valves: Strategically located for maintenance and emergency isolation
  • Pressure Monitoring: Key points monitored to ensure adequate pressure

Gravity System Limitations

Critical Constraints:
  • Flow Rate Limitations: Cannot exceed gravity-fed capacity
  • Pressure Recovery: No pumping capability to boost pressure
  • High Demand Issues: Pressure may drop during peak usage
  • Elevation Dependencies: Higher elevations may have reduced pressure
  • Emergency Response: Limited to gravity-fed backup options
  • Treatment Constraints: Filtration and UV must operate within pressure limits

Daily Operational Procedures

Daily Check Frequency: Every 24 hours including weekends and holidays
Responsible Person: Duty Site Supervisor or nominated deputy

Morning System Check (08:00 - 09:00)

PRIORITY CHECK: Boil water notice signage and bottled water supplies FIRST
  1. Boil Water Notice Management
    • Verify boil water notices are posted at all taps and accommodation units
    • Check bottled water supplies at reception are adequate for 24 hours
    • Confirm guest information boards are updated
    • Test PA system for daily boil water announcements
  2. Gravity System Status Check
    • Check system pressure readings (target: 2.0-2.5 bar)
    • Verify water is flowing to all accommodation areas
    • Monitor source flow rate from hill catchment
    • Check for any pressure drops indicating leaks or blockages
  3. Source Water Inspection
    • Visual inspection of source water quality and color at collection pond
    • Check intake for debris or blockages affecting gravity flow
    • Note weather conditions affecting source quality and flow rate
    • Record any unusual observations that might affect boil water notice status
  4. Storage Tank Levels (Gravity Feed Critical)
    • Record water levels in all storage tanks (critical for gravity system)
    • Check tank overflows are functioning and not backing up
    • Verify tank access covers are secure and locked
    • Inspect tank surroundings for contamination sources
    • Confirm adequate tank levels for continuous gravity flow
  5. Treatment System Status
    • Check filter differential pressures (record readings - critical for gravity flow)
    • Verify UV system operation and intensity readings (critical during boil water notice)
    • Monitor pressure vessel pre-charge pressures
    • Review control system alarms and status
    • Check treatment plant is receiving adequate gravity flow
  6. Water Quality Assessment During Boil Water Notice
    • Visual assessment of treated water clarity
    • Record turbidity reading at UV inlet (critical parameter)
    • Document UV intensity and ensure above minimum threshold
    • Check for any taste or odour complaints from guests
    • Verify boil water notice compliance in food service areas

Evening System Check (17:00 - 18:00)

  1. Gravity System Performance Review
    • Review daily water flow totals and pressure trends
    • Check pressure has remained adequate throughout peak demand
    • Verify source flow has been sufficient for system demand

  2. Boil Water Notice Status
    • Confirm bottled water stocks remain adequate for overnight/next day
    • Check no guests have reported illness or water quality concerns
    • Verify food service has maintained boil water precautions
    • Prepare for evening PA announcement reinforcing boil water advice

    • Clear any nuisance alarms related to gravity system fluctuations

Maintenance Procedures

Filter Backwash Procedure

Safety Warning: Ensure adequate water reserves before commencing backwash operation
  1. Pre-Backwash Checks
    • Verify minimum 50% storage tank capacity available
    • Check backwash discharge route is clear
    • Ensure personnel are clear of discharge area
    • Don appropriate PPE including waterproofs
  2. Backwash Sequence
    • Initiate backwash cycle from control panel
    • Monitor backwash water quality - continue until clear
    • Typical duration: 10-15 minutes per filter
    • Allow system to settle for 5 minutes before restart
  3. Post-Backwash Verification
    • Check filter differential pressure has reduced
    • Verify water quality has returned to normal
    • Record backwash volume and duration
    • Update maintenance log

UV Lamp Replacement Procedure

Critical Safety Warning: UV lamps contain mercury. Handle with extreme care and dispose as hazardous waste.
  1. System Isolation
    • Isolate UV system at electrical supply
    • Close water isolation valves
    • Drain UV chamber completely
    • Lock out/tag out electrical supply
  2. Lamp Replacement
    • Allow UV chamber to cool (minimum 30 minutes)
    • Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection
    • Remove quartz sleeve carefully
    • Replace UV lamp with manufacturer approved type
    • Clean quartz sleeve with appropriate cleaner
    • Reassemble system ensuring correct O-ring placement
  3. System Commissioning
    • Pressure test system at 1.5 x operating pressure
    • Restore electrical supply and check lamp operation
    • Verify UV intensity readings are within specification
    • Reset lamp hour counter
    • Update equipment records

Storage Tank Cleaning Procedure

Confined Space Warning: Tank entry requires permit to work and trained personnel only
  1. Preparation and Isolation
    • Isolate tank from system operation
    • Drain tank completely via bottom outlet
    • Ventilate tank for minimum 30 minutes
    • Test atmosphere before entry
  2. Cleaning Process
    • Entry only by trained confined space personnel
    • Continuous atmospheric monitoring during entry
    • Mechanical cleaning of all surfaces
    • Disinfection with sodium hypochlorite solution (50mg/L)
    • Contact time minimum 2 hours
  3. Commissioning
    • Thorough flushing to remove disinfectant residual
    • Microbiological sampling before return to service
    • Visual inspection of water quality
    • Update cleaning records

Sodium Hypochlorite Handling

Hazard: Corrosive, toxic by inhalation, environmental hazard
  • PPE Requirements: Chemical resistant gloves, eye protection, respiratory protection if poorly ventilated
  • Storage: Cool, dry, well-ventilated area away from acids and organic materials
  • Handling: Use only plastic or stainless steel containers and pipework
  • Spillage: Dilute with large quantities of water, neutralize with sodium bisulfite
  • Emergency: Eye contact - irrigate with water for 15 minutes, seek medical attention

UV Lamp Disposal

  • UV lamps classified as hazardous waste due to mercury content
  • Store used lamps in original packaging or approved containers
  • Arrange collection by licensed hazardous waste carrier
  • Maintain waste transfer records for minimum 3 years
  • Never dispose of UV lamps in general waste

Sampling Procedures

Critical Requirement: All sampling must be conducted using aseptic techniques to prevent false positive results.
  1. Sample Point Preparation
    • Remove any attachments (aerators, hose connections)
    • Disinfect external surfaces with 70% alcohol
    • Flush for minimum 2 minutes at full flow
    • Reduce flow to gentle stream for sampling
  2. Sample Collection
    • Use sterile sample bottles provided by laboratory
    • Do not touch bottle neck or cap interior
    • Fill bottle leaving small air space
    • Cap immediately and label clearly
  3. Sample Transport
    • Keep samples chilled (1-4°C) during transport
    • Deliver to laboratory within 24 hours of collection
    • Complete chain of custody documentation
    • Record sample collection details

Specific Emergency Procedures

CRITICAL: UV System Failure (Current Risk)

CURRENT STATUS: System currently under boil water notice - UV effectiveness may be compromised
IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (Within 15 minutes):
  1. System Assessment
    • Verify current UV system status and intensity readings
    • Check if failure is related to current boil water notice
    • Maintain current boil water notice until resolution
    • Ensure bottled water supply is adequate
  2. Immediate Notifications
    • Contact Argyll & Bute Environmental Health: 01546 605519
    • Notify site management and emergency response team
    • Reinforce boil water notice to all guests via PA and notices
    • Contact backup water supplier: Highland Spring 01764 679200
  3. Gravity System Considerations
    • Cannot isolate supply using pumps - use gravity-fed isolation valves
    • Maintain pressure in system using existing pressure vessels
    • Monitor pressure levels throughout emergency response
    • Plan for reduced flow rates during emergency supply setup
  4. Emergency Water Supply
    • Increase emergency bottled water distribution
    • Arrange emergency water tanker delivery (gravity-compatible connection)
    • Set up emergency water distribution points
    • Implement enhanced water rationing due to gravity system constraints

MAJOR: Gravity System Pressure Loss

TRIGGER EVENTS: System pressure below 2.0 bar, inadequate flow to accommodation units
  1. Immediate Assessment (Within 15 minutes)
    • Check all storage tank levels immediately
    • Verify pressure vessel pre-charge pressures
    • Inspect for major leaks or pipe failures
    • Monitor source flow rate from hill collection
  2. System Diagnosis
    • Identify if problem is source flow, storage, or distribution
    • Check for blockages in treatment plant reducing flow
    • Assess if seasonal source yield is insufficient
    • Determine if high demand is exceeding gravity supply capacity
  3. Emergency Actions
    • Implement immediate demand reduction measures
    • Deploy emergency bottled water for critical needs
    • Prepare for potential accommodation unit isolation if needed
    • Consider emergency pumping equipment if available

MAJOR: Source Water Contamination

TRIGGER EVENTS: Visible contamination, unusual odor, dead animals in catchment, chemical spill upstream
  1. Source Isolation (Within 30 minutes)
    • Close source water intake valve
    • Investigate contamination source
    • Take photographs and water samples
    • Secure contamination area if accessible
  2. System Assessment
    • Check if contamination has entered storage tanks
    • Assess remaining water reserves
    • Estimate time to alternative supply needed
    • Review treatment system capability
  3. Customer Protection
    • Issue boil water notice if contamination suspected
    • Provide bottled water for drinking and cooking
    • Monitor customer complaints and health issues
    • Liaise with NHS Highland if health concerns arise

MAJOR: Power Supply Failure

GRAVITY SYSTEM ADVANTAGE: Water flow continues during power failure, but UV disinfection and monitoring systems affected
  1. Immediate Response (Within 10 minutes)
    • Check main electrical supply and local distribution
    • Start emergency generator (automatic or manual)
    • Prioritize power to UV disinfection system FIRST
    • Verify critical systems have power (UV, controls, monitoring)
    • Contact Scottish Power Networks: 0800 092 9290
  2. System Prioritization for Gravity-Fed System
    • Priority 1: UV disinfection system (CRITICAL - maintain boil water notice if offline)
    • Priority 2: Monitoring and alarm systems
    • Priority 3: Treatment plant automation (filters can operate manually)
    • Priority 4: Communications and emergency lighting
    • Water flow continues via gravity - NO PUMPING REQUIRED
  3. Manual Operation Procedures
    • Water continues to flow by gravity during power outage
    • Monitor system manually at 2-hour intervals
    • Manual filter backwashing if automated systems offline
    • Implement fuel conservation for extended outages
    • Maintain boil water notice if UV system cannot be powered
  4. Guest Communication
    • Inform guests that water continues to flow (gravity-fed advantage)
    • Maintain or strengthen boil water notice during UV downtime
    • Provide battery-powered PA announcements
    • Increase bottled water distribution as precaution

MAJOR: Storage Tank Contamination

  1. Contamination Assessment
    • Identify contaminated tank(s) and isolate immediately
    • Determine contamination source and extent
    • Take water samples for emergency analysis
    • Assess impact on remaining system capacity
  2. System Reconfiguration
    • Switch to uncontaminated storage tanks
    • Bypass contaminated tanks from system
    • Increase monitoring of remaining tanks
    • Implement enhanced disinfection if needed
  3. Tank Remediation
    • Drain contaminated tank completely
    • Clean and disinfect using approved procedures
    • Test water quality before return to service
    • Investigate and eliminate contamination source

Alternative Operating Strategies

Scenario 1: Current Status - Boil Water Notice (Ongoing)

Current Situation: Boil water notice active, gravity system operational, UV disinfection compromised
  • Water Supply Management:
    • FREE bottled water available 24/7 at reception for all guests
    • Maintain gravity-fed supply for non-potable uses (toilets, showers)
    • Clear boil water notices at all taps and accommodation units
    • Enhanced monitoring of UV system and source water quality
  • Operational Adjustments:
    • Continue normal guest accommodation with enhanced water safety messaging
    • Modify food service operations (use bottled water for all food preparation)
    • Increase staff presence for guest support and information
    • Daily communication with Environmental Health authority
  • Guest Services:
    • Comprehensive check-in briefing on water safety precautions
    • Written information in all accommodation units
    • Daily PA announcements reinforcing boil water advice
    • Additional bottled water supplies in units upon request

Scenario 2: Gravity System Pressure Loss (4-24 hours)

Triggers: Pressure below 2.0 bar, source flow reduction, major leaks
  • Water Supply Strategy:
    • Immediate demand reduction through guest communication
    • Deploy emergency bottled water for all drinking/cooking needs
    • Use remaining gravity pressure for essential non-potable needs
    • Consider temporary accommodation unit isolation if pressure insufficient
  • Operational Response:
    • Implement water conservation measures throughout site
    • Suspend non-essential water-using activities
    • Increase bottled water distribution points
    • Prepare for potential guest relocation if pressure cannot be restored

Scenario 3: Extended System Failure (>24 hours)

Triggers: Major source contamination, catastrophic UV failure, gravity system collapse
  • Emergency Water Supply:
    • Install temporary water tanker with gravity-compatible connections
    • Establish multiple water distribution points throughout park
    • Implement comprehensive bottled water supply for all needs
    • Consider partial site evacuation if gravity system cannot support basic needs
  • Business Continuity:
    • Reduce site capacity by 50-75% depending on water availability
    • Relocate guests to partner accommodations with confirmed safe water supply
    • Suspend all non-essential services
    • Focus all resources on water system restoration
    • Activate business interruption insurance
