Why safety belongs in the awards, and why now

Safety in the UK is having a national moment. RoSPA, the Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents, has published Safer Lives, Stronger Nation, its call for a National Accident Prevention Strategy, built on research bringing together twenty years of accident data from all four UK nations. The numbers are stark.

20,000+

accidental deaths in the UK every year, more than the capacity of the O2 Arena

42%

rise in the rate of accidental deaths over the last decade

£12bn

annual cost of preventable accidents to the UK economy

Crucially, the strategy names leisure as one of the core sectors it must cover, alongside home, work, product and transport. Holiday parks sit squarely in that space: open water, play areas, pools and hot tubs, vehicles and grounds, all enjoyed by families on holiday. This is exactly the territory the national conversation is now focused on, and our sector is rarely at the table.

That conversation is live in Parliament, debated and backed across party lines:

An earlier Westminster Hall debate, led by Sir Andrew Mitchell MP, drew cross party support after figures showed accidental deaths had risen by 8 per cent in a single year. RoSPA has also launched Towards a Safer Scotland, its manifesto for the 2026 Scottish Parliamentary election. The momentum is real, and the Holiday Park Awards could be one of the first places the sector steps into it.

How the safety assessment would work

It would be kept deliberately simple and encouraging. A park answers a short, guided set of questions about how it keeps people safe, adding a little supporting evidence as it goes. That is reviewed against a holiday park safety matrix, a slimmed down version of the respected RoSPA approach, tailored to parks. The park then receives a clear result and warm, constructive feedback: what it is doing well, and a gentle steer on where it could go further. That score feeds the safety dimension of Holiday Park of the Year, giving the headline award a credible backbone.

Positive, never exposing

This is the part that matters most. People tense up the moment they hear "safety" or "compliance", and the last thing we want is a park worrying that entering means being picked apart. The whole approach is built the other way round: it celebrates good practice, frames everything as a path forward, and keeps a park's detail confidential. All of the credibility, none of the fear, and nobody scared off entering.

How it links with RoSPA

The same effort a park puts in does not have to stop at your ceremony. It can carry on, with support, into a full entry for the RoSPA Health and Safety Awards, the world's longest running and most respected safety awards, now in their 70th year and drawing around 2,000 entries from nearly 50 countries each year. The natural fit is the RoSPA Achievement Award, which recognises an organisation's own safety management at Gold, Silver, Bronze or Merit level. So a single piece of work can climb three rungs:

Rung 1

Recognition in the Holiday Park Awards

Feeding Holiday Park of the Year.

Rung 2

A ready made evidence pack

The hard part is done, and reusable.

Rung 3

A full RoSPA Award

Internationally recognised recognition the park earns in its own right.

RoSPA also runs Sector Awards, Fleet Safety Awards for parks running vehicles, and Inspiration Awards for outstanding teams and individuals, so there is somewhere meaningful for every park to aim.

What it could mean for the parks

You were clear the awards need to stay accessible, exciting and commercially valuable for parks to enter. A safety strand can strengthen all three, because it gives a park something tangible to take away.

A real commercial return: insurance

Insurers price on risk. A park that can show documented, independently recognised safety management, with a clear audit trail, is a better risk, and that is rewarded at renewal. Industry guidance points to premium reductions in the order of 5 to 15 per cent for businesses that demonstrate proactive, well evidenced risk management, and public liability is a significant cost for any holiday park. Entering can help a park's bottom line, not just its trophy cabinet.

  • A credible mark that means something, because it is independently assessed rather than self declared.
  • A clear route to world recognised RoSPA accreditation, with the hard part already done.
  • A confidential, encouraging health check of their safety, with a steer on where to improve.
  • A reason to be proud, and a story to tell their guests and owners.
