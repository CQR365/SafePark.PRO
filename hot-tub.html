Standard Operating Procedure

SOP-HT-001 — Hot Tub Management | Version 1.0 | February 2026
Omnibosh Safety | Managed via: SafeSystems.PRO | SafeWater.PRO (HT)
Review Date: October 2026
Quick Navigation

1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure sets out the required process for managing hot tubs across holiday park sites. It covers two distinct operational scenarios: Changeover Days (when guests depart and new guests arrive) and Routine Guest Days (when guests are in residence). All team members responsible for hot tub management must follow this SOP to ensure compliance with HSG282, guest safety, and water quality standards. All checks and records are managed through SafeWater.PRO, the dedicated hot tub management module within SafeSystems.PRO.

⚠ IMPORTANT: No guest may use a hot tub until all checks are completed and water chemistry is confirmed within acceptable parameters. A hot tub with failed readings must be taken out of service immediately.

2. Scope

This SOP applies to all team members involved in the daily management, inspection, and maintenance of hot tubs at any park operated by Highland Holidays. It should be read alongside the site-specific risk assessment for hot tub operations and the manufacturer's guidance for each hot tub model in use.

3. References & Legislation

HSG282 — The Control of Legionella and Other Infectious Agents in Spa-Pool Systems; Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974; Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999; Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH); Manufacturer's operating and maintenance manuals for each hot tub.

4. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Before handling any chemicals or cleaning products, the following PPE must be worn: chemical-resistant gloves, safety goggles or eye protection, and appropriate footwear (non-slip, closed-toe).

NOTE: Always check the COSHH data sheets for each product before use. These are available on SafeSystems.PRO and must be kept accessible on site.

5. Chemical Handling, Transport & Dosing

⚠ CRITICAL SAFETY SECTION

Hot tubs across Highland Holidays sites are located outdoors and are fully exposed to the elements. The procedures below must be followed to ensure chemicals are transported and added safely, regardless of weather conditions.

Pre-Measuring at the Chemical Store: All chemical doses must be measured out inside the chemical store, not at the hot tub. This provides a sheltered, dry, and controlled environment for handling bulk products. The required dose should be measured using calibrated scoops or measuring devices and placed into a clean, dry, screw-top plastic dosing container clearly labelled with the product name and hot tub. Household containers, food containers, or unmarked vessels must never be used.

Transporting Chemicals: Pre-measured doses should be transported in a dedicated plastic carry caddy or chemical bucket with a handle. Bromine and pH adjustment products must always be kept in their own separate sealed containers within the caddy — they must never come into direct contact with one another, as a spill of one into the other could cause a chemical reaction. PPE must be worn from leaving the chemical store through to completion of dosing and return.

Pre-Dissolving: Bromine granules must never be scattered directly into the hot tub in their dry form. In outdoor conditions, wind can blow dry granules back towards the operator's face, onto surrounding decking, or into areas where guests may walk. The correct method is to pre-dissolve the measured dose in a clean plastic dosing jug using warm water drawn from the hot tub itself. Stir until fully dissolved before adding to the tub water.

Adding the Solution: With jets running, pour the pre-dissolved solution close to the water surface rather than from height. Where possible, add near a jet intake to ensure it is drawn into circulation immediately. Always stand upwind. The same method applies to granular pH adjusters. Liquid pH adjusters can be added directly but should still be poured close to the surface near a jet.

Wet Weather: In rain, the pre-dissolve method remains safest since the chemical is already in solution. The key precaution is to avoid opening any dry chemical containers outdoors. Moisture entering bulk bromine or pH products causes degradation, clumping, and inaccurate dosing. All containers should remain sealed during transport and only opened inside the chemical store.

Post-Dosing Cleanup: Return the dosing jug, containers, and carry caddy to the chemical store immediately. Rinse everything with clean water after every use — never leave chemical residue. Store clean, dry, and ready for next use. Ensure the chemical store is left secure and locked.

⚠ IMPORTANT: Never carry open or unsealed chemicals outdoors. Never mix products together. Never scatter dry granules directly into the tub. If chemicals are spilled during transport, do not attempt to scoop them back into the container — contain the spill, clean the area with water, and report to the duty manager.

PART A — Changeover Day Procedure

Example timings based on 09:30 start with typical 15:00–16:00 guest arrival.

09:30
Isolate & Drain
Switch off at isolator. Remove the main drainage plug — this must always be used to ensure maximum system drainage. Additional methods (submersible pumps, siphon hoses) may be used alongside. Remove used filter cartridge, clean thoroughly, set aside to dry completely — this becomes the spare for the next changeover.
09:45
Shell Cleaning
Approved non-foaming hot tub surface cleaner on all surfaces. Pay particular attention to waterline, jet housings, seat areas, and textured surfaces. Rinse thoroughly — no cleaning product residue.
10:15
Jet & Pipework Flush
If scheduled (refer to maintenance log), run flush product through the system before final rinse. Removes biofilm buildup from internal pipework. Follow manufacturer's instructions for the flush product.
10:30
Inspect Equipment
Shell for cracks, chips, or damage. Check all jet fittings secure. Inspect pump housing and connections for leaks. Check control panel functioning. Install the clean, dry spare filter cartridge. Ensure main drainage plug securely refitted before refilling.
10:45
Inspect Surroundings
Check decking/paving condition. Remove trip hazards. Ensure drainage clear. Confirm privacy screening intact. Check all safety signage displayed, legible, and not faded — safety rules, maximum capacity, temperature warning, emergency contacts, and health warnings.
11:00
Refill
Begin refilling with fresh water. Monitor to avoid overflow. Ensure water level covers all jets and reaches recommended operating level.
11:30
Initial Chemical Dose
Switch tub back on at isolator, start circulation pump. Add initial dose: bromine sanitiser, pH adjuster if needed, and any start-up products specified. Run jets for minimum 15 minutes for full distribution.
12:00
Heating Period
Allow tub to begin heating. This may take several hours depending on incoming water temperature and tub model. The tub must reach between 37°C and 40°C before guest use is permitted.
13:00
First Retest
Approximately one hour after dosing, test water chemistry. Record all readings in SafeWater.PRO. If any parameter outside range, re-dose and allow a further 30 minutes circulation before retesting.
14:00
Final Pre-Arrival Check
Final water test and temperature check. Confirm all readings within acceptable parameters. Verify cover is clean and locking mechanism works. Ensure surrounding area clean and presentable. Record all final readings and confirm tub as "fit for use" in SafeWater.PRO.
15:00
Guest Arrival
Hot tub available for guest use. If temperature has not yet reached 37°C, leave cover on and inform guest of expected ready time. Do not allow use below 37°C with chemical readings outside range.
DRAINAGE: The main drainage plug must always be removed as part of every changeover drain. This ensures the lowest point of the system is fully emptied, preventing standing water in pipework where bacteria could develop.
FILTER ROTATION: Each hot tub is allocated two filter cartridges. On every changeover, the used filter is removed, cleaned, and left to dry fully. The previously cleaned and dried spare is then fitted. Both filters replaced with new cartridges every 3 months. Filter replacement dates recorded in SafeWater.PRO.
⚠ IMPORTANT: If at any point during changeover you identify damage to the shell, equipment, or pipework that cannot be resolved, report it immediately to your duty manager. The tub must not be brought back into service until the issue is resolved.

PART B — Routine Guest Day Procedure

Carried out every day that guests are in residence, at the same time each day. Example timings based on 09:30 start.

09:30
Arrive & Visual Check
Check cover is in place and secured. Look for debris on cover, water on decking, displaced signage, or damage to surroundings.
09:35
Remove Cover & Inspect Water
Visually inspect for clarity, colour, foaming, or surface debris. If cloudy, discoloured, or strong chemical odour — do not proceed with a simple top-up, investigate further. Remove floating debris with a clean skimmer net.
09:40
Temperature Check
Must be 37–40°C. If dropped significantly overnight, check heating system and thermostat. If exceeds 40°C, reduce thermostat immediately — no guest use until within range.
09:45
Water Chemistry Testing
Using approved test kit or strips, test and record: total bromine, pH, and total alkalinity. Record all readings in SafeWater.PRO before making any adjustments.
09:50
Chemical Adjustment (if needed)
Dose in order: alkalinity first, then pH, then sanitiser. Run jets for minimum 15 minutes to circulate. Do not allow guest use until retested and confirmed in range.
10:05
Filter Check
Remove filter cartridge and inspect. Rinse under running water to remove surface debris. If heavily soiled or showing wear, swap with the clean dry spare from the previous changeover. Each hot tub has two allocated filters that rotate on each changeover. Refit securely after inspection.
10:10
Equipment & Jets Check
Turn on jets briefly, confirm all functioning. Listen for unusual sounds from pump or motor. Check control panel responsive. Verify emergency stop button accessible and working.
10:15
Surrounding Area
Inspect decking/paving for new trip hazards, damage, or contamination. Confirm area clean and tidy. Check drainage clear. Verify all safety signage displayed and legible.
10:20
Cover & Lock Check
Inspect cover for damage, heavy water pooling, or contamination. Clean if necessary. Confirm locking mechanism functioning correctly.
10:25
Record & Sign Off
Complete daily check in SafeWater.PRO. Log all readings, any actions taken, and confirm tub status as either "fit for use" or "out of service". If out of service, ensure cover locked and appropriate sign displayed.
10:30+
Retest (if dosed)
If chemicals adjusted at 09:50, return after circulation period and retest. Record new readings. Only update status to "fit for use" once all parameters confirmed in range.
NOTE: Total routine check time is approximately 30–45 minutes per hot tub (longer if chemical correction is required). Plan your route to allow for retesting where needed.

7. Procedure for Failed Checks

If any check fails during either a changeover or routine inspection, the following steps must be taken immediately:

1. Restrict access — place cover on and lock the tub. Display an "Out of Service" notice if available.

2. Record the failure — log exact readings and the nature of the failure in SafeWater.PRO.

3. Take corrective action — dose chemicals, clean filter, or address the issue as appropriate. Wear correct PPE at all times.

4. Allow circulation — run jets for at least 30 minutes after any chemical adjustment.

5. Retest and record — test the water again and log results. If within range, update tub status to "fit for use".

6. Notify the duty manager — regardless of whether the issue is resolved, always report failed checks to your duty manager on the day they occur.

7. Escalate if unresolved — if the issue cannot be corrected (e.g., persistent cloudiness, equipment failure, shell damage), the tub must remain out of service until a competent person has assessed and resolved it.

⚠ IMPORTANT: Never "top up and hope for the best." The correct sequence is always: test, dose, circulate, retest, confirm. No shortcuts, and no guest access until readings are verified and recorded.

8. Record Keeping

All hot tub checks, readings, and actions must be recorded in SafeWater.PRO at the time they are carried out. Records must include the date and time of each check, the name of the team member carrying out the check, all water chemistry and temperature readings, any corrective actions taken, and the final status of the tub. These records are retained for a minimum of five years in line with HSG282 guidance and are subject to review by the site safety advisor.

9. Filter Cleaning, Drying & Allocation

⚠ CRITICAL — UKAS COMPLIANCE

Each hot tub on site is allocated two named filter cartridges. These filters must only ever be used in the specific hot tub they are assigned to. Filters must never be swapped between tubs, even temporarily. This limits the risk of cross-contamination between units, and ensures that if a UKAS-accredited water sample returns adverse findings for a specific tub, only the filters allocated to that tub need to be replaced.

Filter Identification: Both filters must be clearly labelled with the name or reference number of the hot tub. Labels should be checked on every changeover to confirm the correct filter is being fitted. If a label is missing or illegible, replace it before the filter is put back into service. Filter allocation records should be maintained in SafeWater.PRO.

Cleaning Procedure:

1. Remove the filter cartridge and rinse under running water to remove loose debris.

2. Soak in an approved filter cleaning solution for the time specified by the manufacturer. Do not use household products, bleach, or washing-up liquid.

3. After soaking, rinse thoroughly under clean running water, working between the pleats to remove all residue.

4. Visually inspect for damage, tearing, discolouration, or degradation. If damaged or at end of 3-month cycle, dispose and replace with a new cartridge — labelled to the correct hot tub before first use.

5. Shake off excess water and place in a clean, dry, well-ventilated area to air dry completely. Filters must not be stored damp.

Drying & Storage: Cleaned filters must be allowed to dry fully before being stored or refitted. A damp filter can harbour bacteria and compromise water quality. Filters should be dried in a clean area away from chemicals, dust, and direct sunlight. Once dry, store in a clean bag or container clearly labelled with the hot tub name, ready for the next changeover.

3-Month Replacement Cycle: Regardless of visible condition, both filters allocated to each hot tub must be replaced with new cartridges every 3 months. New filters must be labelled to the correct tub before first use. Replacement dates should be recorded in SafeWater.PRO to ensure the cycle is tracked and no filter exceeds its service life.

UKAS Sample Failures: If a UKAS-accredited water sample returns findings that require remedial action for a specific hot tub, both filters allocated to that tub must be disposed of immediately and replaced with new cartridges. Because filters are never shared between tubs, this action is contained to the affected unit only. The replacement must be recorded in SafeWater.PRO along with the details of the UKAS finding and corrective actions taken.

⚠ IMPORTANT: Filters must never be moved between hot tubs. Cross-use of filters risks spreading contamination between units and undermines the ability to isolate and respond to adverse UKAS sample results.

10. Training Requirements

All team members carrying out hot tub checks must have received training covering: water chemistry testing and interpretation, safe handling of hot tub chemicals (including COSHH awareness), the correct use of PPE, operation of the specific hot tub models on site, use of SafeWater.PRO for recording checks and reporting issues, and the emergency procedures for chemical spills or contamination incidents. Training records are held on SafeSystems.PRO and must be kept up to date.
