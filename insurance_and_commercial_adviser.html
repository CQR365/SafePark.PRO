Insurance and Commercial Adviser is not a title defined in law. Two things in law bear on it. First, advising on or arranging a particular contract of insurance by way of business is a regulated activity, and no one may carry on a regulated activity in the United Kingdom unless authorised or exempt; general advice that does not relate to a particular contract is not regulated. Second, the duties stay with the Company: it is the Company, as the insured, that must give insurers a fair presentation of the risk and must hold employers' liability insurance. The adviser advises. The Managing Director decides.

Authority of the post

The organisation document GSS-COM-ORG-001 Draft 3 gives this post no authority. The lines below state that position and are proposed wording for the written scope of engagement.

  • Proposed: give advice to the Managing Director, in writing where it will be relied on.
  • Proposed: speak to the Company's broker and insurers for information where the Managing Director asks.
  • No authority to decide for the Company, to bind it to any contract or insurance, to instruct its staff, or to discharge any of its duties.
