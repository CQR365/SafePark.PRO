1. Module Overview

The cozzZstays.pro module is designed to ensure all accommodation units meet safety and compliance standards before guest arrival. This module provides a comprehensive 9-point safety checklist that must be completed during each changeover period.

Purpose

The accommodation safety inspection serves several critical purposes:

  • Ensure guest safety across all accommodation units
  • Maintain regulatory compliance with health and safety legislation
  • Identify maintenance issues before they become hazards
  • Create an auditable record of safety inspections
  • Protect both guests and staff through systematic safety checks

When to Complete Inspections

An accommodation safety inspection must be completed:

  • During every changeover between guest stays
  • Before any unit is marked as ready for occupation
  • After any maintenance work has been completed on a unit
  • Following any reported incidents or concerns

2. Accessing the Module

The cozzZstays.pro module is accessed through the main SafeSystems.PRO dashboard. You must be logged in with valid credentials to use this module.

Login Requirements

To access the module, you need:

  • A valid SafeSystems.PRO user account
  • An assigned park location
  • Appropriate access permissions for accommodation inspections

Navigation

From the main dashboard, locate and select the cozzZstays.pro tile. The module will load and automatically detect your assigned location. Admin users may have the option to select from multiple locations.

Important: You cannot access this module directly via URL. Always access through the main SafeSystems.PRO dashboard to ensure proper authentication.

Follow these steps to complete an accommodation safety inspection:

1

Select the date of the changeover inspection using the date picker. This should be the actual date the inspection is being performed.

2

3

4

Work through each of the nine safety checks. Tick each checkbox only when you have physically verified that all elements of that check are satisfactory. See Section 4 for detailed guidance on each check.

5

6

7

Click Complete and Submit Inspection. You will be presented with a training agreement declaration that you must acknowledge before final submission.

8
Confirm with Your Initials

Enter your initials to confirm the training declaration and complete the submission. Your inspection will then be recorded in the system.

4. The 9-Point Safety Checklist

Each safety check covers specific areas that must be physically inspected. Only tick a check when you have verified all elements listed are satisfactory.

Check 1: Fire Safety Systems

This check ensures all fire detection, suppression, and evacuation equipment is present and functional.

What to Verify:
  • Smoke alarms are present and functional (test button produces audible alarm)
  • Fire extinguishers are present, correctly mounted, and pressure gauge shows green/charged
  • Escape routes are clear of obstructions and accessible
  • Emergency lighting is operational (where fitted)
  • Fire blankets are available in kitchen areas (where required)

Check 2: Gas Safety

This check covers all gas installations including LPG bottles, connections, appliances, and detection equipment.

What to Verify:
  • Interior LPG connections are secure with no visible damage
  • Exterior gas bottle connections are secure and properly fitted
  • Gas appliances (cooker, heater, boiler) operate correctly when tested
  • Ventilation grilles are unobstructed and clear of debris
  • Carbon monoxide detectors are present and functional
  • Gas shut-off valves are accessible and clearly marked
  • Exterior gas bottle storage is secure and compliant with regulations
  • No gas odours are detected anywhere in or around the unit

Check 3: Electrical Safety

This check covers all electrical installations, fixtures, and portable appliances both inside and outside the unit.

What to Verify:
  • RCD (residual current device) protection is operational - test and reset
  • Interior sockets are undamaged with no scorch marks or loose fittings
  • Exterior sockets are undamaged and have weatherproof covers
  • Interior lighting systems are functional in all rooms
  • Exterior security lighting is operational
  • Portable appliances appear safe with no damaged cables or plugs
  • No evidence of circuit overloading (multiple adapters, extension leads)
  • Exterior electrical connections are weatherproof and secure

Check 4: Water Systems

This check covers all water supply, heating, and drainage systems throughout the unit and exterior areas.

What to Verify:
  • Hot water temperature is appropriate (hot but not scalding)
  • Water pressure is adequate at all taps and shower
  • No visible leaks under sinks, around toilets, or from pipework
  • Exterior water taps are secure and not dripping
  • External water connections are properly fitted
  • Legionella controls are in place (run all outlets if unit has been vacant)
  • Pressure relief valves are present and not discharging
  • Drainage systems are clear (sinks, shower, toilet flush properly)

Check 5: Structural Safety

This check covers the physical structure of the unit including doors, windows, steps, decking, and handrails.

What to Verify:
  • All doors open, close, and lock securely
  • All windows open, close, and lock securely
  • Exterior steps are stable with no loose treads or rot
  • Decking is stable, not slippery, and free from damage
  • Handrails are secure and properly fixed at all steps and raised areas
  • Door handles and locks are operational
  • Exterior door mats are secure and present no trip hazard
  • No visible structural damage to walls, roof, skirting, or foundations

Check 6: Security Systems

This check ensures the unit is secure and that guests can safely access and secure their accommodation.

What to Verify:
  • Entry door controls (locks, latches, chains) are functional
  • All window locks are secure and operational
  • Exterior security lighting activates correctly
  • Access routes to the unit are well-lit and clear of hazards
  • Safe storage areas (if provided) are secure
  • Guest safety information is visible and up to date
  • Exterior door handles operate correctly from both sides

Check 7: Emergency Equipment

This check ensures guests have access to emergency information and communication in case of emergency.

What to Verify:
  • Emergency contact information is clearly displayed
  • Contact numbers for reception/management are visible
  • Emergency services information (999) is displayed
  • Communication devices (phone signal or provided phone) are functional
  • Evacuation procedures are posted in a visible location
  • Assembly point information is included in guest information

Check 8: General Condition

This check covers the overall condition and cleanliness of the unit and its immediate exterior areas.

What to Verify:
  • Interior is clean, tidy, and presentable for guests
  • Exterior decking, steps, and handrails are in good condition
  • Door mats are clean and properly positioned
  • Access routes to the unit are clear and safe
  • Exterior lighting is functional
  • No visible maintenance issues inside or outside the unit
  • Guest welfare considerations are met (heating works, ventilation adequate)
  • Any previously identified hazards have been removed or reported

Check 9: Pest Control and Bug Indicators

This check requires thorough inspection for any signs of pest activity, with particular attention to bed bug indicators.

What to Verify:
  • Bug indicator monitors (if installed) checked for signs of activity
  • Mattress thoroughly inspected - check all surfaces, seams, piping, and tags
  • Bed frame inspected including slats, joints, and crevices
  • Headboard checked front, back, and mounting points
  • Bed base and surrounding area inspected
  • Bedside furniture and drawers checked inside and underneath
  • Upholstered furniture examined, including seams and cushion crevices
  • Carpet edges and skirting boards checked for signs of pest activity
  • No evidence of pest droppings, shed skins, or live insects found
  • Any findings are documented and reported immediately

Tip: Work systematically through the unit. Start at the entrance and work clockwise through each room, checking all relevant items for each safety category as you go. This prevents you from missing areas.

5. Handling Uncompleted Checks

There may be legitimate reasons why a particular safety check cannot be completed. The system requires you to document these reasons.

When a Check Cannot Be Completed

If you cannot complete a safety check, do not tick the checkbox. The system will automatically display a reason field for any unchecked items.

Valid Reasons for Not Completing a Check

  • Equipment not installed in this unit type (e.g., no gas supply)
  • Area inaccessible due to ongoing maintenance work
  • Specialist inspection required (e.g., electrical testing)
  • Item already flagged and awaiting repair

Documenting Your Reason

When a check is left uncompleted, you must:

  1. Enter a clear explanation in the reason text field
  2. Be specific about why the check could not be completed
  3. Include any relevant details (work order numbers, dates, etc.)
  4. Flag the issue for action if follow-up is required

Important: You cannot submit an inspection with unchecked items unless you have provided a valid reason for each one. This ensures accountability and creates a clear audit trail.

6. Flagging Issues for Follow-Up

When you identify an issue that requires follow-up action, you must flag it using the Action Required feature.

When to Flag Action Required

Use the Action Required flag when you identify:

  • Safety hazards that could not be immediately resolved
  • Maintenance issues requiring repair before guest arrival
  • Equipment failures or damage
  • Compliance issues that need management attention
  • Any situation where the unit should not be occupied until resolved

How to Flag an Issue

  1. Tick the Action Required checkbox in the red-bordered section
  2. The action details field will appear
  3. Describe the issue clearly and specifically
  4. State what action needs to be taken
  5. Include the location within the unit if relevant

Writing Effective Action Descriptions

Your action description should include:

Element Example
What is the issue Smoke alarm in main bedroom not responding to test
Where is it located Main bedroom ceiling, above bed
What action is needed Replace battery or unit before guest arrival
Urgency level Must be resolved before unit can be occupied

Tip: Flagged issues appear in the Maintenance module dashboard for follow-up. Be as specific as possible to help the maintenance team understand and resolve the issue quickly.

7. Completing Your Submission

When you click Complete and Submit Inspection, a training agreement modal will appear requiring your acknowledgement.

Training Agreement Declaration

By submitting the inspection, you declare that:

  • You have received appropriate training in accommodation services and safety procedures
  • You are competent to perform accommodation safety inspections and identify hazards
  • You understand fire safety, gas safety, electrical safety, and water safety requirements
  • You will report any safety concerns or maintenance issues immediately
  • You acknowledge responsibility for guest safety and regulatory compliance
  • You will follow all safe working procedures and emergency protocols

Confirming Your Submission

  1. Read the training agreement declaration carefully
  2. Tick the checkbox to confirm you agree
  3. Enter your initials in the confirmation field (minimum 2 characters)
  4. Click the Confirm and Submit button

Your inspection will be saved to the system with a timestamp and your details as the inspector.

Important: Your initials serve as your electronic signature. Only enter your own initials and only when you have personally completed the inspection.

9. Your Responsibilities

As an accommodation inspector, you have specific responsibilities that ensure guest safety and regulatory compliance.

Before Each Inspection

  • Ensure you have access to all areas of the unit
  • Have appropriate tools available (torch, test equipment if required)
  • Know how to contact maintenance for urgent issues
  • Be familiar with the location of emergency equipment

During Each Inspection

  • Complete all checks thoroughly - do not rush
  • Only tick items you have physically verified
  • Document any concerns, even minor ones
  • Flag anything that could affect guest safety
  • Take photographs of significant issues if possible

After Each Inspection

  • Ensure the inspection is fully submitted
  • Follow up on any flagged actions
  • Communicate urgent issues to your supervisor immediately
  • Do not mark a unit as ready if safety issues remain unresolved

Ongoing Requirements

  • Maintain your competency through regular training updates
  • Report any changes to equipment or procedures
  • Raise concerns about your ability to complete inspections safely
  • Keep your login credentials secure and do not share them

Remember: Your inspection is the last line of defence before a guest occupies the unit. Your thoroughness and attention to detail directly protects guest safety and wellbeing.

1. Module Overview

The safemaint.pro module provides a comprehensive maintenance management system for tracking, managing, and reporting on maintenance activities across your holiday park or accommodation sites. It integrates with other SafeSystems.PRO modules to capture issues flagged during inspections.

Purpose

The maintenance management system serves several critical purposes:

  • Centralise all maintenance requests in one location
  • Track work from initial report through to completion
  • Capture flagged issues from safety inspections automatically
  • Record time, costs, and materials for each job
  • Prioritise work based on urgency and safety implications
  • Generate reports for management and compliance purposes
  • Create an auditable trail of maintenance activities
  • Enable proactive maintenance planning

Key Features

  • Dashboard Overview - See pending, in-progress, and completed work at a glance
  • Flagged Items Integration - Issues flagged in other modules appear automatically
  • Request Submission - Easy form to log new maintenance needs
  • Status Tracking - Full workflow from submission to completion
  • Cost and Time Recording - Track labour hours and materials costs
  • Receipt Upload - Attach purchase receipts for expense tracking
  • Reporting - Generate summaries by category, location, or time period
  • Data Export - Download records as CSV for external analysis

Important: The maintenance module is a critical link between identifying issues and resolving them. All flagged items from safety inspections should be actioned through this system to maintain a complete audit trail.

2. Accessing the Module

The safemaint.pro module is accessed through the main SafeSystems.PRO dashboard.

Login Requirements

To access the module, you need:

  • A valid SafeSystems.PRO user account
  • Appropriate access permissions for maintenance operations
  • Assignment to a park location

Navigation

From the main dashboard:

  1. Click on the safemaint.pro tile or navigate via the SafeCore sidebar
  2. The module opens showing the Dashboard view by default
  3. Use the navigation buttons to access different sections

Module Sections

The module is organised into four main sections:

4. Flagged Items from Inspections

When issues are flagged as "Action Required" during safety inspections in other modules (such as accommodation inspections, play park checks, or hot tub inspections), they automatically appear in the maintenance dashboard.

How Flagged Items Work

  1. An inspector identifies an issue during a routine inspection
  2. They tick "Action Required" and describe the problem
  3. The flagged item appears in the safemaint.pro dashboard
  4. Maintenance staff can see what needs attention
  5. Work is tracked through to completion

Flagged Items Display

Each flagged item shows:

  • Source module (where the issue was found)
  • Location or unit affected
  • Description of the issue
  • Date and time flagged
  • Inspector who identified it

Actioning Flagged Items

When you action a flagged item:

  1. Review the issue description and any photos attached
  2. Assess the priority and resources needed
  3. Either convert to a maintenance request or mark as resolved
  4. Update the status to track progress

Note: Flagged items represent potential safety issues. They should be reviewed and actioned promptly to maintain safe conditions for guests and staff.

6. Request Form Fields Explained

Understanding each field helps ensure requests contain all necessary information.

Request Title

A brief, descriptive summary of the maintenance need.

Best Practice:
  • Keep it concise but specific
  • Include the item/area affected
  • Mention the problem type

Good: "Leaking shower in Lodge 24" or "Broken swing seat - Main playground"

Poor: "Needs fixing" or "Problem in lodge"

Category

The type of maintenance work required.

Options Available:
  • Electrical - Lighting, sockets, wiring, appliances
  • Plumbing - Pipes, taps, toilets, drains, water heaters
  • Heating/HVAC - Boilers, radiators, air conditioning, ventilation
  • Grounds and Landscaping - Gardens, paths, fencing, outdoor areas
  • Building Structure - Walls, roofs, windows, doors, decking
  • Safety Equipment - Fire equipment, first aid, safety systems
  • Cleaning - Deep cleaning, specialist cleaning needs
  • Equipment Repair - Appliances, machinery, leisure equipment
  • Other - Anything not fitting above categories

Location

Where the maintenance work is needed.

Options Available:
  • Reception
  • Restaurant/Bar
  • Pool Area
  • Play Park
  • Toilet Blocks
  • Caravan Pitch
  • Static Accommodation
  • Maintenance Shed
  • Car Park
  • Site Roadways
  • Other Location

For accommodation units, add the specific unit number in the description.

Priority Level

How urgent the work is.

Priority Definitions:
  • Low - Routine maintenance - No immediate impact. Can be scheduled within normal work plans. Example: Touch-up painting, minor cosmetic repairs.
  • Medium - Non-urgent repair - Should be addressed within a few days. Example: Dripping tap, stiff door lock, minor appliance issue.
  • High - Urgent/Safety issue - Requires prompt attention, same day if possible. Example: No hot water, heating failure, broken window, safety equipment issue.

Detailed Description

Full information about the maintenance need.

What to Include:
  • Exactly what is wrong or needs doing
  • Specific location (room, area, unit number)
  • Any safety concerns
  • When the issue was discovered
  • Whether it affects guests currently
  • Any temporary measures already taken
  • Access requirements or restrictions

Reported By and Contact

Who submitted the request and how to reach them.

Purpose:
  • Enables follow-up questions about the issue
  • Allows notification when work is complete
  • Creates accountability trail

8. Status Workflow

Requests progress through a defined workflow from submission to completion.

Pending

What it means: Request has been submitted but work has not yet started.

Actions available:

  • Move to "In Progress" when work begins
  • Move to "Pending (with reason)" if waiting for materials, contractor, etc.
  • Move to "Completed" if resolved quickly
In Progress

What it means: Work is actively being carried out.

Actions available:

  • Move to "Completed" when work is finished
  • Move to "Pending" if work is paused (awaiting parts, etc.)
  • Update with progress notes and estimated completion
Completed

What it means: Work has been finished and verified.

Required information:

  • Hours taken to complete
  • Materials cost (if applicable)
  • Completion notes (optional)
  • Receipt uploads (if applicable)

10. Recording Completed Work

When marking work as Completed, you must provide information about the work done.

Required Information

Hours Taken to Complete

The total time spent on this job.

Guidance:
  • Enter time in hours (use decimals for partial hours, e.g., 2.5)
  • Include all time: travel, preparation, actual work, cleanup
  • For multi-day jobs, enter total accumulated time
  • This data feeds into time tracking reports

Materials Cost

Approximate cost of materials used.

Guidance:
  • Enter the total cost of parts and materials
  • Include consumables (screws, sealant, etc.)
  • Exclude labour costs (tracked separately via hours)
  • If exact cost unknown, provide best estimate

Receipt Upload

Attach photos of receipts for materials purchased.

Guidance:
  • Take clear photos of all receipts
  • Multiple receipts can be uploaded
  • Accepted formats: images and PDF
  • Receipts provide verification for cost tracking

Completion Notes

Optional notes about the work completed.

What to Include:
  • Summary of work carried out
  • Any parts replaced or installed
  • Recommendations for future maintenance
  • Any ongoing concerns or follow-up needed

8. Section 4: The 8-Point Safety Checklist

This is the core of the inspection. Each item must be assessed and marked as Pass, Fail, or N/A (Not Applicable). Only mark Pass when you have physically verified the item is satisfactory.

Check 1: Visual Inspection - Equipment and Infrastructure

Check all equipment for visible damage, wear, or deterioration.

What to Verify:
  • No visible cracks, splits, or breaks in any equipment
  • No rust, corrosion, or structural deterioration
  • All bolts and fixings appear present and in place
  • No sharp edges, protruding bolts, or exposed metal
  • Wooden components free from splinters or rot
  • Plastic components not cracked, faded, or brittle
  • Rope and net elements not frayed or damaged
  • Paint/coating intact with no peeling exposing hazards

If failed, examples to record: "Cracked swing seat", "Loose bolts on slide", "Rust on frame joints"

Check 2: Surface and Fall Zone Inspection

Ensure surfaces are level, clean, and provide adequate impact protection.

What to Verify:
  • Impact-absorbing surface is present under and around all equipment
  • Surface depth is adequate (for loose-fill surfaces)
  • No compaction or displacement creating hard spots
  • Surface extends to required distance from equipment (fall zones)
  • No gaps, holes, or trip hazards in the surface
  • Rubber tiles/matting not lifted, curled, or loose
  • Surface free from standing water or excessive mud
  • No contamination (glass, animal waste, needles, litter)

If failed, examples to record: "Surface compacted under swings", "Rubber tile lifted near slide", "Insufficient bark depth"

Check 3: Equipment Function and Safety Check

Test moving parts, check fasteners, verify safety features.

What to Verify:
  • Swing seats move freely without excessive squeaking or resistance
  • Swing chains/ropes not twisted, tangled, or wrapped over bar
  • Roundabouts rotate smoothly without jamming
  • See-saws move freely and return to level
  • Spring riders spring back properly
  • Slide surfaces smooth with no obstructions
  • Safety barriers and handrails secure when pushed/pulled
  • No entrapment hazards (head, finger, clothing traps)
  • All caps and covers in place over bolt ends

If failed, examples to record: "Swing bearing seized", "Roundabout shaft grinding", "Handrail loose on climbing frame"

Check 4: Perimeter Fencing and Boundaries

Check fencing integrity, secure boundaries, and containment.

What to Verify:
  • All fence panels secure and upright
  • No gaps under fencing where children could crawl through
  • No gaps between panels where children could squeeze through
  • Fence posts firmly fixed in ground
  • No damage from vandalism, weather, or vehicle impact
  • No sharp edges or protruding fixings on fencing
  • Fencing height adequate for containment purpose
  • No climbable features on outside of boundary fence near roads

If failed, examples to record: "Gap under fence near gate", "Panel damaged by wind", "Post leaning"

Check 5: Gate Function and Security

Check gate operation, self-closing mechanism, and latches.

What to Verify:
  • Gate opens and closes freely without sticking
  • Self-closing mechanism working (gate returns to closed)
  • Latch engages properly when gate closes
  • Latch height appropriate (child-resistant but adult accessible)
  • No finger-trap hazards in hinges or latch mechanism
  • Gate frame and hinges secure
  • Gate does not drag on ground
  • No gap under gate allowing exit

If failed, examples to record: "Self-closer not working", "Latch broken", "Gate dragging on ground"

Check 6: Safety Signage and Information

Check all signs are present, visible, and in good condition.

What to Verify:
  • Playground rules sign displayed and legible
  • Age restriction/recommendation signs visible
  • Emergency contact information displayed
  • Site owner/operator contact details shown
  • Signs not faded, damaged, or obscured
  • Signs securely fixed and not vandalised
  • Any equipment-specific warnings in place
  • No dogs sign displayed (if applicable)

If failed, examples to record: "Rules sign faded and illegible", "Emergency contact sign missing", "Age sign vandalised"

Check 7: Environmental Hazards and Cleanliness

Check for debris, hazards, vandalism, or contamination.

What to Verify:
  • No broken glass on surfaces or equipment
  • No litter that could cause harm (cans, bottles, food waste)
  • No animal waste (dog fouling, bird droppings on equipment)
  • No evidence of drug paraphernalia (needles, foil, etc.)
  • No graffiti containing offensive content
  • No vandalism damage creating hazards
  • No overhanging branches or vegetation encroaching
  • No wasp nests, ant hills, or pest issues
  • No standing water creating slip hazards or mosquito breeding

If failed, examples to record: "Broken bottle on bark surface", "Dog waste by entrance gate", "Wasps nest under platform"

Check 8: Accessibility and General Condition

Verify accessibility features and overall condition.

What to Verify:
  • Pathways to playground accessible and clear
  • Wheelchair access route available (if provided)
  • Accessible equipment functioning (if provided)
  • Seating for carers/parents in good condition
  • Bins present and not overflowing
  • Overall appearance tidy and well-maintained
  • No trip hazards on access routes
  • Adequate drainage - no flooding of access areas

If failed, examples to record: "Wheelchair ramp blocked by equipment", "Bench broken", "Path flooded"

7. Understanding Severity Levels

Selecting the correct severity level is crucial for prioritisation and response. Choose the level that best matches the actual or potential impact of the incident.

Low Severity

Definition: Minor incident with no injury and minimal impact on operations.

Examples:

  • Minor property damage with no safety implications
  • Near miss with low potential for harm
  • Equipment malfunction causing no danger
  • Minor customer complaint about safety perception
  • Small spill quickly contained
  • False fire alarm with known cause

Response: Standard investigation, address within normal operations. Management notified within 24 hours.

Medium Severity

Definition: Moderate incident requiring first aid or having noticeable operational impact.

Examples:

  • Minor injuries requiring first aid only (cuts, bruises, minor burns)
  • Slips, trips, or falls with minor injuries
  • Equipment failure affecting service delivery
  • Near miss with moderate potential for harm
  • Security breach with no immediate threat
  • Customer requiring first aid treatment
  • Minor food safety issue identified and contained

Response: Priority investigation required. Management notified within 4 hours. May require follow-up actions.

High Severity

Definition: Serious incident requiring medical attention or having significant operational impact.

Examples:

  • Injuries requiring professional medical attention (doctor, A&E visit)
  • Significant property damage
  • Water safety incident with potential for harm
  • Fire (contained) or significant smoke
  • Aggressive behaviour requiring intervention
  • Suspected food poisoning
  • Serious security breach
  • Major equipment failure affecting safety

Response: Immediate management notification required. Formal investigation mandatory. Senior management informed within 1 hour.

Critical Severity

Definition: Major incident requiring emergency services or with severe, potentially life-threatening impact.

Examples:

  • Serious injuries requiring ambulance
  • Life-threatening situations
  • Uncontrolled fire requiring fire service
  • Drowning or near-drowning
  • Violent assault
  • Major structural failure
  • Any fatality
  • Mass casualty event
  • Major environmental incident

Response: Emergency response activated. Immediate senior management and director notification. Potential HSE/RIDDOR reporting required. Crisis management procedures may be invoked.

Important: When in doubt, select a higher severity level. It is always better to escalate an incident that turns out to be less serious than to under-report a significant event. The severity can be adjusted later during investigation if appropriate.

8. Writing an Effective Description

The incident description is the most important part of your report. A well-written description enables effective investigation, appropriate follow-up, and provides essential evidence for legal and insurance purposes.

What to Include

Use the "5 Ws and 1 H" approach:

  • Who - Who was involved? Who witnessed it? Who responded?
  • What - What exactly happened? What was the outcome? What injuries or damage occurred?
  • When - When did it happen? What time? How long did it last?
  • Where - Where exactly did it occur? Be as specific as possible.
  • Why - What were the contributing factors? (Do not speculate - state only known facts)
  • How - How did the incident unfold? What was the sequence of events?

Writing Guidelines

  • Write in clear, factual language
  • Use chronological order - describe events as they happened
  • Avoid speculation, assumptions, or blame
  • Use objective terms (e.g., "the guest fell" not "the guest was careless")
  • Include specific details that would help an investigator
  • Describe what you observed, not what you assumed
  • Note any statements made by those involved (use quotation marks for direct quotes)
  • Include relevant conditions (weather, lighting, equipment state)
  • Be thorough - more detail is better than less

Example of a Good Description

"At approximately 14:30 on 15 December 2025, a female guest (approximately 40 years old, staying in Lodge 12 with booking reference HH12345) slipped on the pool deck approximately 3 metres from the entrance to the changing rooms. The deck surface was wet from recent rainfall - the pool had been closed for 30 minutes due to weather but guests were still accessing the changing facilities. The guest was wearing flip-flop style sandals at the time of the fall. She fell onto her right side, landing on her right hip and elbow. The guest reported immediate pain in her right hip. First aid was administered by John Smith (Duty Manager) who applied an ice pack to the affected area. The guest declined an ambulance but stated she would visit her GP on return home. She was able to walk with assistance. The area was immediately dried by maintenance staff and additional 'Caution Wet Floor' signage was placed at both entrances to the pool deck. The guest's husband (Mr J Brown) was present and witnessed the entire incident. Weather conditions: overcast with recent rain shower. Pool deck last inspected at 13:00 - no issues noted at that time."

Example of a Poor Description

"Guest fell by the pool. She wasn't looking where she was going. We helped her up and she said she was fine. Gave her some ice."

Why this is poor: No specific time, no location details, no description of conditions, blame implied ("wasn't looking"), no witness information, no detail on injuries or treatment, no follow-up actions documented.

Immediate Actions Field

Document all actions taken immediately after the incident:

  • First aid provided (what treatment, by whom)
  • Emergency services called (which service, arrival time, outcome)
  • Area secured or isolated
  • Equipment switched off or isolated
  • Other people moved to safety
  • Supervisor/manager notified
  • Evidence preserved (photos taken, equipment retained)
  • Temporary controls put in place

Witnesses Field

Record details of anyone who witnessed the incident:

  • Full name if known
  • Contact details (phone, email, accommodation number)
  • Their role (guest, staff, contractor, visitor)
  • Brief note of what they saw

2. What is COSHH

COSHH stands for Control of Substances Hazardous to Health. It is the law that requires employers to control substances that are hazardous to health. You can prevent or reduce workers' exposure to hazardous substances by finding out what the health hazards are, deciding how to prevent harm to health, providing control measures, and ensuring they are used and maintained.

Substances Covered by COSHH

COSHH covers a wide range of substances including:

Cleaning products and detergents
Paints, varnishes and solvents
Adhesives and sealants
Pesticides and herbicides
Fuels and lubricants
Pool and spa chemicals
Bleach and disinfectants
Acids and alkalis
Fumes, dusts and vapours
Biological agents
Holiday Park Context

In a holiday park environment, you will typically encounter hazardous substances in maintenance (paints, solvents, lubricants), housekeeping (cleaning chemicals, bleach), pool and hot tub management (chlorine, pH adjusters, algaecides), groundskeeping (pesticides, fertilisers), and food service areas (cleaning and sanitising agents).

Substances Not Covered by COSHH

Some substances have their own specific regulations:

Lead Control of Lead at Work Regulations 2002
Asbestos Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012
Radioactive substances Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017

3. Legal Requirements

The Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (as amended) place specific duties on employers regarding hazardous substances.

Regulation Requirement

Legal Consequences

Failure to comply with COSHH regulations can result in enforcement notices from the HSE, prosecution and unlimited fines, and imprisonment in serious cases. The HSE can also issue prohibition notices stopping work until compliance is achieved.

What a COSHH Assessment Must Include

A suitable and sufficient COSHH assessment must:

Identify the hazardous substances present or likely to be present
Consider the risks these substances present to health
Consider the way the substance is used and the likelihood of exposure
Identify all persons who may be exposed
Record the findings and the steps taken to control exposure
Be reviewed regularly and whenever there is reason to suspect it is no longer valid

2. Why Contractor Management Matters

Under UK health and safety legislation, you have legal duties towards contractors working on your premises that are just as important as those towards your own employees. Poor contractor management can result in serious incidents, legal liability, and significant fines.

Legal Framework

Several pieces of legislation govern your responsibilities towards contractors:

Legislation Key Requirement
Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 Duty to ensure the health and safety of all persons affected by your work activities, including contractors
Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 Requirement to coordinate with contractors and share relevant safety information
Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 Specific duties when construction work is undertaken, including contractor competency checks
Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 Duty to coordinate with contractors regarding fire safety arrangements
Important

The HSE (Health and Safety Executive) states that clients who engage contractors can be held liable for accidents caused by contractor incompetence if they failed to carry out reasonable checks before engaging them.

IR35 Compliance

Since April 2021, medium and large private sector businesses are responsible for determining the IR35 status of contractors. Getting this wrong can result in:

Unpaid tax and National Insurance contributions
Interest on late payments
Potential penalties of up to 100% of unpaid tax

The IR35 assessment section in this module helps you document the factors that determine employment status.

18. Legal Requirements

This section summarizes the key legal requirements relating to contractor management that safecontractor.pro+ helps you meet.

Health and Safety at Work Act 1974

Section 3 requires employers to conduct their undertaking in such a way as to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that persons not in their employment (including contractors) are not exposed to risks to their health or safety.

Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999

Regulation 11 requires employers to cooperate with other employers sharing a workplace (including contractors) and coordinate measures for health and safety. Regulation 12 requires provision of comprehensible information to other employers about risks and protective measures.

Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015

For construction work, these regulations impose duties on clients to make suitable arrangements for managing projects including ensuring competence of contractors, providing pre-construction information, and appointing principal designers and contractors where applicable.

Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007

An organisation can be guilty of corporate manslaughter if the way its activities are managed or organised causes a death and amounts to a gross breach of duty of care. This includes failures in contractor management that lead to fatalities.

Off-payroll Working Rules (IR35)

Since April 2021, medium and large private sector organisations are responsible for determining whether the off-payroll working rules apply to their contractors. The IR35 assessment section helps document the factors considered in making this determination.

Record Keeping

The Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR require that personal data is kept secure and only retained for as long as necessary. Contractor records should be retained for at least 6 years after the contractor relationship ends, or longer if required for ongoing projects or legal claims.

1. Module Overview

The safewater.pro (HT) module is designed to manage hot tub safety inspections and water quality monitoring across your holiday park or accommodation sites. This module ensures compliance with HSG282 (Health and Safety Executive guidance on managing hot tubs) and helps prevent waterborne illness including Legionella.

Purpose

Hot tub safety inspections serve several critical purposes:

  • Protect guest health by maintaining safe water quality
  • Ensure compliance with HSG282 and public health regulations
  • Prevent Legionella and other waterborne diseases
  • Document chemical dosing and water treatment activities
  • Create an auditable record of all hot tub maintenance
  • Identify equipment issues before they become serious problems

Inspection Frequency Requirements

Hot tubs require regular inspection to maintain safe operating conditions:

Inspection Type Frequency Purpose
Daily Check 1 Every morning before use Physical inspection and water testing
Daily Check 2 As required (afternoon/evening) Chemical dosing and adjustment
Changeover Between guest stays Complete water change and deep clean

Important: Hot tubs must be inspected at least once daily when in use. Failure to maintain proper water quality can result in serious illness to guests and significant legal liability.

5. The 10-Point Physical Inspection Checklist

Each item in the physical inspection must be assessed and marked as Pass, Fail, or N/A. Only mark Pass when you have confirmed the item meets the required standard.

Check 1: Water Temperature

Verify the water temperature is within safe operating range.

What to Verify:
  • Water temperature is between 37-40 degrees Celsius
  • Temperature display on the hot tub matches your thermometer reading
  • Temperature is consistent across the tub (no cold spots)
  • Heating system is functioning correctly

Check 2: Chemical Levels (pH, Sanitiser)

Test and verify water chemistry is within safe parameters.

What to Verify:
  • pH level is between 7.2 and 7.6
  • Sanitiser (bromine) level is between 3.0 and 5.0 ppm
  • Total alkalinity is between 80 and 120 ppm
  • Test strips or reagents are within date and stored correctly
  • Record actual readings in the Chemical Level Readings section

Check 3: Water Volume

Ensure the hot tub has adequate water level for safe operation.

What to Verify:
  • Water level is at the correct operating height
  • Jets are fully submerged and not drawing air
  • Skimmer is functioning at the correct level
  • No significant water loss indicating leaks

Check 4: General Surface Clean

Assess the cleanliness of all hot tub surfaces.

What to Verify:
  • Shell interior is clean and free from debris
  • No visible algae growth or discolouration
  • Seats and floor area are clean
  • Jet faces are clean and free from buildup
  • No floating debris in the water

Check 5: Water Line Clean

Inspect the water line for scum, buildup, or biofilm.

What to Verify:
  • Water line is free from scum ring or buildup
  • No biofilm visible at water level
  • Edge of shell at water line is clean
  • If buildup present, clean before marking as Pass

Check 6: Access and Egress

Ensure safe access to and from the hot tub.

What to Verify:
  • Steps are stable and secure
  • Handrails (if fitted) are secure
  • Non-slip surfaces are in good condition
  • Access pathway is clear of hazards
  • Adequate lighting for evening use

Check 7: General Condition of Lid

Inspect the hot tub cover for damage and functionality.

What to Verify:
  • Lid is intact with no tears or damage
  • Lid fits securely on the hot tub
  • Lid locks or straps are functional
  • Lid is not waterlogged or excessively heavy
  • Underside of lid is clean

Check 8: General Condition of Hot Tub

Overall assessment of the hot tub equipment and shell.

What to Verify:
  • Shell has no cracks, chips, or damage
  • Jets are all functioning correctly
  • Control panel is working and responsive
  • Pump and motor sounds normal
  • No unusual odours from equipment
  • All fittings are secure

Check 9: General Condition of Surrounding Area

Assess the immediate area around the hot tub.

What to Verify:
  • Decking or paving is in good condition
  • No trip hazards present
  • Area is clean and tidy
  • Drainage is adequate and clear
  • Privacy screening is intact (if applicable)
  • No overhanging hazards

Check 10: Signage in Place

Verify all required safety signage is displayed.

What to Verify:
  • Hot tub safety rules sign is displayed and legible
  • Maximum user capacity is clearly shown
  • Temperature warning sign is present
  • Emergency contact information is displayed
  • Health warnings (pregnancy, heart conditions, etc.) are visible
  • All signage is clean and not faded

6. Changeover Additional Items

When performing a Changeover inspection, three additional items must be completed in addition to the standard 10-point checklist.

Check 11: Complete Water Change

Confirm the hot tub water has been completely drained and refilled.

What to Verify:
  • All water has been completely drained
  • Fresh water has been added to the correct level
  • Water source is from approved supply
  • Initial chemical treatment has been added to fresh water
  • Water has been heated to operating temperature

Check 12: Filter Clean/Replace

Confirm filters have been properly cleaned or replaced.

What to Verify:
  • Filter cartridge has been removed and inspected
  • Filter has been thoroughly cleaned with appropriate cleaner
  • Filter has been rinsed completely
  • If damaged or worn, filter has been replaced
  • Filter is correctly reinstalled
  • Record filter condition in notes if concerns exist

Check 13: Deep Clean and Sanitise

Confirm the hot tub has been thoroughly cleaned and sanitised.

What to Verify:
  • Shell has been scrubbed and cleaned while empty
  • Water line has been descaled if necessary
  • Jets and fittings have been cleaned
  • Pipework has been flushed with pipe cleaner
  • Cover underside has been cleaned
  • Surrounding area has been cleaned
  • Sanitiser shock dose has been added to fresh water

Important: A complete water change should be performed at least weekly, or more frequently during periods of heavy use. Between guest stays, a full changeover including water change is mandatory.

7. Recording Chemical Additions

When adding chemicals to the hot tub (typically during Daily Check 2), you must record exactly what was added and in what quantity.

Chemical Categories

The module organises chemicals into categories for easy recording:

Bromine Sanitisers

  • Bromine Tablets (20g) - Slow-dissolving tablets for continuous sanitisation
  • Bromine Granules - Fast-acting granular sanitiser for quick adjustments
  • Sodium Bromide (Bromine Bank) - Establishes bromine reserve in water

pH Adjusters

  • pH Plus (Increaser) - Raises pH when reading is below 7.2
  • pH Minus (Reducer) - Lowers pH when reading is above 7.6

Water Balance

  • Total Alkalinity Increaser - Raises alkalinity to stabilise pH
  • Calcium Hardness Increaser - Prevents corrosion from soft water

Shock Treatments

  • Non-Chlorine Shock (Oxidiser) - Removes organic waste, clears cloudy water
  • Bromine Shock - Activates bromine reserve, kills bacteria

Water Clarity and Maintenance

  • Water Clarifier - Clumps fine particles for filter removal
  • Anti-Foam / Foam Reducer - Eliminates surface foam
  • Scale and Stain Preventer - Prevents mineral buildup
  • Metal Remover / Sequestrant - Removes dissolved metals
  • Filter Cleaner - For cleaning filter cartridges
  • Pipe/Line Flush Cleaner - For cleaning internal pipework

How to Record Additions

  1. Tick the checkbox next to each chemical you have added
  2. Enter the quantity in the field that appears (e.g., "3 tablets", "15g", "30ml")
  3. Use the Chemical Addition Notes field for any relevant observations
  4. Record updated chemical readings after allowing time for distribution

Safety Warning: Never mix chemicals directly. Always add chemicals to the water separately, allowing each to disperse before adding another. Follow manufacturer instructions for dosing quantities.

8. Recording Chemical Level Readings

Accurate recording of water test results is essential for compliance and guest safety. Always use calibrated test equipment.

Required Readings

Parameter How to Test Where to Record
pH Level Test strip or electronic meter pH Level field
Total Alkalinity Test strip or titration kit Total Alkalinity (ppm) field
Sanitiser Level Bromine test strip or DPD test Sanitizer Level (ppm) field
Water Temperature Thermometer or hot tub display Water Temperature field

Testing Best Practices

  • Take samples from at least 30cm below the surface
  • Test away from jets and water inlets
  • Wait 10-15 minutes after adding chemicals before re-testing
  • Store test strips in a cool, dry place
  • Check expiry dates on all test equipment
  • Rinse testing equipment between uses

9. Target Chemical Levels

Maintain water chemistry within these target ranges for safe operation:

Parameter Target Range Action Required If Outside Range
pH Level 7.2 - 7.6 Below 7.2: Add pH Plus. Above 7.6: Add pH Minus
Total Alkalinity 80 - 120 ppm Below 80: Add Alkalinity Increaser. Above 120: Partial water change
Bromine (Sanitiser) 3.0 - 5.0 ppm Below 3.0: Add bromine. Above 5.0: Allow to dissipate or dilute
Water Temperature 37 - 40 degrees C Adjust thermostat. Never exceed 40 degrees C

Critical: If sanitiser levels fall below 3.0 ppm, the hot tub must not be used until levels are corrected. Low sanitiser levels allow harmful bacteria to multiply rapidly.

Understanding pH and Sanitiser Relationship

pH affects how effectively your sanitiser works:

  • At pH 7.2-7.6, bromine is most effective at killing bacteria
  • High pH (above 7.8) significantly reduces sanitiser effectiveness
  • Low pH (below 7.0) can cause skin irritation and equipment corrosion
  • Always adjust pH first, then check sanitiser levels

1. Module Overview

docsafe.pro is a comprehensive document management system designed to organise, store, and track all your compliance and safety documentation. The module provides secure cloud storage through Firebase, automatic expiry tracking, and organised category management to ensure you always know where your important documents are and when they need renewal.

Key Features

Organised Categories 11 pre-configured categories for common document types
Custom Folders Create your own categories with custom colours and icons
Cloud Storage Secure Firebase cloud storage accessible from any device
Expiry Tracking Visual alerts for documents approaching expiry
Multiple File Types Support for PDF, Word, images, and text files
Reference Numbers Automatic reference numbering with category prefixes

What Documents to Store

docsafe.pro is designed to store all your compliance and safety documentation including certificates, licences, policies, risk assessments, training records, insurance documents, and inspection reports. Having all documents in one accessible location simplifies audits, inspections, and day-to-day management.

2. Why Document Management Matters

Effective document management is essential for regulatory compliance, operational efficiency, and risk management. Many safety and compliance requirements mandate that specific documents are maintained, accessible, and kept up to date.

Compliance Benefits

Audit Readiness All documents organised and instantly accessible for inspections
Expiry Management Never miss a certificate renewal with visual expiry tracking
Evidence Trail Maintain records of compliance for legal protection
Version Control Upload dates show when documents were added

Operational Benefits

Centralised Access One location for all important documents
Cloud Storage Access from any device with internet connection
Time Savings No more searching through filing cabinets or email attachments
Team Access All authorised users can view documents when needed
Legal Document Retention

Many documents have legal retention requirements. For example, accident records must be kept for 3 years (or longer for incidents involving minors), training records should be retained for the duration of employment plus a reasonable period, and some certificates like EICR reports should be kept until the next inspection. Always check specific retention requirements for each document type.

11. Deleting Documents

Documents can be deleted when they are no longer needed. However, care should be taken to ensure legal retention requirements are met before deletion.

Deleting a Document

  1. Locate the document in its category
  2. Click the delete button (X) in the Actions column
  3. Confirm the deletion when prompted
  4. The document will be permanently removed
Warning: Permanent Deletion

Deleted documents cannot be recovered. Before deleting, ensure: the document is no longer required for compliance, legal retention periods have been met, you have a backup if the document may be needed in future, and you have appropriate authority to delete records.

When to Delete Documents

Consider deletion when:

Replaced by newer version Superseded certificates or policies
Retention period expired After legal retention requirements met
No longer applicable For equipment or locations no longer in use
Duplicate upload Same document uploaded multiple times

13. Expiry Tracking and Alerts

docsafe.pro provides visual expiry tracking to help you manage document renewals proactively.

How Expiry Tracking Works

When you set an expiry date on a document, the system calculates the status based on the current date and displays colour-coded indicators:

Status Colour Condition
Valid Green More than 30 days until expiry
Expiring Soon Yellow/Amber 30 days or less until expiry
Expired Red Past the expiry date
Not Set Grey No expiry date configured

Managing Expiring Documents

  1. Regularly review documents showing yellow (expiring soon) status
  2. Arrange for renewal before the expiry date
  3. Upload the new certificate/document
  4. Update the expiry date on the new document
  5. Consider archiving or deleting the old document
Proactive Renewal

Don't wait until documents expire. Build in lead time for renewals - some certifications require booking inspectors weeks or months in advance. Set calendar reminders 60-90 days before expiry for critical documents.
