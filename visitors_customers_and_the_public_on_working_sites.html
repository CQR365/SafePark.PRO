Risk assessments / Visitors, customers and the public on working sites

RISK ASSESSMENT

Visitors, customers and the public on working sites

Download this assessment (Word)

Draft 0.1 for assessment by the competent person. Every control, score and legal reference is to be checked before adoption, and the points to confirm are to be settled first.

Task descriptionThe presence on GSS working sites of people who do not work there day to day: visitors to the offices, marina and boatyard customers, customers' own contractors working on boats, visiting technicians, auditors, surveyors and advisers, delivery drivers on foot, members of the public, and GSS office staff who go into the yard, onto vessels or to client sites for stock checks, measurement, valuation, tender and survey visits
Assessment typeGeneric. Requires a task and location specific supplement before the work begins. For a visit to a client's or another party's site the supplement is that site's own induction and rules, together with a short visit plan.
Assessed byRisk and Compliance Manager (draft for assessment)
Approved byProposed: Silvers Yard Manager and Operations Manager
Location and scopeThe whole of the Silverhills site: offices, stores, workshops, the Silvers yard and sheds, the boat hoist and crane working areas, boats stored ashore, pontoons and berths, the quayside, car parks and site roads; GSS vessels when visited alongside; civils and dive sites; and client sites, structures and vessels visited by GSS office staff. Keeping out people who have no business on site is assessed in the new draft assessment Site security and unauthorised access, and the separation of vehicles and people in the new draft assessment Workplace transport and deliveries. This assessment covers the control of people who are on site with permission, which neither covers as such.
Main equipment usedThe sign in record and visitor badges, the visitor and contractor induction, site rules and site plan, signs, barriers, marked walkways and exclusion zone markers, visitor personal protective equipment (high visibility vests, hard hats, lifejackets), radios, ladders and access staging provided for boats ashore, and the customer and contractor agreements.
Persons at riskMarina and boatyard customers and their families, including children; customers' own contractors; visiting technicians, auditors, surveyors, inspectors, insurers, accountants and advisers; delivery drivers and couriers on foot; members of the public; GSS office staff including the Financial Controller, the accounts staff, the Commercial Manager and the Senior Quantity Surveyor when visiting yards, vessels and sites; and yard personnel and plant operators whose work is affected by people nearby.
Related permit or procedurePRC 19 Induction and PRC 17 Health and Safety Signage, both drafts. To confirm which draft procedure governs the control of visitors, customers and customers' own contractors, since none has been seen that does so as such. A hot work permit is required for any hot work by a customer or a customer's contractor, under RA 27 Hot Work.

1. Legal and guidance framework

RegimeInstrument
Occupier's duty, ScotlandOccupiers' Liability (Scotland) Act 1960. Section 2(1) requires an occupier to show towards a person entering the premises such care as in all the circumstances is reasonable to see that the person will not suffer injury or damage by reason of dangers due to the state of the premises or to anything done or omitted to be done on them. Section 1 treats a person occupying or having control of land or other premises as the occupier, and extends the Act to fixed or moveable structures including any vessel. Section 2(3) provides that no obligation is owed in respect of risks the person has willingly accepted as theirs.
Persons not employedHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, section 3(1). Every employer must conduct the undertaking in such a way as to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that persons not in their employment who may be affected are not exposed to risks to their health or safety. Customers, their contractors, visitors, the public and the self employed on site are all within this duty.
Control of premisesHealth and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, section 4. A person who has, to any extent, control of non domestic premises made available to others as a place of work, or of the access to them or of plant or substances in them, must take such measures as are reasonable for a person in that position to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that they are safe and without risks to health. This applies to the yard and berths when a customer's contractor works there.
Information for visiting workersManagement of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, regulation 12. The Company must give the employer of any person from an outside undertaking who works on its premises, and any self employed person working there, comprehensible information on the risks arising from the Company's undertaking and the measures taken, and must see that such persons are given appropriate instructions and can identify the persons nominated to carry out evacuation. Regulation 8 requires access to danger areas to be restricted to those who have been adequately instructed.
Fire, ScotlandFire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006 apply to the premises, and visitors and customers are among the persons whose safety from fire the duty holder must secure. The sections that define relevant persons were not read for this draft and the wording is to be confirmed.
ReportingReporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013. HSE guidance states that a work related accident to a member of the public is reportable where the person is taken directly from the scene to hospital for treatment, and that the death of any person in a work related accident is reportable. An injury to a customer or visitor is therefore recorded and assessed for reporting in the same way as one to a worker.
MarineMerchant Shipping and Fishing Vessels (Health and Safety at Work) Regulations 1997 and the Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers apply aboard GSS vessels, where the Master controls who comes aboard, enforced by the MCA. What they require for visitors aboard was not read and is to be confirmed. Whether the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 govern visitors to GSS civils sites is likewise to be confirmed.

2. Hazard assessment

Rating = likelihood (L) x severity (S), each 1 to 5: LOW 1 to 6, MEDIUM 8 to 10, HIGH 12 to 25. Severity assumes the controls fail, so it is the same before and after controls.

1. No control over who is on site: no sign in, no host, no induction and no record, so that visitors, customers and contractors move about a working yard and marina unknown to those running it

Potential consequence. A person walks into a lift, a plant route or an unsafe area because nobody told them. Nobody knows they are on site in a fire. Crushing, a fall, drowning, death, and no record of what they were told.

Persons at risk. All visitors, customers, customers' contractors, visiting staff

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Every person who is not based on the site reports to the office on arrival and signs in, giving the time, who they are visiting or which boat they are working on, and a contact number, and signs out on leaving. Signs at each entrance say so and show the way to the office on a safe route. Each visitor has a named host from GSS who is responsible for them while they are on site. Each is given a short induction suited to why they are there: a business visitor to the office is told the fire alarm, the assembly point and not to enter the yard unescorted; a person going into the yard, onto the pontoons or aboard is given the site rules, the plan showing walkways, lifting areas and no go areas, the protective equipment required, and how to raise an alarm. The induction is recorded and repeated at a set interval for regular customers and contractors. Visitors wear a badge or vest that shows they are visitors and whether they are escorted. Staff are told to speak to anybody they do not recognise or who is without a badge and to walk them back to the office. Berth holders and storage customers receive the site rules with their agreement and sign for them.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set up the sign in record and badges at the main office and the Silvers office, write the short visitor induction and the yard induction, and decide how regular customers are signed in outside office hours. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager and the Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

2. People on foot who do not know the site sharing the yard and site roads with the boat hoist, cranes, the telehandler, lorries, vans and customers' cars and trailers, including office staff walking between the main office and the Silvers office

Potential consequence. A person is struck or run over by plant or a reversing vehicle, or crushed against a boat or building. Death.

Persons at risk. Visitors, customers, customers' contractors, delivery drivers on foot, office and accounts staff

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. The arrangements in the new draft assessment Workplace transport and deliveries apply and are relied on. In addition, for people who do not know the site: a marked pedestrian route runs from the car park to each office and to the pontoon access without crossing the hoist run or the main plant route where the layout allows, and where a crossing cannot be avoided it is marked, signed and has clear sight lines. Visitors park in a signed visitor area and do not drive into the yard without permission. Visitors to the yard are escorted by their host unless they have had the yard induction, and all wear high visibility clothing beyond the office and car park. Plant operators stop when a person on foot is within the distance set for that plant and do not move again until the person is clear and has been seen. Movements of the hoist and long vehicle manoeuvres are attended by a banksman who keeps people back. Staff who walk between the two offices use the marked route, wear high visibility clothing, and are included in the yard induction. Customers are asked not to bring vehicles and trailers into the yard during lifts and launches, and site speed is limited and signed.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Walk the routes from each car park and entrance to each office, the sheds and the pontoons, mark a pedestrian route on the ground and on the site plan, and identify where it crosses plant routes. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Visitors and customers in or near a lifting area: beneath or beside the boat hoist or a crane lift, at the slipway or dock during a launch or recovery, or approaching their own boat while it is in the slings

Potential consequence. A person is struck by the load or the machine, or crushed if a boat shifts, a sling slips or a support fails. Death.

Persons at risk. Customers, especially the owner of the boat being lifted, their families and contractors, visitors, bystanders

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Every lift, launch, recovery and boat move has an exclusion zone set by the person in charge of the lift, marked with barriers or cones before the lift starts, and nobody other than the lifting team enters it. Customers are told this in the site rules and again when the lift is booked, and are told where they may stand to watch. The owner does not ride on the boat, climb aboard, reach for lines or fenders, or walk under or beside the boat while it is in the slings or on the move, and nobody works on or under a boat until it has been set down, supported and released by the yard team. A member of the lifting team is given the task of watching for people approaching, and the person in charge stops the lift if anybody enters the zone. Lifts are timed, where tide and programme allow, for when fewer people are about, and neighbouring owners are told when a lift will pass their boat. Visitors under escort are kept out of the yard during lifts unless the lift is the purpose of the visit, in which case the host keeps them at the viewing point. RA 21a Shore Lifting applies to the lift itself.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Set the exclusion distance for each type of lift and move, provide barriers and signs for it, and add the customer rules for lifts and launches to the booking confirmation. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Assistant Yard Supervisor. Target: to be set on adoption.

4. Falls into the water from pontoons, the quay edge, slipway, gangways and boats, by people unused to them, including children, people carrying gear, and people alone or at night

Potential consequence. Cold water shock, inability to climb out, drowning. Head injury or crushing between a boat and the pontoon. Death.

Persons at risk. Customers and their families, customers' contractors, visitors, office staff on stock checks and site visits

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. RA 18 Working on or Near Water applies to everybody working for or visiting on behalf of GSS: a lifejacket is worn on the pontoons, at an unprotected quay edge, on the slipway and when boarding, and the host provides one to any visitor and checks that it is fitted and fastened. Site rules state that customers are strongly expected to wear lifejackets on the pontoons, that children must wear them and be supervised by an adult at all times, and that customers' contractors must wear them. Pontoons, gangways and access bridges are kept in repair, with a surface that grips when wet, clear of hoses, cables, lines and stores, and are lit. Ladders or other means of climbing out of the water are fitted at intervals along the pontoons and quay and are marked so that they can be seen from the water, and lifebuoys or throw lines are in place and checked. Edges that the public can reach and that have no need to be open are fenced. Escorted visitors are kept back from the edge and do not step onto a vessel without the Master's or owner's permission and a proper means of boarding. In ice, high wind or heavy swell the manager restricts access to the pontoons.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Survey the pontoons, quay and slipway for ladders, rescue equipment, lighting, edge protection and surface condition, and decide the Company's rule on lifejackets for customers. Hold a stock of visitor lifejackets in a range of sizes with a record of their inspection. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

5. Customers and customers' own contractors working unsupervised on boats stored ashore: access by ladder, work at deck height, hot work, grinding, paint and antifouling, trailing electrical leads, gas, and interference with the boat's supports

Potential consequence. A fall from the deck or a ladder. Fire spreading between boats packed close together. A boat falling from its props or cradle onto the person or a neighbour. Electric shock. Exposure of others to dust and fumes. Death.

Persons at risk. Customers, customers' contractors, neighbouring boat owners, yard personnel

Initial rating. L 4 x S 5 = 20 HIGH

Control measures. Work on boats ashore is allowed only on the terms of the customer agreement and the site rules, which each customer signs. A customer's contractor must be approved before starting: they register at the office, show insurance and, for the trade concerned, evidence of competence, state what work they will do, receive the yard induction and the information on site risks, and sign in each day. Props, cradles, shores and chocks are set by the yard team only and are never moved, adjusted or removed by a customer or contractor; a request to move one goes to the yard. Access to the deck is by a ladder tied at the top or by staging provided or approved by the yard, and nobody works on deck beside an unguarded edge without a means of preventing a fall. Hot work, including welding, burning and the use of a blowlamp or heat gun, needs a hot work permit from the yard under RA 27 Hot Work. The rules state where and how sanding, grinding, spraying and antifouling may be done so that dust and paint are contained. Electrical leads are of a type suited to outdoor use, protected by a residual current device and not left live on an unattended boat. Gas cylinders are turned off when the boat is left. Yard staff walk the storage area each day, stop unsafe work and report it to the manager, who deals with the customer under the agreement.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Write or revise the customer agreement, the contractor registration and the yard rules to include these controls, and set up the register of approved contractors. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

6. Visiting technicians, engineers, inspectors and auditors working or walking in plant areas, roofs, stores, server and switch rooms, and on vessels, without knowing the hazards there or being known to those working nearby

Potential consequence. A fall through or from a roof, electric shock, contact with moving machinery, being shut in a space, or being struck by a lift or vehicle. Death. Damage to safety critical equipment that is not reported.

Persons at risk. Third party technicians working on IT, communications, alarm, lifting, electrical and other equipment; certifying bodies, auditors, surveyors and inspectors; insurers and advisers

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. A visiting technician is booked in advance through the GSS person who ordered the work, who is the host. Before the visit the host gives the technician's employer the information on site risks that bear on the job and asks for their risk assessment and method where the work is more than routine. On arrival the technician signs in, is inducted, and agrees with the host exactly where they will work, what will be isolated and by whom, and what else is going on nearby. The supervisor of the area is told that they are there. A technician does not go onto a roof, into a roof space, a plant room, a switch room, a tank or a vessel's machinery space alone or without the permit or isolation that GSS requires of its own people; RA 32 Working at Height, RA 37 Confined Spaces and the new draft assessment Electrical work and isolation apply as they would to anyone. Auditors, surveyors and inspectors who need to see operations are escorted throughout by a person who knows the area, wear the protective equipment required there, and do not handle plant or equipment. Before leaving, the technician tells the host what was done and what has been left isolated or out of service, and signs out.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Tell every person who orders outside services that they are the host and what that involves, and issue a short information sheet on site risks for visiting contractors. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with each department manager and IT. Target: to be set on adoption.

7. GSS office staff going into stores, the yard and onto vessels for stock and fixed asset checks, to deliver or collect papers, or to see operations, without the training or equipment of those who work there

Potential consequence. A fall when boarding or below decks, a fall into the water, being struck by plant, injury from lifting or moving stock, or entry into an unsafe space. Serious injury or death.

Persons at risk. Financial Controller, Accounts Assistant and accounts team, Office Manager, office administrators, advisers accompanying them

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Stock and asset checks are planned with the manager of the area or the Master of the vessel, for a time when lifts, bunkering, diving and other high risk work are not going on. Office staff who make these visits are given the yard induction and are escorted by a person from that area, who opens stores, moves anything that has to be moved and reaches anything at height. The visitor counts and records and does not climb racking, lift heavy items, operate plant or enter engine rooms, tanks, lockers or holds; items there are counted by the crew or from records. A vessel is boarded only alongside, by its proper means of access, with the Master's or engineer's permission, wearing a lifejacket and suitable footwear, and RA 1 Boarding at Pier applies. Stout closed shoes, a high visibility vest and, where required, a hard hat are kept at the office for these visits, in sizes that fit the people who use them. Where the same result can be had from a photograph, a tag scan or a count by the stores or crew, checked on a sample basis, that is done in preference to a visit. RA 20 Manual Handling applies to any handling.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Agree with the Financial Controller how stock and asset checks will be done, which need a visit and which can be done from records, and give the yard induction to the office staff concerned. Owner: Financial Controller with the Operations Manager and the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

8. Tender, survey, measurement and valuation visits by GSS staff to client sites, harbours, structures and vessels they do not know: unfinished or damaged structures, open edges beside water, tidal areas, work going on around them, often alone

Potential consequence. A fall from a quay, jetty or structure into the water or onto the foreshore, being cut off by the tide, being struck by site plant, or collapse of unfinished work underfoot. Death, with nobody aware.

Persons at risk. Commercial Manager, Civils Manager, Senior Quantity Surveyor, Project Manager, Diving Manager, Operations Manager

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. Each visit is planned: who is going, where, who the contact on the site is, what the visitor needs to see and reach, the state of the tide and the weather, and what access the client is providing. The visitor reports to the person in control of the site, takes that site's induction, follows its rules and stays with the guide provided. On a GSS civils site the visitor reports to the site supervisor and is treated as any other visitor. The visitor looks, measures and photographs from safe, finished and protected areas, and does not go onto unfinished work, damaged structures, unprotected edges, ladders, scaffolds, the foreshore or a vessel unless the access has been confirmed as safe by the person in control and the protection required is in place. Measurements that would need exposure of that kind are taken from drawings, by drone or instrument, or by the working team. A lifejacket is worn within reach of the water, with suitable footwear, high visibility clothing and a hard hat. Visits to remote or tidal locations are not made alone; for any visit made alone, RA 40 Lone Worker applies, with a named contact and a time to check in. The visitor carries a charged telephone and has the authority to cut the visit short. RA 18 Working on or Near Water, RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls and RA 59 Access and Egress apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Issue a one page visit plan for tender, survey and measurement visits and hold personal protective equipment, including lifejackets, for the staff who make them. Owner: Civils Manager with the Risk and Compliance Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

9. Slips, trips and falls on yard and marina surfaces by people who do not know them: uneven or loose ground, hoses, shore power cables and mooring lines, wet or icy pontoons, steps, poor lighting, and ladders and staging at boats

Potential consequence. Fractures, head injury, and a fall into the water where the trip is on a pontoon or quay.

Persons at risk. Customers and their families, visitors, customers' contractors, office staff

Initial rating. L 4 x S 4 = 16 HIGH

Control measures. The routes that visitors and customers use, from the car parks to the offices, the sheds, the storage rows and the pontoons, are identified and kept to a higher standard than the working areas: level, drained, free of stored material and lit during the hours when people are on site. Hoses and cables are routed along edges or overhead and are not laid across walkways, and shore power leads on pontoons are kept tidy at the pedestal. Pontoons and steps have a surface that grips when wet, are cleaned of weed and algae, and are gritted or closed in ice. Changes of level are marked. Customers are told in the site rules to keep the ground around their boat clear and to remove their own ladders and trestles from walkways. Yard staff clear obstructions when they see them and report surface defects, which are logged and repaired. Escorted visitors are warned of the ground conditions and are asked to wear flat, closed footwear. RA 33 Slips Trips and Falls and RA 59 Access and Egress apply.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 4 = 8 MEDIUM

Further action required. Inspect the visitor and customer routes for surface, lighting and obstructions, and set up a defect log with target dates. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

10. Visitors and customers in a fire or other emergency: not knowing the alarm, the way out or the assembly point, being aboard or under a boat where the alarm cannot be heard, and not being counted

Potential consequence. A person remains in a building, shed or boat, or walks towards the danger. A warden goes back to look for somebody who has already left. Burns, smoke inhalation, death.

Persons at risk. Visitors to the offices, customers on boats ashore and afloat, customers' contractors, visiting technicians

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The induction for every visitor, however short, covers what the alarm sounds like, the nearest way out, the assembly point and the instruction to go there and stay. The host takes their visitor to the assembly point and answers for them at the count. The sign in record is taken to the assembly point and used for the count together with the staff list. Fire action notices and a site plan showing assembly points are displayed in the offices, the sheds and at the head of the pontoons. Because an alarm in a building may not be heard by a person inside a boat or at the far end of the yard, a second means of warning, such as a horn, is used by the wardens and is described in the site rules. Customers staying aboard overnight tell the office, so that their presence is known out of hours. Evacuation drills are held at times when customers and contractors are on site. Visitors who would need help to leave are asked at sign in, and the host arranges it. The new draft assessment Fire and emergency evacuation sets out the arrangements, and for a visitor to the offices RA 31 Office Duties applies.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Confirm how people in the yard, on boats ashore and on the pontoons are warned of a fire, and how those aboard overnight are recorded. Owner: Risk and Compliance Manager with the Silvers Yard Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

11. Children, dogs and members of the public in the yard and marina: families visiting boats, people walking through, sightseers at a lift or launch, and anybody who cannot be expected to recognise the dangers of a working site

Potential consequence. A child runs into the path of plant, climbs a ladder, cradle or stack, or falls from a pontoon. A dog causes a trip or a fall into the water. Death.

Persons at risk. Children, customers' families and guests, members of the public, yard personnel distracted by them

Initial rating. L 3 x S 5 = 15 HIGH

Control measures. The site rules state that children under an age set by the Company are not allowed in the working yard or sheds, that on the pontoons they are supervised by an adult within reach at all times and wear lifejackets, and that dogs are on a lead everywhere on site. Customers are responsible for their guests, who sign in with them. The boundary between the areas the public may use, such as the car park, the office and the pontoon access, and the working yard is marked by signs, barriers or gates, and the working yard is closed to people on foot during lifts. Ladders are removed from or locked off at unattended boats, and plant is left with keys removed and attachments lowered. Staff are told to stop work and speak to an adult at once if a child is seen unsupervised, and to stop plant until the child has been taken out of the area. More care is taken where children are present, because they cannot be expected to recognise the dangers of a working yard. People with no business on site are dealt with under the new draft assessment Site security and unauthorised access.

Residual rating. L 2 x S 5 = 10 MEDIUM

Further action required. Decide the Company's rules on children, guests and dogs, add them to the customer agreement and the signs, and mark the boundary of the working yard. Owner: Silvers Yard Manager with the Silvers Office Manager. Target: to be set on adoption.

3. Monitoring, emergency arrangements and related documents

Monitoring and verification

  • The sign in records at both offices are checked weekly by the office managers for completeness, and sampled each quarter by the Risk and Compliance Manager against contractors and visitors known to have been on site.
  • The Silvers Assistant Yard Supervisor or a nominated person walks the storage rows, sheds and pontoons each working day, records unsafe work by customers or contractors and what was done about it, and reports repeat cases to the manager.
  • Every accident, incident and near miss involving a customer, a visitor, a contractor or a member of the public is reported under PRC 01 in the same way as one involving staff, and is assessed for statutory reporting.
  • The register of approved customers' contractors is reviewed each year for current insurance and evidence of competence.
  • Visitor routes, signs, barriers, pontoon ladders and rescue equipment are inspected monthly and the defects logged and tracked to closure.
  • Visitor lifejackets and other visitor protective equipment are inspected at the interval the maker sets, and the inspection is recorded.
  • This assessment and the site rules are reviewed each year, before each launch and lift out season, and after any incident involving a visitor or customer.

Emergency arrangements

  • Injury to a visitor or customer: give first aid under RA 56 First Aid, call 999 where needed, send a person to the entrance to meet the ambulance, tell the manager, and record the person's details and whether they were taken to hospital from the scene.
  • Person in the water: shout, throw a line or lifebuoy, reach from a secure position, call 999 for the Coastguard, and do not enter the water.
  • Person in a lifting area or the path of plant: the operator stops at once and the lift or movement does not resume until the person in charge of it has confirmed the area is clear.
  • Fire or evacuation: hosts take their visitors to the assembly point, wardens warn people in the yard and on boats by the agreed means, and the sign in record is used for the count. Missing persons are reported to the Fire and Rescue Service with their last known position.
  • Unsupervised child in the working yard: stop plant nearby, stay with the child, and find the adult responsible.
  • A visitor from GSS on a client's site follows that site's emergency instructions and tells their own manager as soon as they are safe.

PPE requirements

For any visitor beyond the offices and car park: a high visibility vest or jacket and flat, closed footwear, with safety footwear and a hard hat where the area or the work nearby requires them. A lifejacket, fitted and fastened, on the pontoons, at an unprotected quay edge, on the slipway and when boarding, in line with RA 18 Working on or Near Water. Eye and hearing protection where the visitor is taken close to work that needs them. The Company holds visitor equipment in a range of sizes and the host issues it and checks it is worn. Customers' contractors provide and wear the equipment their own work requires, and the yard may stop work where they do not.
